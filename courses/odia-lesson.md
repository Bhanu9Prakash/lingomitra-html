# Odia: A pattern-first foundation

This course uses modern standard conversational Odia, broadly the central coastal standard, in Odia script. More formal identity copulas are acknowledged without forcing literary wording into every conversation. Romanisation is readable and spelling-aware: ā, ī, and ū help identify written vowel choices, without claiming that modern Odia has a simple contrastive length system identical to Sanskrit; ṭ, ḍ, ṇ, and ḷ identify retroflex letters; ṛ represents the modified flap letter; a tilde marks nasalisation. Final vowels are retained where represented. Ś and ṣ preserve spelling distinctions and are not a promise that every speaker distinguishes them in speech. The main course uses respectful ଆପଣ, familiar ତୁମେ where labelled, and explicit first-person singular/plural contrasts.

### References and review notes

[Yap and Sahoo, Attitudinal copulas in Odia](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/68392/1/Attitudinal_copulas_SLE_2017.pdf) was consulted for the distinction among identity, locative, and auxiliary uses and the role of person and honorific agreement rather than gender. [Odia grammar overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_grammar) was consulted for nominal case and pronoun forms. Explanations, scenarios, and exercises are original. A fluent reviewer should prioritise optional copula omission, spelling and pronunciation of everyday contractions, object marking, and the register of practical requests.

## Lesson 1: Be here and say how you are

### Outcome

Locate yourself and ask a respectful listener how they are.

### Think before reading

If ଅଛି works with I, will the same form automatically be right with respectful you?

### Build the pattern

ମୁଁ (mũ) means I. ମୁଁ ଏଠାରେ ଅଛି says I am here. ଏଠାରେ (eṭhāre) means here, while ସେଠାରେ (seṭhāre) means there. The presence verb comes at the end, after the person and place. Odia is comfortable with this verb-final information order.

ଆପଣ (āpaṇa) is respectful you and takes ଅଛନ୍ତି (achhanti): ଆପଣ ଏଠାରେ ଅଛନ୍ତି. Familiar ତୁମେ (tume) takes ଅଛ (achha), while ଆମେ (āme), we, takes ଅଛୁ (achhu). These are person and respect distinctions, not male and female forms. Keep the whole pronoun-predicate pair together when changing whom you address.

ଭଲ (bhala) means good or well. ମୁଁ ଭଲ ଅଛି is I am well. କେମିତି (kemiti) asks how, giving ଆପଣ କେମିତି ଅଛନ୍ତି. The nasalisation in ମୁଁ is marked by the tilde in mũ. Odia commonly retains final vowels such as the a in bhala; do not routinely delete them as if reading Bengali.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୁଁ ଏଠାରେ ଅଛି। | Mũ eṭhāre achhi. | I am here. |
| ଆପଣ କେମିତି ଅଛନ୍ତି? | Āpaṇa kemiti achhanti? | How are you? (respectful) |
| ମୁଁ ଭଲ ଅଛି। | Mũ bhala achhi. | I am well. |
| ଆମେ ଏଠାରେ ଅଛୁ। | Āme eṭhāre achhu. | We are here. |
| ତୁମେ ସେଠାରେ ଅଛ। | Tume seṭhāre achha. | You are there. (familiar) |
| ଆପଣ ଏଠାରେ ଅଛନ୍ତି। | Āpaṇa eṭhāre achhanti. | You are here. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not use the ordinary ଅଛି with every respectful subject. Also avoid changing the verb for the speaker’s gender; the relevant distinctions here are person and respect.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I am well.
2. Say: We are here.
3. Say: You are there. (familiar)
4. Say: You are here. (respectful)
5. Say: I am there.
6. Say: You are there. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `ମୁଁ ଭଲ ଅଛି।` - `Mũ bhala achhi.`
2. `ଆମେ ଏଠାରେ ଅଛୁ।` - `Āme eṭhāre achhu.`
3. `ତୁମେ ସେଠାରେ ଅଛ।` - `Tume seṭhāre achha.`
4. `ଆପଣ ଏଠାରେ ଅଛନ୍ତି।` - `Āpaṇa eṭhāre achhanti.`
5. `ମୁଁ ସେଠାରେ ଅଛି।` - `Mũ seṭhāre achhi.`
6. `ଆପଣ ସେଠାରେ ଅଛନ୍ତି।` - `Āpaṇa seṭhāre achhanti.`

### Why these answers work

The place or condition precedes the final presence predicate. The answers select achhi, achhu, achha, or achhanti from the subject. The final two answers transfer the same location pattern from a speaker to a respected listener.

### Retrieve and recombine

Open an imagined phone call by saying how you are and where you are. Ask the listener how they are, using the respectful pronoun and matching predicate.


## Lesson 2: Identify people and objects

### Outcome

Introduce yourself and classify a familiar object in conversational Odia.

### Think before reading

Does every English is express presence at a place, or can it simply link a person with an identity?

### Build the pattern

A conversational identity sentence can omit an explicit present copula: ମୁଁ ଶିକ୍ଷକ, I am a teacher. Formal and emphatic Odia also uses identity copulas such as ଅଟେ; do not conclude that Odia has no copulas. The point is narrower: the short conversational identity pattern used here is complete without inserting ଅଛି as a universal translation of English am.

ଶିକ୍ଷକ (śikṣaka) means teacher, ଛାତ୍ର (chhātra) male student, ବନ୍ଧୁ (bandhu) friend, and ଡାକ୍ତର (ḍāktara) doctor. Some role nouns have gender-specific lexical counterparts; that is different from a verb changing its ending for gender. The pronoun ସେ (se) can refer to he or she.

ଏହା (ehā), this, and ତାହା (tāhā), that, are clear standard demonstratives. Everyday speech also has shorter regional forms. We keep these written forms stable. ଏହା ବହି identifies this as a book. A counted noun phrase such as ଗୋଟିଏ ବହି will make one book explicit later.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୁଁ ଶିକ୍ଷକ। | Mũ śikṣaka. | I am a teacher. |
| ସେ ଡାକ୍ତର। | Se ḍāktara. | He or she is a doctor. |
| ମୁଁ ଛାତ୍ର। | Mũ chhātra. | I am a male student. |
| ଆମେ ବନ୍ଧୁ। | Āme bandhu. | We are friends. |
| ମୁଁ ରୀନା। | Mũ Rīnā. | I am Rina. |
| ଏହା ବହି। | Ehā bahi. | This is a book. |

### Common trap

Do not treat the optional omission in a conversational identity as permission to omit every presence verb. “I am here” still uses the location pattern introduced in Lesson 1.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I am a male student.
2. Say: We are friends.
3. Say: I am Rina.
4. Say: This is a book.
5. Say: I am a doctor.
6. Say: That is a pen.

***Answers:***

1. `ମୁଁ ଛାତ୍ର।` - `Mũ chhātra.`
2. `ଆମେ ବନ୍ଧୁ।` - `Āme bandhu.`
3. `ମୁଁ ରୀନା।` - `Mũ Rīnā.`
4. `ଏହା ବହି।` - `Ehā bahi.`
5. `ମୁଁ ଡାକ୍ତର।` - `Mũ ḍāktara.`
6. `ତାହା କଲମ।` - `Tāhā kalama.`

### Why these answers work

The answers identify a person or thing with a noun expression. There is no present location claim, so the conversational frame does not need ଅଛି. The final two answers extend the pattern to a medical role and a pen.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce yourself by name and by role. Identify two objects. Then say where one of them is, noticing that the location sentence uses a different predicate pattern.


## Lesson 3: Ask who, what, and where

### Outcome

Build identity and location questions by replacing the unknown information.

### Think before reading

Why does a where-question retain a presence predicate while a what-is-this question can use the conversational identity frame?

### Build the pattern

କିଏ (kie) asks who, କ’ଣ (kaṇa) what, and କେଉଁଠି (keũṭhi) where. The apostrophe in କ’ଣ is part of a common written convention, not an extra spoken syllable. A question word takes the position of the missing information: ଏହା କ’ଣ asks what this is.

ଆପଣ କେଉଁଠି ଅଛନ୍ତି asks where a respectful listener is. Because it asks about location, the presence predicate remains and still agrees respectfully. Do not move it to the front just because English begins with where are.

ନାମ (nāma) means name and ଆପଣଙ୍କ (āpaṇaṅka) your respectfully. ଆପଣଙ୍କ ନାମ କ’ଣ is a useful introduction question. This possessive form is not the same as the ordinary subject ଆପଣ. A question can be grammatically short but socially abrupt: asking a name is often more suitable than opening with “Who are you?” when meeting someone casually.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ଏହା କ’ଣ? | Ehā kaṇa? | What is this? |
| ଆପଣ କେଉଁଠି ଅଛନ୍ତି? | Āpaṇa keũṭhi achhanti? | Where are you? (respectful) |
| ଆପଣ କିଏ? | Āpaṇa kie? | Who are you? (respectful) |
| ଆପଣଙ୍କ ନାମ କ’ଣ? | Āpaṇaṅka nāma kaṇa? | What is your name? (respectful) |
| ସେ କେଉଁଠି ଅଛି? | Se keũṭhi achhi? | Where is he or she? (ordinary) |
| ତୁମେ କେଉଁଠି ଅଛ? | Tume keũṭhi achha? | Where are you? (familiar) |

### Common trap

Do not add an English do auxiliary or move the presence verb before the subject. Keep the respectful ending even when the location is unknown.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Who are you? (respectful)
2. Say: What is your name? (respectful)
3. Say: Where is he or she? (ordinary)
4. Say: Where are you? (familiar)
5. Say: What is that?
6. Say: Where are we?

***Answers:***

1. `ଆପଣ କିଏ?` - `Āpaṇa kie?`
2. `ଆପଣଙ୍କ ନାମ କ’ଣ?` - `Āpaṇaṅka nāma kaṇa?`
3. `ସେ କେଉଁଠି ଅଛି?` - `Se keũṭhi achhi?`
4. `ତୁମେ କେଉଁଠି ଅଛ?` - `Tume keũṭhi achha?`
5. `ତାହା କ’ଣ?` - `Tāhā kaṇa?`
6. `ଆମେ କେଉଁଠି ଅଛୁ?` - `Āme keũṭhi achhu?`

### Why these answers work

The identity questions use କିଏ or କ’ଣ, while the location questions retain a final presence predicate. The last two examples transfer a familiar what-question to a farther object and ask about a group with the plural first-person verb.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask a fictional visitor for a name and location. Point to an unfamiliar object and ask what it is. Explain what type of answer each question requires.


## Lesson 4: Two different kinds of not

### Outcome

Distinguish a negative identity from the absence of a person or thing.

### Think before reading

Does “not a book” reject a category, while “no book here” denies existence?

### Build the pattern

ନୁହେଁ (nuhẽ) rejects an identity or classification: ଏହା ବହି ନୁହେଁ, this is not a book. First-person ମୁଁ also uses ନୁହେଁ in the elementary pattern here. Respectful ଆପଣ uses ନୁହନ୍ତି (nuhanti). These are not forms of the ordinary location predicate.

ନାହିଁ (nāhĩ) expresses absence: ଏଠାରେ ପାଣି ନାହିଁ, there is no water here. With a speaker, ମୁଁ ଘରେ ନାହିଁ says I am not at home. A respectful absent person can use ନାହାନ୍ତି (nāhānti). The noun ଘର (ghara), house, takes the locative form ଘରେ (ghare), at home or in the house.

This contrast lets you make a meaningful correction. If somebody labels water as tea, deny the classification with ନୁହେଁ. If no water is available, use ନାହିଁ. Translating both English not and no with a single memorised word would hide this grammatical and semantic distinction.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ଏହା ବହି ନୁହେଁ। | Ehā bahi nuhẽ. | This is not a book. |
| ଏଠାରେ ପାଣି ନାହିଁ। | Eṭhāre pāṇi nāhĩ. | There is no water here. |
| ମୁଁ ଶିକ୍ଷକ ନୁହେଁ। | Mũ śikṣaka nuhẽ. | I am not a teacher. |
| ଆପଣ ଡାକ୍ତର ନୁହନ୍ତି। | Āpaṇa ḍāktara nuhanti. | You are not a doctor. (respectful) |
| ମୁଁ ଘରେ ନାହିଁ। | Mũ ghare nāhĩ. | I am not at home. |
| ଆପଣ ଏଠାରେ ନାହାନ୍ତି। | Āpaṇa eṭhāre nāhānti. | You are not here. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not use ନୁହେଁ for every absence statement or treat ନାହିଁ as the normal identity negative. Decide what is being denied before choosing the predicate.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I am not a teacher.
2. Say: You are not a doctor. (respectful)
3. Say: I am not at home.
4. Say: You are not here. (respectful)
5. Say: There is no tea here.
6. Say: That is not a pen.

***Answers:***

1. `ମୁଁ ଶିକ୍ଷକ ନୁହେଁ।` - `Mũ śikṣaka nuhẽ.`
2. `ଆପଣ ଡାକ୍ତର ନୁହନ୍ତି।` - `Āpaṇa ḍāktara nuhanti.`
3. `ମୁଁ ଘରେ ନାହିଁ।` - `Mũ ghare nāhĩ.`
4. `ଆପଣ ଏଠାରେ ନାହାନ୍ତି।` - `Āpaṇa eṭhāre nāhānti.`
5. `ଏଠାରେ ଚା ନାହିଁ।` - `Eṭhāre chā nāhĩ.`
6. `ତାହା କଲମ ନୁହେଁ।` - `Tāhā kalama nuhẽ.`

### Why these answers work

Category corrections end in ନୁହେଁ or respectful ନୁହନ୍ତି. Absence uses ନାହିଁ and respectful ନାହାନ୍ତି. The final two answers apply absence to a second resource and identity negation to a new object label.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create one false label and one missing-object situation. Correct each using its proper negative. Then deny your presence at a place without denying your identity.


## Lesson 5: Read, eat, and drink as routines

### Outcome

Put the object before an ordinary action verb and preserve respectful agreement.

### Think before reading

Which word should stay at the end when you build “I read books” in Odia?

### Build the pattern

ମୁଁ ବହି ପଢ଼େ (mũ bahi paṛhe) means I read books. Odia's neutral order places the object before the verb. The spelling ପଢ଼ includes the modified consonant; do not drop its dot when typing. Our ṛh helps distinguish it from an ordinary d or dh in transliteration.

କାମ କରେ (kāma kare) means I work or do work. ଖାଏ (khāe) is I eat, and ପିଏ (pie) I drink. This course distinguishes eating rice, ଭାତ ଖାଏ, from drinking tea, ଚା ପିଏ. Do not import a neighboring language's preferred consume verb simply because the English sentence is similar.

Respectful ଆପଣ takes ପଢ଼ନ୍ତି, କରନ୍ତି, ଖାଆନ୍ତି, and ପିଅନ୍ତି. The first-person singular and ordinary third-person forms can coincide in this tense, but respectful forms remain distinct. The subject's gender does not change the verb. These simple forms describe routines and general actions; ongoing activity receives a separate pattern later.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୁଁ ବହି ପଢ଼େ। | Mũ bahi paṛhe. | I read books. |
| ଆପଣ ବହି ପଢ଼ନ୍ତି। | Āpaṇa bahi paṛhanti. | You read books. (respectful) |
| ମୁଁ ଚା ପିଏ। | Mũ chā pie. | I drink tea. |
| ମୁଁ ଭାତ ଖାଏ। | Mũ bhāta khāe. | I eat rice. |
| ମୁଁ କାମ କରେ। | Mũ kāma kare. | I work. |
| ଆପଣ କାମ କରନ୍ତି। | Āpaṇa kāma karanti. | You work. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not erase the written modification in ପଢ଼େ or copy a Hindi gender-marked verb pattern. Also keep ପିଏ for the drinking examples taught here.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I drink tea.
2. Say: I eat rice.
3. Say: I work.
4. Say: You work. (respectful)
5. Say: I drink water.
6. Say: He or she drinks tea. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `ମୁଁ ଚା ପିଏ।` - `Mũ chā pie.`
2. `ମୁଁ ଭାତ ଖାଏ।` - `Mũ bhāta khāe.`
3. `ମୁଁ କାମ କରେ।` - `Mũ kāma kare.`
4. `ଆପଣ କାମ କରନ୍ତି।` - `Āpaṇa kāma karanti.`
5. `ମୁଁ ପାଣି ପିଏ।` - `Mũ pāṇi pie.`
6. `ସେ ଚା ପିଅନ୍ତି।` - `Se chā pianti.`

### Why these answers work

The object stays before the finite action. Respectful subjects select the -ନ୍ତି forms, while the first-person examples use their ordinary singular forms. The final two answers transfer drinking to water and attribute a routine respectfully to another person.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe one reading routine, one food habit, and one drink habit. Then tell a respectful listener that they do the same activities, changing the verb forms where required.


## Lesson 6: Questions and negative habits

### Outcome

Ask a yes-or-no routine question and deny a routine with ନାହିଁ.

### Think before reading

What changes between a question about what you read and a question about whether you read books?

### Build the pattern

A yes-or-no question can add କି (ki) at the end: ଆପଣ ଚା ପିଅନ୍ତି କି. The subject, object, and verb retain statement order. The question particle does not replace respectful agreement.

କ’ଣ asks for a missing object: ଆପଣ କ’ଣ ପଢ଼ନ୍ତି, what do you read. A simple yes does not answer that question because the listener is requesting a category or title. Read the question by locating its information gap rather than assuming all questions ask for confirmation.

Routine negation uses the finite verb followed by ନାହିଁ in the clear written pattern taught here: ମୁଁ ଚା ପିଏ ନାହିଁ. Connected speech may reduce negative forms, but the full form is useful for a learner. It differs from the identity predicate ନୁହେଁ. Build the positive sentence first, preserve its person marking, then add the negative. This keeps respectful ପିଅନ୍ତି intact in a negative routine.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ଆପଣ ଚା ପିଅନ୍ତି କି? | Āpaṇa chā pianti ki? | Do you drink tea? (respectful) |
| ଆପଣ କ’ଣ ପଢ଼ନ୍ତି? | Āpaṇa kaṇa paṛhanti? | What do you read? (respectful) |
| ମୁଁ ଚା ପିଏ ନାହିଁ। | Mũ chā pie nāhĩ. | I do not drink tea. |
| ଆପଣ ବହି ପଢ଼ନ୍ତି କି? | Āpaṇa bahi paṛhanti ki? | Do you read books? (respectful) |
| ମୁଁ ବହି ପଢ଼େ ନାହିଁ। | Mũ bahi paṛhe nāhĩ. | I do not read books. |
| ସେ ଚା ପିଅନ୍ତି ନାହିଁ। | Se chā pianti nāhĩ. | He or she does not drink tea. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not use ନୁହେଁ after a normal routine action as if all English not constructions were alike. A question particle also does not remove the need for a respectful verb.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I do not drink tea.
2. Say: Do you read books? (respectful)
3. Say: I do not read books.
4. Say: He or she does not drink tea. (respectful)
5. Say: I do not work.
6. Say: Do you drink water? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `ମୁଁ ଚା ପିଏ ନାହିଁ।` - `Mũ chā pie nāhĩ.`
2. `ଆପଣ ବହି ପଢ଼ନ୍ତି କି?` - `Āpaṇa bahi paṛhanti ki?`
3. `ମୁଁ ବହି ପଢ଼େ ନାହିଁ।` - `Mũ bahi paṛhe nāhĩ.`
4. `ସେ ଚା ପିଅନ୍ତି ନାହିଁ।` - `Se chā pianti nāhĩ.`
5. `ମୁଁ କାମ କରେ ନାହିଁ।` - `Mũ kāma kare nāhĩ.`
6. `ଆପଣ ପାଣି ପିଅନ୍ତି କି?` - `Āpaṇa pāṇi pianti ki?`

### Why these answers work

The yes-or-no questions keep their action and add final କି; the what-question replaces the object. Negative routines retain the finite verb before ନାହିଁ. The final two answers transfer the same operations to work and water.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask whether someone drinks tea, then imagine a negative reply. Ask what they drink instead. Identify which question asks for confirmation and which asks for content.


## Lesson 7: Familiar people and speaker groups

### Outcome

Use familiar and first-person plural forms without importing gender agreement.

### Think before reading

If I and we are different participants in Odia, must their routine verbs always look identical?

### Build the pattern

ତୁମେ (tume) is familiar you, with କର (kara), ପଢ଼ (paṛha), ଖାଅ (khāa), and ପିଅ (pia) in these routine patterns. ଆପଣ has more explicit honorific forms. Choosing a familiar pronoun is a social decision tied to the relationship; it is not simply a shortcut for any person younger than you.

ଆମେ (āme), we, takes କରୁ (karu), ପଢ଼ୁ (paṛhu), ଖାଉ (khāu), and ପିଉ (piu). Thus Odia marks a first-person singular/plural distinction in these verbs where Bengali commonly does not. The final u is meaningful rather than an optional pronunciation flourish.

ସେ (se) means he or she. An ordinary reference can use କରେ, while respectful reference to the same pronoun can use କରନ୍ତି. Respect is therefore not confined to the visible choice of second-person pronoun. The most intimate ତୁ has its own forms and pragmatic risks, so this foundation recognises it without making it the default production target.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ତୁମେ କାମ କର। | Tume kāma kara. | You work. (familiar) |
| ଆମେ କାମ କରୁ। | Āme kāma karu. | We work. |
| ତୁମେ ବହି ପଢ଼। | Tume bahi paṛha. | You read books. (familiar) |
| ଆମେ ବହି ପଢ଼ୁ। | Āme bahi paṛhu. | We read books. |
| ତୁମେ ଭାତ ଖାଅ। | Tume bhāta khāa. | You eat rice. (familiar) |
| ଆମେ ଭାତ ଖାଉ। | Āme bhāta khāu. | We eat rice. |

### Common trap

Do not assume the same verb serves ମୁଁ and ଆମେ in every Odia tense. Also do not infer gender from ସେ or attach gender agreement to its verb.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: You read books. (familiar)
2. Say: We read books.
3. Say: You eat rice. (familiar)
4. Say: We eat rice.
5. Say: We drink tea.
6. Say: He or she works. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `ତୁମେ ବହି ପଢ଼।` - `Tume bahi paṛha.`
2. `ଆମେ ବହି ପଢ଼ୁ।` - `Āme bahi paṛhu.`
3. `ତୁମେ ଭାତ ଖାଅ।` - `Tume bhāta khāa.`
4. `ଆମେ ଭାତ ଖାଉ।` - `Āme bhāta khāu.`
5. `ଆମେ ଚା ପିଉ।` - `Āme chā piu.`
6. `ସେ କାମ କରନ୍ତି।` - `Se kāma karanti.`

### Why these answers work

Familiar ତୁମେ selects the a-series routine forms, while ଆମେ uses the first-person plural u-series. The final two answers transfer these choices to drinking and respectful third-person work.

### Retrieve and recombine

Recast one work sentence about yourself, your group, a friend, and a respected person. Explain which changes are about number and which about respect.


## Lesson 8: Actions unfolding now

### Outcome

Use the present progressive and distinguish it from a habit.

### Think before reading

What meaning is added when କରେ changes to କରୁଛି?

### Build the pattern

କରୁଛି (karuchhi) means am doing or ordinary is doing. The u element belongs to the imperfective or ongoing pattern, while the final chhi-series carries present person information. ମୁଁ କାମ କରୁଛି describes work unfolding now rather than a general routine.

Respectful ଆପଣ takes କରୁଛନ୍ତି (karuchhanti); familiar ତୁମେ takes କରୁଛ (karuchha); ଆମେ takes କରୁଛୁ (karuchhu). The same distinctions recur with ପଢ଼ୁଛି, am reading, ଖାଉଛି, am eating, and ପିଉଛି, am drinking. The vowel combinations in eating and drinking should be read as part of the actual forms rather than simplified to imitate another language.

ଏବେ (ebe) means now. ମୁଁ ଏବେ କାମ କରୁଛି explicitly supplies the current time, but the progressive construction does not require this word in every sentence. A common current-action negative is କରୁନାହିଁ (karunāhĩ), am not doing. Learn that compact negative alongside the affirmative କରୁଛି rather than assuming the whole affirmative must survive unchanged.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୁଁ କାମ କରୁଛି। | Mũ kāma karuchhi. | I am working. |
| ଆପଣ କାମ କରୁଛନ୍ତି। | Āpaṇa kāma karuchhanti. | You are working. (respectful) |
| ମୁଁ ବହି ପଢ଼ୁଛି। | Mũ bahi paṛhuchhi. | I am reading a book. |
| ସେ ଚା ପିଉଛନ୍ତି। | Se chā piuchhanti. | He or she is drinking tea. (respectful) |
| ତୁମେ ଭାତ ଖାଉଛ। | Tume bhāta khāuchha. | You are eating rice. (familiar) |
| ମୁଁ ଏବେ କାମ କରୁଛି। | Mũ ebe kāma karuchhi. | I am working now. |

### Common trap

Do not use a progressive merely because English contains a present-tense verb. A habit such as reading every day can remain in the simple routine pattern.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I am reading a book.
2. Say: He or she is drinking tea. (respectful)
3. Say: You are eating rice. (familiar)
4. Say: I am working now.
5. Say: We are reading books.
6. Say: I am not working now.

***Answers:***

1. `ମୁଁ ବହି ପଢ଼ୁଛି।` - `Mũ bahi paṛhuchhi.`
2. `ସେ ଚା ପିଉଛନ୍ତି।` - `Se chā piuchhanti.`
3. `ତୁମେ ଭାତ ଖାଉଛ।` - `Tume bhāta khāuchha.`
4. `ମୁଁ ଏବେ କାମ କରୁଛି।` - `Mũ ebe kāma karuchhi.`
5. `ଆମେ ବହି ପଢ଼ୁଛୁ।` - `Āme bahi paṛhuchhu.`
6. `ମୁଁ ଏବେ କାମ କରୁନାହିଁ।` - `Mũ ebe kāma karunāhĩ.`

### Why these answers work

The positive progressive verbs keep their activity stem and person ending. The group form uses -ଛୁ. The current negative uses କରୁନାହିଁ, whose u-based action form combines directly with the negative; ଏବେ makes its current-time meaning explicit.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe three things happening in an imagined room. Then describe the same people’s routines. Listen for the progressive element that disappears in the routine account.


## Lesson 9: Owners and names

### Outcome

Build possessive phrases with mo, tuma, and the respectful āpaṇaṅka.

### Think before reading

Why does the pronoun change from ମୁଁ to ମୋ before a possessed noun?

### Build the pattern

ମୋ (mo) means my before a noun; ମୋର (mora) is a fuller possessive form also used independently or before nouns. This course uses the short ମୋ in common phrases such as ମୋ ବହି, my book, and ମୋ ନାମ, my name. The possessive is not an ordinary nominative subject.

ତୁମ (tuma) means your familiarly, ଆପଣଙ୍କ (āpaṇaṅka) your respectfully, and ଆମ (āma) our. The owner expression precedes its noun. କାହାର (kāhāra) asks whose. ଏହା କାହାର ବହି asks which person the book belongs to without moving the noun to match English order.

A relationship need not be literal ownership. ମୋ ବନ୍ଧୁ means my friend, and ମୋ ନାମ means my name. The same possessive noun phrase can be an identity complement or the subject of a location: ମୋ ବହି ଏଠାରେ ଅଛି. Its internal order remains stable even when its role in the larger sentence changes.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ଏହା ମୋ ବହି। | Ehā mo bahi. | This is my book. |
| ତାହା ଆପଣଙ୍କ କଲମ। | Tāhā āpaṇaṅka kalama. | That is your pen. (respectful) |
| ଏହା କାହାର ବହି? | Ehā kāhāra bahi? | Whose book is this? |
| ମୋ ନାମ ରୀନା। | Mo nāma Rīnā. | My name is Rina. |
| ମୋ ବହି ଏଠାରେ ଅଛି। | Mo bahi eṭhāre achhi. | My book is here. |
| ତାହା ତୁମ କଲମ। | Tāhā tuma kalama. | That is your pen. (familiar) |

### Common trap

Do not place ମୁଁ directly before a noun to mean my. Keep respectful ଆପଣଙ୍କ distinct from the familiar ତୁମ even when no finite verb appears in the phrase.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Whose book is this?
2. Say: My name is Rina.
3. Say: My book is here.
4. Say: That is your pen. (familiar)
5. Say: This is our book.
6. Say: He or she is my friend.

***Answers:***

1. `ଏହା କାହାର ବହି?` - `Ehā kāhāra bahi?`
2. `ମୋ ନାମ ରୀନା।` - `Mo nāma Rīnā.`
3. `ମୋ ବହି ଏଠାରେ ଅଛି।` - `Mo bahi eṭhāre achhi.`
4. `ତାହା ତୁମ କଲମ।` - `Tāhā tuma kalama.`
5. `ଏହା ଆମ ବହି।` - `Ehā āma bahi.`
6. `ସେ ମୋ ବନ୍ଧୁ।` - `Se mo bandhu.`

### Why these answers work

The owner comes before the noun. A location adds its own final ଅଛି, while an identification can remain short. The last two answers extend the possession pattern to a group and to a person described as a friend.

### Retrieve and recombine

Assign a book, pen, and bag to yourself, your group, and the listener. Ask whose the book is and answer. Contrast the subject pronoun with its possessive form.


## Lesson 10: Have something with you

### Outcome

Express available possessions using pākhare and the presence predicate.

### Think before reading

What additional relation does ପାଖରେ add to a plain possessive noun phrase?

### Build the pattern

ପାଖରେ (pākhare) means near or with in relevant possession contexts. ମୋ ପାଖରେ ବହି ଅଛି means I have a book with me. Literally it presents a book as existing by or with the speaker. It is especially useful for objects available now, such as a pen or money.

ଆପଣଙ୍କ ପାଖରେ uses the respectful possessor before the same postposition. The final predicate refers to the available thing, so ଅଛି remains appropriate even though the owner is respected. Compare this with ଆପଣ ଏଠାରେ ଅଛନ୍ତି, where the respected person is directly located.

For absence, replace ଅଛି with ନାହିଁ. ମୋ ପାଖରେ କଲମ ନାହିଁ means I have no pen with me. ଟଙ୍କା (ṭaṅkā) means money or rupees in a numerical context. The construction can report money available on hand without asserting anything about every resource a person owns. Context determines how broad the claim is.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୋ ପାଖରେ ବହି ଅଛି। | Mo pākhare bahi achhi. | I have a book with me. |
| ମୋ ପାଖରେ କଲମ ନାହିଁ। | Mo pākhare kalama nāhĩ. | I have no pen with me. |
| ଆପଣଙ୍କ ପାଖରେ କଲମ ଅଛି କି? | Āpaṇaṅka pākhare kalama achhi ki? | Do you have a pen with you? (respectful) |
| ମୋ ପାଖରେ ପାଣି ଅଛି। | Mo pākhare pāṇi achhi. | I have water with me. |
| ଆପଣଙ୍କ ପାଖରେ ବହି ଅଛି। | Āpaṇaṅka pākhare bahi achhi. | You have a book with you. (respectful) |
| ମୋ ପାଖରେ ଟଙ୍କା ନାହିଁ। | Mo pākhare ṭaṅkā nāhĩ. | I have no money with me. |

### Common trap

Do not change the final ଅଛି to an honorific merely because the possessor is respectful. The available object is the subject of the presence claim in this construction.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Do you have a pen with you? (respectful)
2. Say: I have water with me.
3. Say: You have a book with you. (respectful)
4. Say: I have no money with me.
5. Say: We have a book with us.
6. Say: We have money with us.

***Answers:***

1. `ଆପଣଙ୍କ ପାଖରେ କଲମ ଅଛି କି?` - `Āpaṇaṅka pākhare kalama achhi ki?`
2. `ମୋ ପାଖରେ ପାଣି ଅଛି।` - `Mo pākhare pāṇi achhi.`
3. `ଆପଣଙ୍କ ପାଖରେ ବହି ଅଛି।` - `Āpaṇaṅka pākhare bahi achhi.`
4. `ମୋ ପାଖରେ ଟଙ୍କା ନାହିଁ।` - `Mo pākhare ṭaṅkā nāhĩ.`
5. `ଆମ ପାଖରେ ବହି ଅଛି।` - `Āma pākhare bahi achhi.`
6. `ଆମ ପାଖରେ ଟଙ୍କା ଅଛି।` - `Āma pākhare ṭaṅkā achhi.`

### Why these answers work

The possessor plus ପାଖରେ precedes the available item. Absence uses ନାହିଁ. The last two answers transfer the pattern to collective possession and to money while preserving the difference between an owner and the located thing.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine preparing for class. State which supplies you have with you and which you lack. Ask a respectful listener whether they have a pen available.


## Lesson 11: Count and identify objects

### Outcome

Use goṭie and the classifier ṭi, then distinguish human and object reference.

### Think before reading

How does a noun-final classifier differ from a numeral-plus-classifier before a noun?

### Build the pattern

ଗୋଟିଏ (goṭie) means one or a with many ordinary things: ଗୋଟିଏ ବହି, a book. ଦୁଇଟି (duiṭi) and ତିନୋଟି (tinoṭi) give two and three counted objects in the standard forms used here. The noun itself does not need an English-like plural ending after this exact count.

ବହିଟି (bahiṭi) identifies the particular book. କଲମଟି similarly identifies a pen. The classifier is attached to the noun in reference expressions, while a counting expression usually comes before the noun. Other conversational classifiers and word orders exist, but keeping these models stable helps make their different purposes visible.

Human counting uses ଜଣ (jaṇa), with ଜଣେ (jaṇe) for a person: ଜଣେ ଶିକ୍ଷକ, a teacher. Plural human reference can use ମାନେ, as in ଶିକ୍ଷକମାନେ. This is not an invitation to attach ମାନେ indiscriminately to all things. The category and discourse use of the noun matter when choosing a counting or plural expression.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ଏହା ଗୋଟିଏ ବହି। | Ehā goṭie bahi. | This is a book. |
| ବହିଟି ଏଠାରେ ଅଛି। | Bahiṭi eṭhāre achhi. | The book is here. |
| ମୋ ପାଖରେ ଦୁଇଟି ବହି ଅଛି। | Mo pākhare duiṭi bahi achhi. | I have two books with me. |
| ମୋ ପାଖରେ ତିନୋଟି କଲମ ଅଛି। | Mo pākhare tinoṭi kalama achhi. | I have three pens with me. |
| ସେ ଜଣେ ଶିକ୍ଷକ। | Se jaṇe śikṣaka. | He or she is a teacher. |
| କଲମଟି ମୋର। | Kalamaṭi mora. | The pen is mine. |

### Common trap

Do not combine an exact count with an unnecessary English-inspired plural ending. Also do not use an object classifier as your default way to count a respected person.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I have two books with me.
2. Say: I have three pens with me.
3. Say: He or she is a teacher.
4. Say: The pen is mine.
5. Say: I have two pens with me.
6. Say: The book is there.

***Answers:***

1. `ମୋ ପାଖରେ ଦୁଇଟି ବହି ଅଛି।` - `Mo pākhare duiṭi bahi achhi.`
2. `ମୋ ପାଖରେ ତିନୋଟି କଲମ ଅଛି।` - `Mo pākhare tinoṭi kalama achhi.`
3. `ସେ ଜଣେ ଶିକ୍ଷକ।` - `Se jaṇe śikṣaka.`
4. `କଲମଟି ମୋର।` - `Kalamaṭi mora.`
5. `ମୋ ପାଖରେ ଦୁଇଟି କଲମ ଅଛି।` - `Mo pākhare duiṭi kalama achhi.`
6. `ବହିଟି ସେଠାରେ ଅଛି।` - `Bahiṭi seṭhāre achhi.`

### Why these answers work

The counts precede the nouns, while definite reference forms attach ଟି to the noun. Human reference uses ଜଣେ. The last two answers transfer quantity to pens and apply an identified book as the subject of a location sentence.

### Retrieve and recombine

## Lesson 12: In, on, under, and beside

### Outcome

Build a spatial phrase and keep the reference noun before its postposition.

### Think before reading

Why is ଟେବୁଲ ଉପରେ ordered differently from English on the table?

### Build the pattern

ଉପରେ (upare) means on or above, ତଳେ (taḷe) under, ଭିତରେ (bhitare) inside, and ପାଖରେ (pākhare) near. These relational expressions follow their reference noun. ବହିଟି ଟେବୁଲ ଉପରେ ଅଛି locates the book on the table. The English preposition is not moved before the noun in Odia.

The short locative ଘରେ (ghare) means at home or in the house. ଘର ଭିତରେ explicitly means inside the house. Genitive-linked variants also occur with postpositions, but the everyday spatial models here keep a direct noun plus relation: ଟେବୁଲ ତଳେ, under the table.

A spatial phrase can replace ଏଠାରେ without altering the rest of a sentence. That gives a practical construction method: build “the book is here,” then replace here with the precise location. A presence verb still comes at the end and still reflects whether the located subject is an ordinary thing, a group, or a respected person.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୁଁ ଘରେ ଅଛି। | Mũ ghare achhi. | I am at home. |
| ବହିଟି ଟେବୁଲ ଉପରେ ଅଛି। | Bahiṭi ṭebula upare achhi. | The book is on the table. |
| କଲମଟି ଟେବୁଲ ତଳେ ଅଛି। | Kalamaṭi ṭebula taḷe achhi. | The pen is under the table. |
| ସେ ଘର ଭିତରେ ଅଛନ୍ତି। | Se ghara bhitare achhanti. | He or she is inside the house. (respectful) |
| ଆମେ ଘରେ ଅଛୁ। | Āme ghare achhu. | We are at home. |
| କଲମଟି ବହି ପାଖରେ ଅଛି। | Kalamaṭi bahi pākhare achhi. | The pen is near the book. |

### Common trap

Do not confuse the possession use of ପାଖରେ with a claim of permanent ownership. In a spatial description, the same word may simply mean physically near.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: The pen is under the table.
2. Say: He or she is inside the house. (respectful)
3. Say: We are at home.
4. Say: The pen is near the book.
5. Say: The pen is on the table.
6. Say: You are at home. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `କଲମଟି ଟେବୁଲ ତଳେ ଅଛି।` - `Kalamaṭi ṭebula taḷe achhi.`
2. `ସେ ଘର ଭିତରେ ଅଛନ୍ତି।` - `Se ghara bhitare achhanti.`
3. `ଆମେ ଘରେ ଅଛୁ।` - `Āme ghare achhu.`
4. `କଲମଟି ବହି ପାଖରେ ଅଛି।` - `Kalamaṭi bahi pākhare achhi.`
5. `କଲମଟି ଟେବୁଲ ଉପରେ ଅଛି।` - `Kalamaṭi ṭebula upare achhi.`
6. `ଆପଣ ଘରେ ଅଛନ୍ତି।` - `Āpaṇa ghare achhanti.`

### Why these answers work

The reference object precedes the spatial word, and the whole place phrase precedes the final presence verb. The last two answers transfer a table location to a pen and a home location to a respectful person.

### Retrieve and recombine

Arrange an imaginary table with a book above it and a pen below it. Describe the scene, then move the pen near the book and revise only the spatial phrase.


## Lesson 13: Destinations and sources

### Outcome

Use -ku for a destination and -ru for a source with movement verbs.

### Think before reading

How do ଘରେ, ଘରକୁ, and ଘରୁ differ even though all contain the word for home?

### Build the pattern

ଘରକୁ (gharaku) means to the house or home, and ଘରୁ (gharu) means from home. The short source form shows a conventional combination of ଘର and the source ending; do not assume every noun retains all its final sounds before every suffix. ଘରେ is static location, not motion toward or away.

ଯାଏ (jāe) is I go or ordinary he/she goes, while respectful ଯାଆନ୍ତି (jāanti) means go. ଆସେ (āse) is I come, with respectful ଆସନ୍ତି (āsanti). ମୁଁ ବିଦ୍ୟାଳୟକୁ ଯାଏ describes a school-going routine; the destination receives କୁ.

କେଉଁଠିକୁ (keũṭhiku) asks where to, and କେଉଁଠୁ (keũṭhu) where from. Replacing a named source or destination with the corresponding question form preserves the final movement verb. In conversation the direction of come and go depends on the relevant person's location. Imagine the route before choosing the verb rather than translating it in isolation.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୁଁ ଘରକୁ ଯାଏ। | Mũ gharaku jāe. | I go home. |
| ମୁଁ ଘରୁ ଆସେ। | Mũ gharu āse. | I come from home. |
| ଆପଣ ଘରକୁ ଯାଆନ୍ତି। | Āpaṇa gharaku jāanti. | You go home. (respectful) |
| ସେ ଏଠାକୁ ଆସନ୍ତି। | Se eṭhāku āsanti. | He or she comes here. (respectful) |
| ମୁଁ ବିଦ୍ୟାଳୟକୁ ଯାଏ। | Mũ bidyāḷayaku jāe. | I go to school. |
| ଆମେ ଘରକୁ ଯାଉ। | Āme gharaku jāu. | We go home. |

### Common trap

Do not use the same unanalysed home expression for location, destination, and source. The endings carry the relationship even when the English word home appears unchanged.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: You go home. (respectful)
2. Say: He or she comes here. (respectful)
3. Say: I go to school.
4. Say: We go home.
5. Say: Where do you go to? (respectful)
6. Say: Where do you come from? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `ଆପଣ ଘରକୁ ଯାଆନ୍ତି।` - `Āpaṇa gharaku jāanti.`
2. `ସେ ଏଠାକୁ ଆସନ୍ତି।` - `Se eṭhāku āsanti.`
3. `ମୁଁ ବିଦ୍ୟାଳୟକୁ ଯାଏ।` - `Mũ bidyāḷayaku jāe.`
4. `ଆମେ ଘରକୁ ଯାଉ।` - `Āme gharaku jāu.`
5. `ଆପଣ କେଉଁଠିକୁ ଯାଆନ୍ତି?` - `Āpaṇa keũṭhiku jāanti?`
6. `ଆପଣ କେଉଁଠୁ ଆସନ୍ତି?` - `Āpaṇa keũṭhu āsanti?`

### Why these answers work

Destinations use କୁ, sources use the appropriate ରୁ form, and the movement verb remains final. The last two questions ask for a missing destination or source while retaining respectful agreement.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give a short route from home to school. State where you start and where you go. Ask the listener the same two questions respectfully.


## Lesson 14: Give, receive, and make requests

### Outcome

Use a recipient form with a respectful imperative.

### Think before reading

Where should to me appear if Odia already has the recipient form ମୋତେ?

### Build the pattern

ମୋତେ (mote) means me or to me in a recipient or object role. ମୋତେ କଲମଟି ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ asks someone respectfully to give the pen to the speaker. The recipient precedes the thing given and the imperative stays last. There is no need to add an English-like to before ମୋତେ.

ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ (diantu) is the respectful imperative give. Other useful requests are ଆସନ୍ତୁ (āsantu), come; ବସନ୍ତୁ (basantu), sit; ପଢ଼ନ୍ତୁ (paṛhantu), read; and କୁହନ୍ତୁ (kuhantu), say or tell. These are distinct from ordinary respectful statements ending in -ନ୍ତି.

ଦୟାକରି (dayākari) means please. It can make a request explicitly courteous, but respectful verb morphology and tone already matter. A visitor may simply hear ବସନ୍ତୁ as an invitation to sit. The subject ଆପଣ is often omitted in a direct request because the addressee is obvious. Omitting it does not remove the respectful imperative ending.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ଦୟାକରି ବସନ୍ତୁ। | Dayākari basantu. | Please sit. |
| ଏଠାକୁ ଆସନ୍ତୁ। | Eṭhāku āsantu. | Come here. (respectful) |
| ମୋତେ କଲମଟି ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ। | Mote kalamaṭi diantu. | Give me the pen. (respectful) |
| ବହିଟି ପଢ଼ନ୍ତୁ। | Bahiṭi paṛhantu. | Read the book. (respectful) |
| ଆପଣଙ୍କ ନାମ କୁହନ୍ତୁ। | Āpaṇaṅka nāma kuhantu. | Tell me your name. (respectful) |
| କାମ କରନ୍ତୁ। | Kāma karantu. | Do the work. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not confuse statement ଆସନ୍ତି with request ଆସନ୍ତୁ. A small final-vowel difference changes the clause type, so it deserves careful pronunciation.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Give me the pen. (respectful)
2. Say: Read the book. (respectful)
3. Say: Tell me your name. (respectful)
4. Say: Do the work. (respectful)
5. Say: Give me the book. (respectful)
6. Say: Come to the house. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `ମୋତେ କଲମଟି ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ।` - `Mote kalamaṭi diantu.`
2. `ବହିଟି ପଢ଼ନ୍ତୁ।` - `Bahiṭi paṛhantu.`
3. `ଆପଣଙ୍କ ନାମ କୁହନ୍ତୁ।` - `Āpaṇaṅka nāma kuhantu.`
4. `କାମ କରନ୍ତୁ।` - `Kāma karantu.`
5. `ମୋତେ ବହିଟି ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ।` - `Mote bahiṭi diantu.`
6. `ଘରକୁ ଆସନ୍ତୁ।` - `Gharaku āsantu.`

### Why these answers work

The final forms are respectful imperatives. ମୋତେ marks a recipient and the object retains its classifier. The last two answers transfer the giving pattern to another object and add a destination to the coming request.

### Retrieve and recombine

Welcome a visitor, offer a seat, and ask for a pen. Then ask the visitor to read a book. Identify each final imperative and its statement counterpart.


## Lesson 15: Objects and people affected by an action

### Outcome

Use -ku with a definite human object and keep the agent unmarked in ordinary clauses.

### Think before reading

How can ରୀନା and ରୀନାକୁ play different roles in a seeing sentence?

### Build the pattern

ଦେଖେ (dekhe) means I see or ordinary he/she sees; respectful ଦେଖନ୍ତି is the matching honorific form. ମୁଁ ରୀନାକୁ ଦେଖେ means I see Rina. The ending କୁ marks the human object in this model, while the subject ମୁଁ remains unmarked.

ରୀନା ବହି ପଢ଼େ says Rina reads books. Unlike Assamese noun-agent marking, Odia does not require an extra agent suffix on this ordinary nominative subject. This matters if you are learning several eastern languages: shared verb-final order does not imply identical case marking.

ମୋତେ, ତୁମକୁ (tumaku), and ଆପଣଙ୍କୁ (āpaṇaṅku) are useful object or recipient forms. Case tells you the participant's role, while respectful wording reflects the relationship. The same କୁ ending can appear with a destination or a recipient in other constructions. Its precise interpretation comes from the governing verb and the whole clause, not a universal one-word English translation.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୁଁ ରୀନାକୁ ଦେଖେ। | Mũ Rīnāku dekhe. | I see Rina. |
| ରୀନା ମୋତେ ଦେଖେ। | Rīnā mote dekhe. | Rina sees me. |
| ମୁଁ ଆପଣଙ୍କୁ ଦେଖେ। | Mũ āpaṇaṅku dekhe. | I see you. (respectful object) |
| ଆପଣ ମୋତେ ଦେଖନ୍ତି। | Āpaṇa mote dekhanti. | You see me. (respectful) |
| ରୀନା ବହି ପଢ଼େ। | Rīnā bahi paṛhe. | Rina reads books. |
| ସେ ମୋତେ ଦେଖନ୍ତି। | Se mote dekhanti. | He or she sees me. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not add an Assamese-style agent ending to an ordinary Odia named subject. Also do not treat every କୁ as the English word to; here it can mark the person seen.

### Practice

2. Say: You see me. (respectful)
3. Say: Rina reads books.
4. Say: He or she sees me. (respectful)
5. Say: I see you. (familiar object)
6. Say: Rina drinks tea.

***Answers:***

1. `ମୁଁ ଆପଣଙ୍କୁ ଦେଖେ।` - `Mũ āpaṇaṅku dekhe.`
2. `ଆପଣ ମୋତେ ଦେଖନ୍ତି।` - `Āpaṇa mote dekhanti.`
3. `ରୀନା ବହି ପଢ଼େ।` - `Rīnā bahi paṛhe.`
4. `ସେ ମୋତେ ଦେଖନ୍ତି।` - `Se mote dekhanti.`
5. `ମୁଁ ତୁମକୁ ଦେଖେ।` - `Mũ tumaku dekhe.`
6. `ରୀନା ଚା ପିଏ।` - `Rīnā chā pie.`

### Why these answers work

The seeing sentences mark the human object and keep the subject in its ordinary form. Respectful verbs remain tied to the subject. The last two answers transfer object marking to a familiar listener and reading to a named agent.

### Retrieve and recombine

## Lesson 16: Describe and compare

### Outcome

Use unchanged adjectives and mark the comparison standard with ṭhāru.

### Think before reading

Does a bigger book need a special comparative adjective ending in Odia?

### Build the pattern

ବଡ଼ (baṛa), big, ଛୋଟ (chhoṭa), small, ନୂଆ (nūā), new, and ଭଲ (bhala), good, describe familiar objects. They do not change their form to agree with the noun's gender in the basic constructions here. ଗୋଟିଏ ନୂଆ ବହି places the adjective before the noun; ବହିଟି ନୂଆ describes an identified book.

ବହୁତ (bahuta), very or much, intensifies a quality: ବହୁତ ଭଲ. To compare two objects, use a standard followed by ଠାରୁ (ṭhāru). ଏହା ତାହାଠାରୁ ବଡ଼ means this is bigger than that. The adjective stays ବଡ଼; the comparison relation is carried by ଠାରୁ rather than a new adjective suffix.

A comparison assumes two identifiable items. Picture two books while using this and that, then replace one with a named object if the listener would otherwise be unsure. The language can leave a noun unspoken when a demonstrative and context identify it, but a learner should not rely on context that has never been established.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ବହିଟି ନୂଆ। | Bahiṭi nūā. | The book is new. |
| ବହିଟି ବହୁତ ଭଲ। | Bahiṭi bahuta bhala. | The book is very good. |
| ଏହା ଗୋଟିଏ ନୂଆ ବହି। | Ehā goṭie nūā bahi. | This is a new book. |
| ଟେବୁଲଟି ବଡ଼। | Ṭebulaṭi baṛa. | The table is big. |
| ଏହା ତାହାଠାରୁ ବଡ଼। | Ehā tāhāṭhāru baṛa. | This is bigger than that. |
| ଏହା ତାହାଠାରୁ ଛୋଟ। | Ehā tāhāṭhāru chhoṭa. | This is smaller than that. |

### Common trap

Do not invent a feminine or plural form of ଭଲ to match an English noun. Preserve the modified consonant in ବଡ଼ and the retroflex stop in ଠାରୁ.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: This is a new book.
2. Say: The table is big.
3. Say: This is bigger than that.
4. Say: This is smaller than that.
5. Say: The pen is new.
6. Say: The table is very good.

***Answers:***

1. `ଏହା ଗୋଟିଏ ନୂଆ ବହି।` - `Ehā goṭie nūā bahi.`
2. `ଟେବୁଲଟି ବଡ଼।` - `Ṭebulaṭi baṛa.`
3. `ଏହା ତାହାଠାରୁ ବଡ଼।` - `Ehā tāhāṭhāru baṛa.`
4. `ଏହା ତାହାଠାରୁ ଛୋଟ।` - `Ehā tāhāṭhāru chhoṭa.`
5. `କଲମଟି ନୂଆ।` - `Kalamaṭi nūā.`
6. `ଟେବୁଲଟି ବହୁତ ଭଲ।` - `Ṭebulaṭi bahuta bhala.`

### Why these answers work

Adjectives remain stable, while the comparison standard receives ଠାରୁ. The last two answers transfer plain descriptions to a pen and table, demonstrating that the classifier does not create adjective agreement.

### Retrieve and recombine

Compare two books for size, then describe one for quality. Replace the books with pens and keep the adjective forms unchanged.


## Lesson 17: Days, clock times, and duration

### Outcome

Place a routine in time and distinguish a point on the clock from an interval.

### Think before reading

Why does “at three” need a different expression from “three hours”?

### Build the pattern

ଆଜି (āji) means today, ଏବେ (ebe) now, and ପ୍ରତିଦିନ (pratidina) every day. ସକାଳେ (sakāḷe) means in the morning, and ରାତିରେ (rātire) at night. A time expression can sit before the object and final verb without changing the action's person ending.

ତିନିଟାରେ (tiniṭāre) means at three o'clock. ତିନି ଘଣ୍ଟା (tini ghaṇṭā) means three hours. The former identifies a point; the latter measures how long an action lasts. ମୁଁ ତିନି ଘଣ୍ଟା କାମ କରେ expresses a three-hour work duration without inserting a separate word for English for.

କେତେବେଳେ (ketebeḷe) asks when or at what time. ଆପଣ କେତେବେଳେ ଆସନ୍ତି asks about the listener's usual arrival time in this simple-present context. A specific future appointment will need the future form introduced later. English yesterday/tomorrow distinctions require context with କାଲି; explicit ଆସନ୍ତାକାଲି will be used for tomorrow.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୁଁ ସକାଳେ ଚା ପିଏ। | Mũ sakāḷe chā pie. | I drink tea in the morning. |
| ମୁଁ ରାତିରେ ବହି ପଢ଼େ। | Mũ rātire bahi paṛhe. | I read books at night. |
| ଆମେ ପ୍ରତିଦିନ କାମ କରୁ। | Āme pratidina kāma karu. | We work every day. |
| ଆପଣ କେତେବେଳେ ଆସନ୍ତି? | Āpaṇa ketebeḷe āsanti? | When do you come? (respectful, routine) |
| ମୁଁ ତିନିଟାରେ ଆସେ। | Mũ tiniṭāre āse. | I come at three o'clock. |
| ମୁଁ ତିନି ଘଣ୍ଟା କାମ କରେ। | Mũ tini ghaṇṭā kāma kare. | I work for three hours. |

### Common trap

Do not interpret every କାଲି in isolation as the same English day. Also keep the clock ending in ତିନିଟାରେ distinct from the duration noun ଘଣ୍ଟା.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: We work every day.
2. Say: When do you come? (respectful, routine)
3. Say: I come at three o'clock.
4. Say: I work for three hours.
5. Say: I read books in the morning.
6. Say: We drink tea at night.

***Answers:***

1. `ଆମେ ପ୍ରତିଦିନ କାମ କରୁ।` - `Āme pratidina kāma karu.`
2. `ଆପଣ କେତେବେଳେ ଆସନ୍ତି?` - `Āpaṇa ketebeḷe āsanti?`
3. `ମୁଁ ତିନିଟାରେ ଆସେ।` - `Mũ tiniṭāre āse.`
4. `ମୁଁ ତିନି ଘଣ୍ଟା କାମ କରେ।` - `Mũ tini ghaṇṭā kāma kare.`
5. `ମୁଁ ସକାଳେ ବହି ପଢ଼େ।` - `Mũ sakāḷe bahi paṛhe.`
6. `ଆମେ ରାତିରେ ଚା ପିଉ।` - `Āme rātire chā piu.`

### Why these answers work

The time expression precedes the finite action, and the question keeps respectful ଆସନ୍ତି. The final two answers transfer morning and night to different routines without making them progressive automatically.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a morning routine, a night routine, a usual arrival time, and a work duration. Ask a respectful visitor when they usually come.


## Lesson 18: A finished event in the past

### Outcome

Report a completed action with the correct past person ending.

### Think before reading

Which person distinction changes କଲି into କଲୁ or କଲେ?

### Build the pattern

କଲି (kali) is I did, କଲୁ (kalu) we did, କଲ (kala) familiar you did, କଲା (kalā) ordinary he/she did, and କଲେ (kale) respectful did. The final vowel carries an important distinction, including the difference between familiar କଲ and ordinary third-person କଲା in writing and pronunciation.

ପଢ଼ିଲି (paṛhili) means I read a completed event, ଖାଇଲି (khāili) I ate, ପିଇଲି (piili) I drank, and ଗଲି (gali) I went. The common verb କରିବା uses the contracted past କଲି, so do not assume every verb follows one unchanged stem plus the same visible suffix.

Use a clear past context when first practising: ମୁଁ କାଲି ବହିଟି ପଢ଼ିଲି says I read the book yesterday. In that past sentence କାଲି receives the yesterday interpretation. A short report can contain several independent past clauses before you learn more elaborate narrative connections. Keep each clause's person form accurate rather than rushing into a long story.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୁଁ କାମ କଲି। | Mũ kāma kali. | I did the work. |
| ମୁଁ ଚା ପିଇଲି। | Mũ chā piili. | I drank tea. |
| ମୁଁ ଘରକୁ ଗଲି। | Mũ gharaku gali. | I went home. |
| ଆପଣ କାମ କଲେ। | Āpaṇa kāma kale. | You did the work. (respectful) |

### Outcome

### Think before reading

How does କରିଛି differ from କରୁଛି in its vowel and in the way it presents the event?

### Build the pattern

କରିଛି (karichhi) means I have done in the completed-result context here, while କରୁଛି presents an ongoing action. The i versus u contrast matters. ପଢ଼ିଛି means have read, ଖାଇଛି have eaten, and ପିଇଛି have drunk. Respectful subjects use the corresponding -ଛନ୍ତି ending, as in କରିଛନ୍ତି.

## Lesson 20: Future plans and questions

### Outcome

Plan a future activity with the correct singular, plural, or respectful ending.

### Think before reading

How do କରିବି and କରିବୁ distinguish the speaker from a group of speakers?

### Build the pattern

କରିବି (karibi) means I will do; କରିବୁ (karibu), we will do; କରିବ (kariba), familiar you or ordinary he/she will do; and କରିବେ (karibe), respectful will do. These forms encode distinctions that should not be replaced by neighboring Bengali or Assamese paradigms.

ଯିବି (jibi) is I will go, ଆସିବି (āsibi) I will come, ପଢ଼ିବି (paṛhibi) I will read, and ପିଇବି (piibi) I will drink. ଆସନ୍ତାକାଲି (āsantākāli) explicitly means tomorrow. It is useful when arranging a real appointment because it does not rely on the ambiguous day word alone.

A future negative follows the future form with ନାହିଁ: ମୁଁ ଯିବି ନାହିଁ. This differs from denying a completed event with a completion-negative form. A yes-or-no future question adds କି to the end, while a time question uses କେତେବେଳେ within the clause. In both types, the future verb still agrees with the subject.

Do not replace the first-person singular future -ବି with plural -ବୁ. Also keep future negation distinct from the negative of a completed action.

## Lesson 21: Ability and desired actions

### Outcome

Combine an activity with can or want and distinguish their nonfinite forms.

### Think before reading

Why does କହିପାରେ use a different linking shape from ଯିବାକୁ ଚାହେଁ?

### Build the pattern

କହିପାରେ (kahipāre) means can speak or say in the first-person singular pattern here. The action link କହି combines with the finite ability verb ପାରେ. ମୁଁ ଓଡ଼ିଆ କହିପାରେ says I can speak Odia. Respectful ଆପଣ uses କହିପାରନ୍ତି.

Other useful ability forms include ପଢ଼ିପାରେ, can read, and କରିପାରେ, can do. The first action does not also receive a separate first-person ending. A future ability form such as ଆସିପାରିବି means I will be able to come or can come on the relevant future occasion.

Desire uses a different nonfinite construction: ମୁଁ ଘରକୁ ଯିବାକୁ ଚାହେଁ, I want to go home. The desired action is ଯିବାକୁ, to go, followed by the first-person desire verb ଚାହେଁ. With a noun, ମୁଁ ଚା ଚାହେଁ means I want tea. Do not assume all English two-verb combinations require an identical Odia linking form.

Ability combines the action link with a person-marked ପାରେ-series verb. Desire uses the -ବାକୁ action form before ଚାହେଁ. The final two answers transfer ability to a future visit and desire to a school destination.

## Lesson 22: Needs and things you like

### Outcome

Use an experiencer construction for liking and a genitive construction for a need.

### Think before reading

Why does English I appear as ମୋତେ in a liking sentence rather than the ordinary subject ମୁଁ?

### Build the pattern

ମୋତେ ଚା ଭଲ ଲାଗେ (mote chā bhala lāge) means I like tea. The person experiencing the favorable reaction is expressed with ମୋତେ. The predicate describes the thing as feeling good to that person. It is not built by changing ଲାଗେ to a first-person form because the English translation uses I.

Respectful ଆପଣଙ୍କୁ gives ଆପଣଙ୍କୁ ଚା ଭଲ ଲାଗେ କି, do you like tea. The respectful marking is in the experiencer expression, while the predicate stays in the shape shown. ଆମକୁ (āmaku) is to us and supports a group liking statement.

Need can use ମୋର with ଦରକାର: ମୋର ପାଣି ଦରକାର, I need water. A counted object can fill the required-item slot: ମୋର ଗୋଟିଏ କଲମ ଦରକାର. Liking, needing, and wanting are different: someone may like tea yet want water now and need a pen for work. Choose the intended meaning before selecting the construction.

### Examples to take apart

| ମୋର ପାଣି ଦରକାର। | Mora pāṇi darakāra. | I need water. |
| ଆପଣଙ୍କୁ ଚା ଭଲ ଲାଗେ କି? | Āpaṇaṅku chā bhala lāge ki? | Do you like tea? (respectful) |

Do not force every English I into ମୁଁ. Experiencer and need constructions select their own person forms, and the resulting sentence is not an exception to be ignored.

## Lesson 23: Connect a reason and an action sequence

### Outcome

Join clauses with because or but and sequence two actions with a shared subject.

### Think before reading

Does a nonfinite first action have to be past merely because it happens before another action?

### Build the pattern

କାରଣ (kāraṇa) means because. ମୁଁ ଯିବି ନାହିଁ, କାରଣ ମୋର କାମ ଅଛି explains a future decision with a present reason. The clauses do not need to share a tense or the same person case; each keeps its own construction.

କିନ୍ତୁ (kintu) means but. ମୁଁ ଚା ଚାହେଁ, କିନ୍ତୁ ପାଣି ଚାହେଁ ନାହିଁ contrasts two desires. In the second clause the understood subject can remain unspoken because the speaker is still the same person. Ellipsis should rely on real context rather than being used to hide an uncertain verb form.

For action sequence, a linking form such as ଖାଇ (khāi), after eating, can precede a final finite verb: ମୁଁ ଭାତ ଖାଇ କାମ କରିବି. The eating occurs before the work, but both may be future because the final verb establishes the planned sequence. Similarly, ପଢ଼ି links reading and କରି links doing. Begin with shared-subject sequences before attempting more complex changes of actor.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୁଁ ଯିବି ନାହିଁ, କାରଣ ମୋର କାମ ଅଛି। | Mũ jibi nāhĩ, kāraṇa mora kāma achhi. | I will not go because I have work. |
| ମୁଁ ଭାତ ଖାଇ କାମ କରିବି। | Mũ bhāta khāi kāma karibi. | I will work after eating rice. |
| ମୁଁ ଚା ଚାହେଁ, କିନ୍ତୁ ପାଣି ଚାହେଁ ନାହିଁ। | Mũ chā chāhẽ, kintu pāṇi chāhẽ nāhĩ. | I want tea but do not want water. |
| ମୁଁ କାମ କରି ଘରକୁ ଯିବି। | Mũ kāma kari gharaku jibi. | I will go home after doing the work. |
| ମୁଁ ଚା ପିଇ ବହି ପଢ଼ିବି। | Mũ chā pii bahi paṛhibi. | I will read a book after drinking tea. |
| ମୋର ବହି ଅଛି, କିନ୍ତୁ କଲମ ନାହିଁ। | Mora bahi achhi, kintu kalama nāhĩ. | I have a book but no pen. |

### Common trap

Do not interpret the linking i form as a complete past-tense sentence on its own. Its time relationship is read with the final finite verb and context.

### Practice

2. `ମୁଁ କାମ କରି ଘରକୁ ଯିବି।` - `Mũ kāma kari gharaku jibi.`
3. `ମୁଁ ଚା ପିଇ ବହି ପଢ଼ିବି।` - `Mũ chā pii bahi paṛhibi.`
4. `ମୋର ବହି ଅଛି, କିନ୍ତୁ କଲମ ନାହିଁ।` - `Mora bahi achhi, kintu kalama nāhĩ.`
5. `ମୁଁ ବହିଟି ପଢ଼ି କାମ କରିବି।` - `Mũ bahiṭi paṛhi kāma karibi.`
6. `ମୁଁ ଘରକୁ ଯାଇ ବହି ପଢ଼ିବି।` - `Mũ gharaku jāi bahi paṛhibi.`

### Why these answers work

The connectors preserve complete clause grammar, while action links depend on the final verb for the overall plan. The last two answers transfer sequence to reading and returning home, retaining one understood actor.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give a plan with a reason, a desire with a contrast, and two activities in sequence. Explain which relationship each connecting expression contributes.


## Lesson 24: A purchase and a request for clarification

### Outcome

Ask a price, request a quantity, and keep a conversation going when you miss information.

### Think before reading

If you understand the object but not the price, which single information gap should your question target?

### Build the pattern

ଦାମ (dāma) means price and କେତେ (kete) how much or how many. ବହିଟିର ଦାମ କେତେ asks for the price of the particular book. The genitive ending ର comes after the classifier, so the whole identified book acts as the price owner.

ପଚାଶ ଟଙ୍କା (pachāśa ṭaṅkā) means fifty rupees in the Indian purchase context. A price statement can be short: ଦାମ ପଚାଶ ଟଙ୍କା. Requesting two pens uses the count you already know: ମୋତେ ଦୁଇଟି କଲମ ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ.

ଆଉ ଥରେ କୁହନ୍ତୁ asks the person to say it once more. ଟିକେ ଧୀରେ କୁହନ୍ତୁ asks them to speak a little slowly. ମୁଁ ବୁଝିପାରୁନାହିଁ expresses that you are not understanding or cannot make sense of what is being said at the moment. These are practical repair moves, not admissions that the exchange must stop. Use them before agreeing to an amount you did not understand.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ବହିଟିର ଦାମ କେତେ? | Bahiṭira dāma kete? | What is the price of the book? |
| ଦାମ ପଚାଶ ଟଙ୍କା। | Dāma pachāśa ṭaṅkā. | The price is fifty rupees. |
| ମୋତେ ଦୁଇଟି କଲମ ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ। | Mote duiṭi kalama diantu. | Give me two pens. (respectful) |
| ମୁଁ ବହିଟି ଚାହେଁ। | Mũ bahiṭi chāhẽ. | I want the book. |
| ମୁଁ ବୁଝିପାରୁନାହିଁ। | Mũ bujhipārunāhĩ. | I am not understanding. |
| ଆଉ ଥରେ କୁହନ୍ତୁ। | Āu thare kuhantu. | Say it once more. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not treat a memorised number as evidence that you can recognise every spoken price. Ask again when needed. Keep the genitive outside the classifier in ବହିଟିର.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Give me two pens. (respectful)
2. Say: I want the book.
3. Say: I am not understanding.
4. Say: Say it once more. (respectful)
5. Say: Speak a little slowly. (respectful)
6. Say: What is the price of the pen?

***Answers:***

1. `ମୋତେ ଦୁଇଟି କଲମ ଦିଅନ୍ତୁ।` - `Mote duiṭi kalama diantu.`
2. `ମୁଁ ବହିଟି ଚାହେଁ।` - `Mũ bahiṭi chāhẽ.`
3. `ମୁଁ ବୁଝିପାରୁନାହିଁ।` - `Mũ bujhipārunāhĩ.`
4. `ଆଉ ଥରେ କୁହନ୍ତୁ।` - `Āu thare kuhantu.`
5. `ଟିକେ ଧୀରେ କୁହନ୍ତୁ।` - `Ṭike dhīre kuhantu.`
6. `କଲମଟିର ଦାମ କେତେ?` - `Kalamaṭira dāma kete?`

### Why these answers work

The price question asks for a value with କେତେ. The purchase request keeps count, noun, and imperative in the familiar order. The final two answers transfer repair to slower speech and the price frame to a pen.

### Retrieve and recombine

Role-play a short purchase. Ask the price, request an item, and ask for repetition. Then change the item and count, rebuilding the request instead of repeating it unchanged.


## Lesson 25: Tell a connected personal story

### Outcome

Select identity, location, routine, completion, and future patterns in one coherent exchange.

### Think before reading

Before translating a paragraph, can you identify the type of claim made by each sentence?

### Build the pattern

A short personal account can begin with ମୋ ନାମ ରୀନା, move to a location with ଅଛି, describe a routine with ପଢ଼େ, report a completed book with ପଢ଼ିଛି, and end with a plan in -ବି. These are different constructions because they do different jobs. A long sentence is not automatically better than several accurate short ones.

When the speaker changes to a group, remember Odia's first-person plural forms: ଅଛୁ, ପଢ଼ୁ, ପଢ଼ିଛୁ, and କରିବୁ. When addressing a respected person, select ଆପଣ with the appropriate respectful verb; in a possession or experiencer construction, use the matching case form instead. Respect and case are simultaneous choices, not competing alternatives.

The cumulative examples connect a home study session to tomorrow's work. The transfer task changes the setting without requiring a brand-new grammar rule. This is the purpose of a foundation: making a limited but useful set of patterns available for recombination. The draft does not establish fluency, certify pronunciation, or substitute for listening to and receiving feedback from fluent Odia speakers.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ମୋ ନାମ ରୀନା। | Mo nāma Rīnā. | My name is Rina. |
| ମୁଁ ଘରେ ଅଛି। | Mũ ghare achhi. | I am at home. |
| ମୁଁ ପ୍ରତିଦିନ ବହି ପଢ଼େ। | Mũ pratidina bahi paṛhe. | I read books every day. |
| ମୁଁ ବହିଟି ପଢ଼ିଛି। | Mũ bahiṭi paṛhichhi. | I have read the book. |
| ମୁଁ ଆସନ୍ତାକାଲି କାମ କରିବି। | Mũ āsantākāli kāma karibi. | I will work tomorrow. |
| ଆପଣ ଆସନ୍ତାକାଲି କ’ଣ କରିବେ? | Āpaṇa āsantākāli kaṇa karibe? | What will you do tomorrow? (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not use one unchanged pronoun or ending throughout a paragraph for visual consistency. The grammatical role and number of each participant still matter from clause to clause.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I read books every day.
2. Say: I have read the book.
3. Say: I will work tomorrow.
4. Say: What will you do tomorrow? (respectful)
5. Say: I need a new book.
6. Say: I will work after reading the book.

***Answers:***

1. `ମୁଁ ପ୍ରତିଦିନ ବହି ପଢ଼େ।` - `Mũ pratidina bahi paṛhe.`
2. `ମୁଁ ବହିଟି ପଢ଼ିଛି।` - `Mũ bahiṭi paṛhichhi.`
3. `ମୁଁ ଆସନ୍ତାକାଲି କାମ କରିବି।` - `Mũ āsantākāli kāma karibi.`
4. `ଆପଣ ଆସନ୍ତାକାଲି କ’ଣ କରିବେ?` - `Āpaṇa āsantākāli kaṇa karibe?`
5. `ମୋର ଗୋଟିଏ ନୂଆ ବହି ଦରକାର।` - `Mora goṭie nūā bahi darakāra.`
6. `ମୁଁ ବହିଟି ପଢ଼ି କାମ କରିବି।` - `Mũ bahiṭi paṛhi kāma karibi.`

### Why these answers work

The review combines a possessive naming phrase, a routine, a perfect result, a future plan, and a respectful question. The last two prompts transfer need and shared-subject sequence to a study plan, using structures already built earlier.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create a six-sentence self-introduction with a name, location, routine, completed task, need, and future plan. Add a respectful question. Retell two sentences about your group and adjust the endings.
