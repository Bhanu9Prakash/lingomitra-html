# Malayalam: A pattern-first foundation

This course uses modern Malayalam script, including Unicode chillu letters, and broadly shared contemporary standard Malayalam with everyday vocabulary. Formal literary syntax and regionally shortened conversation are not interchangeable; selected conversational requests are labelled. It is not a transcript of a single Kerala dialect. Romanisation marks long ā ī ū ē ō, retroflex ṭ ḍ ṇ ḷ, the Malayalam sounds ḻ and ṟ, and ñ for ഞ. A final ŭ represents the reduced final vowel often written with a virama; ordinary u represents the written full vowel. Double consonants are longer. These aids do not replace listening or the native spelling.

## Lesson 1: Identifying yourself and an object

### Build the pattern

You will introduce yourself and identify a book, house or drink. Malayalam commonly uses ആണ് āṇŭ for present identification. It can join the preceding word in writing: രവിയാണ് raviyāṇŭ means is Ravi, and പുസ്തകമാണ് pustakamāṇŭ means is a book. ഞാൻ ñān means I, ഇത് itŭ means this, and അത് atŭ means that. The joining sounds are part of ordinary word combination, not additional English words. A noun ending in m often appears with -māṇŭ; a vowel-final name can use a linking y. Malayalam does not require an indefinite article corresponding to every English a.

### Think before reading on

If പുസ്തകം pustakam means book and ആണ് āṇŭ expresses identification, what remains of the noun when the two join?

The final m links directly to āṇŭ, giving pustakamāṇŭ. The combined word still contains both the noun and the identifying predicate. Recognising the boundary helps you read longer written forms without treating each as an unrelated vocabulary item.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ഞാൻ രവിയാണ്. | Ñān raviyāṇŭ. | I am Ravi. |
| ഞാൻ മീനയാണ്. | Ñān mīnayāṇŭ. | I am Meena. |
| ഇത് പുസ്തകമാണ്. | Itŭ pustakamāṇŭ. | This is a book. |
| അത് വീടാണ്. | Atŭ vīṭāṇŭ. | That is a house. |
| ഇത് വെള്ളമാണ്. | Itŭ veḷḷamāṇŭ. | This is water. |
| അത് ചായയാണ്. | Atŭ cāyayāṇŭ. | That is tea. |

### Worked examples

`ഞാൻ രവിയാണ്.` - `Ñān raviyāṇŭ.` means “I am Ravi.” The name joins the identifying predicate through y, while the first-person pronoun itself does not demand a gendered verb ending.

`ഞാൻ മീനയാണ്.` - `Ñān mīnayāṇŭ.` means “I am Meena.” The vowel-final name again takes a linking y, and the same identifying predicate works regardless of the speaker's gender.

`ഇത് പുസ്തകമാണ്.` - `Itŭ pustakamāṇŭ.` means “This is a book.” The book noun retains its final m before āṇŭ, producing one written word with two recognisable components.

### Common trap

Do not use uṇṭŭ as a universal translation of is. Identification with āṇŭ and existence with uṇṭŭ perform different jobs, even when English uses is for both.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Malayalam. Keep the stated person and register.

1. I am Ravi.
2. I am Meena.
3. This is a book.
4. That is a house.
5. This is water.
6. That is tea.

***Answers:***

1. `ഞാൻ രവിയാണ്.` - `Ñān raviyāṇŭ.`
2. `ഞാൻ മീനയാണ്.` - `Ñān mīnayāṇŭ.`
3. `ഇത് പുസ്തകമാണ്.` - `Itŭ pustakamāṇŭ.`
4. `അത് വീടാണ്.` - `Atŭ vīṭāṇŭ.`
5. `ഇത് വെള്ളമാണ്.` - `Itŭ veḷḷamāṇŭ.`
6. `അത് ചായയാണ്.` - `Atŭ cāyayāṇŭ.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The final reduced vowel of vīṭŭ gives way before the identifying predicate, revealing the house stem in the joined form.

Answer 5: The doubled retroflex lateral in veḷḷam belongs to the water word and should not be shortened in pronunciation.

Answer 6: The tea noun ends in a vowel and joins through y, giving a useful contrast with m-final nouns.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce yourself, identify a nearby book and a distant house, then change only the pointing distance. Keep the identifying ending while replacing the noun.


## Lesson 2: Questions with what and the final question vowel

### Build the pattern

You will ask what something is and check whether an identification is right. എന്ത് entŭ means what. In a common identity question, it combines with āṇŭ as എന്താണ് entāṇŭ. Yes-or-no questions commonly use a final -ഓ ō: ആണോ āṇō means is it or is that so in context. This differs from the question vowel used in some neighbouring languages. അതെ ate is a short yes confirming a statement. The main sentence order remains stable: the object being identified comes first, followed by the question expression. There is no English-style support word do to insert.

### Think before reading on

How does ഇത് എന്താണ് itŭ entāṇŭ differ from ഇത് പുസ്തകമാണോ itŭ pustakamāṇō?

The open question asks the listener to supply the object's identity. The yes-or-no question offers book as a candidate and asks whether it is correct. The question ending changes the kind of information requested, not the pointing distance.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ഇത് എന്താണ്? | Itŭ entāṇŭ? | What is this? |
| അത് എന്താണ്? | Atŭ entāṇŭ? | What is that? |
| ഇത് പുസ്തകമാണോ? | Itŭ pustakamāṇō? | Is this a book? |
| അത് വീടാണോ? | Atŭ vīṭāṇō? | Is that a house? |
| ഇത് ചായയാണോ? | Itŭ cāyayāṇō? | Is this tea? |
| അതെ. | Ate. | Yes. |

### Worked examples

`ഇത് എന്താണ്?` - `Itŭ entāṇŭ?` means “What is this?” The what expression combines with the identifying predicate and asks for a category without proposing one in advance.

`അത് എന്താണ്?` - `Atŭ entāṇŭ?` means “What is that?” The distant demonstrative changes the referent, while the open question expression remains exactly the same in function.

`ഇത് പുസ്തകമാണോ?` - `Itŭ pustakamāṇō?` means “Is this a book?” The final ō turns the identifying predicate into a yes-or-no question about the proposed book category.

### Common trap

Do not import Tamil or Telugu final ā as the default Malayalam yes-or-no marker. The relevant forms here end in long ō, which must be pronounced.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Malayalam. Keep the stated person and register.

1. What is this?
2. What is that?
3. Is this a book?
4. Is that a house?
5. Is this tea?
6. Yes.

***Answers:***

1. `ഇത് എന്താണ്?` - `Itŭ entāṇŭ?`
2. `അത് എന്താണ്?` - `Atŭ entāṇŭ?`
3. `ഇത് പുസ്തകമാണോ?` - `Itŭ pustakamāṇō?`
4. `അത് വീടാണോ?` - `Atŭ vīṭāṇō?`
5. `ഇത് ചായയാണോ?` - `Itŭ cāyayāṇō?`
6. `അതെ.` - `Ate.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The house stem combines with the interrogative identifying predicate, rather than adding a separate word corresponding to English is.

Answer 5: The linking y remains between the tea noun and the predicate, while the final vowel marks the question.

Answer 6: This short answer confirms the preceding proposition; it does not itself supply the identity if the original question was open.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask what a drink is, then check whether it is tea. Confirm the guess with a short yes and repeat the full identification to make the exchange clear.


## Lesson 3: Names and respectful reference

### Build the pattern

You will ask a person's name and recognise different forms of you. നിങ്ങൾ niṅṅaḷ is plural you and can be respectful singular, although local address preferences vary. നീ nī is familiar singular and should not be the automatic choice for strangers. Titles or names often replace pronouns in courteous Malayalam interaction. നിങ്ങളുടെ niṅṅaḷuṭe means your respectfully or plurally, and എന്റെ enṟe means my. പേര് pērŭ means name. For third-person reference, അവൻ avan is he, അവൾ avaḷ is she, and അവർ avar can mean they or a respected person. Modern finite verbs do not acquire gender endings to match these pronouns.

### Think before reading on

If your name needs a possessive, should you place niṅṅaḷ unchanged before pērŭ?

Use niṅṅaḷuṭe. The possessive form identifies whose name is being discussed. A subject form and an owner form are related but not interchangeable. This difference becomes useful immediately with books, houses and other personal objects.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| നിങ്ങളുടെ പേര് എന്താണ്? | Niṅṅaḷuṭe pērŭ entāṇŭ? | What is your name? (respectful) |
| എന്റെ പേര് രവിയാണ്. | Enṟe pērŭ raviyāṇŭ. | My name is Ravi. |
| നിങ്ങൾ മീനയാണോ? | Niṅṅaḷ mīnayāṇō? | Are you Meena? (respectful) |
| നിന്റെ പേര് എന്താണ്? | Ninṟe pērŭ entāṇŭ? | What is your name? (familiar) |
| ഇത് നിങ്ങളുടെ പുസ്തകമാണോ? | Itŭ niṅṅaḷuṭe pustakamāṇō? | Is this your book? (respectful) |
| അവർ ആരാണ്? | Avar ārāṇŭ? | Who is that person? (respectful) |

### Worked examples

`നിങ്ങളുടെ പേര് എന്താണ്?` - `Niṅṅaḷuṭe pērŭ entāṇŭ?` means “What is your name? (respectful)” The possessive identifies the name's owner, and the final question expression asks for the missing identifying information.

`എന്റെ പേര് രവിയാണ്.` - `Enṟe pērŭ raviyāṇŭ.` means “My name is Ravi.” The speaker's possessive forms the subject phrase, while the name joins the identifying predicate through y.

`നിങ്ങൾ മീനയാണോ?` - `Niṅṅaḷ mīnayāṇō?` means “Are you Meena? (respectful)” The respectful subject form belongs here because the listener is being identified directly, rather than appearing as an owner.

### Common trap

Do not assume niṅṅaḷ is the perfect address in every social setting. It is a useful standard form, but local titles and relationship terms may be more natural.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Malayalam. Keep the stated person and register.

1. What is your name? (respectful)
2. My name is Ravi.
3. Are you Meena? (respectful)
4. What is your name? (familiar)
5. Is this your book? (respectful)
6. Who is that person? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `നിങ്ങളുടെ പേര് എന്താണ്?` - `Niṅṅaḷuṭe pērŭ entāṇŭ?`
2. `എന്റെ പേര് രവിയാണ്.` - `Enṟe pērŭ raviyāṇŭ.`
3. `നിങ്ങൾ മീനയാണോ?` - `Niṅṅaḷ mīnayāṇō?`
4. `നിന്റെ പേര് എന്താണ്?` - `Ninṟe pērŭ entāṇŭ?`
5. `ഇത് നിങ്ങളുടെ പുസ്തകമാണോ?` - `Itŭ niṅṅaḷuṭe pustakamāṇō?`
6. `അവർ ആരാണ്?` - `Avar ārāṇŭ?`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The familiar possessive marks a different relationship to the listener while preserving the same grammatical question structure.

Answer 5: The owner expression precedes the book noun, and the final interrogative predicate checks the whole identification.

Answer 6: Ārŭ asks who, and the respectful pronoun can refer to one person without forcing a plural English translation.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask a visitor's name, give your own and ask whether a book is theirs. Then identify a respected person without changing the verb merely because of gender.


## Lesson 4: Existence and location with undu

### Build the pattern

You will say that a person or object is present. ഉണ്ട് uṇṭŭ expresses existence or presence; ഇവിടെ iviṭe means here and അവിടെ aviṭe means there. Unlike Tamil and Telugu, modern Malayalam normally does not change finite verbs for person, gender or number. Thus uṇṭŭ remains the same with I, he, she and a book in these examples. This does not make pronouns socially unimportant: the pronoun still identifies a participant and level of respect. Location can also be expressed through other constructions, but this lesson isolates the presence pattern so it can later support possession and absence.

### Think before reading on

If the subject changes from ഞാൻ ñān to അവൾ avaḷ, should uṇṭŭ acquire a feminine ending?

No. The verb remains uṇṭŭ. The pronoun identifies the person, while the existential states presence. This is a central Malayalam contrast with neighbouring languages that have more extensive person or gender agreement on finite verbs.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ഞാൻ ഇവിടെ ഉണ്ട്. | Ñān iviṭe uṇṭŭ. | I am here. |
| അവൻ അവിടെ ഉണ്ട്. | Avan aviṭe uṇṭŭ. | He is there. |
| അവൾ ഇവിടെ ഉണ്ട്. | Avaḷ iviṭe uṇṭŭ. | She is here. |
| പുസ്തകം അവിടെ ഉണ്ട്. | Pustakam aviṭe uṇṭŭ. | The book is there. |
| ഇവിടെ വെള്ളം ഉണ്ട്. | Iviṭe veḷḷam uṇṭŭ. | There is water here. |
| അവർ ഇവിടെ ഉണ്ട്. | Avar iviṭe uṇṭŭ. | They are here. |

### Worked examples

`ഞാൻ ഇവിടെ ഉണ്ട്.` - `Ñān iviṭe uṇṭŭ.` means “I am here.” The first-person pronoun identifies the person present, and the existential does not require a special first-person ending.

`അവൻ അവിടെ ഉണ്ട്.` - `Avan aviṭe uṇṭŭ.` means “He is there.” The masculine pronoun changes the referent, while the verb remains the same presence expression used with the speaker.

`അവൾ ഇവിടെ ഉണ്ട്.` - `Avaḷ iviṭe uṇṭŭ.` means “She is here.” The feminine subject introduces no feminine verb ending, illustrating the ordinary absence of gender agreement in modern Malayalam.

### Common trap

Do not borrow Tamil -āḷ or Telugu -di endings to mark a Malayalam female subject. The ordinary modern verb does not need those agreement changes.

2. He is there.
3. She is here.
4. The book is there.

6. They are here.

***Answers:***

1. `ഞാൻ ഇവിടെ ഉണ്ട്.` - `Ñān iviṭe uṇṭŭ.`
2. `അവൻ അവിടെ ഉണ്ട്.` - `Avan aviṭe uṇṭŭ.`
3. `അവൾ ഇവിടെ ഉണ്ട്.` - `Avaḷ iviṭe uṇṭŭ.`
4. `പുസ്തകം അവിടെ ഉണ്ട്.` - `Pustakam aviṭe uṇṭŭ.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The nonhuman noun combines with the same existential, so agreement does not distinguish the book from the earlier human subjects.

Answer 5: The place-first arrangement highlights availability at this location, providing a useful practical statement about a resource.

Answer 6: The plural human pronoun leaves the existential unchanged; context distinguishes plural avar from respectful singular reference.

## Lesson 5: Two negatives for two different claims

### Build the pattern

You will deny an identification and report that something is absent. അല്ല alla negates identity: ഇത് പുസ്തകം അല്ല means this is not a book. ഇല്ല illa negates existence: ഇവിടെ വെള്ളം ഇല്ല means there is no water here. The vowel difference is small but the grammatical distinction is useful. Malayalam often joins words across these boundaries in ordinary writing, but the separated teaching examples make the construction visible. The negative does not gain person or gender endings when the subject changes. A short അല്ല response can reject a proposed identification, while ഇല്ല can answer an availability question negatively.

### Think before reading on

Does this is not water deny that anything is present, or deny that the visible substance is water?

It rejects the identification and therefore uses alla. There is no water reports absence and uses illa. Work backward to the positive claim: an āṇŭ identification and a uṇṭŭ existence statement lead to different negatives.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ഇത് പുസ്തകം അല്ല. | Itŭ pustakam alla. | This is not a book. |
| അത് വെള്ളം അല്ല. | Atŭ veḷḷam alla. | That is not water. |
| ഇവിടെ വെള്ളം ഇല്ല. | Iviṭe veḷḷam illa. | There is no water here. |
| ഞാൻ അവിടെ ഇല്ല. | Ñān aviṭe illa. | I am not there. |
| അവൾ ഇവിടെ ഇല്ല. | Avaḷ iviṭe illa. | She is not here. |
| പുസ്തകം ഇവിടെ ഇല്ല. | Pustakam iviṭe illa. | The book is not here. |

### Worked examples

`ഇത് പുസ്തകം അല്ല.` - `Itŭ pustakam alla.` means “This is not a book.” The object remains present as a referent, but the proposed category is denied by the identity negative.

`അത് വെള്ളം അല്ല.` - `Atŭ veḷḷam alla.` means “That is not water.” The negative rejects the identification of the visible substance and does not claim the location is empty.

`ഇവിടെ വെള്ളം ഇല്ല.` - `Iviṭe veḷḷam illa.` means “There is no water here.” The existential negative reports absence within the named location, corresponding to positive uṇṭŭ rather than positive āṇŭ.

### Common trap

Do not treat alla and illa as spelling variants of a single word. Their vowel contrast corresponds to a real distinction between classification and existence in these examples.

2. That is not water.

***Answers:***

1. `ഇത് പുസ്തകം അല്ല.` - `Itŭ pustakam alla.`
2. `അത് വെള്ളം അല്ല.` - `Atŭ veḷḷam alla.`
3. `ഇവിടെ വെള്ളം ഇല്ല.` - `Iviṭe veḷḷam illa.`
4. `ഞാൻ അവിടെ ഇല്ല.` - `Ñān aviṭe illa.`
5. `അവൾ ഇവിടെ ഇല്ല.` - `Avaḷ iviṭe illa.`

Answer 4: The subject is the speaker, but the negative stays invariant instead of taking an imported first-person suffix.

Answer 5: The feminine pronoun identifies the absent person without causing the negative to adopt a gender-specific ending.

Answer 6: The same absence construction applies to a particular object whose identity is already established in the conversation.

## Lesson 6: Reading, drinking and present actions

### Build the pattern

You will describe simple current actions. വായിക്കുന്നു vāyikkunnu means reads or is reading, കുടിക്കുന്നു kuṭikkunnu means drinks or is drinking, and സംസാരിക്കുന്നു samsārikkunnu means speaks or is speaking. The -unnu series is central to present statements, while a dedicated ongoing construction comes next. In the neutral pattern an object precedes the final verb. The same verb form appears with I, he and she; the subject provides person information. ഇപ്പോൾ ippōḷ, now, gives an immediate-time reading, while ദിവസവും divasavum, every day, can identify a routine. A language name can function as what is spoken without an English preposition.

### Think before reading on

Will changing the reader from I to she require changing vāyikkunnu?

No. The subject changes, but the finite verb remains the same. Malayalam tense and aspect matter, yet person and gender do not normally create different modern finite forms. Keep those grammatical dimensions separate.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ഞാൻ പുസ്തകം വായിക്കുന്നു. | Ñān pustakam vāyikkunnu. | I am reading a book. |
| അവൾ പുസ്തകം വായിക്കുന്നു. | Avaḷ pustakam vāyikkunnu. | She is reading a book. |
| ഞാൻ വെള്ളം കുടിക്കുന്നു. | Ñān veḷḷam kuṭikkunnu. | I am drinking water. |
| അവൻ ചായ കുടിക്കുന്നു. | Avan cāya kuṭikkunnu. | He is drinking tea. |
| ഞാൻ മലയാളം സംസാരിക്കുന്നു. | Ñān malayāḷam samsārikkunnu. | I speak Malayalam. |

### Worked examples

## Lesson 7: Making an ongoing activity explicit

### Build the pattern

The person remains recoverable from the pronoun or context, because the construction does not change for a male or female subject. The longer spelling should be read as meaningful pieces rather than memorised as a shapeless string. ഇപ്പോൾ ippōḷ reinforces the current activity interpretation.

Why can āṇŭ appear in a sentence about reading when it was first introduced with names and object identities?

## Lesson 8: Human objects and object pronouns

### Build the pattern

You will distinguish the person doing an action from the person affected by it. Malayalam commonly marks human objects with -എ e. എന്നെ enne means me, നിങ്ങളെ niṅṅaḷe means you respectfully or plurally, and അവളെ avaḷe means her. കാണുന്നു kāṇunnu means sees or is seeing, and നോക്കുന്നു nōkkunnu means looks or is looking in the examples used here. Nonhuman objects often remain unmarked in ordinary activity descriptions, so object marking is not simply an equivalent of English the. The subject and object can both name people without requiring a different person ending on the verb.

### Think before reading on

In he sees me, why is the speaker expressed by enne rather than ñān?

The speaker is the object of seeing, not its agent. The object form marks that grammatical role. The subject avan identifies the observer, while the verb remains the same modern finite form that other subjects could use.

## Lesson 9: Courteous commands and invitations

Does Malayalam need a different command ending for a male visitor and a female visitor?

## Lesson 10: Inside a place and toward a place

### Build the pattern

You will distinguish location from destination. വീട് vīṭŭ means house; വീട്ടിൽ vīṭṭil means at home or in the house, and വീട്ടിലേക്ക് vīṭṭilēkkŭ means to or toward home. The house stem doubles its consonant before these endings. കട kaṭa, shop, gives കടയിൽ kaṭayil and കടയിലേക്ക് kaṭayilēkkŭ. The destination expression is longer because it marks a movement relation rather than mere presence. പോകുന്നു pōkunnu means goes or is going. Place endings follow their nouns, while English generally places prepositions before them. The noun's role should be clear before choosing the ending.

### Think before reading on

Will vīṭṭil answer the same question as vīṭṭilēkkŭ when someone describes a journey?

No. Vīṭṭil locates someone at home; vīṭṭilēkkŭ identifies home as a destination. The difference is not just stylistic length. It changes the relationship between the place and the event.

## Lesson 11: Wanting and needing with enikku

### Build the pattern

You will request a drink or help with the dative experiencer. എനിക്ക് enikkŭ means to me, and നിങ്ങൾക്ക് niṅṅaḷkkŭ means to you respectfully or plurally. വേണം vēṇam expresses wanting or needing in the examples here. വേണ്ട vēṇṭa rejects something as unwanted or unnecessary. The desired noun stands before the final expression. A speaker who reads uses ñān as an agent; a speaker who needs water uses enikkŭ in this construction. That is a grammatical difference, not an indication of uncertainty or weak desire. കുറച്ച് kuṟaccŭ means a little and can specify a manageable quantity.

### Think before reading on

Why does I want water begin with enikkŭ rather than the subject form ñān?

The need is presented as applying to an experiencer in the dative. English chooses an ordinary subject with want, but Malayalam's construction assigns a different role. Learning the whole pattern makes it easy to substitute tea, a book or help.

`എനിക്ക് വെള്ളം വേണം.` - `Enikkŭ veḷḷam vēṇam.` means “I want water.” The dative experiencer identifies whose need is involved, while the desired substance appears before the final need expression.

`നിങ്ങൾക്ക് എന്ത് വേണം?` - `Niṅṅaḷkkŭ entŭ vēṇam?` means “What do you need? (respectful)” The unknown desired item is replaced by entŭ, leaving the respectful dative person and final predicate in place.

### Common trap

Do not use vēṇṭa as the negative for every action. It rejects desire or necessity in this construction; absent objects and actions that did not happen use different negatives.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Malayalam. Keep the stated person and register.

1. I want water.
2. I need one book.
3. What do you need? (respectful)
4. I do not want tea.
5. I need help.
6. I want a little water.

***Answers:***

1. `എനിക്ക് വെള്ളം വേണം.` - `Enikkŭ veḷḷam vēṇam.`
2. `എനിക്ക് ഒരു പുസ്തകം വേണം.` - `Enikkŭ oru pustakam vēṇam.`
3. `നിങ്ങൾക്ക് എന്ത് വേണം?` - `Niṅṅaḷkkŭ entŭ vēṇam?`
4. `എനിക്ക് ചായ വേണ്ട.` - `Enikkŭ cāya vēṇṭa.`
5. `എനിക്ക് സഹായം വേണം.` - `Enikkŭ sahāyam vēṇam.`
6. `എനിക്ക് കുറച്ച് വെള്ളം വേണം.` - `Enikkŭ kuṟaccŭ veḷḷam vēṇam.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The negative need expression declines tea rather than claiming that tea does not exist in the location.

Answer 5: The same construction expresses a practical need for assistance, demonstrating transfer beyond food and drink requests.

Answer 6: The amount expression narrows the requested quantity while preserving the rest of the need statement unchanged.

### Retrieve and recombine

## Lesson 12: Having things and identifying owners

### Build the pattern

You will distinguish ownership from availability. എന്റെ enṟe means my, and നിങ്ങളുടെ niṅṅaḷuṭe means your respectfully or plurally. These modify a noun directly. A common possession statement uses a dative person and uṇṭŭ: എനിക്ക് പുസ്തകം ഉണ്ട് means I have a book. The possessed thing is presented as existing for the person. This construction can also describe resources such as time, not only physical property. ആരുടെ āruṭe means whose. The identity of an owner can be questioned independently from whether an object is currently available. Neither construction requires an English-style verb have with person endings.

### Think before reading on

Does the same grammatical expression necessarily answer both whose book is this and do you have a book available?

No. The first asks for a possessive relationship, while the second asks about availability to a person. Malayalam uses an owner phrase for one and a dative existential construction for the other in these examples.

`എനിക്ക് പുസ്തകം ഉണ്ട്.` - `Enikkŭ pustakam uṇṭŭ.` means “I have a book.” The dative possessor and existential express availability without requiring a separate finite verb corresponding to English have.

Do not put enṟe in the place of enikkŭ in the existential possession frame. They are related person forms, but one modifies a noun and the other marks a dative participant.

## Lesson 13: Plural objects, people and inclusive we

### Build the pattern

You will request small quantities and distinguish two kinds of we. ഒന്ന് onnŭ is one independently, while ഒരു oru precedes an ordinary noun. രണ്ട് raṇṭŭ and മൂന്ന് mūnnŭ mean two and three. പുസ്തകങ്ങൾ pustakaṅṅaḷ is the plural of book. Human counts can use രണ്ട് പേർ raṇṭŭ pēr, two people. Malayalam distinguishes നമ്മൾ nammaḷ, we including the listener, from ഞങ്ങൾ ñaṅṅaḷ, we excluding the listener. This distinction concerns membership, not politeness or gender. The finite verb remains unchanged with either group. A clear invitation often needs inclusive reference, while reporting your own group's earlier action may be exclusive.

### Think before reading on

If you tell a visitor we will come tomorrow but mean your family without the visitor, which we is appropriate?

Use ñaṅṅaḷ for a group excluding the listener. Nammaḷ would normally include that person. English leaves this distinction unstated, so Malayalam requires attention to who belongs to the group being described.

## Lesson 14: Descriptions before nouns and as predicates

### Build the pattern

You will describe a good book, a large house and a high price. നല്ല nalla, good, വലിയ valiya, big, and ചെറിയ ceṟiya, small, usually precede the noun. Predicative forms can differ: വലുതാണ് valutāṇŭ means is big for a thing, and നല്ലതാണ് nallatāṇŭ means is good. These forms contain nominal descriptive material plus the identifying predicate. വില vila means price, and കൂടുതലാണ് kūṭutalāṇŭ means is more or is high in an amount context. Descriptions do not acquire masculine or feminine finite endings to match an owner. The structure depends on how the description relates to the noun, not merely on the English adjective.

### Think before reading on

Can valiya simply be placed after house unchanged to make every sentence meaning the house is big?

The useful predicative form taught here is vīṭŭ valutāṇŭ. Attributive valiya and predicative valutu-related forms have different shapes. Recognise the whole descriptive pattern rather than moving an English adjective mechanically across the noun.

## Lesson 15: Completed events without person endings

### Build the pattern

You will report a few completed actions. വന്നു vannu means came, പോയി pōyi means went, വായിച്ചു vāyiccu means read in the past, and കുടിച്ചു kuṭiccu means drank. ഇന്നലെ innale means yesterday. These past forms do not change with I, he or she, but their stems vary by verb. The lack of agreement does not mean that past tense is optional: a completed event needs an appropriate past form in the neutral reports taught here. A destination such as vīṭṭilēkkŭ remains a destination when the going verb changes from present to past. The time expression and verb work together.

### Think before reading on

If I went home becomes she went home, which word must change in these examples?

The subject changes from ñān to avaḷ, while pōyi remains unchanged. If instead present going becomes past going, the verb itself must change. Malayalam separates subject identity from the tense distinctions carried by the predicate.

## Lesson 16: Asking about the past and denying an event

### Build the pattern

You will ask when someone came and say that an action did not happen. എപ്പോൾ eppōḷ means when. A past yes-or-no question can use വന്നോ vannō, did someone come. The negative വന്നില്ല vannilla means did not come, and വായിച്ചില്ല vāyiccilla means did not read. The negative combines with past material, so it differs from a future negative such as varilla introduced later. Neither positive nor negative requires person endings in the modern forms used here. ഇനിയും iniyum or ഇതുവരെ ituvare can provide continuing-expectation contexts; this lesson first uses explicit yesterday to keep the past interpretation clear.

### Think before reading on

Does putting the question word eppōḷ into a sentence require moving the past verb before the subject?

No. The question word asks for the missing time while the finite verb remains in its usual final position. A yes-or-no question instead changes the final form to vannō. The two question types request different information.

`നിങ്ങൾ ഇന്നലെ വന്നോ?` - `Niṅṅaḷ innale vannō?` means “Did you come yesterday? (respectful)” The final long ō makes the past arrival a yes-or-no question while the subject remains respectful or plural.

### Common trap

## Lesson 17: Future events and regular habits

### Build the pattern

You will predict an arrival and describe a routine with the -um series. വരും varum means will come or comes habitually in context; പോകും pōkum means will go; വായിക്കും vāyikkum means will read. നാളെ nāḷe, tomorrow, points to a future event, while ദിവസവും divasavum, every day, supports a repeated interpretation. The future form remains the same across persons and genders. A first-person promise can also use forms such as വരാം varām, which are introduced with invitations and permission later. Begin by separating the broad future or habitual -um form from present -unnu and past forms.

### Think before reading on

Why can vāyikkum receive either will read or reads every day as its English translation?

The verb series combines with time expressions and context to determine whether one future event or a recurring activity is meant. Malayalam need not add an independent word corresponding to English will in these examples.

## Lesson 18: Future negatives and prohibitions

### Build the pattern

You will decline a future action and ask someone not to act. വരില്ല varilla means will not or does not come, while പോകില്ല pōkilla means will not or does not go. The intended time is clarified by context or nāḷe. Negative commands use വരരുത് vararutŭ, do not come, and സംസാരിക്കരുത് samsārikkarutŭ, do not speak. These forms address another person's behaviour rather than merely reporting a future nonaction. ദയവായി dayavāyi can soften the prohibition. The final reduced vowel in arutŭ should not be confused with a full long command vowel such as the ū in varū.

### Think before reading on

Is varilla a direct substitute for vararutŭ when you want another person not to come?

No. Varilla states or predicts nonarrival; vararutŭ prohibits or requests avoidance of arrival. The speaker's communicative aim determines which negative construction is appropriate, even though both relate to the same action.

## Lesson 19: Knowing, liking and understanding

### Build the pattern

You will express preference and knowledge with dative experiencers. ഇഷ്ടമാണ് iṣṭamāṇŭ expresses liking, അറിയാം aṟiyām expresses knowing, and അറിയില്ല aṟiyilla is the knowledge negative in these examples. എനിക്ക് enikkŭ introduces the experiencer: enikkŭ cāya iṣṭamāṇŭ means I like tea. മനസ്സിലായില്ല manassilāyilla means did not understand, often useful after an unclear reply. Why is enikkŭ useful with liking and knowing even though ordinary speaking uses ñān?

Liking and knowledge are expressed as states applying to an experiencer in these constructions. Speaking is an action with an agent. Malayalam does not force every English I-verb sentence into the same subject pattern.

## Lesson 20: Ability, permission and proposed action

### Build the pattern

You will state an ability and ask permission for a proposed action. വായിക്കാൻ vāyikkān means to read, and കഴിയും kaḻiyum expresses ability or possibility; കഴിയില്ല kaḻiyilla is its negative. എനിക്ക് വായിക്കാൻ കഴിയും therefore means I can read. Permission questions can use വരാമോ varāmō, may I come, and ഇരിക്കാമോ irikkāmō, may I sit. The -ām series can also express a proposal or commitment in context, so it is not a universal permission label detached from the sentence. Explicit ñān keeps first-person permission questions clear. നമുക്ക് namukkŭ is useful in let's constructions that include the listener.

### Think before reading on

Does the physical ability to come automatically tell you whether a visit is permitted or convenient?

No. Ability and permission are different relationships. The kaḻiyum construction addresses ability or possibility, while the first-person -āmō questions here request acceptance of a proposed action. Context makes their conversational purpose clear.

## Lesson 21: Companions, sources and recipients

### Build the pattern

You will distinguish travelling with someone, speaking to someone and coming from a place. കൂടെ kūṭe means with or together with in the companion construction: എന്റെ കൂടെ enṟe kūṭe, with me. എന്നോട് ennōṭŭ means to me or with me in speech-oriented contexts. വീട്ടിൽ നിന്ന് vīṭṭil ninnŭ means from home, combining a locative phrase with a source expression. These English prepositions overlap, but Malayalam distinguishes the relationships. തരൂ tarū means give toward the speaker in a practical request; കൊടുക്കൂ koṭukkū is used for giving to another recipient. Direction in giving is useful to recognise rather than flatten into one dictionary equivalent.

### Think before reading on

Is with me in come with me necessarily expressed the same way as to me in tell me?

No. A travelling companion and a speech recipient have different roles. Enṟe kūṭe identifies accompaniment, while ennōṭŭ is appropriate with telling or speaking in the examples here. Choose the relation before choosing the form.

5. `എനിക്ക് പുസ്തകം തരൂ.` - `Enikkŭ pustakam tarū.`
6. `അവൾക്ക് പുസ്തകം കൊടുക്കൂ.` - `Avaḷkkŭ pustakam koṭukkū.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The shop becomes the source of the journey without changing the past coming verb for the feminine subject.

Answer 5: The dative recipient and speaker-directed giving verb create a natural request for an object to be handed toward the speaker.

Answer 6: The third-person recipient pairs with koṭukkū, contrasting the direction of transfer with the preceding request to give to me.

### Retrieve and recombine

Invite a friend to come with you, report coming from home, and ask them to tell you something. Then request a book using the give-toward-me verb.


## Lesson 22: Linking actions and describing a noun by an event

### Build the pattern

You will connect two actions and identify a book through reading. വന്നിട്ട് vanniṭṭŭ means after coming in the sequential examples, while വായിച്ച് vāyiccŭ is a nonfinal reading form. The final finite verb supplies the main tense or command force. A different form, വായിച്ച vāyicca, modifies a following noun: ഞാൻ വായിച്ച പുസ്തകം means the book I read. Malayalam puts this event description before the noun rather than adding an English relative pronoun after it. The contrast between final reduced ŭ and final a is useful: vāyiccŭ links actions, while vāyicca modifies a noun in the examples here.

### Think before reading on

How does the position of book help distinguish read and then came from the book that I read?

In the relative construction, the event description comes immediately before the noun it identifies. In the action sequence, the nonfinal verb leads to another verb. The following word's role helps reveal how the earlier verb form functions.

### Useful language in context

| അവൾ വായിച്ച പുസ്തകം ഇതാണ്. | Avaḷ vāyicca pustakam itāṇŭ. | This is the book she read. |

| ഞാൻ പുസ്തകം എടുത്ത് വന്നു. | Ñān pustakam eṭuttŭ vannu. | I brought the book. |

### Worked examples

`വന്നിട്ട് പറയൂ.` - `Vanniṭṭŭ paṟayū.` means “Come and then tell me. (polite)” The first action is explicitly prior to the final telling request, whose imperative supplies the conversational force of the sequence.

`ഞാൻ വായിച്ച് വന്നു.` - `Ñān vāyiccŭ vannu.` means “I read and came.” The nonfinal reading form leads into a past arrival, with the final verb carrying the main finite time information.

`ഞാൻ വായിച്ച പുസ്തകം നല്ലതാണ്.` - `Ñān vāyicca pustakam nallatāṇŭ.` means “The book I read is good.” The reading event precedes and identifies the book noun, which then receives the main evaluative predicate.

### Common trap

Do not substitute vāyiccŭ for vāyicca before a noun. The small ending difference separates two useful constructions and should not be erased in romanisation.

***Answers:***

4. `അവൾ വായിച്ച പുസ്തകം ഇതാണ്.` - `Avaḷ vāyicca pustakam itāṇŭ.`
5. `പുസ്തകം എടുത്ത് വരൂ.` - `Pustakam eṭuttŭ varū.`
6. `ഞാൻ പുസ്തകം എടുത്ത് വന്നു.` - `Ñān pustakam eṭuttŭ vannu.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The feminine subject belongs inside the modifying event, while the main clause identifies the resulting book phrase.

Answer 5: Taking combines with coming to express bringing, and the final imperative makes the sequence a practical request.

Answer 6: The final verb changes from a command to a past report, while the take-and-come relationship remains intact.

## Lesson 23: Conditions and contrast in a short plan

### Build the pattern

You will make a plan dependent on time and contrast availability with intention. ഉണ്ടെങ്കിൽ uṇṭeṅkil means if there is or if someone has in the dative possession construction. വന്നാൽ vannāl means if someone comes. The conditional form presents a possible circumstance rather than asserting that it happened. പക്ഷേ pakṣē means but and introduces contrast. സമയം samayam means time, and the already familiar dative existential expresses having time. A conditional invitation can end with varū, while a conditional commitment can end with varām or varum according to the intended meaning. Explicit subjects keep different participants clear.

### Think before reading on

Does vannāl report a definite past arrival because it resembles vannu?

No. The conditional ending makes arrival a condition. The final clause states a consequence, request or commitment if that condition is met. Do not let the resemblance to a past stem override the function of the complete form.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| നിങ്ങൾക്ക് സമയം ഉണ്ടോ? | Niṅṅaḷkkŭ samayam uṇṭō? | Do you have time? (respectful) |
| സമയം ഉണ്ടെങ്കിൽ വരൂ. | Samayam uṇṭeṅkil varū. | Come if there is time. (polite) |
| നിങ്ങൾ വന്നാൽ ഞാൻ വരാം. | Niṅṅaḷ vannāl ñān varām. | If you come, I will come. (respectful) |
| എനിക്ക് സമയം ഇല്ല. | Enikkŭ samayam illa. | I do not have time. |
| എനിക്ക് സമയം ഉണ്ട് പക്ഷേ ഞാൻ വരില്ല. | Enikkŭ samayam uṇṭŭ pakṣē ñān varilla. | I have time, but I will not come. |
| അവർ വന്നാൽ പറയൂ. | Avar vannāl paṟayū. | Tell me if they come. (polite) |

### Worked examples

`നിങ്ങൾക്ക് സമയം ഉണ്ടോ?` - `Niṅṅaḷkkŭ samayam uṇṭō?` means “Do you have time? (respectful)” The dative existential asks about available time before the conversation proposes a visit or shared activity.

`സമയം ഉണ്ടെങ്കിൽ വരൂ.` - `Samayam uṇṭeṅkil varū.` means “Come if there is time. (polite)” The existential conditional supplies the circumstance, while the final imperative turns the main clause into an invitation.

`നിങ്ങൾ വന്നാൽ ഞാൻ വരാം.` - `Niṅṅaḷ vannāl ñān varām.` means “If you come, I will come. (respectful)” The listener's arrival is the condition, and the explicit first-person subject makes the resulting commitment belong to the speaker.

### Common trap

Do not interchange pakṣē and a conditional ending. But contrasts two claims, while if makes one circumstance the condition for another action or result.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Malayalam. Keep the stated person and register.

1. Do you have time? (respectful)
2. Come if there is time. (polite)
3. If you come, I will come. (respectful)
4. I do not have time.
5. I have time, but I will not come.
6. Tell me if they come. (polite)

***Answers:***

1. `നിങ്ങൾക്ക് സമയം ഉണ്ടോ?` - `Niṅṅaḷkkŭ samayam uṇṭō?`
2. `സമയം ഉണ്ടെങ്കിൽ വരൂ.` - `Samayam uṇṭeṅkil varū.`
3. `നിങ്ങൾ വന്നാൽ ഞാൻ വരാം.` - `Niṅṅaḷ vannāl ñān varām.`
4. `എനിക്ക് സമയം ഇല്ല.` - `Enikkŭ samayam illa.`
5. `എനിക്ക് സമയം ഉണ്ട് പക്ഷേ ഞാൻ വരില്ല.` - `Enikkŭ samayam uṇṭŭ pakṣē ñān varilla.`
6. `അവർ വന്നാൽ പറയൂ.` - `Avar vannāl paṟayū.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The negative existential denies the availability of time and can serve as a reason to decline a proposed visit.

Answer 5: The contrast word marks a departure from the expected link between available time and willingness to attend.

Answer 6: The conditional arrival sets the trigger for a later report, while the final request leaves the recipient understood from context.

### Retrieve and recombine

Say that you have time but will not come. Then construct an invitation that depends on the listener having time, explaining how the clause relationship differs.


## Lesson 24: A shop exchange and a repair request

### Build the pattern

You will buy a small quantity and ask for repetition when the reply is unclear. എത്ര etra asks how much or how many, വില vila means price, and രൂപ rūpa names rupees in an amount expression. വീണ്ടും vīṇṭum means again. The request വീണ്ടും പറയൂ asks the speaker to repeat the relevant message. Combine these with oru, two-book quantities, enikkŭ and vēṇam. A price question can be compact because the item is visible; an explicit price noun helps when the intended amount might otherwise be unclear. Good repair keeps the understood topic and asks only for missing information.

### Think before reading on

If you know the item but miss the seller's price, what can you ask without restarting the whole interaction?

Ask the price again or say vīṇṭum paṟayū. The repeated message remains anchored to the current purchase. A focused request is easier to answer than a vague claim that you do not know anything.

### Worked examples

`ഇതിന്റെ വില എത്രയാണ്?` - `Itinṟe vila etrayāṇŭ?` means “What is the price of this?” The possessive form of this modifies price, and the amount question combines with the identifying predicate through y.

`എനിക്ക് ഒരു ചായ വേണം.` - `Enikkŭ oru cāya vēṇam.` means “I want one tea.” The beverage is counted as a serving in the ordering context, and the dative need construction expresses the request.

`എനിക്ക് രണ്ട് പുസ്തകങ്ങൾ തരൂ.` - `Enikkŭ raṇṭŭ pustakaṅṅaḷ tarū.` means “Give me two books. (polite)” The speaker-directed giving verb combines with a recipient and a plural quantity phrase to request the selected items.

### Common trap

Do not use a guessed number merely to keep the exchange moving. Ask for repetition or a slower reply, then confirm the quantity and price separately.

***Answers:***

1. `ഇതിന്റെ വില എത്രയാണ്?` - `Itinṟe vila etrayāṇŭ?`

4. `വില പത്ത് രൂപയാണ്.` - `Vila pattŭ rūpayāṇŭ.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The amount expression identifies the price, and the currency noun joins the predicate through a linking y.

Answer 5: The repetition request refers to the preceding message and is complete when its content is clear from the exchange.

Answer 6: The manner word names the needed adjustment, while the please expression and request form support courteous conversation repair.

## Lesson 25: Cumulative review through a visit

### Build the pattern

You will combine identification, present location, past activity and a future proposal in a short account. Imagine Meena at home with a book, preparing to visit a shop tomorrow. Malayalam offers different predicates for identification, existence, action and permission. The same person can appear as ñān when acting, enikkŭ when needing something, and enṟe when modifying an owned noun. These choices depend on grammatical role, not on changing identity. Modern verbs remain free of the person-gender agreement patterns found in some neighbouring languages. The review asks you to preserve these distinctions while telling a coherent story rather than reciting a tense list.

### Think before reading on

What changes when I am at home becomes I went to the shop yesterday: only the verb or the place relationship too?

The house location is replaced by a shop destination, the existential becomes a past movement verb, and yesterday supplies the time. A meaningful change must consider the event, participants and noun roles together. Simply replacing the last word is insufficient.

`എന്റെ പേര് മീനയാണ്.` - `Enṟe pērŭ mīnayāṇŭ.` means “My name is Meena.” The possessive name phrase receives an identifying predicate, without using the existential reserved for presence and availability.

`ഞാൻ ഇപ്പോൾ വീട്ടിൽ ഉണ്ട്.` - `Ñān ippōḷ vīṭṭil uṇṭŭ.` means “I am at home now.” The present-time word and locative house expression combine with the existential to report the speaker's current presence.

`ഞാൻ ഇന്നലെ പുസ്തകം വായിച്ചു.` - `Ñān innale pustakam vāyiccu.` means “I read a book yesterday.” The past-time expression agrees with the completed reading verb, while the object retains the neutral preverbal position.

### Common trap

Do not treat this foundation as evidence of unrestricted conversational fluency. Region-specific address, connected pronunciation and longer authentic discourse still need listening practice and fluent-speaker feedback beyond these lessons.
