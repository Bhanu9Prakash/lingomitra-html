# Gujarati: A pattern-first foundation

This course uses contemporary standard Gujarati in Gujarati script, with neutral everyday sentences. It does not attempt to represent every regional or community variety. Romanisation keeps ā ī ū for helpful spelling distinctions, tildes for nasal vowels, ṭ ḍ ṇ ḷ for retroflex consonants, ś for sh and chh for the aspirated sound in છે. These aids do not fully represent Gujarati vowel quality or breathy voice. The short o-like vowel in loanword coffee is written ŏ here.

### References and review notes

The introductory script and sound discussion was checked with Jagdish Dave’s [Colloquial Gujarati publisher preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781317305354_A25776814/preview-9781317305354_A25776814.pdf). Explanations and exercises are original; spoken preferences, negative constructions and register remain fluent-speaker review priorities.

## Lesson 1: A first identity with છું

**Outcome:** Introduce yourself and identify someone else.

### Think before reading

If હું means I and તે means that person, should the final word for be remain identical?

### Build the pattern

Gujarati places an identity before a final form of be. હું વિદ્યાર્થી છું means I am a student. The first-person copula is છું; third-person તે uses છે. There is no separate article corresponding to English a in this ordinary identity statement. Start with the person, add the identity, and finish the grammatical link.

તે can refer to he, she or that. Its form does not itself specify the person’s gender. In everyday speech એ is also very common; this course keeps તે in its early complete examples and introduces એ as an ordinary spoken alternative, not an error. Gender can appear elsewhere in the sentence, including some profession words and later adjective or verb forms.

The Gujarati script is related to Devanagari but has its own letter forms. Learn હું and છું as visually distinct words: both have nasalisation, but their initial consonants differ. The romanisation hũ and chhũ keeps the nasal vowel visible. A tilde does not mean a separate English n after the vowel. The breath in chh also matters. Script recognition and useful sentence construction can develop together rather than waiting for complete alphabet mastery.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| હું વિદ્યાર્થી છું. | Hũ vidyārthī chhũ. | I am a student. |
| તે શિક્ષક છે. | Te śikṣak chhe. | That person is a teacher. |
| હું ગુજરાતી છું. | Hũ gujarātī chhũ. | I am Gujarati. |
| તે વિદ્યાર્થી છે. | Te vidyārthī chhe. | That person is a student. |
| નમસ્તે. | Namaste. | Hello, a respectful greeting. |
| હું તૈયાર છું. | Hũ taiyār chhũ. | I am ready. |

### Common trap

Do not use છે after every pronoun merely because it translates is. હું requires છું in the affirmative present. Also, Gujarati તે does not inherently tell you whether the person is male or female.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you are a student.
2. Identify someone as a teacher, using શિક્ષક.
3. Say you are Gujarati.
4. Say that person is a student.

***Answers:***

1. `હું વિદ્યાર્થી છું.` - `Hũ vidyārthī chhũ.`
2. `તે શિક્ષક છે.` - `Te śikṣak chhe.`
3. `હું ગુજરાતી છું.` - `Hũ gujarātī chhũ.`
4. `તે વિદ્યાર્થી છે.` - `Te vidyārthī chhe.`

### Why these answers work

The first and third answers use હું with છું. The other two use તે with છે. Identity nouns need no English-style a. The examples therefore change the copula with person even when the identity word remains the same.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce yourself, then identify an imaginary student. Retrieve the two copulas before looking back. Notice whether you are speaking as the person or about the person.


## Lesson 2: Respect and who belongs in we

**Outcome:** Address someone respectfully and distinguish inclusive from exclusive we.

### Think before reading

A host says we are ready to a visitor. Is the visitor necessarily included in that we?

### Build the pattern

Gujarati distinguishes અમે, we excluding the listener, from આપણે, we including the listener. English usually leaves this distinction to context; Gujarati can make it explicit in the pronoun. Both use છીએ in the present identity or state construction. The change in pronoun changes group membership without changing this copula.

તું is familiar singular you and takes છે. તમે is plural or respectful singular you and takes છો. Use તમે with a new adult acquaintance unless the relationship supports familiarity. A respectful form is not restricted to elders, and addressing one person does not force તું.

તૈયાર, ready, stays unchanged in the examples. This allows you to concentrate on the social and person distinctions in the final verb. અમે તૈયાર છીએ reports the readiness of the speaker’s group; આપણે તૈયાર છીએ includes the listener in that shared state. A group invitation often needs the inclusive choice.

Gujarati has additional highly formal ways to address people, but the ordinary તમે system is enough for these foundation interactions. First decide whether the listener is being addressed or included in a group. Then choose the pronoun-copula pair. This order is more reliable than translating each English occurrence of you or we with the first dictionary item you remember.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| તમે તૈયાર છો. | Tame taiyār chho. | You are ready, respectfully or plurally. |
| તું તૈયાર છે. | Tũ taiyār chhe. | You are ready, familiarly. |
| અમે તૈયાર છીએ. | Ame taiyār chhīe. | We are ready, excluding the listener. |
| આપણે તૈયાર છીએ. | Āpṇe taiyār chhīe. | We are ready, including the listener. |
| તેઓ તૈયાર છે. | Teo taiyār chhe. | They are ready, in a more formal pronoun style. |
| હું તૈયાર છું. | Hũ taiyār chhũ. | I am ready. |

### Common trap

Do not swap અમે and આપણે without checking whether the listener belongs to the group. Also, respectful singular તમે retains છો even when there is only one person in front of you.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Tell an adult stranger they are ready.
2. Tell a close friend they are ready.
3. Say your group is ready, excluding the listener.
4. Say your group is ready, including the listener.

***Answers:***

1. `તમે તૈયાર છો.` - `Tame taiyār chho.`
2. `તું તૈયાર છે.` - `Tũ taiyār chhe.`
3. `અમે તૈયાર છીએ.` - `Ame taiyār chhīe.`
4. `આપણે તૈયાર છીએ.` - `Āpṇe taiyār chhīe.`

### Why these answers work

તમે છો and તું છે encode different address choices. The two we answers share છીએ because both are first-person plural. Their difference concerns inclusion, which English ready does not reveal and the unchanged adjective તૈયાર cannot supply.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine a family talking to a driver. Report the family’s readiness excluding the driver, then form a statement that includes everyone. Keep respect separate from group membership.


## Lesson 3: Questions and the negative copula

**Outcome:** Ask whether someone is ready and deny a state.

### Think before reading

Would turning a statement into a question require moving છો before તમે as English moves are?

### Build the pattern

A Gujarati yes-or-no question can retain statement order and use question intonation. Written તમે તૈયાર છો? asks are you ready. An initial શું can explicitly mark a yes-or-no question, but the short conversational examples here rely on the normal order and question mark. No auxiliary corresponding to English do is inserted.

નથી is the negative present copula. હું તૈયાર નથી means I am not ready, while તે તૈયાર નથી means that person is not ready. Unlike the affirmative છું and છે contrast, નથી is stable in these basic examples. Do not add it after a complete positive copula as though Gujarati negation simply appended an English not.

હા means yes and ના means no. A complete positive or negative statement can be clearer than a bare answer when the question is complicated or itself negative. This lesson asks affirmative questions so that the answer’s interpretation stays straightforward.

Gujarati શું also means what in information questions. Its function depends on position and the structure around it. Learn the current question pattern as a whole rather than assigning one English gloss to every occurrence. In a live conversation, the rising or checking intonation helps identify a question even when no extra particle appears. In writing, the punctuation carries part of that communicative signal, while person agreement remains grammatical.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| તમે તૈયાર છો? | Tame taiyār chho? | Are you ready, respectfully? |
| હું તૈયાર નથી. | Hũ taiyār nathī. | I am not ready. |
| તે વિદ્યાર્થી છે? | Te vidyārthī chhe? | Is that person a student? |
| તે તૈયાર નથી. | Te taiyār nathī. | That person is not ready. |
| હા. | Hā. | Yes. |
| ના. | Nā. | No. |

### Common trap

Do not invert the subject and copula solely to imitate English. Also, નથી replaces the positive copula in these denials; it is not an extra ending added to છું or છે.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask an adult whether they are ready.
2. Say you are not ready.
3. Ask whether that person is a student.
4. Say that person is not ready.

***Answers:***

1. `તમે તૈયાર છો?` - `Tame taiyār chho?`
2. `હું તૈયાર નથી.` - `Hũ taiyār nathī.`
3. `તે વિદ્યાર્થી છે?` - `Te vidyārthī chhe?`
4. `તે તૈયાર નથી.` - `Te taiyār nathī.`

### Why these answers work

The questions keep the affirmative person-appropriate copula. The denials use નથી without a second positive copula. The person words remain in their ordinary positions because asking or denying does not change who is under discussion.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask whether someone is a student, then deny only their readiness. Explain why a response about readiness would not directly answer the identity question.


## Lesson 4: Three genders in descriptions

**Outcome:** Match a changing adjective to a boy, girl or house.

### Think before reading

If the owner is a woman, must a description of her house become feminine?

### Build the pattern

Gujarati nouns have masculine, feminine or neuter gender. A noun’s gender affects changing adjectives, but it does not always follow the sex of an owner or user. ઘર, house, is neuter; છોકરો, boy, is masculine; and છોકરી, girl, is feminine.

મોટો means big with a masculine singular noun, મોટી with a feminine noun, and મોટું with a neuter singular noun. The nasal vowel in મોટું is part of the form. Thus ઘર મોટું છે describes a house regardless of who lives there. The demonstrative આ, this, does not change across these examples, which distinguishes it from the adjective.

Not every adjective changes. તૈયાર, ready, remains fixed, as does ગરમ, hot. Learn whether a new adjective belongs to the changing or unchanging group rather than forcing every descriptive word into an -o pattern. Compare a changing adjective with a fixed one using the same noun to see which part is grammatical agreement.

Gujarati script keeps the neuter nasalisation visible with the dot mark. The romanisation uses ũ. Saying a separate n or dropping nasalisation entirely can obscure the distinction. Written forms also do not map to English vowel length with perfect simplicity; the reading aid marks useful orthographic distinctions but listening is needed for natural vowel quality.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| આ ઘર મોટું છે. | Ā ghar moṭũ chhe. | This house is big. |
| આ છોકરો મોટો છે. | Ā chhokro moṭo chhe. | This boy is big. |
| આ છોકરી મોટી છે. | Ā chhokrī moṭī chhe. | This girl is big. |
| પાણી ગરમ છે. | Pāṇī garam chhe. | The water is hot. |
| ચા ગરમ છે. | Chā garam chhe. | The tea is hot. |
| આ ઘર નાનું છે. | Ā ghar nānũ chhe. | This house is small. |

### Common trap

The adjective follows the described noun, not the owner’s gender. Do not alter આ to make it masculine, feminine or neuter in these noun phrases; it already works with all three.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say this house is big.
2. Say this boy is big.
3. Say this girl is big.
4. Say the water is hot.

***Answers:***

1. `આ ઘર મોટું છે.` - `Ā ghar moṭũ chhe.`
2. `આ છોકરો મોટો છે.` - `Ā chhokro moṭo chhe.`
3. `આ છોકરી મોટી છે.` - `Ā chhokrī moṭī chhe.`
4. `પાણી ગરમ છે.` - `Pāṇī garam chhe.`

### Why these answers work

The first three choose મોટું, મોટો and મોટી by noun gender. The fourth keeps ગરમ unchanged. All use છે because they describe third-person singular referents. The demonstrative આ contributes nearness without duplicating the adjective’s gender endings.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a small house and a big girl, then change both descriptions to ready where meaningful. Identify which adjective changes and which remains fixed.


## Lesson 5: Finding the missing information

**Outcome:** Ask who, what and where questions.

### Think before reading

If અહીં supplies here, what word can replace it when you do not know the location?

### Build the pattern

ક્યાં means where, કોણ means who, and શું means what. A location question such as તે ક્યાં છે keeps the unknown place before the final copula. You do not need English question inversion. The answer can replace ક્યાં with અહીં, here, or ત્યાં, there.

આ શું છે asks what this is. Here શું occupies the identity slot, unlike its optional initial yes-or-no use mentioned earlier. The surrounding sentence tells you whether what is genuinely unknown or whether the whole proposition is being checked. This is a useful reminder that grammar is more than a list of one-word translations.

તમે કોણ છો asks who you are respectfully. It can help identify a caller or clarify someone’s role, though a direct identity demand may sound abrupt in a casual introduction. Social appropriateness depends on more than selecting a respectful pronoun. Often you can introduce yourself first and then ask for the other person’s name.

Both ક્યાં and અહીં contain nasal vowels in normal forms. Keep kyā̃ distinct from the nonnasal ક્યારે, when, introduced later. A listener who catches only the first syllable could mistake the type of information requested. Before answering, decide whether the question expects a person, an object identity or a place. That choice determines which vocabulary can fill the gap.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| તે ક્યાં છે? | Te kyā̃ chhe? | Where is that person? |
| આ શું છે? | Ā śũ chhe? | What is this? |
| તમે કોણ છો? | Tame koṇ chho? | Who are you, respectfully? |
| હું અહીં છું. | Hũ ahī̃ chhũ. | I am here. |
| તે ત્યાં છે. | Te tyā̃ chhe. | That person is there. |
| શાળા ક્યાં છે? | Śāḷā kyā̃ chhe? | Where is the school? |

### Common trap

Do not answer a where question with an identity just because both end in છે. The question word marks the missing category. Also, ક્યાં and ક્યારે are different words, not interchangeable spellings.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask where that person is.
2. Ask what this is.
3. Ask an adult who they are.
4. Say you are here.

***Answers:***

1. `તે ક્યાં છે?` - `Te kyā̃ chhe?`
2. `આ શું છે?` - `Ā śũ chhe?`
3. `તમે કોણ છો?` - `Tame koṇ chho?`
4. `હું અહીં છું.` - `Hũ ahī̃ chhũ.`

### Why these answers work

ક્યાં requests a location, શું an identity and કોણ a person. The respectful question retains છો with તમે. The final answer supplies અહીં rather than a question word and uses the first-person copula છું.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine a new school building. Ask what an object is, where the school is and who an adult is. State which kind of answer each question needs.


## Lesson 6: Routine actions without a gender shortcut

**Outcome:** Describe ordinary reading, drinking and working.

### Think before reading

Does a woman have to change વાંચું in I read solely because she is female?

### Build the pattern

The common simple present pattern combines a person-marked verb with a present copula: હું વાંચું છું, I read. In this construction the first-person form does not change with the speaker’s gender. That is a crucial difference from some neighbouring language patterns and from Gujarati’s later gender-marked participles.

તમે વાંચો છો gives respectful or plural you read; તે વાંચે છે gives third-person reads. The verb and copula work together. Do not replace only the pronoun and leave both remaining forms untouched. Start with these three useful pairs before building a full paradigm.

Objects normally precede the action: હું પુસ્તક વાંચું છું, I read a book. પુસ્તક is neuter, but its gender does not control વાંચું in this habitual construction. For work, Gujarati commonly uses કામ કરવું, literally do work. A natural expression can contain a noun plus a verb even where English uses one word.

રોજ means every day and helps make the routine interpretation explicit. The same simple present forms can have broader uses in context, so English tense labels are guides rather than exact boundaries. The present lesson uses daily habits and ordinary activities. An explicit ongoing construction later lets you focus on an action currently underway without pretending that each language divides time exactly as English does.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| હું પુસ્તક વાંચું છું. | Hũ pustak vā̃chũ chhũ. | I read a book. |
| તમે પુસ્તક વાંચો છો. | Tame pustak vā̃cho chho. | You read a book, respectfully. |
| તે કામ કરે છે. | Te kām kare chhe. | That person works. |
| હું ચા પીઉં છું. | Hũ chā pīũ chhũ. | I drink tea. |
| તે રોજ વાંચે છે. | Te roj vā̃che chhe. | That person reads every day. |
| હું કામ કરું છું. | Hũ kām karũ chhũ. | I work. |

### Common trap

Do not import a gender ending into હું વાંચું છું. This simple present marks person, and the same form works for male and female speakers. Later constructions will introduce gender for different grammatical reasons.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you read a book.
2. Tell an adult they read a book.
3. Say that person works.
4. Say you drink tea.

***Answers:***

1. `હું પુસ્તક વાંચું છું.` - `Hũ pustak vā̃chũ chhũ.`
2. `તમે પુસ્તક વાંચો છો.` - `Tame pustak vā̃cho chho.`
3. `તે કામ કરે છે.` - `Te kām kare chhe.`
4. `હું ચા પીઉં છું.` - `Hũ chā pīũ chhũ.`

### Why these answers work

The first-person verbs end in the nasal vowel represented by ũ and pair with છું. Respectful વાંચો pairs with છો; third-person કરે pairs with છે. The objects precede those verb groups and do not determine their person endings.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a daily activity as yourself, then address an adult about the same activity. Change both the verb form and copula while keeping the object unchanged.


## Lesson 7: Saying what does not happen

**Outcome:** Negate a routine and distinguish it from a current refusal.

### Think before reading

You know નથી as not be. Can it replace the positive copula while the action still carries its person form?

### Build the pattern

Gujarati offers more than one way to build habitual statements. The simple positive forms just learned mark person, while a very common negative construction uses a gender-marked participle followed by નથી. This lesson makes that change explicit, so that you can produce a natural negative without assuming that one unchanged verb shape works in every construction.

Here the new pattern is the gendered habitual negative: હું ચા પીતો નથી for a male speaker and હું ચા પીતી નથી for a female speaker. The participle પીતો or પીતી identifies the subject’s gender, unlike the simple positive પીઉં છું learned earlier. Both are legitimate Gujarati structures, but they are not formed by a blind one-word swap.

With reading, masculine વાંચતો and feminine વાંચતી work similarly. A third-person woman can be described as તે પુસ્તક વાંચતી નથી. The negative copula remains નથી. The examples make the gender requirement explicit instead of leaving a learner to guess from તે, which itself is ungendered.

A general negative habit is different from a refusal of an offered cup at this moment. This construction says someone does not drink tea or does not perform an activity. Later, નથી જોઈતી will express not wanting an item. Choose the predicate according to the intended message, not merely because English uses the word not.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| હું ચા પીતો નથી. | Hũ chā pīto nathī. | I do not drink tea, male speaker. |
| હું ચા પીતી નથી. | Hũ chā pītī nathī. | I do not drink tea, female speaker. |
| તે પુસ્તક વાંચતી નથી. | Te pustak vā̃chtī nathī. | She does not read a book. |
| તે કામ કરતો નથી. | Te kām karto nathī. | He does not work. |
| હું પુસ્તક વાંચતો નથી. | Hũ pustak vā̃chto nathī. | I do not read a book, male speaker. |
| હું કામ કરતી નથી. | Hũ kām kartī nathī. | I do not work, female speaker. |

### Common trap

The gendered negative pattern introduced here differs from the simple positive first-person વાંચું છું. Do not conclude that every Gujarati present form marks gender, or that none does. Identify the construction first.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you do not drink tea, male speaker.
2. Say you do not drink tea, female speaker.
3. Say she does not read a book.
4. Say he does not work.

***Answers:***

1. `હું ચા પીતો નથી.` - `Hũ chā pīto nathī.`
2. `હું ચા પીતી નથી.` - `Hũ chā pītī nathī.`
3. `તે પુસ્તક વાંચતી નથી.` - `Te pustak vā̃chtī nathī.`
4. `તે કામ કરતો નથી.` - `Te kām karto nathī.`

### Why these answers work

પીતો and પીતી distinguish the two speakers. વાંચતી identifies a feminine subject and કરતો a masculine subject, even though both use તે. All four end with નથી. The objects keep their role but do not control these habitual participles.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe one habit positively and negatively for an imagined woman. Explain why the positive simple finite form and the negative participial form display different visible agreement.


## Lesson 8: Inside, on and at home

**Outcome:** Locate everyday objects with postpositions.

### Think before reading

English says in the house. Would Gujarati put the relationship before the house or after it?

### Build the pattern

Gujarati uses postpositions and attached case-like elements after the noun. ઘરમાં means in the house, while પુસ્તક પર means on the book. The noun comes first and the relationship follows it. In writing, માં is commonly attached to the preceding noun, as the table shows.

ઘરે means at home in a familiar location expression. It is not always identical in emphasis to ઘરમાં, which points more explicitly inside the house. Learn each form through the situation it describes. A person may be home, while a particular book is physically inside the house.

શાળામાં means in the school, and દુકાનમાં means in the shop. The final copula continues to mark the subject, not the location word. Thus હું ઘરમાં છું uses first-person છું, while તે દુકાનમાં છે uses third-person છે. Location information does not remove the need for the correct predicate.

A question replaces the known location with ક્યાં. Do not attach માં to ક્યાં automatically; the ordinary where question already asks for a location. As sentences grow, keep noun phrases intact. Treat ઘરમાં as a unit when you move from a statement about a book to one about a person. This is easier than mentally placing an English in before the noun and then trying to repair the order.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| હું ઘરે છું. | Hũ ghare chhũ. | I am at home. |
| પુસ્તક ઘરમાં છે. | Pustak gharmā̃ chhe. | The book is in the house. |

### Common trap

Do not put માં before its noun as an English preposition. Also, at home and inside the house can highlight different relationships, so do not assume ઘરે and ઘરમાં are universally interchangeable.

## Lesson 9: Possessives follow the possessed noun

**Outcome:** Identify your book, notebook and friend.

### Think before reading

A male and a female speaker each own a notebook. Must their word for my be different?

### Build the pattern

મારો, મારી and મારું mean my with masculine, feminine and neuter singular nouns. The ending follows the possessed noun, not the owner’s gender. પુસ્તક is neuter and takes મારું; ચોપડી, a book or notebook in the practical examples here, is feminine and takes મારી. A masculine મિત્ર takes મારો.

Respectful your has તમારો, તમારી and તમારું. Familiar your uses તારો, તારી and તારું. There are two independent choices: the relationship to the listener determines which possessive family to choose, while the noun determines its ending. A respectful owner with a neuter book still gives તમારું પુસ્તક.

આ મારું પુસ્તક છે identifies this as my book. The demonstrative આ remains unchanged, the possessive agrees with પુસ્તક, and the copula describes a third-person thing. Do not use first-person છું merely because the word my refers to the speaker. The sentence subject is the book, not the owner.

Possession grammar also expresses relationships such as my friend; it does not imply literal ownership of a person. The same pattern can introduce family or colleagues later. For now, track who is related to whom and which noun controls the possessive ending. A stable set of three nouns makes the distinction visible without requiring a large vocabulary.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| આ મારું પુસ્તક છે. | Ā mārũ pustak chhe. | This is my book. |
| આ મારી ચોપડી છે. | Ā mārī chopḍī chhe. | This is my notebook. |
| આ મારો મિત્ર છે. | Ā māro mitra chhe. | This is my male friend. |
| તમારું ઘર મોટું છે. | Tamārũ ghar moṭũ chhe. | Your house is big, respectfully. |
| તારી ચોપડી અહીં છે. | Tārī chopḍī ahī̃ chhe. | Your notebook is here, familiarly. |
| મારું નામ મીના છે. | Mārũ nām Mīnā chhe. | My name is Mina. |

### Common trap

A male speaker still says મારી ચોપડી. Do not make the possessive agree with the owner. Also, my book is a third-person subject, so its copula is છે rather than છું.

### Practice

## Lesson 10: Availability with પાસે

**Outcome:** Say what someone has with them.

### Think before reading

Can locating a book with a person communicate have without using an English-style possession verb?

### Build the pattern

મારી પાસે પુસ્તક છે means I have a book. The construction places a book with me, using પાસે after a related pronoun form. It can describe possession, access or something available with the person. It need not establish legal ownership of the item.

તમારી પાસે is with you respectfully, તેની પાસે is with that person, and અમારી પાસે is with us excluding the listener. Notice the fixed linking form મારી before પાસે. It does not become મારું merely because the available object is neuter પુસ્તક. This phrase is a different structure from the possessive noun phrase મારું પુસ્તક.

A question uses the same order: તમારી પાસે પાણી છે? asks whether the addressee has water. The negative મારી પાસે સમય નથી says I do not have time. સમય is abstract, so the construction extends beyond physical objects in a bag.

Context resolves the intended meaning of with. A seller may have an item in stock, a friend may be carrying it, or a host may have it available at home. If ownership matters, use a possessive identification as well. Keeping these messages separate prevents a learner from treating my book and I have a book as interchangeable. The table supplies singular and mass-noun examples so that possession does not simultaneously become a lesson in plural morphology.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| મારી પાસે પુસ્તક છે. | Mārī pāse pustak chhe. | I have a book. |
| તમારી પાસે પાણી છે? | Tamārī pāse pāṇī chhe? | Do you have water, respectfully? |
| તેની પાસે ચોપડી છે. | Tenī pāse chopḍī chhe. | That person has a notebook. |
| મારી પાસે સમય નથી. | Mārī pāse samay nathī. | I do not have time. |
| અમારી પાસે પુસ્તક છે. | Amārī pāse pustak chhe. | We have a book, excluding the listener. |
| મારું પુસ્તક અહીં છે. | Mārũ pustak ahī̃ chhe. | My book is here. |

### Common trap

Do not replace મારી પાસે with મારું પાસે to match a neuter object. The relation phrase has its own shape. A possessive noun phrase and a statement of availability are grammatically different constructions.

2. Ask an adult whether they have water.
3. Say that person has a notebook.

## Lesson 11: Wanting an item with જોઈએ

**Outcome:** Express a simple want and decline an offered drink.

### Think before reading

If I want water is organised as water is wanted by me, would ordinary હું necessarily be the right first word?

### Build the pattern

મને પાણી જોઈએ expresses I want or need water. મને is the experiencer form, roughly to me. જોઈએ is a useful invariant predicate in this construction, allowing you to state needs without first changing an adjective for every noun gender.

તમને is the respectful or plural counterpart, and તેને refers to that person. Thus તમને શું જોઈએ? asks what you want or need. A seller may ask it to discover the item you seek. The question does not necessarily imply a strong desire; it can be an ordinary practical request for your requirement.

To decline tea, મને ચા નથી જોઈતી uses a gendered negative pattern with feminine ચા. The corresponding neuter water form is મને પાણી નથી જોઈતું. These negatives should be learned as complete constructions rather than generated by attaching નથી to any positive sentence. Gender returns here because the participial form changes.

A statement of need is not automatically a command. મને મદદ જોઈએ says I need help; it does not specify which person must help or what action they should take. The next lesson provides respectful commands to request the concrete action. Distinguishing the need from the request makes the exchange clearer and gives the listener room to respond appropriately.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| મને પાણી જોઈએ. | Mane pāṇī joīe. | I want water. |
| મને મદદ જોઈએ. | Mane madad joīe. | I need help. |
| તમને શું જોઈએ? | Tamne śũ joīe? | What do you want, respectfully? |
| મને ચા નથી જોઈતી. | Mane chā nathī joītī. | I do not want tea. |
| તેને પુસ્તક જોઈએ. | Tene pustak joīe. | That person wants a book. |
| મને પાણી નથી જોઈતું. | Mane pāṇī nathī joītũ. | I do not want water. |

### Common trap

Do not translate English I with હું automatically in a want statement. Also, the negative જોઈતી or જોઈતું agrees with the unwanted item; it is not determined by the speaker’s gender.

### Practice

2. Say you need help.
3. Ask an adult what they want.

## Lesson 12: Requests, politeness and do not

**Outcome:** Ask someone to give, sit, speak or repeat.

### Think before reading

If તમે addresses one person respectfully, should a command automatically become familiar because the audience is singular?

### Build the pattern

Gujarati respectful or plural commands often use forms in -o, such as બેસો, sit, and બોલો, speak. આપો means give. These commands can stand without an explicit તમે because the addressed listener is clear. No present copula is added to make them complete.

મને પાણી આપો asks the listener to give water to me. Here મને identifies a recipient, a concrete use related to the experiencer form in the previous lesson. The water stays before આપો, preserving the usual object-before-verb order.

કૃપા કરીને is an explicit please expression, useful in formal or careful requests. Respectful verb choice, a considerate tone and a suitable context also contribute to politeness. ધીમે બોલો asks for slower speech; ફરીથી કહો asks someone to say it again. The verbs બોલવું and કહેવું overlap in English translations but are not identical in use.

A negative command places ન before the imperative: અહીં ન બેસો, do not sit here. This tries to prevent an action, unlike a statement that someone does not sit here habitually. Keep command negation separate from the નથી constructions learned earlier. You now have enough language to ask for an object and to repair a conversation when its pace exceeds your comprehension.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| કૃપા કરીને અહીં બેસો. | Kṛpā karīne ahī̃ beso. | Please sit here. |
| મને પાણી આપો. | Mane pāṇī āpo. | Give me water, respectfully. |
| ધીમે બોલો. | Dhīme bolo. | Speak slowly, respectfully. |
| અહીં ન બેસો. | Ahī̃ na beso. | Do not sit here, respectfully. |
| ફરીથી કહો. | Pharīthī kaho. | Say it again, respectfully. |
| અહીં આવો. | Ahī̃ āvo. | Come here, respectfully. |

### Common trap

Do not add છે after an imperative. A command is already a complete instruction. Also, ન in a prohibition is not used by simply replacing every ordinary નથી with ન.

## Lesson 13: The action underway with રહ્યો

**Outcome:** Describe what someone is doing now.

### Think before reading

The simple present હું વાંચું છું does not show gender. Where could gender become visible in the explicitly ongoing construction?

### Build the pattern

An explicit ongoing action uses a main verb followed by રહ્યો, રહી or રહ્યું and a present copula. હું વાંચી રહ્યો છું means I am reading for a male speaker; હું વાંચી રહી છું gives the female-speaker form. The ongoing component reflects gender, while the final copula reflects person.

The main verb often appears in a linking form in -ī, as વાંચી or કરી. That form does not change between the male and female reading examples. First identify the activity, then add the ongoing component, then the appropriate copula. This makes the structure manageable without confusing it with an unchanged English present participle.

અત્યારે means now. It can make the current frame explicit but does not replace the ongoing grammar. તે અત્યારે કામ કરી રહી છે tells us a woman is working now. The pronoun તે itself remains ungendered; the predicate supplies the feminine information.

Gujarati simple present forms can also describe current situations in context. The explicit રહ્યો construction is useful when you want to emphasise an activity in progress, not a claim that every English -ing sentence must be translated this way. Contrast a regular habit with an activity observed at this moment. The same book and reader can appear in both, while the speaker chooses a different perspective on the action.

The reading pair changes રહ્યો to રહી while keeping વાંચી and છું. The third uses feminine રહી with third-person છે. The fourth uses masculine રહ્યો, showing that the ongoing subject determines agreement even though પાણી is neuter.

## Lesson 14: Small numbers and counted nouns

**Outcome:** Request a number of books or notebooks and ask how many.

### Think before reading

Does a number always require the same visible plural suffix after every Gujarati noun?

### Build the pattern

The first useful numbers are એક, one; બે, two; ત્રણ, three; ચાર, four; પાંચ, five; and છ, six. They precede the counted noun. બે પુસ્તકો means two books, and ત્રણ ચોપડીઓ means three notebooks. The plural shapes are useful to recognise, though Gujarati can omit overt plural marking in some numbered noun phrases.

This course uses explicit plurals in the early practice to make the counted set clear. It does not label a naturally unmarked noun after a numeral automatically wrong. Number expression varies by noun class and context; learn actual examples instead of importing the English requirement to add s to every counted noun.

કેટલાં asks how many with neuter plural nouns such as પુસ્તકો, while કેટલી applies with feminine ચોપડીઓ. The question word participates in agreement. It is not just one fixed form corresponding to English many.

The copula છે serves third-person singular and plural in these statements. Two books exist is બે પુસ્તકો છે, without changing છે to the first-person plural છીએ. In a request, આપો remains tied to the addressee rather than the number of objects. Counted objects and the person being addressed are different sources of grammatical information. Tracking them separately prevents several common overcorrections.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| બે પુસ્તકો આપો. | Be pustako āpo. | Give two books, respectfully. |
| ત્રણ ચોપડીઓ આપો. | Traṇ chopḍīo āpo. | Give three notebooks, respectfully. |
| કેટલાં પુસ્તકો છે? | Keṭlā̃ pustako chhe? | How many books are there? |
| કેટલી ચોપડીઓ છે? | Keṭlī chopḍīo chhe? | How many notebooks are there? |
| બે પુસ્તકો છે. | Be pustako chhe. | There are two books. |
| પાંચ રૂપિયા. | Pā̃ch rūpiyā. | Five rupees. |

### Common trap

Third-person plural છે does not become છીએ; that latter form belongs to we. Also, the question word changes with the counted noun, so કેટલાં cannot replace every English how many phrase unchanged.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Request two books respectfully.
2. Request three notebooks respectfully.
3. Ask how many books there are.
4. Ask how many notebooks there are.

***Answers:***

1. `બે પુસ્તકો આપો.` - `Be pustako āpo.`
2. `ત્રણ ચોપડીઓ આપો.` - `Traṇ chopḍīo āpo.`
3. `કેટલાં પુસ્તકો છે?` - `Keṭlā̃ pustako chhe?`
4. `કેટલી ચોપડીઓ છે?` - `Keṭlī chopḍīo chhe?`

### Why these answers work

The requests place the number before an explicit plural noun and retain આપો. The questions choose neuter plural કેટલાં versus feminine કેટલી. Both use છે because third-person plurality does not require the first-person plural copula.

### Retrieve and recombine

Prepare supplies for a small class. Ask for two books and three notebooks, then check each quantity. Retrieve both question-word forms before looking at the examples.


## Lesson 15: Days, clock time and appointments

**Outcome:** Arrange a simple visit and distinguish when from where.

### Think before reading

You know ક્યાં for where. What different question must you ask to learn the time of a visit?

### Build the pattern

ક્યારે asks when, while ક્યાં asks where. The difference is small in sound but large in meaning. તમે ક્યારે આવો છો? asks when you come; તમે ક્યાં જાઓ છો? asks where you go. Listen for the complete question word rather than guessing from its beginning.

આજે means today and કાલે commonly means tomorrow in prospective contexts. Gujarati કાલ can also refer to an adjacent day according to context, so yesterday is made explicit here with ગઈ કાલે. Do not assume every language handles these day words like Marathi’s separate काल and उद्या.

An appointment hour uses વાગ્યે: ત્રણ વાગ્યે, at three o’clock. To state the current time, Gujarati instead uses a construction such as ત્રણ વાગ્યા છે. The appointment phrase attaches to an action; the time announcement is itself a predicate.

A respectful invitation can use આવો without a future indicative: કાલે ત્રણ વાગ્યે આવો, come tomorrow at three. The command expresses the requested action and the time phrase schedules it. સવારે means in the morning, સાંજે in the evening, and રોજ every day. These can locate routines without turning the sentence into a future plan. First establish whether you are scheduling a single visit, reporting a habit or announcing the present time.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| કાલે ત્રણ વાગ્યે આવો. | Kāle traṇ vāgye āvo. | Come tomorrow at three, respectfully. |
| તમે ક્યારે આવો છો? | Tame kyāre āvo chho? | When do you come, respectfully? |
| હું રોજ સવારે વાંચું છું. | Hũ roj savāre vā̃chũ chhũ. | I read every morning. |
| તે સાંજે કામ કરે છે. | Te sā̃je kām kare chhe. | That person works in the evening. |
| ગઈ કાલે. | Gaī kāle. | Yesterday. |
| અત્યારે ત્રણ વાગ્યા છે. | Atyāre traṇ vāgyā chhe. | It is three o’clock now. |

### Common trap

Do not confuse ક્યાં with ક્યારે, or વાગ્યે with the complete time announcement વાગ્યા છે. Day words can also depend on context, so use ગઈ કાલે when yesterday must be explicit.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask someone to come tomorrow at three.
2. Ask an adult when they come.
3. Say you read every morning.
4. Say that person works in the evening.

***Answers:***

1. `કાલે ત્રણ વાગ્યે આવો.` - `Kāle traṇ vāgye āvo.`
2. `તમે ક્યારે આવો છો?` - `Tame kyāre āvo chho?`
3. `હું રોજ સવારે વાંચું છું.` - `Hũ roj savāre vā̃chũ chhũ.`
4. `તે સાંજે કામ કરે છે.` - `Te sā̃je kām kare chhe.`

### Why these answers work

The first schedules an imperative with કાલે and વાગ્યે. The second asks for time with ક્યારે. The final two describe routines using સવારે and સાંજે with simple present predicates. They do not need a prospective construction merely because they contain a time phrase.

### Retrieve and recombine

Move the visit from three to four and then from tomorrow to today. Ask when rather than where. Finally, state yesterday explicitly and contrast it with the future appointment.


## Lesson 16: Travel, origin and destination

**Outcome:** Describe going home and coming from a city.

### Think before reading

Can from and to be recovered from the city name alone, or must the relationship also be expressed?

### Build the pattern

જવું means go and આવવું means come. In the simple present, હું જાઉં છું and હું આવું છું distinguish the two actions. Respectful તમે gives જાઓ છો and આવો છો. The movement viewpoint matters: coming normally approaches the speaker, listener or another established centre.

ઘરે can function as a destination in હું ઘરે જાઉં છું, I go home. શાળાએ is useful for to school. Gujarati does not require one separate word corresponding to English to before every destination. Learn these place forms through their travel sentences rather than replacing all destination expressions with one guessed suffix.

થી expresses from among other relationships. હું સુરતથી આવું છું means I come from Surat. This can describe the immediate starting point of a journey, while an origin introduction might use a different construction depending on the intended meaning. A city of departure and a person’s permanent hometown are not automatically the same fact.

બસમાં means in or by bus in a transport statement such as હું બસમાં જાઉં છું. The same માં element earlier located a book inside a house; context now makes it a means of travel. This does not mean every English by should become માં. Keep the concrete transport expression and broaden your repertoire with actual examples as needed.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| હું ઘરે જાઉં છું. | Hũ ghare jāũ chhũ. | I go home. |
| તે શાળાએ જાય છે. | Te śāḷāe jāy chhe. | That person goes to school. |
| હું સુરતથી આવું છું. | Hũ suratthī āvũ chhũ. | I come from Surat. |
| તમે ક્યાં જાઓ છો? | Tame kyā̃ jāo chho? | Where do you go, respectfully? |
| હું બસમાં જાઉં છું. | Hũ basmā̃ jāũ chhũ. | I go by bus. |
| તે અહીં આવે છે. | Te ahī̃ āve chhe. | That person comes here. |

### Common trap

Do not turn an origin statement into a destination statement by leaving થી unchanged. Also, આવવું and જવું reflect movement viewpoint; they are not interchangeable synonyms for travel.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you go home.
2. Say that person goes to school.
3. Say you come from Surat.
4. Ask an adult where they go.

***Answers:***

1. `હું ઘરે જાઉં છું.` - `Hũ ghare jāũ chhũ.`
2. `તે શાળાએ જાય છે.` - `Te śāḷāe jāy chhe.`
3. `હું સુરતથી આવું છું.` - `Hũ suratthī āvũ chhũ.`
4. `તમે ક્યાં જાઓ છો?` - `Tame kyā̃ jāo chho?`

### Why these answers work

ઘરે and શાળાએ supply destinations. સુરતથી supplies a starting point, while આવું identifies movement toward a centre. The respectful question uses જાઓ છો and asks for the missing destination with ક્યાં. The first-person nasal endings remain visible in the complete predicates.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a journey from Surat and then a daily trip to school by bus. Decide which statement concerns origin and which concerns destination before choosing its place expression.


## Lesson 17: Past location and completed motion

**Outcome:** Report where someone was and where they went.

### Think before reading

A woman was at home and then went to school. Must was and went share one past stem?

### Build the pattern

Past states use forms of હોવું such as હતો for masculine singular, હતી for feminine singular and હતું for neuter singular. તે ઘરે હતી says she was at home. The pronoun તે remains ungendered, but the past copula identifies the female reference in this example.

Completed motion uses the movement verb’s past form. જવું has ગયો for masculine singular and ગઈ for feminine singular. આવવું gives આવ્યો and આવી. These frequent forms should be learned directly. They are not created by adding a generic past suffix to the complete present જાય છે.

ગઈ કાલે supplies yesterday explicitly. The word ગઈ inside this time expression should not be mistaken for the main verb she went. Its role is part of the day phrase. Sentence structure tells you whether a similar-looking form belongs to a time expression or reports the actual event.

The current lesson uses third-person people and intransitive movement. Completed actions with objects follow an important additional pattern in the next lesson. Avoid generalising subject agreement from she went to every possible past sentence. You can already report availability, arrival and departure with this small set, provided you distinguish a continuing past state from a completed change of place.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| તે ઘરે હતો. | Te ghare hato. | He was at home. |
| તે ઘરે હતી. | Te ghare hatī. | She was at home. |
| તે શાળાએ ગયો. | Te śāḷāe gayo. | He went to school. |
| તે શાળાએ ગઈ. | Te śāḷāe gaī. | She went to school. |
| તે અહીં આવ્યો. | Te ahī̃ āvyo. | He came here. |
| તે અહીં આવી. | Te ahī̃ āvī. | She came here. |

### Common trap

Do not infer the gender from તે alone, and do not form went from an unchanged present જાય છે. The past motion stem differs from the present, while a past location statement uses a copula instead.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say he was at home.
2. Say she was at home.
3. Say he went to school.
4. Say she went to school.

***Answers:***

1. `તે ઘરે હતો.` - `Te ghare hato.`
2. `તે ઘરે હતી.` - `Te ghare hatī.`
3. `તે શાળાએ ગયો.` - `Te śāḷāe gayo.`
4. `તે શાળાએ ગઈ.` - `Te śāḷāe gaī.`

### Why these answers work

હતો and હતી locate people in a past state. ગયો and ગઈ report completed motion and reflect the person’s gender in these intransitive examples. The destination શાળાએ remains unchanged between the two moving people.

### Retrieve and recombine

Report that a woman was at home and came here. Then change the person to a man. Identify the two predicate forms that change while keeping the place words fixed.


## Lesson 18: Completed actions and the agent form

**Outcome:** Describe a completed reading event with the object controlling agreement.

### Think before reading

If a woman and a man each read a book, could the completed verb stay neuter in both sentences?

### Build the pattern

Gujarati completed transitive events can mark the agent and make the verb agree with the object. મેં પુસ્તક વાંચ્યું means I read a book. The agent form is મેં rather than ordinary હું, and વાંચ્યું is neuter singular because પુસ્તક is neuter.

તેણે પુસ્તક વાંચ્યું works for he or she read a book. The agent pronoun does not itself distinguish gender, and the completed verb here follows the book rather than the reader. Compare તેણે ચા પીધી, that person drank tea: feminine ચા gives પીધી. Neuter પાણી gives પીધું.

This is a construction-specific rule, not a claim that Gujarati verbs always follow objects. Earlier simple present verbs showed person agreement, and intransitive motion forms followed their subjects. The event’s grammatical structure decides which controller matters. Identify whether there is a completed action on an object before choosing the pattern.

The examples keep objects simple and unmarked. Object marking and more elaborate agreement patterns require further study, so this lesson does not offer a complete account of Gujarati ergativity. Its practical goal is narrower: report a few completed everyday events accurately and understand why changing the actor does not necessarily change the final verb. A small controlled contrast is more useful than a sweeping rule that fails in the next sentence.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| મેં પુસ્તક વાંચ્યું. | Mẽ pustak vā̃chyũ. | I read a book. |
| તેણે પુસ્તક વાંચ્યું. | Teṇe pustak vā̃chyũ. | That person read a book. |
| તેણે ચા પીધી. | Teṇe chā pīdhī. | That person drank tea. |
| તેણે પાણી પીધું. | Teṇe pāṇī pīdhũ. | That person drank water. |
| મેં ચા પીધી. | Mẽ chā pīdhī. | I drank tea. |
| મેં પાણી પીધું. | Mẽ pāṇī pīdhũ. | I drank water. |

### Common trap

Do not use feminine પીધી because the drinker is a woman when the object is water. In these completed transitive examples the object controls agreement. Also, મેં is not the ordinary subject form for I read habitually.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you read a book as a completed event.
2. Say that person read a book as a completed event.
3. Say that person drank tea.
4. Say that person drank water.

***Answers:***

1. `મેં પુસ્તક વાંચ્યું.` - `Mẽ pustak vā̃chyũ.`
2. `તેણે પુસ્તક વાંચ્યું.` - `Teṇe pustak vā̃chyũ.`
3. `તેણે ચા પીધી.` - `Teṇe chā pīdhī.`
4. `તેણે પાણી પીધું.` - `Teṇe pāṇī pīdhũ.`

### Why these answers work

The two book answers share neuter વાંચ્યું. Tea selects feminine પીધી; water selects neuter પીધું. મેં and તેણે identify agents in this construction. Their form changes who acted without changing the gender of the thing acted on.

### Retrieve and recombine

Swap I and that person in the tea and water events. Keep the objects fixed and predict the unchanged endings. Then return one sentence to a habitual present and restore its ordinary subject form.


## Lesson 19: A compact future

**Outcome:** Make a simple future statement and recognise person endings.

### Think before reading

If I will come uses આવીશ, would third-person that person require the same ending?

### Build the pattern

Gujarati has useful simple future forms that can stand without a present copula. હું કાલે આવીશ means I will come tomorrow; તે કાલે આવશે means that person will come tomorrow. The future ending carries person information. Do not add છું after આવીશ merely because present statements often need it.

For go, the corresponding first-person form is જઈશ and the third-person form જશે. First-person plural જઈશું lets you state we will go, and respectful or plural તમે uses જશો. The verb forms do not change with the speaker’s gender in this simple future pattern.

The future can express an intention, prediction or assurance depending on context. A schedule is made clearer with કાલે or an hour phrase. A promise to come is not the same social act as a detached prediction that someone will come, even if both use the same tense family. Choose the subject and context carefully.

Negative future meaning can be expressed with નહીં before the future verb: હું કાલે નહીં આવું is another common form, but the practice keeps affirmative simple futures rather than mixing alternative negative constructions prematurely. You already have an inability pattern coming next to explain obstacles. For now, build a reliable affirmative future, recognise its person ending and avoid attaching a redundant present copula.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| હું કાલે આવીશ. | Hũ kāle āvīś. | I will come tomorrow. |
| તે કાલે આવશે. | Te kāle āvśe. | That person will come tomorrow. |
| હું ઘરે જઈશ. | Hũ ghare jaīś. | I will go home. |
| આપણે કાલે જઈશું. | Āpṇe kāle jaīśũ. | We will go tomorrow, including the listener. |
| તમે ક્યારે જશો? | Tame kyāre jaśo? | When will you go, respectfully? |
| અમે ઘરે જઈશું. | Ame ghare jaīśũ. | We will go home, excluding the listener. |

### Common trap

Do not add છું or છે after these complete simple future forms. Also, the same first-person future works for male and female speakers; person rather than gender is the contrast practised here.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you will come tomorrow.
2. Say that person will come tomorrow.
3. Say you will go home.
4. Say we will go tomorrow, including your listener.

***Answers:***

1. `હું કાલે આવીશ.` - `Hũ kāle āvīś.`
2. `તે કાલે આવશે.` - `Te kāle āvśe.`
3. `હું ઘરે જઈશ.` - `Hũ ghare jaīś.`
4. `આપણે કાલે જઈશું.` - `Āpṇe kāle jaīśũ.`

### Why these answers work

આવીશ is first-person singular, આવશે third-person, and જઈશું first-person plural. The inclusive pronoun આપણે establishes who joins the trip. The future forms themselves provide the finite predicate, so no present copula follows them.

### Retrieve and recombine

Make a plan to go home, then include the listener in tomorrow’s trip. Ask respectfully when another person will go. Track the person endings independently of the destination.


## Lesson 20: Can, cannot and a useful skill

**Outcome:** Describe an ability without confusing it with a future plan.

### Think before reading

Someone will read tomorrow, but can they read Gujarati? Which part of the sentence must express ability rather than time?

### Build the pattern

શકવું provides a productive can construction. The main action stem precedes a person-marked form of શકવું: હું વાંચી શકું છું means I can read, and તે વાંચી શકે છે means that person can read. The action form and the ability verb make one predicate group.

The first-person form શકું does not change with the speaker’s gender in this finite construction. Respectful તમે gives શકો છો. This differs from the gender-marked ongoing forms in Lesson 13. Again, the construction determines where agreement appears.

હું ગુજરાતી બોલી શકું છું states an ability to speak Gujarati. It does not claim that the speaker is speaking it now or will speak it at a particular appointment. Time perspective, intention and ability are separate dimensions of meaning. You can combine them in advanced sentences later, but first learn to distinguish them.

For an immediately useful inability statement, હું આવી શકતો નથી is masculine and હું આવી શકતી નથી feminine in the negative participial pattern. The practice labels the speaker explicitly. Do not hide that difference under the ungendered English I. A person may be unable to come because of circumstances even if coming is physically possible in general, so context helps distinguish temporary obstacles from permanent skills.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| હું ગુજરાતી બોલી શકું છું. | Hũ gujarātī bolī śakũ chhũ. | I can speak Gujarati. |
| તે વાંચી શકે છે. | Te vā̃chī śake chhe. | That person can read. |
| તમે આવી શકો છો? | Tame āvī śako chho? | Can you come, respectfully? |
| હું આવી શકતો નથી. | Hũ āvī śakto nathī. | I cannot come, male speaker. |
| હું આવી શકતી નથી. | Hũ āvī śaktī nathī. | I cannot come, female speaker. |
| હું કામ કરી શકું છું. | Hũ kām karī śakũ chhũ. | I can work. |

### Common trap

A future form does not automatically mean can. Also, the affirmative finite શકું and the negative participial શકતો or શકતી belong to different visible patterns; do not erase the labelled gender in the negative.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you can speak Gujarati.
2. Say that person can read.
3. Ask an adult whether they can come.
4. Say you cannot come, male speaker.

***Answers:***

1. `હું ગુજરાતી બોલી શકું છું.` - `Hũ gujarātī bolī śakũ chhũ.`
2. `તે વાંચી શકે છે.` - `Te vā̃chī śake chhe.`
3. `તમે આવી શકો છો?` - `Tame āvī śako chho?`
4. `હું આવી શકતો નથી.` - `Hũ āvī śakto nathī.`

### Why these answers work

બોલી, વાંચી and આવી identify the main actions. The positive ability component changes by person, while the final negative uses masculine શકતો with નથી. Respectful શકો છો is appropriate even when addressing only one adult.

### Retrieve and recombine

State one language ability and one future plan separately. Then explain that a female speaker cannot come now, retrieving the appropriate negative participle without copying the masculine answer.


## Lesson 21: Preferences organised around the liked thing

**Outcome:** Say what you like and ask about another person’s preference.

### Think before reading

If tea is pleasing to me, which noun might determine the verb rather than the person who likes it?

### Build the pattern

મને ચા ગમે છે means I like tea. The experiencer form મને is familiar from wanting, but the predicate ગમે છે expresses preference rather than need. Something can be liked without being wanted at this moment.

This lesson keeps the liked items singular or mass nouns, allowing you to practise the experiencer construction before adding number contrasts. મને આ પુસ્તક ગમે છે expresses liking for a particular book. તમે શું પસંદ કરો છો uses another preference verb, but the course focuses on the reusable ગમવું construction first.

તમને શું ગમે છે? asks what you like respectfully. તેને ચા ગમે છે means that person likes tea. The third-person experiencer does not itself identify gender. The ordinary subject pronoun તે cannot simply replace તેને in this construction, even though English places he or she as the subject of like.

A negative preference is મને ચા ગમતી નથી, with feminine ગમતી agreeing with ચા in the negative participial construction. The neuter book version uses ગમતું. This is another reason to learn the complete positive and negative patterns instead of assuming that one word for not can be inserted anywhere. Preference, consumption and a momentary refusal remain distinct messages.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| મને ચા ગમે છે. | Mane chā game chhe. | I like tea. |
| મને આ પુસ્તક ગમે છે. | Mane ā pustak game chhe. | I like this book. |
| તમને શું ગમે છે? | Tamne śũ game chhe? | What do you like, respectfully? |
| મને ચા ગમતી નથી. | Mane chā gamtī nathī. | I do not like tea. |
| તેને ચા ગમે છે. | Tene chā game chhe. | That person likes tea. |
| મને આ પુસ્તક ગમતું નથી. | Mane ā pustak gamtũ nathī. | I do not like this book. |

### Common trap

Do not confuse liking with wanting or drinking. In the negative examples, ગમતી follows feminine tea and ગમતું follows the neuter book, not the gender of the person with the preference.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you like tea.
2. Say you like this book.
3. Ask an adult what they like.
4. Say you do not like tea.

***Answers:***

1. `મને ચા ગમે છે.` - `Mane chā game chhe.`
2. `મને આ પુસ્તક ગમે છે.` - `Mane ā pustak game chhe.`
3. `તમને શું ગમે છે?` - `Tamne śũ game chhe?`
4. `મને ચા ગમતી નથી.` - `Mane chā gamtī nathī.`

### Why these answers work

The first three use an experiencer plus ગમે છે. The negative selects feminine ગમતી with ચા and ends in નથી. The respectful experiencer is તમને, which cannot be replaced by ordinary તમે without rebuilding the construction.

### Retrieve and recombine

Say that you like tea but do not want it now as two separate statements. Then ask someone about their preference. Keep the desired item and the person experiencing it distinct.


## Lesson 22: And, but and because

**Outcome:** Connect two facts and explain a simple reason.

### Think before reading

If two clauses have different subjects, does adding and make their verbs agree with a combined group?

### Build the pattern

અને means and and joins words, phrases or clauses. તે વાંચે છે અને હું કામ કરું છું connects two complete facts. Each clause retains its own person marking; the conjunction does not turn both subjects into one plural subject automatically.

પણ means but and presents a contrast. મને ચા ગમે છે પણ કોફી ગમતી નથી contrasts a positive and negative preference. The experiencer મને can be understood across both halves when the structure is clear. A repeated word can be omitted naturally, but do not omit the grammatical material needed to identify the two claims.

કારણ કે introduces a reason clause, because. હું ઘરે છું કારણ કે હું કામ કરું છું gives a reason for being at home. The reason clause preserves ordinary Gujarati order. The connector changes the relationship between facts rather than reversing their internal word order.

અથવા means or, while કે commonly introduces alternatives in a short choice such as ચા કે કોફી? The same કે also has other functions, including introducing reported content, so context matters. Begin with the complete patterns in the table. A good connected sentence lets the listener identify both the facts and whether they are being added, contrasted, offered as alternatives or linked by a reason.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| તે વાંચે છે અને હું કામ કરું છું. | Te vā̃che chhe ane hũ kām karũ chhũ. | That person reads and I work. |
| મને ચા ગમે છે પણ કોફી ગમતી નથી. | Mane chā game chhe paṇ kŏphī gamtī nathī. | I like tea but do not like coffee. |
| હું ઘરે છું કારણ કે હું કામ કરું છું. | Hũ ghare chhũ kāraṇ ke hũ kām karũ chhũ. | I am at home because I work. |
| ચા કે કોફી? | Chā ke kŏphī? | Tea or coffee, as a choice? |
| પુસ્તક અને ચોપડી. | Pustak ane chopḍī. | A book and a notebook. |
| આજે અથવા કાલે. | Āje athvā kāle. | Today or tomorrow. |

### Common trap

Do not let the conjunction erase the person distinction between the two predicates. Also, because states a reason, while but signals a contrast; two true facts do not make those connectors interchangeable.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say that person reads and you work.
2. Say you like tea but do not like coffee.
3. Say you are at home because you work.
4. Offer the choice tea or coffee.

***Answers:***

1. `તે વાંચે છે અને હું કામ કરું છું.` - `Te vā̃che chhe ane hũ kām karũ chhũ.`
2. `મને ચા ગમે છે પણ કોફી ગમતી નથી.` - `Mane chā game chhe paṇ kŏphī gamtī nathī.`
3. `હું ઘરે છું કારણ કે હું કામ કરું છું.` - `Hũ ghare chhũ kāraṇ ke hũ kām karũ chhũ.`
4. `ચા કે કોફી?` - `Chā ke kŏphī?`

### Why these answers work

The first preserves third-person વાંચે છે and first-person કરું છું. The second shares an experiencer across contrasting preferences. The third introduces a reason with કારણ કે. The final short choice uses કે between alternatives rather than a complete proposition with a yes-or-no answer.

### Retrieve and recombine

Join a place and an activity with a reason connector, then join two drinks as a choice. Explain how the relationship changes even though both sentences combine familiar vocabulary.


## Lesson 23: A practical purchase

**Outcome:** Ask the cost, check stock and request a quantity.

### Think before reading

Does asking how many books there are tell you the price of a particular book?

### Build the pattern

Shopping questions resolve different uncertainties. આની કિંમત કેટલી છે? asks what this costs, literally how much its price is. કિંમત is feminine, so કેટલી agrees with it. This differs from કેટલાં પુસ્તકો છે, which asks for a count of books.

તમારી પાસે ચોપડીઓ છે? checks whether a seller has notebooks. It uses the availability construction, not the want construction with તમને. The seller’s stock and the seller’s personal wishes are different topics. Once availability is established, મને બે ચોપડીઓ જોઈએ states the quantity you need.

બે ચોપડીઓ આપો then requests the action of handing over two notebooks. The command remains respectful and does not agree with the notebooks. તમે may be omitted because the addressee is obvious at the counter.

A complete transaction also involves listening to the answer. If you miss a price, ફરીથી કહો asks for repetition; if the speech is too fast, ધીમે બોલો asks for a slower delivery. Do not pretend that a memorised price question guarantees comprehension of every possible number in the reply. The course’s small number set is a starting point. Ask for clarification and check the quantity before confirming a purchase. These language choices transfer from a bookshop to a general store while keeping the underlying decisions clear.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| આની કિંમત કેટલી છે? | Ānī kimmat keṭlī chhe? | What is the price of this? |
| તમારી પાસે ચોપડીઓ છે? | Tamārī pāse chopḍīo chhe? | Do you have notebooks, respectfully? |
| મને બે ચોપડીઓ જોઈએ. | Mane be chopḍīo joīe. | I want two notebooks. |
| બે ચોપડીઓ આપો. | Be chopḍīo āpo. | Give two notebooks, respectfully. |
| ફરીથી કહો. | Pharīthī kaho. | Say it again, respectfully. |
| આભાર. | Ābhār. | Thank you. |

### Common trap

A count question and a price question require different noun phrases. Also, તમારી પાસે checks availability; તમને શું જોઈએ asks about the listener’s wants and would reverse the buyer-seller roles if used carelessly.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask the price of this item, using કિંમત.
2. Ask a seller whether they have notebooks.
3. Say you want two notebooks.
4. Request two notebooks respectfully.

***Answers:***

1. `આની કિંમત કેટલી છે?` - `Ānī kimmat keṭlī chhe?`
2. `તમારી પાસે ચોપડીઓ છે?` - `Tamārī pāse chopḍīo chhe?`
3. `મને બે ચોપડીઓ જોઈએ.` - `Mane be chopḍīo joīe.`
4. `બે ચોપડીઓ આપો.` - `Be chopḍīo āpo.`

### Why these answers work

કેટલી follows feminine કિંમત in the price question. The stock question uses પાસે. The want statement identifies the buyer with મને, while the request uses આપો to address the seller. These are separate turns with different grammatical jobs.

### Retrieve and recombine

Move the purchase from notebooks to books. Rebuild the stock question and quantity request, then ask for repetition of an unfamiliar price. Keep the respectful register throughout.


## Lesson 24: A delayed visit and conversation repair

**Outcome:** Explain an obstacle, set a new time and ask for repetition.

### Think before reading

If a person planned to come tomorrow but cannot come now, are the future plan and present inability contradictory?

### Build the pattern

A visit can involve several time frames at once. હું કાલે આવીશ promises or predicts coming tomorrow. હું અત્યારે આવી શકતો નથી explains a male speaker’s present inability. Both can be true, because now and tomorrow are different times and ability differs from intention.

A female speaker uses શકતી in that negative participial construction. The pronoun હું remains the same. In a practical message, include the relevant time word if it prevents confusion. Otherwise a listener may interpret cannot come as cancelling the entire plan.

કાલે ચાર વાગ્યે આવો proposes a specific appointment through a respectful command. The hour phrase uses વાગ્યે because it schedules coming. You are not announcing the current clock time. If the listener repeats the appointment, check both the day and the hour rather than assuming one correct detail guarantees the whole message.

કૃપા કરીને ફરીથી કહો politely requests repetition. ધીમે બોલો requests slower speech. These are different repairs: you may have heard the sound but missed the content, or understood the words individually but not their rapid combination. A useful learner can identify the missing information and ask for it. The goal is a shared, clear plan, not the appearance of understanding. Use the small grammatical patterns you know to keep the exchange moving honestly.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| હું અત્યારે આવી શકતો નથી. | Hũ atyāre āvī śakto nathī. | I cannot come now, male speaker. |
| હું કાલે આવીશ. | Hũ kāle āvīś. | I will come tomorrow. |
| કાલે ચાર વાગ્યે આવો. | Kāle chār vāgye āvo. | Come tomorrow at four, respectfully. |
| કૃપા કરીને ફરીથી કહો. | Kṛpā karīne pharīthī kaho. | Please say it again. |
| હું અત્યારે આવી શકતી નથી. | Hũ atyāre āvī śaktī nathī. | I cannot come now, female speaker. |
| ધીમે બોલો. | Dhīme bolo. | Speak slowly, respectfully. |

### Common trap

Do not cancel tomorrow accidentally by leaving a present inability’s time frame unclear. Also, a female speaker needs શકતી in the negative form shown here, even though the affirmative future આવીશ does not change with gender.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you cannot come now, male speaker.
2. Say you will come tomorrow.
3. Ask someone to come tomorrow at four.
4. Politely ask someone to say it again.

***Answers:***

1. `હું અત્યારે આવી શકતો નથી.` - `Hũ atyāre āvī śakto nathī.`
2. `હું કાલે આવીશ.` - `Hũ kāle āvīś.`
3. `કાલે ચાર વાગ્યે આવો.` - `Kāle chār vāgye āvo.`
4. `કૃપા કરીને ફરીથી કહો.` - `Kṛpā karīne pharīthī kaho.`

### Why these answers work

The first uses an explicitly current negative ability construction. The second uses a complete future without a present copula. The third schedules an imperative, and the fourth repairs communication with a respectful request. The predicates carry different jobs despite sharing the visit context.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create a three-turn appointment exchange with an obstacle, a new time and a repetition request. Change the speaker to a woman and revise only the form that actually depends on gender.


## Lesson 25: Recombining a complete day

**Outcome:** Use several construction types in one coherent account.

### Think before reading

Does English I always correspond to હું in sentences about reading, possession and wanting?

### Build the pattern

A short account becomes reliable when each fact receives its own construction. Use ordinary હું for identity, location and the simple present. Use મારી પાસે for availability, મને for a want or preference, and મેં for the completed transitive events introduced here. These forms are not stylistic alternatives; they express different grammatical relationships.

The review situations connect a location, a completed event, a current need and a future group plan. A book read earlier takes neuter વાંચ્યું after an agent form. Water wanted now uses જોઈએ with an experiencer. An inclusive trip uses આપણે with a first-person plural future. Similar English word order therefore hides several Gujarati choices.

Before building a sentence, identify the actor or experiencer, the relevant noun and the time perspective. Check whether the predicate is a state, a habit, an ongoing action, a completed event, a need or a future intention. Then retrieve the pattern that matches that meaning. This is more dependable than attaching a familiar ending to every verb.

Continue with listening and short messages reviewed by a fluent speaker of the variety you intend to use. Gujarati vowel quality, nasalisation, breathy voice and everyday reductions need audio beyond this romanisation. The written course is a foundation, not a proficiency certificate. A useful outcome is being able to recombine known material, explain a grammatical choice and recognise the point where you need clarification or more vocabulary.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| હું ઘરે છું. | Hũ ghare chhũ. | I am at home. |
| મેં પુસ્તક વાંચ્યું. | Mẽ pustak vā̃chyũ. | I read a book. |
| મને પાણી જોઈએ. | Mane pāṇī joīe. | I want water. |
| આપણે કાલે જઈશું. | Āpṇe kāle jaīśũ. | We will go tomorrow, including the listener. |
| મારી પાસે પુસ્તક છે. | Mārī pāse pustak chhe. | I have a book. |
| તમને શું ગમે છે? | Tamne śũ game chhe? | What do you like, respectfully? |

### Common trap

Do not force every English I into હું or every past verb into subject agreement. The construction determines both the person form and the agreement controller. Review the whole predicate before correcting an unfamiliar-looking sentence.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you are at home.
2. Say you read a book as a completed event.
3. Say you want water.
4. Say we will go tomorrow, including your listener.

***Answers:***

1. `હું ઘરે છું.` - `Hũ ghare chhũ.`
2. `મેં પુસ્તક વાંચ્યું.` - `Mẽ pustak vā̃chyũ.`
3. `મને પાણી જોઈએ.` - `Mane pāṇī joīe.`
4. `આપણે કાલે જઈશું.` - `Āpṇe kāle jaīśũ.`

### Why these answers work

The first uses an ordinary subject and present copula. The second uses agent-marked મેં and object-controlled neuter વાંચ્યું. The third uses experiencer મને. The final inclusive future uses આપણે and જઈશું without a redundant present copula.

### Retrieve and recombine

Tell a six-sentence story about a visit using a location, a question, a completed event, a need, a plan and a repair request. Change one participant and explain which forms must change and why.
