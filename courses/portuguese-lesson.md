# Portuguese: A pattern-first foundation

This course teaches contemporary Brazilian Portuguese in standard spelling with broadly usable conversational constructions. Você and vocês take third-person verb forms; a gente means we but normally takes a singular verb, while nós takes first-person plural agreement. Tu is used in many Brazilian regions, with regionally variable agreement, but is not the production baseline here. The course uses estar + gerund for ongoing action, common in Brazil, and does not substitute European Portuguese estar a + infinitive. Regional pronunciation and pronoun placement vary. Accents, cedillas and nasal marks are preserved: café, você, não and manhã are meaningful spellings. No romanisation is needed because Portuguese already uses the Latin alphabet.

## Lesson 1: A first action with eu and você

### Build the pattern

You will say that you speak Portuguese and ask another person to use the same language. Eu means I, você means you singular, and falar means to speak. Remove the infinitive ending -ar to expose fal-, then use falo with eu and fala with você. This is a useful regular pattern, not a rule for every verb in the language. Você refers to the listener but takes the same grammatical verb form as ele, he, and ela, she. Neutral statements normally put the subject before the verb and an object or language name after it. Portuguese does not need an extra in before a language used directly with falar. Pronounce the final accented syllable of português clearly; the circumflex is part of the spelling.

### Think before reading on

If você refers to the person addressed, why does the verb not use an English-style special second-person label in its spelling?

Portuguese você historically developed from an address expression and uses third-person agreement in the modern standard. For immediate production, keep the pair você fala together. The meaning is you speak, even though its agreement matches ele fala.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Eu falo português. | I speak Portuguese. |
| Você fala português. | You speak Portuguese. (você) |
| Ela fala português. | She speaks Portuguese. |
| Ele fala inglês. | He speaks English. |
| Eu estudo português. | I study Portuguese. |
| Você estuda inglês. | You study English. (você) |

### Worked examples

`Eu falo português.` means “I speak Portuguese.” The first-person ending -o makes falar a finite verb, and the language name follows without an additional preposition.

`Você fala português.` means “You speak Portuguese. (você)” The listener is addressed with você, which selects fala rather than the first-person form falo used by the speaker.

`Ela fala português.` means “She speaks Portuguese.” The third-person pronoun shares the same finite form as você, so the explicit subject distinguishes who is speaking.

### Common trap

Do not add English do to make a basic statement, and do not use the infinitive falar unchanged after eu when you intend the finite sentence I speak.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Brazilian Portuguese, preserving the stated person and register.

1. I speak Portuguese.
2. You speak Portuguese. (você)
3. She speaks Portuguese.
4. He speaks English.
5. I study Portuguese.
6. You study English. (você)

***Answers:***

1. `Eu falo português.`
2. `Você fala português.`
3. `Ela fala português.`
4. `Ele fala inglês.`
5. `Eu estudo português.`
6. `Você estuda inglês.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The language name changes while the subject and third-person verb remain stable, making this a simple vocabulary substitution.

Answer 5: Estudar is another regular -ar verb, allowing the first-person pattern to transfer to a new action without changing its logic.

Answer 6: The regular third-person form appears with você, while the final language name identifies what the listener studies.

### Retrieve and recombine

Say which language you speak, then change the subject to a woman and a man. Transfer the same regular ending pattern from speaking to studying Portuguese.


## Lesson 2: Identity and origin with ser

### Build the pattern

You will introduce yourself, identify a profession and say where you are from. Ser expresses identity, classification and origin in the patterns here. Its common forms are eu sou, você é, ele é and ela é. These are irregular and should not be manufactured from the regular -ar pattern. A name can follow sou directly. A profession often appears without an indefinite article when simply identifying someone's occupation: sou professor. Professor and professora distinguish a male and female teacher in these examples. De introduces origin, and de + o contracts to do before Brasil. The ser versus estar distinction is broader than permanent versus temporary, so begin with these concrete uses rather than a slogan.

### Think before reading on

If a person changes jobs, does that mean their profession must use estar because the job is not permanent?

No. Classification as a teacher uses ser in this ordinary statement, whether the career lasts months or decades. The grammatical function is identification. Estar will express states and locations in a later lesson, not everything that might eventually change.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Eu sou Ana. | I am Ana. |
| Eu sou professor. | I am a male teacher. |
| Eu sou professora. | I am a female teacher. |
| Ela é estudante. | She is a student. |
| Você é do Brasil. | You are from Brazil. (você) |
| Eu sou do Brasil. | I am from Brazil. |

### Worked examples

`Eu sou Ana.` means “I am Ana.” The first-person form of ser identifies the speaker by name, with no gender change on the verb itself.

`Eu sou professor.` means “I am a male teacher.” The profession noun identifies the speaker's occupation without an obligatory indefinite article in this simple classification statement.

`Eu sou professora.` means “I am a female teacher.” The noun changes to the feminine form, while sou remains the same first-person verb for the speaker.

### Common trap

Do not say eu é by copying the você form. The irregular first-person form is sou. Also avoid adding a profession article automatically whenever English says a teacher.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Brazilian Portuguese, preserving the stated person and register.

1. I am Ana.
2. I am a male teacher.
3. I am a female teacher.
4. She is a student.
5. You are from Brazil. (você)
6. I am from Brazil.

***Answers:***

1. `Eu sou Ana.`
2. `Eu sou professor.`
3. `Eu sou professora.`
4. `Ela é estudante.`
5. `Você é do Brasil.`
6. `Eu sou do Brasil.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: Estudante has the same form for a male or female student here, while the pronoun identifies this subject.

Answer 5: The origin phrase uses the contraction do, combining de with the article associated with Brasil in this expression.

Answer 6: Changing the subject requires the irregular verb sou, while the entire origin phrase stays unchanged.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce yourself by name, identify your occupation or student status, and say you are from Brazil. Then describe another person using the appropriate noun form.


## Lesson 3: Articles, gender and sounds inside useful nouns

### Build the pattern

You will identify a book, a house and water while learning noun gender. Um and uma mean a or one before masculine and feminine singular nouns. O and a are the corresponding definite articles. Livro, book, is masculine; casa, house, is feminine. Noun gender is a grammatical property, not a claim that an object is male or female. Final -o and -a offer useful clues but do not predict every noun: água is feminine, while estudante can describe either gender. In many Brazilian pronunciations, unstressed final o sounds approximately like u and unstressed final e like i. The written vowels remain o and e. Nasal marks in words such as não are not optional decorations.

### Think before reading on

If livro belongs to a woman, should it become uma livra because the owner is female?

No. The noun's grammatical gender stays masculine, so um livro or o livro remains appropriate. Ownership and noun gender are different matters. The article agrees with the noun it introduces, not with the person who owns the object.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| É um livro. | It is a book. |
| É uma casa. | It is a house. |
| O livro é bom. | The book is good. |
| A casa é boa. | The house is good. |
| A água está aqui. | The water is here. |
| É uma estudante. | It is a female student. |

### Worked examples

`É um livro.` means “It is a book.” The indefinite article agrees with masculine livro, while the understood subject can be supplied by an object being pointed to.

`É uma casa.` means “It is a house.” The feminine noun takes uma, illustrating article agreement without requiring a separate feminine form of the verb é.

`O livro é bom.` means “The book is good.” The definite article identifies a known masculine noun, and bom is the corresponding masculine descriptive form.

### Common trap

Do not assume that every word ending in a is feminine or every word ending in o is masculine. Learn the article with the noun and treat the ending as a clue.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Brazilian Portuguese, preserving the stated person and register.

1. It is a book.
2. It is a house.
3. The book is good.
4. The house is good.
5. The water is here.
6. It is a female student.

***Answers:***

1. `É um livro.`
2. `É uma casa.`
3. `O livro é bom.`
4. `A casa é boa.`
5. `A água está aqui.`
6. `É uma estudante.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: Both article and adjective reflect the feminine noun, while the copula remains third-person singular.

Answer 5: Água is feminine even though the initial sound is a vowel; the location verb is introduced as a useful preview.

Answer 6: The article can reveal gender with a noun whose own form does not change between male and female reference.

### Retrieve and recombine

Point to a book and a house, first identifying each with an indefinite article and then referring back with a definite article. Explain why the article changes but gender stays.


## Lesson 4: Questions and a single preverbal não

### Build the pattern

You will ask yes-or-no and information questions and deny an action. Brazilian Portuguese often keeps ordinary statement order in a yes-or-no question, using intonation and written punctuation: você fala português becomes você fala português? Onde means where, o que means what, and quem means who. Não usually precedes the finite verb in a basic negative statement. Unlike English, Portuguese does not need do or does to support these questions and negatives. The same verb forms learned in affirmative statements remain available. Você appears explicitly in the early questions to keep the addressee clear; omission is possible when the context makes the subject recoverable.

### Think before reading on

What needs to be added before fala to turn você fala português into a question corresponding to English do you speak Portuguese?

No auxiliary is required. Question intonation and punctuation can turn the statement into a yes-or-no question. For a negative, place não before fala. The verb does not become an infinitive merely because the sentence is questioned or denied.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Você fala português? | Do you speak Portuguese? (você) |
| Eu não falo inglês. | I do not speak English. |
| Ela não estuda português. | She does not study Portuguese. |
| O que você estuda? | What do you study? (você) |
| Quem é você? | Who are you? (você) |
| Você é professora? | Are you a female teacher? (você) |

### Worked examples

`Você fala português?` means “Do you speak Portuguese? (você)” The statement order remains intact, while rising or otherwise appropriate question intonation signals the request for confirmation.

`Eu não falo inglês.` means “I do not speak English.” Não precedes the first-person finite verb, and no separate Portuguese equivalent of English do is inserted.

`Ela não estuda português.` means “She does not study Portuguese.” The negative leaves the third-person form estuda unchanged and targets the study activity rather than the subject's identity.

### Common trap

Do not translate English do word by word as fazer in these questions. Fazer means do or make as a lexical verb, not the English question-support auxiliary.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Brazilian Portuguese, preserving the stated person and register.

1. Do you speak Portuguese? (você)
2. I do not speak English.
3. She does not study Portuguese.
4. What do you study? (você)
5. Who are you? (você)
6. Are you a female teacher? (você)

***Answers:***

1. `Você fala português?`
2. `Eu não falo inglês.`
3. `Ela não estuda português.`
4. `O que você estuda?`
5. `Quem é você?`
6. `Você é professora?`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The question expression asks for the missing object of study, while você continues to select third-person agreement.

Answer 5: The question requests identity with ser; its directness may sound abrupt outside a context that calls for identification.

Answer 6: The identity question uses the same é as the statement, and the feminine profession noun supplies the gendered description.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask whether a visitor speaks Portuguese, ask what they study and deny that you speak English. Keep the original finite forms rather than returning to infinitives.


## Lesson 5: Current states and location with estar

### Build the pattern

You will say where you are and how you feel. Estar has the forms eu estou, você está, ela está and ele está in the examples here. It locates people and physical objects and expresses many current states. Aqui means here, aí means there near the listener, and lá means there farther away. Em casa is a common expression for at home without an article. Cansado and cansada describe a tired male and female person; the adjective changes while estou does not. Do not reduce ser and estar to permanent and temporary: a city can estar in a place, and a temporary event can use ser for its scheduled location in constructions beyond this lesson.

### Think before reading on

If a house stays in the same place for decades, does its physical location automatically require ser because it is permanent?

No. Ordinary physical location uses estar regardless of how long the object remains there. Duration is not the deciding rule. Ask whether the sentence identifies something, locates it or describes a state before selecting the verb.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Eu estou em casa. | I am at home. |
| O livro está aí. | The book is there near you. |
| Ela está aqui. | She is here. |
| Eu estou cansado. | I am tired. (male speaker) |
| Eu estou cansada. | I am tired. (female speaker) |
| Onde você está? | Where are you? (você) |

### Worked examples

`Eu estou em casa.` means “I am at home.” The first-person form of estar locates the speaker, and em casa is the ordinary article-free home expression.

`O livro está aí.` means “The book is there near you.” The place word locates the book near the listener, while estar expresses physical location rather than identity.

`Ela está aqui.` means “She is here.” The feminine subject uses the same third-person estar form as a masculine person or a singular object.

### Common trap

Do not use sou cansado as the normal equivalent of I am tired right now. Estou cansado or estou cansada describes the current state in the intended meaning.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Brazilian Portuguese, preserving the stated person and register.

1. I am at home.
2. The book is there near you.
3. She is here.
4. I am tired. (male speaker)
5. I am tired. (female speaker)
6. Where are you? (você)

***Answers:***

1. `Eu estou em casa.`
2. `O livro está aí.`
3. `Ela está aqui.`
4. `Eu estou cansado.`
5. `Eu estou cansada.`
6. `Onde você está?`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The adjective takes the masculine form to describe the speaker, while estou remains the first-person state verb.

Answer 5: Only the adjective changes for a female speaker; the finite verb does not acquire a separate feminine ending.

Answer 6: The information question asks for the listener's location and uses the appropriate third-person form with você.

### Retrieve and recombine

Locate yourself at home and a book near the listener, then describe yourself as tired using the appropriate adjective. Separate location choice from adjective agreement.


## Lesson 6: Plural nouns, vocês and two ways to say we

### Build the pattern

You will describe several people and distinguish nós from a gente. Vocês is plural you and takes third-person plural verbs: vocês falam. Eles and elas mean they for masculine or mixed groups and feminine groups respectively. Nós falamos and a gente fala can both mean we speak. A gente is very common in Brazilian conversation but normally takes a singular verb in the standard pattern taught here. It is written as two words; agente is a different word meaning agent. Ordinary plurals often add -s, and articles and adjectives agree: os livros bons, as casas boas. Not every noun plural is regular, but these examples make the agreement chain visible.

### Think before reading on

If a gente means more than one person, should its verb automatically become falamos?

The meaning is plural, but the grammatical expression normally takes third-person singular agreement: a gente fala. Nós instead takes falamos. Keeping each subject expression paired with its standard verb form avoids mixing two valid patterns.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Nós falamos português. | We speak Portuguese. (nós) |
| A gente fala português. | We speak Portuguese. (a gente) |
| Vocês falam inglês. | You speak English. (plural) |
| Elas estudam português. | They study Portuguese. (all female) |
| Os livros são bons. | The books are good. |
| As casas são boas. | The houses are good. |

### Worked examples

`Nós falamos português.` means “We speak Portuguese. (nós)” The first-person plural subject selects falamos, whose ending identifies the group that includes the speaker.

`A gente fala português.` means “We speak Portuguese. (a gente)” The conversational we expression takes singular fala in this standard pattern despite referring to more than one person.

`Vocês falam inglês.` means “You speak English. (plural)” The plural addressee expression requires falam, distinguishing it from singular você fala even when the action is the same.

### Common trap

Do not write agente when you mean we. Also avoid combining vocês with the singular fala in the standard production model, even though spoken agreement variation exists.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Brazilian Portuguese, preserving the stated person and register.

1. We speak Portuguese. (nós)
2. We speak Portuguese. (a gente)
3. You speak English. (plural)
4. They study Portuguese. (all female)
5. The books are good.
6. The houses are good.

***Answers:***

1. `Nós falamos português.`
2. `A gente fala português.`
3. `Vocês falam inglês.`
4. `Elas estudam português.`
5. `Os livros são bons.`
6. `As casas são boas.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The feminine plural pronoun identifies the group, while the verb uses the same third-person plural form as eles.

Answer 5: Article, noun and adjective are plural, and ser uses the irregular plural form são rather than singular é.

Answer 6: The feminine plural noun phrase selects matching articles and adjectives, while the plural copula remains the same as with books.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe your group speaking Portuguese once with nós and once with a gente. Then address several learners with vocês and identify two good books using plural agreement.


## Lesson 7: Wanting an object or wanting to act

### Build the pattern

You will order water and state an intention using querer. Eu quero means I want, while você quer and ela quer use the third-person singular form. These forms are irregular. A noun can follow directly: quero água. An infinitive can also follow: quero estudar, I want to study. The second verb stays in the infinitive because querer is the finite verb carrying person information. Para can introduce a purpose, but it is not needed between querer and its infinitive in this basic pattern. Por favor means please and softens a direct request; a later lesson introduces gostaria for a more indirect request. Brazilian café often refers to coffee, while café da manhã is breakfast.

### Think before reading on

If eu quero estudar becomes ela quer estudar, which verb changes and which stays in the infinitive?

Quero changes to quer because the person changes. Estudar remains the infinitive naming the desired action. This allows a small number of finite verbs to combine with many actions without conjugating every verb in the same sentence.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Eu quero água. | I want water. |
| Eu quero um café, por favor. | I want a coffee, please. |
| Você quer estudar? | Do you want to study? (você) |
| Ela quer estudar português. | She wants to study Portuguese. |
| Eu quero ler um livro. | I want to read a book. |
| A gente quer falar português. | We want to speak Portuguese. (a gente) |

### Worked examples

`Eu quero água.` means “I want water.” The desired substance follows the finite first-person form directly, without an obligatory article in this general request.

`Eu quero um café, por favor.` means “I want a coffee, please.” The indefinite article counts a serving of coffee, while por favor makes the direct request more courteous.

`Você quer estudar?` means “Do you want to study? (você)” The finite wanting verb agrees with você, and the following infinitive names the proposed activity.

### Common trap

Do not say quero estudo when you mean I want to study. Estudo is already finite, while the verb after querer needs the infinitive in this construction.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Brazilian Portuguese, preserving the stated person and register.

1. I want water.
2. I want a coffee, please.
3. Do you want to study? (você)
4. She wants to study Portuguese.
5. I want to read a book.
6. We want to speak Portuguese. (a gente)

***Answers:***

1. `Eu quero água.`
2. `Eu quero um café, por favor.`
3. `Você quer estudar?`
4. `Ela quer estudar português.`
5. `Eu quero ler um livro.`
6. `A gente quer falar português.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The subject changes the wanting verb to quer, while estudar remains unchanged before its language object.

Answer 5: Ler supplies a new infinitive after querer, extending the construction beyond the regular -ar verbs already encountered.

Answer 6: The singular agreement required by a gente appears on quer, while the second verb remains an infinitive.

### Retrieve and recombine

Order water politely, then say that you want to study Portuguese. Transfer the desire frame to reading a book, keeping only the first verb finite.


## Lesson 8: Present verbs and liking with de

### Build the pattern

You will use regular -er and -ir verbs and express a preference. Comer gives eu como and você come; abrir gives eu abro and você abre. Their first-person -o resembles the earlier -ar pattern, but the third-person vowel differs. Ler, read, has eu leio and você lê, so it needs a separately learned form. Gostar de means to like and normally keeps the preposition de before a noun or infinitive. Eu gosto de café means I like coffee; gosto de ler means I like reading. The preposition belongs to the Portuguese verb's construction even though English like does not require it. A general preference can omit an article, while a particular item may use one and trigger a contraction.

### Think before reading on

Why does I like coffee need de although I drink coffee does not?

Different verbs select different complements. Gostar is normally followed by de, while beber or tomar can take a drink directly. Meaning similarity does not guarantee identical grammar, so learn gostar de as a connected pattern.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Eu como arroz. | I eat rice. |
| Você abre a porta. | You open the door. (você) |
| Eu leio um livro. | I read a book. |
| Ela lê o livro. | She reads the book. |
| Eu gosto de café. | I like coffee. |
| Você gosta de ler? | Do you like reading? (você) |

### Worked examples

`Eu como arroz.` means “I eat rice.” The -er verb comer forms the first person with -o, while the food object follows directly without a preposition.

`Você abre a porta.` means “You open the door. (você)” The -ir verb abrir uses abre with você, and the definite article identifies the particular door in context.

`Eu leio um livro.` means “I read a book.” Ler has the irregular first-person form leio, which cannot be obtained by simply copying the regular third-person stem.

### Common trap

Do not transfer fala's final a to every present verb. Come and abre use e, while lê is an irregular accented form. Preserve de after gostar in standard production.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Brazilian Portuguese, preserving the stated person and register.

1. I eat rice.
2. You open the door. (você)
3. I read a book.
4. She reads the book.
5. I like coffee.
6. Do you like reading? (você)

***Answers:***

1. `Eu como arroz.`
2. `Você abre a porta.`
3. `Eu leio um livro.`
4. `Ela lê o livro.`
5. `Eu gosto de café.`
6. `Você gosta de ler?`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The third-person form lê retains a circumflex, distinguishing the written finite form from an unaccented guess.

Answer 5: The preposition de is part of the liking construction, while the article-free noun describes coffee as a general preference.

Answer 6: The same required preposition introduces an infinitive, showing how gostar de works with both things and activities.

### Retrieve and recombine

Say that you like reading, read a book and open a door. Notice which sentence requires a preposition and which verbs need their own present forms.


## Lesson 9: Having things, age and bodily states

### Build the pattern

You will say you have a book, give an age and describe thirst or hunger. Ter has eu tenho, você tem and vocês têm. The circumflex distinguishes the plural form in writing. Age uses ter: tenho vinte anos means I am twenty years old, literally I have twenty years. For an immediate bodily state, Brazilian Portuguese often uses estar com: estou com fome, I am hungry, and estou com sede, I am thirsty. The nouns fome and sede are not ordinary adjectives meaning hungry and thirsty. General expressions with ter also exist, but this lesson uses the immediate-state construction consistently. Um livro can be something owned or merely available, depending on context.

### Think before reading on

If English says I am twenty, should Portuguese use estou vinte or sou vinte?

Neither is the normal age expression. Use tenho vinte anos. Languages do not assign the same predicate to every age or bodily-state expression, so translate the construction as a whole rather than the English verb am alone.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Eu tenho um livro. | I have a book. |
| Você tem tempo? | Do you have time? (você) |
| Vocês têm livros. | You have books. (plural) |
| Eu tenho vinte anos. | I am twenty years old. |
| Eu estou com fome. | I am hungry. |
| Ela está com sede. | She is thirsty. |

### Worked examples

`Eu tenho um livro.` means “I have a book.” The irregular first-person ter form expresses possession or availability, while the indefinite article introduces one book.

`Você tem tempo?` means “Do you have time? (você)” The singular ter form asks whether time is available, and the abstract resource needs no indefinite article here.

`Vocês têm livros.` means “You have books. (plural)” The plural subject selects têm, whose written circumflex preserves the singular-plural contrast even where pronunciation is close.

### Common trap

Do not write vocês tem in the standard written pattern; plural têm needs the circumflex. Also avoid confusing being hungry with being a person described as permanently hungry.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Brazilian Portuguese, preserving the stated person and register.

1. I have a book.
2. Do you have time? (você)
3. You have books. (plural)
4. I am twenty years old.
5. I am hungry.
6. She is thirsty.

***Answers:***

1. `Eu tenho um livro.`
2. `Você tem tempo?`
3. `Vocês têm livros.`
4. `Eu tenho vinte anos.`
5. `Eu estou com fome.`
6. `Ela está com sede.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: Age uses ter with a number and anos, so the entire expression differs structurally from English be plus an age.

Answer 5: The immediate state is expressed through estar com and a noun, rather than a direct adjective after ser.

Answer 6: The same state construction transfers to thirst, with third-person está agreeing with the subject rather than the following noun.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give your age using a suitable number, say you have a book and ask for water because you are thirsty. Keep possession, age and current bodily state distinct.


## Lesson 10: Possessives that agree with the possessed thing

### Build the pattern

You will identify your book, your house and another person's belongings. Meu and minha mean my before masculine and feminine singular nouns; meus and minhas are plural. The ending follows the thing possessed, not the owner's gender. Seu and sua can mean your with você, but they can also refer to his, her or their in context. Dele and dela often make third-person ownership clearer in Brazilian Portuguese. Definite articles before possessives are common but vary; the course uses o meu livro and a minha casa consistently where an article appears. Possessives can also stand after a copula: o livro é meu. Demonstratives such as este and esse have distance uses, but colloquial Brazilian usage often favours esse more broadly than strict textbook distinctions suggest.

### Think before reading on

If a woman owns a book and a man owns a house, should my change according to each owner's gender?

No. The book is masculine, so meu livro is appropriate for either owner. The house is feminine, so minha casa is appropriate for either owner. The possessive agrees with the possessed noun, not with the person speaking.

`Os meus livros estão aqui.` means “My books are here.” Article, possessive, noun and location verb all reflect plural number, showing agreement across the whole clause.

Do not assume seu always means your when several possible owners are being discussed. Dele or dela can clarify a third-person owner without changing the object's gender.

## Lesson 11: Places and contractions with em, de and a

### Build the pattern

You will say where you are, where you come from and where you go. Em combines with articles as no, na, nos and nas. De combines as do, da, dos and das. A combines with o as ao and with a as à, whose grave accent marks the contraction. No mercado means in or at the market, while ao mercado can mark a destination. Em casa remains the ordinary at-home expression without an article. In Brazilian conversation para and its contraction pra are very common for destinations; this course writes para when choosing that construction and also teaches standard ir ao mercado. The choice of preposition depends on the relation and verb, not simply on the place noun.

### Think before reading on

Does no mercado mean the same thing as ao mercado in a sentence about movement or location?

No. No contains em and commonly locates someone at the market; ao contains a and can mark the destination of going. The article is shared, but the preposition changes the relationship to the place.

`Eu vou ao mercado.` means “I am going to the market.” Ao combines the destination preposition a with masculine o, and vou is the irregular first-person form of ir.

## Lesson 12: Plans with ir and an infinitive

### Build the pattern

You will describe a plan using a form of ir followed by an infinitive. Eu vou estudar means I am going to study; você vai estudar means you are going to study; nós vamos estudar means we are going to study. This is a common Brazilian future construction, not necessarily a statement about physically travelling somewhere. Amanhã means tomorrow, hoje means today and depois means later or afterward. Only ir changes for person in the basic construction; the activity verb stays infinitive. A gente pairs with vai, while nós pairs with vamos. A destination can still appear inside the planned action, but avoid confusing future ir with ordinary movement ir in every sentence.

### Think before reading on

If vou estudar contains go, does the speaker have to leave their current location to study?

No. Ir plus an infinitive is a grammatical future construction and can describe studying at home without any journey. A literal movement sentence instead gives a destination, such as vou ao mercado. The following material helps distinguish the uses.

`Eu vou estudar amanhã.` means “I am going to study tomorrow.” Vou carries first-person agreement, while the infinitive names the planned activity and amanhã locates it in the future.

## Lesson 13: Actions in progress with the gerund

### Build the pattern

You will make an ongoing action explicit using estar plus a gerund. Estou estudando means I am studying, está comendo means is eating and está abrindo means is opening. Regular gerunds use -ando for -ar verbs and -endo or -indo for -er and -ir verbs. Lendo, reading, is the gerund of ler. Brazilian Portuguese commonly uses this construction where European Portuguese often prefers estar a plus an infinitive. The gerund itself does not agree with a male or female subject or become plural; estar carries the subject agreement. Agora means now and can emphasise the activity's current relevance. Not every English present form should be translated as a progressive, since habitual actions still use the simple present naturally.

### Think before reading on

When she is reading becomes they are reading, should lendo acquire a plural ending?

No. Lendo stays unchanged. Está becomes estão because the finite auxiliary carries agreement. This division between a changing auxiliary and an unchanged nonfinite form resembles the future construction, although the meaning and second-verb form differ.

| A gente está trabalhando. | We are working. (a gente) |
| Eu não estou dormindo. | I am not sleeping. |

### Worked examples

`Eu estou estudando agora.` means “I am studying now.” The finite estar form and gerund make the ongoing activity explicit, with agora emphasising its current timing.

`Ela está lendo um livro.` means “She is reading a book.” The irregular-looking gerund lendo stays nonfinite, while está agrees with the singular subject and the object follows normally.

`Eles estão comendo arroz.` means “They are eating rice.” Plural agreement appears on estão, while comendo remains unchanged despite the group subject.

### Common trap

Do not use estou estudar as the Brazilian ongoing pattern taught here. The second verb needs the gerund estudando, not the infinitive used after future vou.

## Lesson 14: Polite requests and permission

### Build the pattern

You will request an object, ask permission and understand a few courteous commands. Eu gostaria de means I would like and is a useful indirect request frame. It keeps de before either a noun or an infinitive. Posso means may I or can I in a first-person question, while pode with você can ask someone to perform an action. Brazilian requests commonly use você pode plus an infinitive without an overt object pronoun when the recipient is obvious. Por favor remains useful. Standard você imperatives include fale, abra and espere, but everyday speech varies regionally; these are the consistent written forms in this lesson. Politeness is not guaranteed by grammar alone, so consider tone and context.

### Think before reading on

How does posso abrir a janela differ from você pode abrir a janela, although both involve poder and the same action?

The first asks permission or possibility for the speaker to open the window. The second asks whether the listener can do it and often functions as a request. The finite person form changes whose action is being proposed.

### Worked examples

`Eu gostaria de água, por favor.` means “I would like water, please.” The indirect request frame keeps de before the desired noun, and por favor reinforces the courteous tone.

`Eu gostaria de ler este livro.` means “I would like to read this book.” The same required preposition introduces an infinitive, allowing the request frame to express a desired action as well as an object.

`Posso abrir a janela?` means “May I open the window?” The first-person modal makes the proposed action belong to the speaker, and the infinitive stays unchanged after it.

### Common trap

Do not drop de after gostaria when using the frame taught here. Also distinguish the permission question posso from an instruction telling another person what to do.

## Lesson 15: Quantities, prices and a café order

### Build the pattern

You will ask a price and order small quantities. Quanto custa means how much does it cost with a singular item; quanto custam matches a plural item. Um and uma mean one with masculine and feminine nouns; dois and duas distinguish two. Três has the same form with either gender. Reais is the plural of real, the Brazilian currency unit. Um copo de água means a glass of water, with de linking the container to its contents. Sem means without and com means with. Café sem açúcar therefore specifies a drink without sugar. Quantities can make a substance countable through servings or containers rather than changing its basic noun meaning.

### Think before reading on

Why does two houses need duas while two books uses dois, even though both describe the same number?

The number two agrees with the grammatical gender of the following noun. Casas is feminine and livros is masculine. The people buying or owning them do not control this agreement; the counted noun does.

## Lesson 16: Routines, time and reflexive actions

### Build the pattern

You will describe a daily routine and say when an action happens. Todos os dias means every day, de manhã means in the morning and à noite means at night. Clock times commonly use às before plural hours: às oito, at eight. Me levanto means I get up, using a reflexive pronoun with levantar. Brazilian conversation commonly places this unstressed pronoun before the verb, so eu me levanto is the production model here. Você se levanta uses se with third-person agreement. A gente se levanta is similarly singular. A routine uses the simple present, while estar plus gerund describes an action currently underway. Do not convert every daily habit into a progressive just because English can mention a present schedule.

### Think before reading on

If eu me levanto becomes você se levanta, is changing only the subject enough?

No. The reflexive pronoun changes from me to se, and the verb changes from levanto to levanta. The pronoun refers back to the subject, so both pieces must match the chosen person construction.

## Lesson 17: Completed past actions with regular verbs

### Build the pattern

You will report a finished activity using the pretérito perfeito, commonly called the preterite. For a regular -ar verb, eu falei contrasts with você falou. Estudei and estudou follow the same pattern. Regular -er and -ir verbs use forms such as comi, comeu, abri and abriu. Ontem means yesterday and helps locate a completed event, but the time word does not replace the past verb form. The first-person plural of many regular verbs has the same written form in present and preterite, as in nós estudamos; context resolves the timing. A gente studied uses a gente estudou, retaining the singular agreement familiar from the present.

### Think before reading on

Can you express I studied yesterday by adding ontem to eu estudo and leaving the present form unchanged in these neutral reports?

Use estudei for the completed past. The time expression tells when, while the verb selects the past viewpoint. Portuguese needs both pieces to fit the ordinary narrative meaning taught here, rather than relying on an English-style time word alone.

## Lesson 18: Useful irregular past forms

### Build the pattern

You will report going, having, doing and seeing in the preterite. Eu fui means I went or I was, because ir and ser share their preterite forms; context identifies the intended verb. Você foi, ela foi and a gente foi use the singular third-person form. Eu tive means I had, fiz means I did or made, and vi means I saw. Their third-person forms are teve, fez and viu. These common verbs cannot be generated by adding regular endings to the present forms. A destination after fui strongly suggests movement, while a profession or identity description can indicate ser. The course starts with unambiguous journey examples.

### Think before reading on

If fui can mean both went and was, how can a reader understand eu fui ao mercado without guessing randomly?

The destination phrase ao mercado supports the movement meaning of ir. Grammar and the surrounding words work together. Shared verb forms are common in languages; context usually narrows the interpretation far more than an isolated dictionary list does.

## Lesson 19: Past habits and background with the imperfect

### Build the pattern

You will describe what used to happen and what was true in a past setting. The imperfect includes eu estudava, você estudava and a gente estudava for regular -ar verbs. Comer gives comia, and estar gives estava. Ser is irregular with era. These forms often establish a habit, ongoing background or situation viewed without focusing on its endpoint. The preterite can instead present a completed event as a whole. This is not a simple long-versus-short rule: a long activity can be bounded, and a short repeated habit can use the imperfect. Quando eu era criança means when I was a child and provides a useful setting for past routines.

### Think before reading on

Does an action lasting several hours automatically need the imperfect, while a brief action automatically needs the preterite?

No. The distinction concerns viewpoint and context, not a stopwatch. Imperfect estudava can describe a routine or background, while preterite estudei can present even a long study session as a completed event.

| Eu estava em casa. | I was at home. |
| Eu era estudante. | I was a student. |
| Quando eu era criança, eu estudava aqui. | When I was a child, I used to study here. |

### Worked examples

`Eu estudava de manhã.` means “I used to study in the morning.” The imperfect presents a past routine rather than selecting one completed morning as the focus of the statement.

`Ela trabalhava à noite.` means “She used to work at night.” The third-person imperfect shares the same regular -ava shape as the first person, so the pronoun identifies the worker.

`A gente comia arroz todos os dias.` means “We used to eat rice every day. (a gente)” The repeated-time expression supports a habitual past reading, while a gente retains singular agreement on comia.

### Common trap

Do not translate every English was with estava. Identity and background classification can use era, while states and physical locations often use estava in the constructions learned here.

## Lesson 20: Combining a background with a completed event

### Build the pattern

You will tell a short story with an ongoing background and an event that occurs within it. Eu estava lendo quando ela chegou means I was reading when she arrived. Estava plus a gerund presents the activity as underway; chegou presents the arrival as a completed event. Quando means when and links the two clauses. The order can be reversed without reversing the real-world sequence. Depois means afterward and can connect a later event in a simple narrative. Portuguese does not require an English past-progressive construction every time the imperfect appears, but the explicit progressive is useful when you want to emphasise an activity in progress at a reference time.

### Think before reading on

In I was reading when she arrived, which clause sets the background and which introduces the event that advances the story?

The reading clause presents an activity already underway. The arrival clause gives a bounded event within that setting. Choosing the two verb constructions reflects this relationship; the words reading and arriving do not permanently belong to one tense category.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Eu estava lendo quando ela chegou. | I was reading when she arrived. |
| Ela chegou quando eu estava estudando. | She arrived when I was studying. |
| A gente estava em casa quando ele chegou. | We were at home when he arrived. (a gente) |
| Eu comi e depois fui para casa. | I ate and then went home. |
| Eu não estava dormindo. | I was not sleeping. |
| Você estava trabalhando quando ela chegou? | Were you working when she arrived? (você) |

### Worked examples

`Eu estava lendo quando ela chegou.` means “I was reading when she arrived.” The past progressive supplies an ongoing background, while the preterite arrival introduces the completed event within that setting.

`Ela chegou quando eu estava estudando.` means “She arrived when I was studying.” Reversing the clause order leaves the event relationship intact: studying is still background and arrival is still the bounded event.

`A gente estava em casa quando ele chegou.` means “We were at home when he arrived. (a gente)” The background is a location rather than a progressive action, and a gente keeps singular estava.

### Common trap

Do not claim that every quando clause must use the preterite. The intended time relationship determines the verb form; these examples teach one common narrative configuration, not a universal conjunction rule.

### Practice

3. We were at home when he arrived. (a gente)
4. I ate and then went home.

***Answers:***

1. `Eu estava lendo quando ela chegou.`
2. `Ela chegou quando eu estava estudando.`

5. `Eu não estava dormindo.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: Two preterite verbs narrate completed events in sequence, while depois explicitly identifies the later journey.

Answer 5: The negative denies the ongoing background activity, placing não before the finite auxiliary rather than before the gerund alone.

Answer 6: The question checks the listener's background activity at the time of the completed arrival, preserving the two distinct verb constructions.

### Retrieve and recombine

## Lesson 21: Ability, obligation and negative scope

### Build the pattern

You will distinguish can, must and do not have to. Poder has eu posso and você pode; it combines with an infinitive to express ability, possibility or permission according to context. Ter que plus an infinitive expresses obligation: tenho que estudar, I have to study. Precisar de introduces a needed noun, while precisar plus an infinitive commonly omits de: preciso de ajuda, but preciso estudar. Não preciso estudar means I do not need to study, which differs from não posso estudar, I cannot or may not study. The position of não and the chosen modal determine what is denied. This distinction matters in practical plans and instructions.

### Think before reading on

Does I do not have to go mean the same thing as I must not go?

No. The first removes an obligation; the second forbids or rules out the action. Portuguese likewise distinguishes não preciso ir from a prohibition or lack of permission expressed in context with não posso ir. Track the meaning of the entire modal phrase.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Eu posso ler português. | I can read Portuguese. |
| Você pode entrar. | You may come in. (permission, você) |
| Eu tenho que estudar amanhã. | I have to study tomorrow. |
| Eu preciso de ajuda. | I need help. |
| Eu preciso estudar. | I need to study. |
| Eu não preciso ir. | I do not need to go. |

### Worked examples

`Eu posso ler português.` means “I can read Portuguese.” The finite modal carries first-person agreement, while ler remains an infinitive naming the ability being claimed.

`Você pode entrar.` means “You may come in. (permission, você)” The modal gives permission in this situation, showing that pode is interpreted through context rather than always meaning physical ability.

`Eu tenho que estudar amanhã.` means “I have to study tomorrow.” The obligation construction uses ter que followed by an infinitive, with the time expression specifying when the action is required.

### Common trap

Do not add de between precisar and every infinitive by copying preciso de ajuda. The noun and infinitive constructions differ in the standard patterns taught here.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Brazilian Portuguese, preserving the stated person and register.

1. I can read Portuguese.
2. You may come in. (permission, você)
3. I have to study tomorrow.
4. I need help.
5. I need to study.
6. I do not need to go.

***Answers:***

1. `Eu posso ler português.`
2. `Você pode entrar.`
3. `Eu tenho que estudar amanhã.`
4. `Eu preciso de ajuda.`
5. `Eu preciso estudar.`
6. `Eu não preciso ir.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The noun complement follows de in the standard precisar de construction, identifying the resource the speaker needs.

Answer 5: The action complement appears as an infinitive without de in this common standard construction, contrasting with the noun example.

Answer 6: The negative removes necessity rather than prohibiting the journey or claiming that the speaker lacks the ability to make it.

### Retrieve and recombine

Say that you can read Portuguese, must study tomorrow and do not need help. Then contrast not needing to go with being unable to go, keeping their meanings separate.


## Lesson 22: Objects, recipients and Brazilian pronoun choices

### Build the pattern

You will identify an object and a recipient while keeping the course's pronoun choices clear. Me can mean me or to me in common Brazilian sentences such as ela me chamou and você pode me ajudar. With você, the pronoun te is widespread in many regions, but patterns vary; this course avoids requiring one regional mixture as the only correct answer. For a clear third-person object, standard writing uses o, a, os and as, while ordinary Brazilian speech often uses full noun phrases or stressed pronouns in ways that formal writing treats differently. The lesson therefore begins with me and explicit para você for a recipient. A noun after para can name a beneficiary or intended recipient without becoming the direct object.

### Think before reading on

In I give the book to you, are the book and you doing the same grammatical job?

No. The book is the transferred object, while the listener is the recipient. Eu dou o livro para você keeps these roles explicit and avoids forcing an unfamiliar clitic placement before the learner understands the relationship.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Você pode me ajudar? | Can you help me? (você) |
| Ela me chamou ontem. | She called me yesterday. |
| Eu dou o livro para você. | I give the book to you. (você) |
| Ela deu água para mim. | She gave water to me. |
| Este livro é para você. | This book is for you. (você) |
| Pode me dizer o preço? | Can you tell me the price? (a request) |

### Worked examples

`Você pode me ajudar?` means “Can you help me? (você)” Me identifies the person receiving help, while the finite modal agrees with the listener being asked to act.

`Ela me chamou ontem.` means “She called me yesterday.” The object pronoun precedes the finite verb in a common Brazilian pattern, and the preterite reports a completed call.

`Eu dou o livro para você.` means “I give the book to you. (você)” The direct object names the transferred item, and the para phrase makes the recipient explicit without a clitic ambiguity.

### Common trap

Do not treat every object-pronoun pattern heard in Brazil as identical across region and writing register. The clear me and para você constructions here are a bounded production choice.

### Practice

***Answers:***

Answer 4: After a preposition, the first-person form is mim in this recipient phrase, while deu is the irregular past of dar.

Answer 6: The understood listener is asked to provide information, while me identifies the recipient of the telling action.

## Lesson 23: Reasons, purpose and identifying clauses

### Build the pattern

You will connect a reason, a purpose and a description of a noun. Porque means because in an answer or explanation, while por que asks why in a direct question. Para plus an infinitive can express purpose: estudo para aprender, I study in order to learn. Que can introduce a relative clause: o livro que eu li, the book that I read. These connectors perform different jobs and should not be reduced to a single English joining word. The relative clause follows the noun it identifies, while a purpose phrase explains the goal of an action. Se means if and can introduce a simple condition; this lesson uses present-tense conditions without introducing the broader future-subjunctive system.

### Think before reading on

Does the book that I read explain why the speaker read, or identify which book is being discussed?

It identifies the book. A purpose phrase with para explains a goal, while a porque clause explains a reason. Recognising the relationship between clauses helps you choose the connector before worrying about the rest of the sentence.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Eu estudo porque quero aprender. | I study because I want to learn. |
| Por que você estuda português? | Why do you study Portuguese? (você) |
| Eu estudo para aprender. | I study in order to learn. |
| O livro que eu li é bom. | The book that I read is good. |
| Se você tem tempo, pode esperar. | If you have time, you can wait. (você) |
| Eu quero ir, mas não posso. | I want to go, but I cannot. |

### Worked examples

`Eu estudo porque quero aprender.` means “I study because I want to learn.” Porque introduces the reason, and quero is a finite verb inside its own clause followed by the infinitive aprender.

`Por que você estuda português?` means “Why do you study Portuguese? (você)” The separated question form por que asks for a reason, contrasting in spelling and function with explanatory porque.

`Eu estudo para aprender.` means “I study in order to learn.” The para phrase expresses the goal of studying, and the following action remains an infinitive rather than a new finite clause.

### Common trap

Do not write porque automatically whenever English why appears. The direct question form is por que, while porque introduces the explanation in the examples here.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Brazilian Portuguese, preserving the stated person and register.

1. I study because I want to learn.
2. Why do you study Portuguese? (você)
3. I study in order to learn.
4. The book that I read is good.
5. If you have time, you can wait. (você)
6. I want to go, but I cannot.

***Answers:***

1. `Eu estudo porque quero aprender.`
2. `Por que você estuda português?`

4. `O livro que eu li é bom.`
5. `Se você tem tempo, pode esperar.`
6. `Eu quero ir, mas não posso.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The relative clause follows and identifies livro, while the main clause evaluates that book with ser and an adjective.

Answer 5: The present-tense condition concerns current availability, and the second clause states an action permitted or possible under that condition.

Answer 6: Mas signals contrast between desire and ability or permission, while the omitted second infinitive is recoverable from the first clause.

## Lesson 24: A visit, directions and conversation repair

### Build the pattern

You will arrange a visit, ask for a place and repair an unclear reply. Amanhã and depois help propose a time, while agora can locate the current situation; the examples use one question at a time to keep the reply manageable. Onde fica asks where a place is located, often useful for a café or a school. Ficar has several meanings, including stay and be located, so this question should be learned as a complete practical pattern. De novo means again, and mais devagar means more slowly. Não entendi reports that a particular message was not understood, while não sei reports not knowing. A focused repair request tells the other person what to change.

### Think before reading on

If you know the topic but the reply was too fast, is não sei as informative as não entendi followed by a repetition request?

No. Não sei denies knowledge, while não entendi reports a comprehension problem with the message. Asking for repetition or slower speech gives the partner a concrete way to help without restarting the whole exchange.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Posso ir amanhã? | May I go tomorrow? |
| Você vai estar em casa amanhã? | Are you going to be at home tomorrow? (você) |
| Onde fica o café? | Where is the café? |
| Eu não entendi. | I did not understand. |
| Pode repetir, por favor? | Can you repeat that, please? |
| Pode falar mais devagar? | Can you speak more slowly? |

### Worked examples

`Posso ir amanhã?` means “May I go tomorrow?” The first-person permission question proposes a future visit or journey and asks whether that plan is acceptable in context.

`Você vai estar em casa amanhã?` means “Are you going to be at home tomorrow? (você)” The future construction combines ir with the infinitive estar, allowing a question about a later location rather than an action alone.

`Onde fica o café?` means “Where is the café?” The location question uses a common ficar construction to ask where a place can be found.

### Common trap

Do not treat every form of ficar as a synonym of estar in all contexts. Onde fica is a useful location question, but other constructions give ficar different meanings.

## Lesson 25: Cumulative review through a day and a plan

### Build the pattern

You will introduce yourself, report a past event, describe the present and make a future plan in a coherent short account. The review keeps Brazilian você and a gente conventions explicit. A gente means a group including the speaker but takes singular agreement in the standard model, while nós requires first-person plural forms. Ser identifies who someone is, estar locates them or gives a state, and ter expresses possession or age in the relevant constructions. The past requires choosing a viewpoint, not merely adding ontem. The future with ir keeps the second verb infinitive, while ongoing estar takes a gerund. These contrasts are more useful than translating the English verb be or do word by word.

### Think before reading on

What must change when I am studying at home now becomes we are going to study tomorrow using a gente?

The subject becomes a gente, the finite verb becomes vai, the ongoing gerund becomes the infinitive estudar, and the time expression becomes amanhã. The intended event changes from current activity to a future plan, so several linked choices must change together.

### Useful language in context

| Portuguese | Meaning |

| Eu estou estudando português agora. | I am studying Portuguese now. |
| A gente vai estudar amanhã. | We are going to study tomorrow. (a gente) |
| Nós vamos ler o livro amanhã. | We are going to read the book tomorrow. (nós) |

### Worked examples

`Eu sou estudante e moro no Brasil.` means “I am a student and live in Brazil.” Ser identifies the speaker, while morar reports residence and the location phrase uses the contraction no.

`Eu tenho um livro em casa.` means “I have a book at home.” Ter expresses availability or possession, and em casa locates the book without inserting an unnecessary article before casa.

`Eu fui ao mercado ontem.` means “I went to the market yesterday.” The destination makes fui a movement verb, and ontem anchors the preterite event in a finished past day.

### Common trap

Do not infer unrestricted fluency from completing these controlled examples. Regional listening, wider vocabulary, subjunctive constructions and spontaneous conversation remain beyond this foundation and need further practice and review.
