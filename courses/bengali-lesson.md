# Bengali: A pattern-first foundation

This course uses modern standard colloquial Bengali (চলিত বাংলা), with vocabulary leaning toward West Bengal usage. জল is the productive word for water here; পানি is also widely used, especially in Bangladesh. Bengali script is primary. The readable romanisation uses sh for the ordinary sh sound, ṭ and ḍ for retroflex stops, ṛ for the flap, æ for an open front vowel, and a tilde for nasalisation. It follows ordinary pronunciation rather than a strict letter-for-letter transliteration. Ch and chh distinguish the affricate and its aspirated counterpart; in progressive endings chh follows the familiar learner convention. Written final o is sometimes supplied in romanisation when pronounced. This is Bengali, not Assamese: respectful আপনি, first-person আমি, and Bengali verb paradigms are maintained throughout.

### References and review notes

[Hanne-Ruth Thompson, Bengali: A Comprehensive Grammar, public publisher preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781000284645_A40643614/preview-9781000284645_A40643614.pdf) was consulted for colloquial versus literary register and the distinction between spelling and pronunciation. The preview was not treated as access to the complete grammar.

[Bengali grammar overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_grammar) was consulted for the pronoun and conjugation comparisons, especially person versus number agreement and the distinction between ordinary negation and the -নি event-negative. Examples and explanatory sequencing are original. Regional pronunciation, pragmatic register, and the naturalness of connected dialogues need fluent-speaker review before claiming classroom validation. No exact day boundary is asserted between Bengali past constructions.

## Lesson 1: Introduce yourself without an English copula

### Outcome

Introduce yourself and identify another person using a complete Bengali sentence.

### Think before reading

English needs “am” in “I am a student.” If Bengali simply places the speaker beside a role, what information has actually been lost?

### Build the pattern

Nothing essential is missing. A neutral Bengali present identity sentence normally has no spoken equivalent of English am, is, or are. Put আমি (ami, I) before a name or role. আমি শিক্ষক (ami shikkhok) is a complete assertion, not broken speech. The same pattern identifies somebody else: তিনি শিক্ষক (tini shikkhok), he or she is a teacher, respectfully.

Use আপনি (apni) when addressing an unfamiliar adult. তুমি (tumi) belongs in established familiar relationships. This choice will affect verbs later; it is not a decorative word that can be swapped independently of the rest of a sentence. Bengali pronouns do not require you to announce the person's gender. A teacher may have any gender while the pronoun তিনি remains respectful.

For sound, pronounce a as an open vowel and i as in machine, without stretching it into two syllables. The romanisation sh represents the sound at the beginning of English ship. Bengali spelling preserves distinctions that ordinary pronunciation sometimes merges. Romanisation here guides speech rather than assigning one Latin letter to every Bengali letter. In particular, শিক্ষক is written shikkhok to suggest its ordinary pronunciation.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি শিক্ষক। | Ami shikkhok. | I am a teacher. |
| আপনি শিক্ষক। | Apni shikkhok. | You are a teacher. (respectful) |
| আমি ছাত্র। | Ami chhatro. | I am a male student. |
| তিনি শিক্ষক। | Tini shikkhok. | He or she is a teacher. (respectful) |
| আমরা বন্ধু। | Amra bondhu. | We are friends. |
| আমি রিনা। | Ami Rina. | I am Rina. |

### Common trap

Do not add আছে (achhe) merely because English contains “is.” That word expresses being present or available in constructions taught later. An identity and a location are different patterns.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I am a male student.
2. Say: He or she is a teacher. (respectful)
3. Say: We are friends.
4. Say: I am Rina.
5. Say: I am a doctor.
6. Say: You are a doctor. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `আমি ছাত্র।` - `Ami chhatro.`
2. `তিনি শিক্ষক।` - `Tini shikkhok.`
3. `আমরা বন্ধু।` - `Amra bondhu.`
4. `আমি রিনা।` - `Ami Rina.`
5. `আমি ডাক্তার।` - `Ami daktar.`
6. `আপনি ডাক্তার।` - `Apni daktar.`

### Why these answers work

The answer sentences join a subject with a role, name, or description. Neither plural আমরা nor respectful আপনি requires an added present copula. The last two answers transfer the identity frame to a workplace introduction rather than changing its grammar.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce yourself first by name and then by role. Imagine introducing a respected colleague, and replace the first-person pronoun with তিনি. Keep the role constant so you can hear exactly what changed.


## Lesson 2: Ask who and what

### Outcome

Ask for an identity and distinguish a question about a person from a question about a thing.

### Think before reading

Compare কে and কী. Why would exchanging them change what sort of answer you expect?

### Build the pattern

কে (ke) asks who; কী (ki) asks what. The long vowel sign in written কী distinguishes the content question from the yes-or-no particle কি, although their spoken forms are alike for many speakers. It is a spelling distinction to notice rather than an instruction to invent a large vowel-length contrast.

In আপনি কে (apni ke), the question word occupies the identity position. There is no English-style inversion and no auxiliary do. এটা কী (eṭa ki) asks what this is. এটা points to a thing close at hand; ওটা (oṭa) points to a thing farther away or already identified. Do not use these object demonstratives as your default respectful way to introduce adults.

A question can be very short because context supplies the scene. At a desk, এটা কী is enough while pointing to an unfamiliar object. The reply এটা বই (eṭa boi) classifies it as a book. A fuller noun phrase with a classifier will appear later. For now, the important reasoning step is to replace the unknown piece, not translate every English word separately.

Use আপনার নাম কী (apnar nam ki) as a complete name question. The possessive আপনার is introduced here as a useful chunk; its relationship to আপনি becomes productive in the possession lesson.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| এটা কী? | Eṭa ki? | What is this? |
| ওটা কী? | Oṭa ki? | What is that? |
| আপনি কে? | Apni ke? | Who are you? (respectful) |
| আপনার নাম কী? | Apnar nam ki? | What is your name? (respectful) |
| এটা বই। | Eṭa boi. | This is a book. |
| ওটা চা। | Oṭa cha. | That is tea. |

### Common trap

আপনি কে can sound abrupt when the situation does not justify identifying someone. Asking a name with আপনার নাম কী is often the more appropriate introductory question. Grammar alone does not make every question socially neutral.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Who are you? (respectful)
2. Say: What is your name? (respectful)
3. Say: This is a book.
4. Say: That is tea.
5. Say: This is a pen.
6. Say: That is a bag.

***Answers:***

1. `আপনি কে?` - `Apni ke?`
2. `আপনার নাম কী?` - `Apnar nam ki?`
3. `এটা বই।` - `Eṭa boi.`
4. `ওটা চা।` - `Oṭa cha.`
5. `এটা কলম।` - `Eṭa kolom.`
6. `ওটা ব্যাগ।` - `Oṭa bæg.`

### Why these answers work

Questions about a thing use কী; the person question uses কে. The answers preserve the order demonstrative plus question word. The new pen and bag examples show that a category label can replace the unknown element without changing the rest of the frame.

### Retrieve and recombine

Point to two nearby objects. Ask what each is, then answer with a category you know. Ask a fictional new colleague for a name using the full respectful question.


## Lesson 3: Say what you are not

### Outcome

Negate identities and give a short positive or negative answer.

### Think before reading

If positive identity has no copula, can you simply insert the ordinary action negative না anywhere you like?

### Build the pattern

Bengali uses special negative identity forms. For the first person, আমি শিক্ষক নই (ami shikkhok noi) means I am not a teacher. The respectful form is নন (non): তিনি ডাক্তার নন (tini daktar non). With a thing, নয় (noy) gives এটা বই নয় (eṭa boi noy), this is not a book. These are small predicate forms to learn as a set.

The choice belongs to the person and respect level of the subject, not the noun after it. A person saying “I am not a doctor” uses নই regardless of their gender. আমরা also uses নই. The respectful আপনি and তিনি take নন. For familiar তুমি the form is নও (noo); that is useful to recognise, but respectful conversation remains our default.

Standalone না (na) is a negative answer, while হ্যাঁ (hæ̃) is yes. The nasal mark in the romanisation of yes indicates that air also passes through the nose. A reply can combine a short answer with a correcting sentence in conversation. Practice keys keep these as individual sentences so that each construction can be checked clearly.

Notice how “this is tea” and “this is not tea” differ only by adding নয় at the end. Negation leaves the order of the two main pieces intact.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি শিক্ষক নই। | Ami shikkhok noi. | I am not a teacher. |
| তিনি ডাক্তার নন। | Tini daktar non. | He or she is not a doctor. (respectful) |
| আমি ছাত্র নই। | Ami chhatro noi. | I am not a male student. |
| আপনি শিক্ষক নন। | Apni shikkhok non. | You are not a teacher. (respectful) |
| আমরা ডাক্তার নই। | Amra daktar noi. | We are not doctors. |
| এটা চা নয়। | Eṭa cha noy. | This is not tea. |

### Common trap

Do not make every negative sentence end in নয়. That form fits an ordinary third-person identity or classification; an action such as reading uses a different negative strategy.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I am not a male student.
2. Say: You are not a teacher. (respectful)
3. Say: We are not doctors.
4. Say: This is not tea.
5. Say: That is not a book.
6. Say: This is not a pen.

***Answers:***

1. `আমি ছাত্র নই।` - `Ami chhatro noi.`
2. `আপনি শিক্ষক নন।` - `Apni shikkhok non.`
3. `আমরা ডাক্তার নই।` - `Amra daktar noi.`
4. `এটা চা নয়।` - `Eṭa cha noy.`
5. `ওটা বই নয়।` - `Oṭa boi noy.`
6. `এটা কলম নয়।` - `Eṭa kolom noy.`

### Why these answers work

First-person singular and plural select নই. Respectful pronouns select নন. The last two answers apply নয় to new object categories, demonstrating that it is the subject type and construction, not the vocabulary item, that determines the negative form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Correct three imaginary mistakes about yourself and objects on your desk. First say the incorrect claim positively, then negate it using the form appropriate to the subject.


## Lesson 4: Locate people and things

### Outcome

Say that someone is here and ask where a person is.

### Think before reading

Why might “I am a teacher” need no verb while “I am here” uses আছি?

### Build the pattern

A location sentence asserts somebody's presence at a place. Bengali uses আছি (achhi) with আমি and আমরা, আছেন (achhen) with respectful আপনি or তিনি, and আছে (achhe) with a thing or ordinary third person. Compare the identity pattern from Lesson 1 with আমি এখানে আছি (ami ekhane achhi), I am here. The meaning of being present supplies a real predicate.

এখানে (ekhane) means here and সেখানে (sekhane) means there. কোথায় (kothay) asks where. A location question simply replaces the place expression: আপনি কোথায় আছেন (apni kothay achhen). The pronoun stays at the beginning and the presence verb stays at the end. No word equivalent to English “are” is moved in front of the subject.

A small object can be located in exactly the same information order. বই এখানে আছে (boi ekhane achhe) says a book or books are here, with context determining reference. Later classifiers will make a particular book explicit. Do not infer that an unmarked noun must always be singular.

The table repeats place words deliberately so that the changing verb ending is audible. Read the speaker and the respected person side by side, then keep the respected form while switching from a statement to a question.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি এখানে আছি। | Ami ekhane achhi. | I am here. |
| তিনি সেখানে আছেন। | Tini sekhane achhen. | He or she is there. (respectful) |
| আপনি কোথায় আছেন? | Apni kothay achhen? | Where are you? (respectful) |
| আমরা এখানে আছি। | Amra ekhane achhi. | We are here. |
| তিনি এখানে আছেন। | Tini ekhane achhen. | He or she is here. (respectful) |
| আমি সেখানে আছি। | Ami sekhane achhi. | I am there. |

### Common trap

আছে is not a universal translation of English “is.” It cannot be pasted into every identity sentence, and its ending must not replace respectful আছেন when the subject calls for respect.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Where are you? (respectful)
2. Say: We are here.
3. Say: He or she is here. (respectful)
4. Say: I am there.
5. Say: A book is here.
6. Say: A pen is there.

***Answers:***

1. `আপনি কোথায় আছেন?` - `Apni kothay achhen?`
2. `আমরা এখানে আছি।` - `Amra ekhane achhi.`
3. `তিনি এখানে আছেন।` - `Tini ekhane achhen.`
4. `আমি সেখানে আছি।` - `Ami sekhane achhi.`
5. `বই এখানে আছে।` - `Boi ekhane achhe.`
6. `কলম সেখানে আছে।` - `Kolom sekhane achhe.`

### Why these answers work

The here and there expressions precede the presence verb. First-person আমরা takes the same আছি as আমি. The two new book and pen locations use আছে because their subjects are things, not people receiving honorific agreement.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine a telephone call. State where you are, ask where the listener is, and locate an object they need. Recover the pronoun-to-ending relationship without looking at the table.


## Lesson 5: Build daily actions

### Outcome

Put an object before a verb and describe a routine using first-person and respectful forms.

### Think before reading

In “I read a book,” which piece should you save until the end of the Bengali sentence?

### Build the pattern

The ordinary neutral order is subject, object, verb. আমি বই পড়ি (ami boi poṛi) literally arranges I, book, read. The first-person form পড়ি is shared by আমরা. Respectful আপনি and তিনি take পড়েন (poṛen). Bengali verbs here distinguish person and respect, not the subject's gender, and the first-person plural does not need a separate verb ending.

খাই (khai) means I eat or consume. Bengali naturally uses খাওয়া (khaoa) for drinking tea and water as well as eating food. Thus আমি চা খাই (ami cha khai) is the ordinary way to say I drink tea. Treat that as the language's own categorisation instead of insisting that one English verb must always map to one Bengali verb.

করি (kori) combines with কাজ (kaj, work) in কাজ করি, I work or do work. This is a noun-plus-verb combination. The respectful counterpart করেন (koren) carries the same person pattern as পড়েন. Word boundaries matter: the object stays separate, while the person ending belongs to the verb.

These simple present forms are useful for habits and general statements. They do not automatically mean an action is unfolding at this very second. The ongoing pattern will add a different internal shape in Lesson 8.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি বই পড়ি। | Ami boi poṛi. | I read books. |
| আপনি বই পড়েন। | Apni boi poṛen. | You read books. (respectful) |
| আমি চা খাই। | Ami cha khai. | I drink tea. |
| তিনি চা খান। | Tini cha khan. | He or she drinks tea. (respectful) |
| আমি কাজ করি। | Ami kaj kori. | I work. |
| আপনি কাজ করেন। | Apni kaj koren. | You work. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not attach a Hindi-style gender ending to পড়ি or খাই. Changing the speaker from a man to a woman does not alter these Bengali verb forms.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I drink tea.
2. Say: He or she drinks tea. (respectful)
3. Say: I work.
4. Say: You work. (respectful)
5. Say: I drink water.
6. Say: We read books.

***Answers:***

1. `আমি চা খাই।` - `Ami cha khai.`
2. `তিনি চা খান।` - `Tini cha khan.`
3. `আমি কাজ করি।` - `Ami kaj kori.`
4. `আপনি কাজ করেন।` - `Apni kaj koren.`
5. `আমি জল খাই।` - `Ami jol khai.`
6. `আমরা বই পড়ি।` - `Amra boi poṛi.`

### Why these answers work

The object precedes the final action. পড়ি and করি match the first person; পড়েন and করেন match respectful subjects. The water sentence transfers খাই beyond tea, while the group reading sentence shows that আমরা preserves the first-person verb ending.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe two daily routines, then attribute them respectfully to a colleague. Keep each object fixed while changing the pronoun and verb together. Listen for the difference between the two complete sentences.


## Lesson 6: Ask about routines and deny them

### Outcome

Ask a yes-or-no action question and negate a routine without changing the subject.

### Think before reading

Compare আপনি চা খান and আপনি কি চা খান. Which added word asks whether the whole statement is true?

### Build the pattern

The particle কি (ki) can mark a yes-or-no question: আপনি কি চা খান (apni ki cha khan). The object and verb keep their normal order. In conversation intonation can also make a question, but the particle gives a clear written pattern. Written কি here is the question particle, while কী asks for missing content, as in “what.”

For an ordinary negative routine, place না (na) after the finite verb: আমি চা খাই না (ami cha khai na). There is no support verb do and no replacement infinitive. The positive first-person খাই survives intact, followed by the negative particle. Respectful খান likewise stays intact in আপনি চা খান না.

A question about an unknown action uses কী differently. আপনি কী পড়েন (apni ki poṛen) asks what you read; a simple yes would not supply the missing information. Compare this with আপনি কি বই পড়েন, which asks whether you read books. The spelling difference helps reveal the intended question.

Build the affirmative first when you hesitate. Identify its last verb and put না after it. Then check whether the subject calls for a respectful form. This two-step reasoning avoids the common mistake of using an identity negative such as নন for an action.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আপনি কি চা খান? | Apni ki cha khan? | Do you drink tea? (respectful) |
| আমি চা খাই না। | Ami cha khai na. | I do not drink tea. |
| আপনি কি বই পড়েন? | Apni ki boi poṛen? | Do you read books? (respectful) |
| আমি বই পড়ি না। | Ami boi poṛi na. | I do not read books. |
| তিনি চা খান না। | Tini cha khan na. | He or she does not drink tea. (respectful) |
| আপনি কী পড়েন? | Apni ki poṛen? | What do you read? (respectful) |

### Common trap

A negative question can imply an expectation, just as in English. Start with a neutral positive question when you merely want information. Do not assume adding না makes every question more polite.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Do you read books? (respectful)
2. Say: I do not read books.
3. Say: He or she does not drink tea. (respectful)
4. Say: What do you read? (respectful)
5. Say: We do not work.
6. Say: Do you drink water? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `আপনি কি বই পড়েন?` - `Apni ki boi poṛen?`
2. `আমি বই পড়ি না।` - `Ami boi poṛi na.`
3. `তিনি চা খান না।` - `Tini cha khan na.`
4. `আপনি কী পড়েন?` - `Apni ki poṛen?`
5. `আমরা কাজ করি না।` - `Amra kaj kori na.`
6. `আপনি কি জল খান?` - `Apni ki jol khan?`

### Why these answers work

The action negative follows the conjugated verb. The question particle কি appears earlier in the question; the respectful verb still ends in its own honorific form. The new work and water contexts preserve the same mechanics with different objects.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask whether a visitor drinks tea. Imagine a negative reply and offer water by asking a second question. Recover the questions using statements as your starting point.


## Lesson 7: Speak to a friend

### Outcome

Use familiar তুমি consistently without losing the distinction from respectful আপনি.

### Think before reading

If তুমি replaces আপনি, is the old respectful verb ending still the right partner?

### Build the pattern

A pronoun choice establishes a grammatical relationship across the clause. তুমি পড়ো (tumi poṛo) is familiar you read; আপনি পড়েন remains respectful. With খাওয়া, the familiar form is খাও (khao); with করা it is করো (koro). The final o sound is audible even when spellings in other materials sometimes omit an explicit final vowel sign.

Familiarity is not simply a measurement of age. Families, workplaces, and friendships differ in their address conventions. Follow an established relationship rather than deciding that any younger person must receive তুমি. The more intimate তুই is recognised here but is not a productive default in this foundation course; it has its own verb forms and can be insulting in the wrong setting.

Ordinary third-person সে (she) means he or she, with forms such as পড়ে (poṛe), খায় (khay), and করে (kore). It does not express the same respect as তিনি. The romanisation she is the pronoun's sound, not the English feminine pronoun. Gender cannot be inferred from it.

The same negative particle works across these new person choices. তুমি চা খাও না negates a familiar listener's routine; সে চা খায় না negates an ordinary third person's routine. Change person endings before adding the shared negative.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| তুমি বই পড়ো। | Tumi boi poṛo. | You read books. (familiar) |
| সে বই পড়ে। | She boi poṛe. | He or she reads books. (ordinary) |
| তুমি চা খাও। | Tumi cha khao. | You drink tea. (familiar) |
| সে চা খায়। | She cha khay. | He or she drinks tea. (ordinary) |
| তুমি কাজ করো। | Tumi kaj koro. | You work. (familiar) |
| সে কাজ করে। | She kaj kore. | He or she works. (ordinary) |

### Common trap

Do not mix তুমি with পড়েন as a neutral default. Also do not read romanised she as evidence that Bengali সে is female-only.

### Practice

1. Say: You drink tea. (familiar)
2. Say: He or she drinks tea. (ordinary)
3. Say: You work. (familiar)
4. Say: He or she works. (ordinary)
5. Say: You do not drink tea. (familiar)
6. Say: He or she does not work. (ordinary)

***Answers:***

1. `তুমি চা খাও।` - `Tumi cha khao.`
2. `সে চা খায়।` - `She cha khay.`
3. `তুমি কাজ করো।` - `Tumi kaj koro.`
4. `সে কাজ করে।` - `She kaj kore.`
5. `তুমি চা খাও না।` - `Tumi cha khao na.`
6. `সে কাজ করে না।` - `She kaj kore na.`

### Why these answers work

Familiar second-person forms end in the patterns পড়ো, খাও, করো. Ordinary third-person forms are পড়ে, খায়, করে. The last two answers recombine the familiar ending with negation and the third-person form with the work expression.

### Retrieve and recombine

## Lesson 8: Describe what is happening now

### Outcome

Distinguish a routine from an action currently in progress.

### Think before reading

What meaning changes between পড়ি and পড়ছি even though both have a first-person subject?

### Build the pattern

The ongoing form adds a progressive element. আমি পড়ছি (ami poṛchhi) means I am reading, while আমি পড়ি states a routine or general activity. For the regular verb করা, compare করি with করছি (korchhi). The first-person progressive ending is chhi; respectful subjects take chhen, as in করছেন (korchhen). Familiar তুমি takes করছ (korchho), and ordinary সে takes করছে (korchhe).

খাওয়া has a visible stem adjustment: খাচ্ছি (khachchhi), I am eating or drinking. Do not create a form by adding chhi to the entire simple present খাই. The useful comparison is between complete stems in the table. Bengali has regular patterns, but very frequent verbs must be learned with the stem shape appropriate to each construction.

এখন (ekhon) means now. It is a helpful time expression, not a grammatical requirement for progressive meaning. আমি এখন কাজ করছি and আমি কাজ করছি can both describe work in progress. Placing এখন after the subject creates a neutral, easy-to-follow sentence.

A negative current activity keeps the progressive verb and adds না: আমি এখন পড়ছি না. That is distinct from saying you never or generally do not read. The time window is part of the meaning you choose before selecting the verb.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি বই পড়ছি। | Ami boi poṛchhi. | I am reading a book. |
| আপনি কাজ করছেন। | Apni kaj korchhen. | You are working. (respectful) |
| আমি চা খাচ্ছি। | Ami cha khachchhi. | I am drinking tea. |
| তিনি বই পড়ছেন। | Tini boi poṛchhen. | He or she is reading a book. (respectful) |
| আমি এখন কাজ করছি। | Ami ekhon kaj korchhi. | I am working now. |
| আমি এখন পড়ছি না। | Ami ekhon poṛchhi na. | I am not reading now. |

### Common trap

Do not translate every English present with the progressive. “I read books” as a habit remains পড়ি; পড়ছি describes the current unfolding activity.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I am drinking tea.
2. Say: He or she is reading a book. (respectful)
3. Say: I am working now.
4. Say: I am not reading now.
5. Say: You are drinking tea. (familiar)
6. Say: We are working.

***Answers:***

1. `আমি চা খাচ্ছি।` - `Ami cha khachchhi.`
2. `তিনি বই পড়ছেন।` - `Tini boi poṛchhen.`
3. `আমি এখন কাজ করছি।` - `Ami ekhon kaj korchhi.`
4. `আমি এখন পড়ছি না।` - `Ami ekhon poṛchhi na.`
5. `তুমি চা খাচ্ছ।` - `Tumi cha khachchho.`
6. `আমরা কাজ করছি।` - `Amra kaj korchhi.`

### Why these answers work

The progressive shapes remain inside the final verb, and না follows it in the negative answer. Respectful করছেন and পড়ছেন keep their honorific ending. The new friend and group examples show how person changes while progressive meaning remains constant.

### Retrieve and recombine

Look around an imagined study room. Describe one person reading, yourself working, and a friend drinking tea. Then describe those same people’s routines with the simple present.


## Lesson 9: Show whose thing it is

### Outcome

Build possessive noun phrases and ask who owns an object.

### Think before reading

Why does “my book” begin with আমার rather than the subject pronoun আমি?

### Build the pattern

Possession uses a different pronoun form. আমি becomes আমার (amar, my), আমরা becomes আমাদের (amader, our), তুমি becomes তোমার (tomar, your familiar), and আপনি becomes আপনার (apnar, your respectful). The owner expression comes before the thing owned: আমার বই (amar boi). This order resembles English my book, but the forms are Bengali case forms rather than a freely added English-style word.

To identify an owner in a full sentence, use এটা আমার বই (eṭa amar boi), this is my book. The present identity pattern again requires no copula. The same noun phrase can become the subject of a location sentence: আমার বই এখানে আছে. Its internal order does not change when its role in the larger sentence changes.

কার (kar) asks whose. এটা কার বই means whose book is this. The unknown owner occupies the same position before বই as আমার did. This replacement method makes the question predictable: retain the object classification and ask only about its owner.

Possessive forms also appear in descriptions of relationships, not just legal ownership. আমার বন্ধু means my friend; the phrase does not imply owning a person. আমার নাম is my name. A grammatical label such as possessive covers several semantic relationships.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| এটা আমার বই। | Eṭa amar boi. | This is my book. |
| এটা আপনার কলম। | Eṭa apnar kolom. | This is your pen. (respectful) |
| ওটা তোমার ব্যাগ। | Oṭa tomar bæg. | That is your bag. (familiar) |
| এটা কার বই? | Eṭa kar boi? | Whose book is this? |
| আমার বই এখানে আছে। | Amar boi ekhane achhe. | My book is here. |
| আমার নাম রিনা। | Amar nam Rina. | My name is Rina. |

### Common trap

Do not use আমি directly before a noun to mean my. আমি বই is not the ordinary possessive phrase; use আমার বই. Keep আপনার distinct from familiar তোমার when speaking to a new acquaintance.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: That is your bag. (familiar)
2. Say: Whose book is this?
3. Say: My book is here.
4. Say: My name is Rina.
5. Say: This is our book.
6. Say: He or she is my friend. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `ওটা তোমার ব্যাগ।` - `Oṭa tomar bæg.`
2. `এটা কার বই?` - `Eṭa kar boi?`
3. `আমার বই এখানে আছে।` - `Amar boi ekhane achhe.`
4. `আমার নাম রিনা।` - `Amar nam Rina.`
5. `এটা আমাদের বই।` - `Eṭa amader boi.`
6. `তিনি আমার বন্ধু।` - `Tini amar bondhu.`

### Why these answers work

The owner precedes the noun in every answer. Identity sentences stop after that noun phrase, while the book location ends with আছে. The new group and friend contexts use the same case pattern for collective ownership and a human relationship.

### Retrieve and recombine

Choose a pen, book, and bag. Assign them to yourself, a friend, and the listener. Ask whose each one is, then answer with the appropriate possessive phrase.


## Lesson 10: Have something and lack something

### Outcome

Express availability and possession using an owner plus আছে or নেই.

### Think before reading

Compare “my book” with “I have a book.” What additional claim does the second sentence make?

### Build the pattern

A possessive phrase identifies a relationship; a possession sentence asserts that something is available to an owner. Bengali commonly uses the owner's genitive form plus the thing plus আছে: আমার বই আছে (amar boi achhe), I have a book or books. The literal structure is my, book, exists. Do not try to force an English-style verb have between আমি and বই.

The corresponding absence predicate is নেই (nei). আমার কলম নেই (amar kolom nei) means I do not have a pen. It is not necessary to keep আছে and append the routine-action negative. Similarly, এখানে জল নেই says there is no water here, without an owner at all.

আপনার কি সময় আছে (apnar ki shomoy achhe) asks whether you have time. The respectful relationship is carried by আপনার. The predicate আছে concerns the availability of time, so it is not converted to আছেন merely because the owner is respected. This is a useful contrast with আপনি এখানে আছেন, where the respected person is the subject being located.

The possession pattern also works for abstract resources such as time. Learn টাকা (ṭaka, money) here as an everyday mass noun; in a money context it need not mean a specified numerical amount of a particular currency.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমার বই আছে। | Amar boi achhe. | I have a book. |
| আমার কলম নেই। | Amar kolom nei. | I do not have a pen. |
| আপনার কি সময় আছে? | Apnar ki shomoy achhe? | Do you have time? (respectful) |
| আমাদের জল আছে। | Amader jol achhe. | We have water. |
| এখানে জল নেই। | Ekhane jol nei. | There is no water here. |
| আপনার বই আছে। | Apnar boi achhe. | You have a book. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Honorific agreement follows the grammatical subject, not every respected person mentioned. আপনার সময় আছে has time as the available thing, so আছেন is not the expected form.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Do you have time? (respectful)
2. Say: We have water.
3. Say: There is no water here.
4. Say: You have a book. (respectful)
5. Say: I do not have money.
6. Say: We have time.

***Answers:***

1. `আপনার কি সময় আছে?` - `Apnar ki shomoy achhe?`
2. `আমাদের জল আছে।` - `Amader jol achhe.`
3. `এখানে জল নেই।` - `Ekhane jol nei.`
4. `আপনার বই আছে।` - `Apnar boi achhe.`
5. `আমার টাকা নেই।` - `Amar ṭaka nei.`
6. `আমাদের সময় আছে।` - `Amader shomoy achhe.`

### Why these answers work

The answer forms use a genitive owner followed by the available resource. নেই replaces the positive existence predicate to express absence. The last two answers extend the construction from physical possessions to money and time without introducing an English-style have verb.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine lending supplies before class. State one thing you have, one thing you lack, and ask the listener whether they have time. Contrast a possession sentence with a plain “my...” noun phrase.


## Lesson 11: Pick out and count objects

### Outcome

Use a classifier to identify a particular object or count a small set.

### Think before reading

Compare বইটা with একটা বই. What happens when the classifier follows the noun rather than the numeral?

### Build the pattern

Bengali often identifies a particular familiar object with a noun followed by a classifier. বইটা (boiṭa) means the book or that specific book in context. The everyday classifier টা is useful with many things. বইটি is a more formal or careful alternative, but we keep one conversational form stable in the exercises.

Counting normally places the number-classifier expression before the noun: একটা বই (ekṭa boi), one book or a book; দুটো বই (duṭo boi), two books; তিনটে বই (tinṭe boi), three books. The forms of two and three show that this is not always a mechanical sequence of the bare number plus an unchanged টা. Learn these common counted chunks together.

For several identified objects, বইগুলো (boigulo) means the books. It is not the ordinary choice after an exact numeral in the simple counting pattern. দুটো বই is enough to say two books. English plural s is not a reason to add গুলো as well.

Number words alone are এক (ek), দুই (dui), তিন (tin), চার (char), পাঁচ (pãch), and ছয় (chhoy). The table combines a small subset with familiar nouns so that counting does not conceal the grammar under a large numerical vocabulary.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| এটা একটা বই। | Eṭa ekṭa boi. | This is a book. |
| বইটা এখানে আছে। | Boiṭa ekhane achhe. | The book is here. |
| আমার দুটো বই আছে। | Amar duṭo boi achhe. | I have two books. |
| আমার তিনটে কলম আছে। | Amar tinṭe kolom achhe. | I have three pens. |
| কলমটা আমার। | Kolomṭa amar. | The pen is mine. |
| বইগুলো এখানে আছে। | Boigulo ekhane achhe. | The books are here. |

### Common trap

Do not assume টা is a socially neutral classifier for every person. Human counting uses জন in a later lesson; calling someone by a thing classifier can carry unwanted familiarity or disrespect.

### Practice

1. Say: I have two books.
2. Say: I have three pens.
3. Say: The pen is mine.
4. Say: The books are here.
5. Say: We have two pens.
6. Say: The book is yours. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `আমার দুটো বই আছে।` - `Amar duṭo boi achhe.`
2. `আমার তিনটে কলম আছে।` - `Amar tinṭe kolom achhe.`
3. `কলমটা আমার।` - `Kolomṭa amar.`
4. `বইগুলো এখানে আছে।` - `Boigulo ekhane achhe.`
5. `আমাদের দুটো কলম আছে।` - `Amader duṭo kolom achhe.`

### Why these answers work

একটা, দুটো, and তিনটে precede the counted noun; বইটা and কলমটা identify an object by placing the classifier after it. The final two answers transfer counting to a different desk object and use a definite noun as an identity subject.

## Lesson 12: Put things in and near places

### Outcome

Locate an object with a noun ending or a postposition.

### Think before reading

English puts “under” before “the table.” Where does Bengali put নিচে relative to its reference noun?

### Build the pattern

Bengali typically places a relational word after its reference expression. টেবিলের নিচে (ṭebiler niche) is under the table: table plus genitive ending, then below. টেবিলের ওপর (ṭebiler opor) is on the table. The genitive form connects the reference object to its spatial part or relation. Do not stop at learning নিচে without learning its surrounding construction.

A simple locative ending also marks places: ঘরে (ghore), in the room, and বাড়িতে (baṛite), at home or at the house. These are conventional forms rather than a single spelling suffix pasted onto every noun. In ঘরে বই আছে, the sentence asserts the presence of books in the room. With a previously identified object, বইটা ঘরে আছে locates the book.

পাশে (pashe) means beside or near. বাড়ির পাশে (baṛir pashe) illustrates the same genitive-plus-postposition pattern. ভিতরে (bhitore) means inside. The choice between a short locative and an explicit relation depends on how precisely you need to describe the place.

In the table, follow the whole place phrase as one block. You can replace এখানে from an earlier lesson with টেবিলের ওপর and keep the rest of the location sentence intact. That makes a longer sentence without changing its basic architecture.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |

| আমি বাড়িতে আছি। | Ami baṛite achhi. | I am at home. |
| কলমটা টেবিলের ওপর আছে। | Kolomṭa ṭebiler opor achhe. | The pen is on the table. |
| ব্যাগটা টেবিলের নিচে আছে। | Bægṭa ṭebiler niche achhe. | The bag is under the table. |

| বইটা ব্যাগের ভিতরে আছে। | Boiṭa bæger bhitore achhe. | The book is inside the bag. |

Do not reverse a postposition phrase to match English. Also do not discard the linking genitive in the model টেবিলের নিচে when recombining the phrase.

3. `তিনি বাড়ির ভিতরে আছেন।` - `Tini baṛir bhitore achhen.`

6. `আমরা ঘরে আছি।` - `Amra ghore achhi.`

### Why these answers work

Each answer places the location before the final presence verb. The table and house expressions use genitive forms before their postpositions. The new pen and group locations transfer the same place phrases to a thing and to first-person people.

## Lesson 13: Go, come, and say where from

### Outcome

Describe movement and distinguish a destination from a source.

### Think before reading

If এখানে is here, how can “from here” be built by adding a word after it?

### Build the pattern

থেকে (theke) marks a source: এখান থেকে (ekhan theke), from here; বাড়ি থেকে (baṛi theke), from home. Notice the local form এখান before থেকে rather than treating every phrase as এখানে plus an extra word. The complete phrase should be learned as a replaceable unit.

যাই (jai) is I go, যান (jan) is respectful go, and যায় (jay) is ordinary third-person goes. আসি (ashi) is I come, with respectful আসেন (ashen). The difference between come and go is anchored in the speaker's or conversationally relevant location, much as in English, but conversational perspective can shift. Learn a movement sentence with an imagined scene.

আমি স্কুলে যাই (ami skule jai) describes going to school as a routine. স্কুলে is the destination form here. আমি বাড়ি যাই is also an ordinary way to say I go home; not every destination requires an overt suffix. The noun বাড়ি participates in several established location and direction expressions.

Where questions use কোথায়, while source questions use কোথা থেকে (kotha theke). আপনি কোথায় যান asks where you go; আপনি কোথা থেকে আসেন asks where you come from. Keeping those meanings separate matters more than translating “where” with the same chunk every time.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি স্কুলে যাই। | Ami skule jai. | I go to school. |
| আপনি কোথায় যান? | Apni kothay jan? | Where do you go? (respectful) |
| আমি বাড়ি যাই। | Ami baṛi jai. | I go home. |
| তিনি এখানে আসেন। | Tini ekhane ashen. | He or she comes here. (respectful) |
| আমি বাড়ি থেকে আসি। | Ami baṛi theke ashi. | I come from home. |
| আপনি এখান থেকে যান। | Apni ekhan theke jan. | You go from here. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not substitute the progressive automatically into a routine such as going to school. Also keep a source marked with থেকে distinct from an unmarked or locative-marked destination.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I go home.
2. Say: He or she comes here. (respectful)
3. Say: I come from home.
4. Say: You go from here. (respectful)
5. Say: We go to school.
6. Say: Where do you come from? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `আমি বাড়ি যাই।` - `Ami baṛi jai.`
2. `তিনি এখানে আসেন।` - `Tini ekhane ashen.`
3. `আমি বাড়ি থেকে আসি।` - `Ami baṛi theke ashi.`
4. `আপনি এখান থেকে যান।` - `Apni ekhan theke jan.`
5. `আমরা স্কুলে যাই।` - `Amra skule jai.`
6. `আপনি কোথা থেকে আসেন?` - `Apni kotha theke ashen?`

### Why these answers work

The motion verb remains final. The destination school uses স্কুলে, while the source home uses বাড়ি থেকে. The new group movement and respectful source question apply the same source/destination distinction to different participants.

### Retrieve and recombine

Plan a simple route from home to school. Say the starting point, then the destination, using two short sentences. Ask another person where they come from.


## Lesson 14: Make useful polite requests

### Outcome

Ask someone respectfully to come, sit, read, or give you an object.

### Think before reading

Why should a request use a dedicated verb form rather than simply a respectful statement with a question mark?

### Build the pattern

A direct respectful request uses an imperative form such as আসুন (ashun), please come, or বসুন (boshun), please sit. পড়ুন (poṛun) means read, and বলুন (bolun) means say or tell. These are different forms from respectful statements such as আসেন and পড়েন. The imperative is a grammatical instruction or invitation, with politeness shaped by voice and context as well as its ending.

দিন (din), give, is a common irregular request form. আমাকে (amake) means to me or me in an object role. আমাকে কলমটা দিন (amake kolomṭa din) asks the listener to give the pen to the speaker. The recipient comes before the object and final verb in this neutral model. We do not add a separate English-like to before আমাকে.

দয়া করে (doya kore) means please, literally a conventional courtesy expression. দয়া করে বসুন is useful when welcoming a visitor. The respectful imperative already carries appropriate grammatical respect; adding the courtesy phrase can soften it further but is not obligatory in every interaction.

একটু (ekṭu), a little, also softens many requests. একটু আসুন invites someone to come for a moment. Treat it as context-sensitive softening, not a magic word that makes any demand acceptable.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| দয়া করে বসুন। | Doya kore boshun. | Please sit. |
| এখানে আসুন। | Ekhane ashun. | Come here. (respectful) |
| বইটা পড়ুন। | Boiṭa poṛun. | Read the book. (respectful) |
| আমাকে কলমটা দিন। | Amake kolomṭa din. | Give me the pen. (respectful) |
| একটু আসুন। | Ekṭu ashun. | Come for a moment. (respectful) |
| আপনার নাম বলুন। | Apnar nam bolun. | Tell me your name. (respectful) |

### Common trap

পড়েন is a respectful present statement; পড়ুন is the request taught here. A copied statement ending does not automatically make an imperative. Familiar commands have another set of forms.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Read the book. (respectful)
2. Say: Give me the pen. (respectful)
3. Say: Come for a moment. (respectful)
4. Say: Tell me your name. (respectful)
5. Say: Give me the book. (respectful)
6. Say: Come into the room. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `বইটা পড়ুন।` - `Boiṭa poṛun.`
2. `আমাকে কলমটা দিন।` - `Amake kolomṭa din.`
3. `একটু আসুন।` - `Ekṭu ashun.`
4. `আপনার নাম বলুন।` - `Apnar nam bolun.`
5. `আমাকে বইটা দিন।` - `Amake boiṭa din.`
6. `ঘরে আসুন।` - `Ghore ashun.`

### Why these answers work

The commands end with respectful request forms. আমাকে marks the recipient without a preceding preposition. The last two requests transfer দিন to a different object and আসুন to a place expression already known from location lessons.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine receiving a visitor. Invite them in, offer a seat, then request a pen for signing a form. Say each as an independent request and identify the final imperative.


## Lesson 15: Describe and compare objects

### Outcome

Describe an object and make a basic comparison using চেয়ে.

### Think before reading

Does a Bengali adjective need a feminine or plural ending when its noun changes?

### Build the pattern

Ordinary Bengali adjectives do not agree with a noun in grammatical gender. বড় (boṛo, big), ছোট (chhoṭo, small), ভালো (bhalo, good), and নতুন (notun, new) remain stable in these basic constructions. একটা বড় বই places the adjective before the noun, while বইটা বড় uses the adjective as a present description and needs no copula.

খুব (khub) intensifies a description: খুব ভালো, very good. বেশি (beshi) means more or much, and কম (kom) means less or little in quantity. Do not treat খুব and বেশি as interchangeable in every context. Here খুব modifies a quality, while বেশি is used explicitly in the comparison pattern.

To compare two objects, place the standard before চেয়ে (cheye): এই বইটা ওই বইটার চেয়ে বড়. The comparison standard বইটার has the genitive ending after its classifier. You can hear the pieces as that-book's, compared with, big. English says bigger, but Bengali does not add an English-style comparative ending to বড়.

For short practice, ওটার চেয়ে (oṭar cheye), than that one, keeps the comparison manageable. The demonstrative owner-like form ওটার links to চেয়ে. A comparison only has meaning in a context with two identifiable items, so imagine those items rather than reciting an isolated formula.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| বইটা নতুন। | Boiṭa notun. | The book is new. |
| বইটা খুব ভালো। | Boiṭa khub bhalo. | The book is very good. |
| এটা একটা বড় বই। | Eṭa ekṭa boṛo boi. | This is a big book. |
| ওটা একটা ছোট বই। | Oṭa ekṭa chhoṭo boi. | That is a small book. |
| এটা ওটার চেয়ে বড়। | Eṭa oṭar cheye boṛo. | This is bigger than that. |
| এটা ওটার চেয়ে ছোট। | Eṭa oṭar cheye chhoṭo. | This is smaller than that. |

### Common trap

Do not invent adjective agreement endings. Keep the genitive shape in ওটার চেয়ে, and avoid using English word order with চেয়ে placed before the comparison object.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: This is a big book.
2. Say: That is a small book.
3. Say: This is bigger than that.
4. Say: This is smaller than that.
5. Say: The bag is new.
6. Say: The pen is very good.

***Answers:***

1. `এটা একটা বড় বই।` - `Eṭa ekṭa boṛo boi.`
2. `ওটা একটা ছোট বই।` - `Oṭa ekṭa chhoṭo boi.`
3. `এটা ওটার চেয়ে বড়।` - `Eṭa oṭar cheye boṛo.`
4. `এটা ওটার চেয়ে ছোট।` - `Eṭa oṭar cheye chhoṭo.`
5. `ব্যাগটা নতুন।` - `Bægṭa notun.`
6. `কলমটা খুব ভালো।` - `Kolomṭa khub bhalo.`

### Why these answers work

The short descriptions use no present copula. খুব precedes its adjective. Comparison answers retain the adjective বড় or ছোট unchanged and put the comparison standard before চেয়ে. The new bag and pen descriptions demonstrate that noun choice does not control adjective endings.

### Retrieve and recombine

Compare two books, then compare two bags. Use one plain description and one comparison. Explain which phrase identifies the standard of comparison in your longer sentence.


## Lesson 16: Place routines in time

### Outcome

Use daily time expressions and distinguish a clock time from a duration.

### Think before reading

Would “at three” and “for three hours” describe the same kind of time information?

### Build the pattern

আজ (aj) means today, কাল (kal) can mean yesterday or tomorrow depending on tense and context, and প্রতিদিন (protidin) means every day. Because কাল is directionally ambiguous on its own, a sentence's verb and surrounding conversation are important. This lesson uses আজ and প্রতিদিন productively; later past and future forms will make the ambiguity of কাল manageable.

সকালে (shokale), in the morning, and রাতে (rate), at night, are time expressions that resemble locative forms. They can appear near the beginning of a neutral clause: আমি সকালে চা খাই. The object still precedes the verb. Changing the time phrase does not itself require changing the person ending.

তিনটায় (tinṭay) means at three o'clock. The numeral and clock expression combine into a time point. তিন ঘণ্টা (tin ghoṇṭa) means three hours, a duration. আমি তিন ঘণ্টা কাজ করি says I work for three hours; English for is not translated as a separate obligatory preposition here.

কখন (kokhon) asks when. আপনি কখন আসেন asks about the listener's usual coming time in this simple-present context. A question about a planned single arrival will use a future form later. Identify whether you are asking about a routine or an upcoming occasion before choosing the verb.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি সকালে চা খাই। | Ami shokale cha khai. | I drink tea in the morning. |
| আমি রাতে বই পড়ি। | Ami rate boi poṛi. | I read books at night. |
| আমরা প্রতিদিন কাজ করি। | Amra protidin kaj kori. | We work every day. |
| আপনি কখন আসেন? | Apni kokhon ashen? | When do you come? (respectful, routine) |
| আমি তিনটায় আসি। | Ami tinṭay ashi. | I come at three o'clock. |
| আমি তিন ঘণ্টা কাজ করি। | Ami tin ghoṇṭa kaj kori. | I work for three hours. |

### Common trap

Do not translate কাল automatically as tomorrow. Also keep a clock point ending in -টায় distinct from an amount of time expressed with ঘণ্টা.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: We work every day.
2. Say: When do you come? (respectful, routine)
3. Say: I come at three o'clock.
4. Say: I work for three hours.
5. Say: I read books in the morning.
6. Say: We drink tea at night.

***Answers:***

1. `আমরা প্রতিদিন কাজ করি।` - `Amra protidin kaj kori.`
2. `আপনি কখন আসেন?` - `Apni kokhon ashen?`
3. `আমি তিনটায় আসি।` - `Ami tinṭay ashi.`
4. `আমি তিন ঘণ্টা কাজ করি।` - `Ami tin ghoṇṭa kaj kori.`
5. `আমি সকালে বই পড়ি।` - `Ami shokale boi poṛi.`
6. `আমরা রাতে চা খাই।` - `Amra rate cha khai.`

### Why these answers work

Time phrases are added before the final action without disturbing person agreement. The clock expression তিনটায় supplies a point, while তিন ঘণ্টা supplies a duration. The final two answers transfer the same time blocks to reading and a group routine.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a morning routine and a night routine. Give one clock time and one duration. Ask a respectful listener when they come, then imagine a short reply.


## Lesson 17: Report a completed event

### Outcome

Describe a completed action with simple past forms.

### Think before reading

What stays stable when করি becomes করলাম, and what extra information does the new ending add?

### Build the pattern

The simple past of করা includes করলাম (korlam), I or we did; করলে (korle), you did familiar; করল (korlo), he or she did ordinary; and করলেন (korlen), respectful did. The l element is a useful signal, but learn the full person forms so that the vowels are not treated as interchangeable.

পড়লাম (poṛlam) means I read in a completed-event sense. খেলাম (khelam) is the past of খাওয়া in this context, I ate or drank. This form also resembles the past of playing in some contexts, so the object matters: চা খেলাম clearly concerns drinking tea. গেলাম (gelam), I went, is an irregular high-frequency form and should not be constructed by mechanically extending যাই.

Use আজ to place a completed event earlier today: আমি আজ কাজ করলাম. Bengali choices among simple past and other past constructions depend on discourse, region, and how an event is presented. Do not impose a rigid universal cutoff of exactly one day on these forms. Here the simple past is introduced through a short finished-event report.

A sequence of short sentences can already tell a small story. “I went home” and “I read the book” each have their own completed verb. Connecting these actions grammatically will come after the forms themselves are secure.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি কাজ করলাম। | Ami kaj korlam. | I did the work. |
| আমি চা খেলাম। | Ami cha khelam. | I drank tea. |
| আমি বাড়ি গেলাম। | Ami baṛi gelam. | I went home. |
| আপনি কাজ করলেন। | Apni kaj korlen. | You did the work. (respectful) |
| তিনি বই পড়লেন। | Tini boi poṛlen. | He or she read a book. (respectful, completed) |
| আমি আজ কাজ করলাম। | Ami aj kaj korlam. | I did the work today. |

### Common trap

The past of যাই is গেলাম, not a guessed regular form built from the present. Do not use the identity negative নই to deny a past event; past negation receives separate treatment next.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I went home.
2. Say: You did the work. (respectful)
3. Say: He or she read a book. (respectful, completed)
4. Say: I did the work today.
5. Say: We drank tea.
6. Say: I read the book. (completed)

***Answers:***

1. `আমি বাড়ি গেলাম।` - `Ami baṛi gelam.`
2. `আপনি কাজ করলেন।` - `Apni kaj korlen.`
3. `তিনি বই পড়লেন।` - `Tini boi poṛlen.`
4. `আমি আজ কাজ করলাম।` - `Ami aj kaj korlam.`
5. `আমরা চা খেলাম।` - `Amra cha khelam.`
6. `আমি বইটা পড়লাম।` - `Ami boiṭa poṛlam.`

### Why these answers work

The first-person answers use -লাম in the relevant verb shapes. Respectful করলেন and পড়লেন retain the honorific person ending within the past form. The last two answers extend the completed-event pattern to a group and a specific book.

### Retrieve and recombine

Tell a two-event account of this morning using one regular action and the irregular movement verb. Then report one of those events about a respected colleague.


## Lesson 18: Say what has happened and what has not

### Outcome

Use a present perfect form and the distinct negative in -নি.

### Think before reading

If করেছি means have done, would keeping that whole form and adding না be the standard negative pattern taught here?

### Build the pattern

করেছি (korechhi) means I have done, and করেছেন (korechhen) is the respectful counterpart. পড়েছি (poṛechhi) means I have read; খেয়েছি (kheyechhi) means I have eaten or drunk; গেছি (gechhi) means I have gone in ordinary colloquial usage. These forms often present a past event as relevant to the current conversation.

The ordinary negative completion pattern uses forms such as করিনি (korini), I have not done or I did not do, and করেননি (korenni), respectful has not done. This is not simply the affirmative perfect plus the general particle না. Similarly, পড়িনি means I have not read, খাইনি means I have not eaten or drunk, and যাইনি means I did not go or have not gone.

English tense labels are guides, not exact equivalences. Bengali করিনি can correspond to either an English simple-past negative or a present-perfect negative depending on context. Choose the Bengali construction for a denied event, then interpret its time from the conversation rather than forcing one English label everywhere.

এখনও (ekhono) means still or yet in appropriate contexts. আমি এখনও বইটা পড়িনি naturally says I have not read the book yet. The extra time expression makes the remaining relevance explicit without altering the negative verb.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি কাজ করেছি। | Ami kaj korechhi. | I have done the work. |
| তিনি বইটা পড়েছেন। | Tini boiṭa poṛechhen. | He or she has read the book. (respectful) |
| আমি চা খেয়েছি। | Ami cha kheyechhi. | I have drunk tea. |
| আমি কাজ করিনি। | Ami kaj korini. | I have not done the work. |
| আমি বইটা পড়িনি। | Ami boiṭa poṛini. | I have not read the book. |
| তিনি চা খাননি। | Tini cha khanni. | He or she has not drunk tea. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not build the normal negative as করেছি না by mechanically combining the earlier affirmative perfect with না. The ordinary event-negative form is করিনি.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I have drunk tea.
2. Say: I have not done the work.
3. Say: I have not read the book.
4. Say: He or she has not drunk tea. (respectful)
5. Say: We have not done the work.
6. Say: I have not read the book yet.

***Answers:***

1. `আমি চা খেয়েছি।` - `Ami cha kheyechhi.`
2. `আমি কাজ করিনি।` - `Ami kaj korini.`
3. `আমি বইটা পড়িনি।` - `Ami boiṭa poṛini.`
4. `তিনি চা খাননি।` - `Tini cha khanni.`
5. `আমরা কাজ করিনি।` - `Amra kaj korini.`
6. `আমি এখনও বইটা পড়িনি।` - `Ami ekhono boiṭa poṛini.`

### Why these answers work

The affirmative completed forms contain the perfect pattern in পড়েছি or খেয়েছি. The negative answers use -নি forms built on their own stems. The new group and yet examples retain that negative morphology while adding a different subject or time expression.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine a checklist for a trip. Say one thing you have done and one you have not done yet. Contrast the affirmative and negative stems aloud before building the full sentences.


## Lesson 19: Make plans and predictions

### Outcome

Say what you will do and ask about a respectful listener’s plans.

### Think before reading

Compare করব, করবে, and করবেন. Which two familiar person categories share the middle form?

### Build the pattern

The future forms of করা are করব (korbo), I or we will do; করবে (korbe), familiar you or ordinary he/she will do; and করবেন (korben), respectful will do. The first-person final vowel is pronounced although ordinary spelling ends at ব. The romanisation includes it to support speaking.

যাব (jabo) means I will go, আসব (ashbo) means I will come, খাব (khabo) means I will eat or drink, and পড়ব (poṛbo) means I will read. Unlike the irregular past গেলাম, the future of going uses the ja stem. This is why remembering a verb through several useful complete forms is better than assuming the present stem predicts everything mechanically.

আগামীকাল (agamikal) explicitly means tomorrow. কাল can also mean tomorrow when a future verb supplies the direction, but আগামীকাল is especially clear for a learner arranging an appointment. আমি আগামীকাল আসব makes a future commitment; the same sentence can be softened or qualified in real conversation as needed.

The ordinary future negative returns to final না: আমি যাব না. It differs from the past-event negative যাইনি. Keep time and polarity as two choices: first choose the future form, then add the ordinary negative particle. A yes-or-no future question can use কি without moving the final verb.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি আগামীকাল আসব। | Ami agamikal ashbo. | I will come tomorrow. |
| আপনি কখন আসবেন? | Apni kokhon ashben? | When will you come? (respectful) |
| আমি বইটা পড়ব। | Ami boiṭa poṛbo. | I will read the book. |
| আমি চা খাব না। | Ami cha khabo na. | I will not drink tea. |
| তিনি কাজ করবেন। | Tini kaj korben. | He or she will work. (respectful) |
| আমি বাড়ি যাব। | Ami baṛi jabo. | I will go home. |

### Common trap

Do not use যাইনি to mean I will not go. That denies a past event. Future negation is যাব না in the first person.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I will read the book.
2. Say: I will not drink tea.
3. Say: He or she will work. (respectful)
4. Say: I will go home.
5. Say: We will work tomorrow.
6. Say: Will you come here? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `আমি বইটা পড়ব।` - `Ami boiṭa poṛbo.`
2. `আমি চা খাব না।` - `Ami cha khabo na.`
3. `তিনি কাজ করবেন।` - `Tini kaj korben.`
4. `আমি বাড়ি যাব।` - `Ami baṛi jabo.`
5. `আমরা আগামীকাল কাজ করব।` - `Amra agamikal kaj korbo.`
6. `আপনি কি এখানে আসবেন?` - `Apni ki ekhane ashben?`

### Why these answers work

Future answers select the correct stem and person ending, then place না after the verb when needed. Tomorrow is a separate time phrase. The last two answers transfer the plan pattern to a group and turn a future statement into a respectful yes-or-no question.

### Retrieve and recombine

Plan tomorrow with three short sentences: one destination, one activity, and one thing you will not do. Ask a listener whether they will come too.


## Lesson 20: Say what you can and want to do

### Outcome

Combine an infinitive with a conjugated ability or desire verb.

### Think before reading

In “I can read,” which Bengali verb should carry the first-person ending?

### Build the pattern

The linking form পড়তে (poṛte) means to read in this construction. আমি পড়তে পারি (ami poṛte pari) means I can read. The ability verb পারি carries the first-person form; the preceding পড়তে does not also receive a first-person ending. Respectful পারেন (paren) gives আপনি পড়তে পারেন.

Other useful linking forms include করতে (korte), to do; যেতে (jete), to go; আসতে (ashte), to come; and বলতে (bolte), to speak or say. The order is the activity first and the ability verb last. আমি বাংলা বলতে পারি means I can speak Bengali. It is a statement of ability, not a claim that any level of proficiency has been certified.

চাই (chai), I want, combines similarly with an activity: আমি বাড়ি যেতে চাই, I want to go home. Respectful চান (chan) is the corresponding finite form. With a noun directly, আমি চা চাই means I want tea. The presence or absence of the infinitive reflects whether the desired thing is an action or an object.

Negation applies to the final finite verb: পারি না, cannot; চাই না, do not want. A learner can now explain a communication limit with আমি বাংলা বলতে পারি না, though a more nuanced statement about speaking a little is often useful in actual conversation.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি বাংলা বলতে পারি। | Ami Bangla bolte pari. | I can speak Bengali. |
| আপনি বাংলা বলতে পারেন। | Apni Bangla bolte paren. | You can speak Bengali. (respectful) |
| আমি বইটা পড়তে পারি। | Ami boiṭa poṛte pari. | I can read the book. |
| আমি আসতে পারি না। | Ami ashte pari na. | I cannot come. |
| আমি বাড়ি যেতে চাই। | Ami baṛi jete chai. | I want to go home. |
| আমি চা চাই। | Ami cha chai. | I want tea. |

### Common trap

Do not conjugate both verbs as separate first-person predicates. পড়তে পারি is one ability construction; পড়ি পারি is not its ordinary form.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I can read the book.
2. Say: I cannot come.
3. Say: I want to go home.
4. Say: I want tea.
5. Say: We can work.
6. Say: I want to go to school.

***Answers:***

1. `আমি বইটা পড়তে পারি।` - `Ami boiṭa poṛte pari.`
2. `আমি আসতে পারি না।` - `Ami ashte pari na.`
3. `আমি বাড়ি যেতে চাই।` - `Ami baṛi jete chai.`
4. `আমি চা চাই।` - `Ami cha chai.`
5. `আমরা কাজ করতে পারি।` - `Amra kaj korte pari.`
6. `আমি স্কুলে যেতে চাই।` - `Ami skule jete chai.`

### Why these answers work

The action link ends in -তে while পারি or চাই carries the person form. Respectful পারেন retains the same nonfinite first verb. The final two answers transfer the ability frame to a group and the desire frame to a new destination.

### Retrieve and recombine

State something you can do, something you cannot do, and something you want to do tomorrow. Identify the last verb as ability or desire each time.


## Lesson 21: Explain needs and preferences

### Outcome

Express needing an object and liking something without forcing an English subject pattern.

### Think before reading

If আমার means my, why does it appear where English says “I” in a sentence about liking tea?

### Build the pattern

Bengali often presents an experience through a genitive experiencer. আমার চা ভালো লাগে (amar cha bhalo lage) means I like tea. The structure is approximately tea feels good to me. The finite verb লাগে belongs to the experience construction; it is not replaced with the first-person লাগি simply because English translates the experiencer as I.

The respectful listener appears as আপনার: আপনার চা ভালো লাগে. A yes-or-no question can insert কি: আপনার কি চা ভালো লাগে. The pattern is useful for food, books, and activities once their appropriate noun or nonfinite forms are known. This lesson stays with concrete things so the experiencer structure remains clear.

আমার জল দরকার (amar jol dorkar) means I need water. দরকার expresses a need with the genitive person and required item. আমার একটা কলম দরকার is a practical classroom sentence. No English-style have or do auxiliary is needed. দরকার নেই expresses lack of need: আমার চা দরকার নেই, I do not need tea.

Do not equate liking, wanting, and needing. ভালো লাগে reports a favorable experience; চাই expresses desire; দরকার states a requirement. A person can like tea without wanting any now, or need a book without enjoying it. Selecting the correct construction begins with this meaning difference.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমার চা ভালো লাগে। | Amar cha bhalo lage. | I like tea. |
| আপনার কি চা ভালো লাগে? | Apnar ki cha bhalo lage? | Do you like tea? (respectful) |
| আমার জল দরকার। | Amar jol dorkar. | I need water. |
| আমার একটা কলম দরকার। | Amar ekṭa kolom dorkar. | I need a pen. |
| আমার চা দরকার নেই। | Amar cha dorkar nei. | I do not need tea. |
| আমার বইটা ভালো লাগে। | Amar boiṭa bhalo lage. | I like the book. |

### Common trap

Do not replace আমার with আমি in the model আমার চা ভালো লাগে. The experiencer form is a structural part of this Bengali expression, not an optional stylistic flourish.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I need water.
2. Say: I need a pen.
3. Say: I do not need tea.
4. Say: I like the book.
5. Say: You need the book. (respectful)
6. Say: We need water.

***Answers:***

1. `আমার জল দরকার।` - `Amar jol dorkar.`
2. `আমার একটা কলম দরকার।` - `Amar ekṭa kolom dorkar.`
3. `আমার চা দরকার নেই।` - `Amar cha dorkar nei.`
4. `আমার বইটা ভালো লাগে।` - `Amar boiṭa bhalo lage.`
5. `আপনার বইটা দরকার।` - `Apnar boiṭa dorkar.`
6. `আমাদের জল দরকার।` - `Amader jol dorkar.`

### Why these answers work

The genitive person starts the liking and need patterns. ভালো লাগে remains unchanged for first-person and respectful experiencers in these examples. The new book and group-water answers transfer the construction while preserving the difference between enjoyment and requirement.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine planning refreshments for a meeting. State what you like, what you want now, and what the group needs. Use three distinct constructions rather than translating all three with চাই.


## Lesson 22: Connect reasons and contrasts

### Outcome

Join two short clauses with and, but, or because while preserving each clause’s grammar.

### Think before reading

If a sentence contains two clauses, does only the very last verb need its person ending?

### Build the pattern

Each finite clause keeps its own grammatical shape. আমি পড়ি আর তিনি লেখেন joins I read and he or she writes respectfully. আর (ar) is an ordinary connector meaning and or and also in this use. It also has other meanings, including more, which context distinguishes. Do not assume every occurrence is a clause connector.

কিন্তু (kintu) means but. আমার চা ভালো লাগে, কিন্তু আমি এখন চা চাই না contrasts a general preference with a current desire.

কারণ (karon) introduces a reason. আমি যাব না, কারণ আমার কাজ আছে means I will not go because I have work. The first clause is a future negative; the reason is a present possession or availability statement.

লিখি (likhi), I write, and লেখেন (lekhen), respectful write, add one new common action. Notice the stem vowel difference rather than inventing লিখেন from the first-person form.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি পড়ি আর তিনি লেখেন। | Ami poṛi ar tini lekhen. | I read and he or she writes. (respectful) |
| আমি যাব না, কারণ আমার কাজ আছে। | Ami jabo na, karon amar kaj achhe. | I will not go because I have work. |
| আমি চা চাই, কিন্তু জল চাই না। | Ami cha chai, kintu jol chai na. | I want tea but do not want water. |
| আমি লিখি আর আপনি পড়েন। | Ami likhi ar apni poṛen. | I write and you read. (respectful) |
| আমি আসতে পারি না, কারণ আমার কাজ আছে। | Ami ashte pari na, karon amar kaj achhe. | I cannot come because I have work. |
| আমি বইটা চাই, কিন্তু আমার টাকা নেই। | Ami boiṭa chai, kintu amar ṭaka nei. | I want the book but I have no money. |

### Common trap

Do not flatten the genitive experiencer into an ordinary nominative subject after a connector. আমার চা ভালো লাগে remains its own construction even beside আমি চাই না.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I want tea but do not want water.
2. Say: I write and you read. (respectful)
3. Say: I cannot come because I have work.
4. Say: I want the book but I have no money.
5. Say: I have a book but no pen.
6. Say: I will read and you will write. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `আমি চা চাই, কিন্তু জল চাই না।` - `Ami cha chai, kintu jol chai na.`
2. `আমি লিখি আর আপনি পড়েন।` - `Ami likhi ar apni poṛen.`
3. `আমি আসতে পারি না, কারণ আমার কাজ আছে।` - `Ami ashte pari na, karon amar kaj achhe.`
4. `আমি বইটা চাই, কিন্তু আমার টাকা নেই।` - `Ami boiṭa chai, kintu amar ṭaka nei.`
5. `আমার বই আছে, কিন্তু কলম নেই।` - `Amar boi achhe, kintu kolom nei.`
6. `আমি পড়ব আর আপনি লিখবেন।` - `Ami poṛbo ar apni likhben.`

### Why these answers work

Each answer keeps a complete verb or predicate in each clause. কারণ marks the explanatory clause; কিন্তু marks a contrast rather than a cause. The last two answers transfer these connectors to availability and different planned actions while keeping each clause independently interpretable.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give a plan and a reason for it. Then give a preference and a contrasting present choice. Remove each connector mentally and check that the remaining pieces are grammatical short sentences.


## Lesson 23: Link actions in sequence and conditions

### Outcome

Connect a prior action with a later one and build a simple if-clause.

### Think before reading

Compare করে and করলে. Which one presents an action as completed before another action, and which sets a condition?

### Build the pattern

The linking form করে (kore) can mean having done or after doing in a sequence. আমি কাজ করে বাড়ি যাব means I will go home after doing the work. The final finite verb যাব carries the future and first-person information for the overall sequence. The earlier action has a nonfinite linking form.

Other useful sequence forms are খেয়ে (kheye), after eating or drinking, পড়ে (poṛe), after reading, and গিয়ে (giye), after going. They are not formed by leaving a complete first-person past verb in place. In আমি চা খেয়ে কাজ করব, the same speaker first drinks tea and then works.

A simple conditional can use যদি (jodi, if) and a conditional verb form such as করেন in an explicit respectful finite condition or করলে in a more compact construction. This lesson uses যদি আপনি আসেন, আমি চা করব: if you come, I will make tea. The respectful present form আসেন sits inside the condition; the main clause has the future করব.

Conditions are possibilities, not claims that the event has already happened. Do not confuse “after drinking tea” with “if you drink tea.” The linking relationship contributes meaning even when both sentences contain the same two activities.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমি চা খেয়ে কাজ করব। | Ami cha kheye kaj korbo. | I will work after drinking tea. |
| আমি কাজ করে বাড়ি যাব। | Ami kaj kore baṛi jabo. | I will go home after doing the work. |
| আমি বইটা পড়ে আসব। | Ami boiṭa poṛe ashbo. | I will come after reading the book. |
| যদি আপনি আসেন, আমি চা করব। | Jodi apni ashen, ami cha korbo. | If you come, I will make tea. (respectful) |
| আমি বাড়ি গিয়ে পড়ব। | Ami baṛi giye poṛbo. | I will read after going home. |
| যদি আপনি চান, আমি আসব। | Jodi apni chan, ami ashbo. | If you want, I will come. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not assume a sequence form such as খেয়ে independently tells you past time. In a future sentence the drinking is still future, simply earlier than the later action.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I will come after reading the book.
2. Say: If you come, I will make tea. (respectful)
3. Say: I will read after going home.
4. Say: If you want, I will come. (respectful)
5. Say: I will read the book after drinking tea.
6. Say: I will drink tea after doing the work.

***Answers:***

1. `আমি বইটা পড়ে আসব।` - `Ami boiṭa poṛe ashbo.`
2. `যদি আপনি আসেন, আমি চা করব।` - `Jodi apni ashen, ami cha korbo.`
3. `আমি বাড়ি গিয়ে পড়ব।` - `Ami baṛi giye poṛbo.`
4. `যদি আপনি চান, আমি আসব।` - `Jodi apni chan, ami ashbo.`
5. `আমি চা খেয়ে বইটা পড়ব।` - `Ami cha kheye boiṭa poṛbo.`
6. `আমি কাজ করে চা খাব।` - `Ami kaj kore cha khabo.`

### Why these answers work

The sequence answers put a nonfinite action before the final future verb. The conditional answers use যদি with a full respectful clause and then the consequence. The new book and work combinations demonstrate that chronological order and conditional dependence are different relations.

### Retrieve and recombine

Plan two activities in order, then turn the first into a condition for the second. Explain whether your new sentence promises an order or only describes a possible consequence.


## Lesson 24: Manage a small purchase and a misunderstanding

### Outcome

Combine price questions, quantity, requests, and a request for repetition.

### Think before reading

At a shop, what information is still missing after you have named the object you want?

### Build the pattern

দাম (dam) means price, and কত (koto) asks how much or how many depending on what follows. বইটার দাম কত asks the price of the particular book. The genitive ending appears after the classifier: বইটা plus র. The sentence has the familiar no-copula present question structure, with কত in the missing-value position.

পঞ্চাশ টাকা (ponchash ṭaka) is fifty taka or rupees according to the local currency context. This course does not infer a currency system from the word alone; a real purchase supplies that context. দাম পঞ্চাশ টাকা is a complete price statement. Learn the number as a useful chunk here, while recognising that a full number system needs more listening and practice than a single lesson can provide.

আমাকে দুটো কলম দিন combines a recipient, counted object phrase, and respectful imperative. You can also state desire with আমি বইটা চাই. The choice between a direct request and a desire statement depends on the exchange, but both keep the object before the final verb.

Communication repair is part of practical language. আবার বলুন means say it again. একটু আস্তে বলুন means speak a little slowly. বুঝতে পারছি না means I am not managing to understand at the moment; the progressive ability form পারছি contrasts with a general inability পারি না.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| বইটার দাম কত? | Boiṭar dam koto? | What is the price of the book? |
| দাম পঞ্চাশ টাকা। | Dam ponchash ṭaka. | The price is fifty currency units. |
| আমাকে দুটো কলম দিন। | Amake duṭo kolom din. | Give me two pens. (respectful) |
| আমি বইটা চাই। | Ami boiṭa chai. | I want the book. |
| আমি বুঝতে পারছি না। | Ami bujhte parchhi na. | I am not understanding. |
| আবার বলুন। | Abar bolun. | Say it again. (respectful) |

### Common trap

A price sentence is not a promise that prices or currencies are universal. In conversation, establish the local amount and unit. Linguistically, keep বইটার as the price owner rather than dropping its genitive ending.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Give me two pens. (respectful)
2. Say: I want the book.
3. Say: I am not understanding.
4. Say: Say it again. (respectful)
5. Say: Speak a little slowly. (respectful)
6. Say: What is the price of the pen?

***Answers:***

1. `আমাকে দুটো কলম দিন।` - `Amake duṭo kolom din.`
2. `আমি বইটা চাই।` - `Ami boiṭa chai.`
3. `আমি বুঝতে পারছি না।` - `Ami bujhte parchhi na.`
4. `আবার বলুন।` - `Abar bolun.`
5. `একটু আস্তে বলুন।` - `Ekṭu aste bolun.`
6. `কলমটার দাম কত?` - `Kolomṭar dam koto?`

### Why these answers work

The price question asks for a value using কত. The purchase request carries the count before the noun and দিন at the end. The two repair requests extend familiar imperative grammar to a new conversational need, allowing the learner to continue an exchange rather than guess.

### Retrieve and recombine

Role-play a book purchase: ask the price, request the item, then ask the seller to repeat something. Swap the book for two pens and rebuild the request.


## Lesson 25: Build a connected personal account

### Outcome

Combine identity, place, routine, completed events, and a future plan in a short conversation.

### Think before reading

Before translating a whole paragraph, can you decide which clauses are identities, locations, actions, or experiences?

### Build the pattern

A connected account is built from distinct constructions, not from one universal equivalent of English be. Introduce yourself with the no-copula identity frame. State a location with আছি. Describe a routine with a simple present action. Report a completed event with a past or perfect form suited to the context. End with a future plan. Each sentence answers a different question about the speaker.

Consider the progression “My name is Rina; I am at home; I read every morning; I have read this book; tomorrow I will work.” The Bengali patterns already exist in earlier lessons. The new skill is selecting among them while maintaining a coherent topic.  Pronouns can be omitted where context makes them recoverable, but the practice keeps them visible so that the person choices remain inspectable.

Respect remains consistent across a dialogue. When you turn from describing yourself to asking the listener about a plan, use আপনি with the respectful future form. When mentioning that person's need, switch to আপনার because the construction requires a genitive experiencer or owner. This is a grammatical change without a change of social respect.

Do not treat this review as a proficiency certificate. Its purpose is to make a manageable set of sentence patterns retrievable and recombinable. Fluent conversation requires listening to real speakers, feedback on pronunciation, and broader vocabulary beyond this draft foundation.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আমার নাম রিনা। | Amar nam Rina. | My name is Rina. |
| আমি বাড়িতে আছি। | Ami baṛite achhi. | I am at home. |
| আমি প্রতিদিন সকালে পড়ি। | Ami protidin shokale poṛi. | I read every morning. |
| আমি বইটা পড়েছি। | Ami boiṭa poṛechhi. | I have read the book. |
| আমি আগামীকাল কাজ করব। | Ami agamikal kaj korbo. | I will work tomorrow. |
| আপনি আগামীকাল কী করবেন? | Apni agamikal ki korben? | What will you do tomorrow? (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not preserve one pronoun form merely for visual consistency across a paragraph. আমি is right for an ordinary first-person action, while আমার is needed in the possession, need, and liking frames taught here.

### Practice

1. Say: I read every morning.
2. Say: I have read the book.
3. Say: I will work tomorrow.

5. Say: I need a new book.
6. Say: I will work after reading the book.

***Answers:***

1. `আমি প্রতিদিন সকালে পড়ি।` - `Ami protidin shokale poṛi.`
2. `আমি বইটা পড়েছি।` - `Ami boiṭa poṛechhi.`

### Why these answers work

The review answers intentionally mix constructions: genitive naming, first-person location, routine, perfect, future, and respectful question. The last two prompts transfer earlier need and sequence patterns to a small study plan. Their grammar follows the meaning of each clause rather than the surface wording of English.
