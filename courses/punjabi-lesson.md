# Punjabi: A pattern-first foundation

Editorial status: original AI-assisted draft; fluent-speaker review pending.

This course teaches broadly standard Eastern Punjabi, associated with the Majhi-based written standard, in Gurmukhi. Punjabi is also written in Shahmukhi, especially in Pakistan. Neither script identifies every speaker's religion or every feature of a regional variety. Malwai, Doabi, Pothohari and other speech traditions have their own vocabulary and grammatical patterns. The examples here form one consistent beginner model, not a judgement about other Punjabi.

Romanisation uses ā, ī, ū for long vowels, ṭ, ḍ, ṛ and ṇ for retroflex sounds, and a combining tilde to mark nasalised vowels. Double consonants indicate a held consonant. Spelling-based gh, jh, ḍh, dh and bh must not automatically be pronounced as Hindi voiced aspirates: Punjabi tone often accompanies these spellings. The romanisation is a reading aid, not a complete phonetic transcription. Listen to a fluent speaker for tone, vowel quality and connected speech.

### References and review notes

Punjabi University's [learning resource portal](https://www.learnpunjabi.org/) links teaching materials and dictionaries. Its hosted [A Reference Grammar of Punjabi](https://pt.learnpunjabi.org/assets/A%20Reference%20Grammar_Final.pdf) was consulted for tonal contrasts and the relationship between spelling and pronunciation. The linked [A Start in Punjabi](https://pt.learnpunjabi.org/assets/A%20START%20IN%20PUNJABI_web.pdf) provides further guided study. Explanations and exercises below are original. A fluent reviewer should especially assess regional naturalness, vowel transcription and the choice of standard versus conversational auxiliaries.

## Lesson 1: Introduce yourself with a complete sentence

### Outcome and thinking point

Introduce yourself and identify another person without first learning a whole alphabet. If English changes “I am” to “she is”, might Punjabi also change its small linking word? Predict which part should change when a sentence moves from yourself to somebody else.

Punjabi usually places the linking word at the end. `ਮੈਂ ਵਿਦਿਆਰਥੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ vidiārthī hā̃.` means “I am a student.” The sentence contains a person, an identity, and a form of “be”. There is no separate word corresponding to English “a” in this neutral identification. `ਉਹ ਵਿਦਿਆਰਥੀ ਹੈ।` - `Uh vidiārthī hai.` means “He or she is a student.” The pronoun itself does not distinguish he from she.

### Useful building blocks

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਮੈਂ | maĩ | I |
| ਤੁਸੀਂ | tusī̃ | you, respectful or plural |
| ਉਹ | uh | that person, he, she |
| ਵਿਦਿਆਰਥੀ | vidiārthī | student |
| ਅਧਿਆਪਕ | adhiāpak | teacher, masculine form |
| ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ | khush | happy |
| ਹਾਂ | hā̃ | am, are with I or we |
| ਹੈ | hai | is |
| ਹੋ | ho | are with ਤੁਸੀਂ |

### Work through the pattern

`ਮੈਂ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ khush hā̃.` means “I am happy.” Here the middle element describes a condition rather than an occupation. The frame still works. `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ ਹੋ।` - `Tusī̃ khush ho.` addresses somebody respectfully. It also works when speaking to several people. Number and respect overlap in this pronoun, so do not translate every occurrence as “you all”.

The nasal mark in `ਹਾਂ` matters. Air also passes through the nose during the vowel; do not add a strongly pronounced English n afterwards. Gurmukhi is read from left to right. A written vowel sign can appear before its consonant while being read after it. In `ਵਿ`, for example, the short i sign stands to the left of the consonant. Read the syllable as vi, not iv.

### Common trap

Do not attach an English “am” after a Punjabi linking word, and do not use `ਹੈ` for every person. Keep `ਮੈਂ ... ਹਾਂ` and `ਤੁਸੀਂ ... ਹੋ` as meaningful pairs. These are patterns you can rebuild, not separate phrases for every adjective.

### Practice

Build one Punjabi sentence for each prompt.

1. I am a student.
2. I am happy.
3. You are happy. (respectful)
4. That person is a student.

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੈਂ ਵਿਦਿਆਰਥੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ vidiārthī hā̃.`
2. `ਮੈਂ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ khush hā̃.`
3. `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ ਹੋ।` - `Tusī̃ khush ho.`
4. `ਉਹ ਵਿਦਿਆਰਥੀ ਹੈ।` - `Uh vidiārthī hai.`

### Why these answers work

The first two answers preserve the speaker and linking word while changing the description. The third changes both the pronoun and its associated linking word. The fourth uses a pronoun whose gender comes from context. English articles do not need separate translations here.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine meeting somebody at a class. Introduce yourself, then describe yourself as happy. Point to another student and identify that person. Before looking back, explain why your first and last sentences end differently.

## Lesson 2: This, that and a respectful question

### Outcome and thinking point

Ask what something is and identify nearby objects. Suppose the unknown part is the identity rather than the person. Would you need to turn every word around as English does in “What is this?” Try leaving the pointing word first and the linking word last.

`ਇਹ ਕੀ ਹੈ?` - `Ih kī hai?` means “What is this?” The question word occupies the descriptive slot. `ਇਹ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਹੈ।` - `Ih kitāb hai.` means “This is a book.” Replacing the unknown identity with a noun gives the answer. This substitution is the central reasoning move of the lesson.

### Pointing and identifying

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਇਹ | ih | this, this person |
| ਉਹ | uh | that, that person |
| ਕੀ | kī | what; yes-no question marker |
| ਕਿਤਾਬ | kitāb | book |
| ਕਲਮ | kalam | pen |
| ਪਾਣੀ | pāṇī | water |
| ਚਾਹ | chāh | tea |
| ਕੌਣ | kauṇ | who |

### Two sorts of question

`ਉਹ ਕੌਣ ਹੈ?` - `Uh kauṇ hai?` asks “Who is that?” Choose `ਕੌਣ` for a person's identity and `ਕੀ` for the identity of a thing. `ਉਹ ਅਧਿਆਪਕ ਹੈ।` - `Uh adhiāpak hai.` answers “He is a teacher.” A respectful reference to a teacher may require plural agreement in fuller conversation; this sentence uses ordinary singular agreement to focus on identification.

For a yes-no question, place `ਕੀ` before a complete statement: `ਕੀ ਇਹ ਚਾਹ ਹੈ?` - `Kī ih chāh hai?` means “Is this tea?” The same spelling therefore has two jobs. In the middle of the identification pattern it asks what. Before the whole statement it can ask whether that statement is true. In conversation intonation alone also makes yes-no questions, but the explicit marker helps a beginner read them.

`ਕੀ ਤੁਸੀਂ ਵਿਦਿਆਰਥੀ ਹੋ?` - `Kī tusī̃ vidiārthī ho?` respectfully asks whether the listener is a student. The question marker does not cancel the agreement learned earlier. Its final word remains `ਹੋ` because the subject is `ਤੁਸੀਂ`.

### Common trap

Do not interpret every `ਕੀ` as English “what”. Decide whether the speaker is asking for a missing identity or checking a whole proposition. Also avoid reading `ਪਾਣੀ` with the same n as English “no”: `ਣ` represents a retroflex nasal.

### Practice

Write the requested question or statement.

1. What is this?
2. This is water.
3. Is that tea?
4. Who is that?

***Answers:***

1. `ਇਹ ਕੀ ਹੈ?` - `Ih kī hai?`
2. `ਇਹ ਪਾਣੀ ਹੈ।` - `Ih pāṇī hai.`
3. `ਕੀ ਉਹ ਚਾਹ ਹੈ?` - `Kī uh chāh hai?`
4. `ਉਹ ਕੌਣ ਹੈ?` - `Uh kauṇ hai?`

### Why these answers work

The first asks for an identity; the third checks an identification. Both use `ਕੀ`, but its position and the rest of the sentence establish the question type. Water needs no indefinite article. The final answer selects the person question word rather than the thing question word.

### Retrieve and recombine

At a café, point to two containers. Ask what one contains and ask whether the other contains water. Give an identification in reply. Then move the same question pattern to an unfamiliar object on a classroom desk.

## Lesson 3: Negate a claim and give a short answer

### Outcome and thinking point

Correct an identification without rebuilding the entire sentence. In “This is not tea”, English inserts a negative next to its verb. Where would a negative fit if Punjabi keeps the linking word at the end? Try placing it immediately before that word.

`ਇਹ ਚਾਹ ਨਹੀਂ ਹੈ।` - `Ih chāh nahī̃ hai.` means “This is not tea.” The description stays where it was, and `ਨਹੀਂ` negates the claim. In everyday speech the linking word is often omitted in negative clauses, but this lesson retains it to make the sentence structure visible.

### A small correction toolkit

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਨਹੀਂ | nahī̃ | not, no |
| ਹਾਂ | hā̃ | yes; also am in context |
| ਜੀ | jī | respectful particle |
| ਠੀਕ | ṭhīk | fine, correct |
| ਬਿਮਾਰ | bimār | ill |
| ਤਿਆਰ | tiār | ready |
| ਦੁੱਧ | duddh | milk |
| ਚਾਹ | chāh | tea |

### Work through a correction

`ਕੀ ਤੁਸੀਂ ਤਿਆਰ ਹੋ?` - `Kī tusī̃ tiār ho?` asks “Are you ready?” `ਮੈਂ ਤਿਆਰ ਨਹੀਂ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ tiār nahī̃ hā̃.` answers “I am not ready.” Notice the two similar-looking words at the end. `ਨਹੀਂ` carries negation; `ਹਾਂ` still agrees with the speaker. The negative does not replace agreement.

`ਹਾਂ ਜੀ।` - `Hā̃ jī.` is a respectful “Yes.” `ਨਹੀਂ ਜੀ।` - `Nahī̃ jī.` is a respectful “No.” These short replies are complete conversational turns even though they are not full descriptive sentences. Their interpretation depends on the question. `ਹਾਂ` can therefore be either an independent affirmative reply or the linking word after `ਮੈਂ`. Read its function in context.

Now correct a mistaken drink: `ਇਹ ਦੁੱਧ ਹੈ।` - `Ih duddh hai.` means “This is milk.” If somebody called it tea, a full correction can first reject that claim and then supply the right noun. You already possess both pieces. In `ਦੁੱਧ`, the doubled consonant and the tonal behaviour associated with the spelling need listening practice; a plain English “dud” is only a rough visual resemblance, not a pronunciation model.

### Common trap

A word-for-word English question can invite an English-style answer that is too vague. When correcting a negative question, repeat the positive or negative statement if clarity matters. A complete `ਮੈਂ ਤਿਆਰ ਹਾਂ` or `ਮੈਂ ਤਿਆਰ ਨਹੀਂ ਹਾਂ` avoids uncertainty about what “yes” accepts.

### Practice

Make the correction explicit.

1. This is not tea.
2. I am not ill.
3. You are ready. (respectful)
4. Give a respectful short no.

***Answers:***

1. `ਇਹ ਚਾਹ ਨਹੀਂ ਹੈ।` - `Ih chāh nahī̃ hai.`
2. `ਮੈਂ ਬਿਮਾਰ ਨਹੀਂ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ bimār nahī̃ hā̃.`
3. `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਤਿਆਰ ਹੋ।` - `Tusī̃ tiār ho.`
4. `ਨਹੀਂ ਜੀ।` - `Nahī̃ jī.`

### Why these answers work

Negation precedes the linking word in the first two answers. The affirmative third answer has no negative particle. The fourth is a polite conversational fragment; `ਜੀ` adds respect but does not itself mean no. A shorter negative without the final copula is common in speech, though the key shows the full teaching form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Pretend a friend mistakes milk for water. Deny the mistaken identification, supply the correct one, and ask whether the friend is ready. Keep the object correction separate from the question about the person.

## Lesson 4: Describe one thing and several things

### Outcome and thinking point

Describe objects while noticing gender and number. If `ਚੰਗਾ` describes a masculine singular noun, should its ending stay the same beside a feminine noun? Predict from the contrasting forms in the table before reading the explanation.

Punjabi nouns have grammatical gender. Some adjectives change to match it; others remain unchanged. This does not imply that objects have human gender. Learn a noun together with a useful describing phrase, so the grammatical class has something audible attached to it.

### Descriptions in pairs

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਚੰਗਾ ਕਮਰਾ | changā kamrā | a good room |
| ਚੰਗੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ | changī kitāb | a good book |
| ਵੱਡਾ ਕਮਰਾ | vaḍḍā kamrā | a large room |
| ਵੱਡੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ | vaḍḍī kitāb | a large book |
| ਛੋਟਾ | chhoṭā | small, masculine singular |
| ਛੋਟੀ | chhoṭī | small, feminine singular |
| ਨਵਾਂ | navā̃ | new, masculine singular |
| ਨਵੀਂ | navī̃ | new, feminine singular |
| ਸੁੰਦਰ | sundar | beautiful, unchanged |

### Build the contrast

`ਇਹ ਕਮਰਾ ਵੱਡਾ ਹੈ।` - `Ih kamrā vaḍḍā hai.` means “This room is large.” `ਇਹ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਵੱਡੀ ਹੈ।` - `Ih kitāb vaḍḍī hai.` means “This book is large.” The adjective follows the noun when it is the main description of the sentence. Inside a noun phrase it normally precedes it: `ਵੱਡੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ` is “large book”.

`ਇਹ ਕਮਰੇ ਵੱਡੇ ਹਨ।` - `Ih kamre vaḍḍe han.` means “These rooms are large.” Here the regular masculine noun ending changes from `-ā` to `-e`, and the adjective does the same. The plural linking word is `ਹਨ` in the written standard used here. Conversational Punjabi also uses `ਨੇ` in many third-person plural contexts; recognise it without mixing the two models unpredictably.

`ਇਹ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਵੱਡੀਆਂ ਹਨ।` - `Ih kitābā̃ vaḍḍīā̃ han.` means “These books are large.” This feminine plural has nasal endings. Do not extend the room pattern mechanically to every noun. `ਸੁੰਦਰ` stays the same in a beautiful room or a beautiful book, so an adjective's final appearance matters.

### Common trap

The sex of the speaker does not determine an object's adjective. A woman and a man both say `ਵੱਡਾ ਕਮਰਾ` because the noun is masculine. Agreement follows the described noun, not whoever happens to pronounce the sentence.

### Practice

Use the stated noun and number.

1. This room is small.
2. This book is new.
3. These rooms are large.
4. This book is beautiful.

***Answers:***

1. `ਇਹ ਕਮਰਾ ਛੋਟਾ ਹੈ।` - `Ih kamrā chhoṭā hai.`
2. `ਇਹ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਨਵੀਂ ਹੈ।` - `Ih kitāb navī̃ hai.`
3. `ਇਹ ਕਮਰੇ ਵੱਡੇ ਹਨ।` - `Ih kamre vaḍḍe han.`
4. `ਇਹ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਸੁੰਦਰ ਹੈ।` - `Ih kitāb sundar hai.`

### Why these answers work

Room selects the masculine form and book selects the feminine form where the adjective changes. Plural rooms require both plural noun and plural agreement. Beautiful belongs to the unchanged adjective group; no invented feminine ending is necessary.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe one room in a school and two rooms in a hotel. Then describe a book. Explain which changes follow number, which follow gender, and which words have stayed constant throughout your three descriptions.

## Lesson 5: Whose book is this?

### Outcome and thinking point

Identify ownership with an agreeing possessive. If a woman owns a room and a book, will Punjabi “my” have one feminine form in both phrases? Use the last lesson's principle: ask which noun the word describes.

`ਮੇਰਾ ਕਮਰਾ` - `merā kamrā` means “my room”; `ਮੇਰੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ` - `merī kitāb` means “my book”. The possessive agrees with the possessed thing, not with the owner's sex. This makes ownership part of the same agreement system as ordinary changing adjectives.

### Possessive toolkit

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਮੇਰਾ | merā | my, masculine singular |
| ਮੇਰੀ | merī | my, feminine singular |
| ਮੇਰੇ | mere | my, masculine plural or oblique |
| ਤੁਹਾਡਾ | tuhāḍā | your, masculine singular, respectful |
| ਤੁਹਾਡੀ | tuhāḍī | your, feminine singular, respectful |
| ਸਾਡਾ | sāḍā | our, masculine singular |
| ਕਿਸ ਦਾ | kis dā | whose, masculine singular |
| ਨਾਮ | nām | name, masculine |

### Connect a person to a possession

`ਮੇਰਾ ਨਾਮ ਅਮਨ ਹੈ।` - `Merā nām Aman hai.` means “My name is Aman.” This is a particularly useful introduction because the name noun determines the possessive form, regardless of who Aman is. `ਤੁਹਾਡਾ ਨਾਮ ਕੀ ਹੈ?` - `Tuhāḍā nām kī hai?` respectfully asks “What is your name?” The question asks about a name, so the final linking word is singular `ਹੈ`; it does not agree with the respectful listener hidden inside “your”.

`ਇਹ ਅਮਨ ਦੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਹੈ।` - `Ih Aman dī kitāb hai.` means “This is Aman's book.” With an ordinary name, place a linking possessive `ਦਾ`, `ਦੀ`, `ਦੇ` or `ਦੀਆਂ` after the owner. `ਅਮਨ ਦਾ ਕਮਰਾ` - `Aman dā kamrā` gives the corresponding room phrase. The link changes with what is owned.

`ਇਹ ਕਿਸ ਦੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਹੈ?` - `Ih kis dī kitāb hai?` asks “Whose book is this?” Work backwards from `ਕਿਤਾਬ` to choose `ਦੀ`. The owner is unknown, but the gender of the known possession is enough to select the form.

### Common trap

Do not make the final copula agree with the owner. “Your name” is one name; “your books” are several books. The distinction between the possessor inside the noun phrase and the head noun of that phrase becomes essential in longer sentences.

### Practice

Build the ownership expression into a sentence.

1. My name is Aman.
2. This is my book.
3. What is your name? (respectful)
4. This is Aman's room.

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੇਰਾ ਨਾਮ ਅਮਨ ਹੈ।` - `Merā nām Aman hai.`
2. `ਇਹ ਮੇਰੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਹੈ।` - `Ih merī kitāb hai.`
3. `ਤੁਹਾਡਾ ਨਾਮ ਕੀ ਹੈ?` - `Tuhāḍā nām kī hai?`
4. `ਇਹ ਅਮਨ ਦਾ ਕਮਰਾ ਹੈ।` - `Ih Aman dā kamrā hai.`

### Why these answers work

Name and room are masculine, while book is feminine. The respectful possessive remains respectful even though its phrase takes a singular copula. The final example expresses the owner with a name and a separate agreeing link rather than a first-person possessive.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce yourself by name, identify your room, and ask which book belongs to another person. Then replace the owner with Aman. Check ownership agreement before choosing the sentence's final linking word.

## Lesson 6: Put people and things somewhere

### Outcome and thinking point

Say where a person or object is. English puts “in” before a place. If Punjabi uses a word after the place, where should “in the room” finish? Predict the whole location phrase before adding “is”.

`ਕਮਰੇ ਵਿੱਚ` - `kamre vich` means “in the room”. Punjabi commonly uses postpositions, words after their noun phrase. Some nouns change form before them. Our masculine `ਕਮਰਾ` becomes `ਕਮਰੇ`. This is the oblique form, a form prepared for grammatical relationships, not automatically a plural.

### Locations

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਵਿੱਚ | vich | in |
| ਉੱਤੇ | utte | on |
| ਹੇਠਾਂ | heṭhā̃ | below |
| ਇੱਥੇ | itthe | here |
| ਉੱਥੇ | utthe | there |
| ਕਿੱਥੇ | kitthe | where |
| ਮੇਜ਼ | mez | table |
| ਘਰ | ghar | home, house |
| ਸਕੂਲ | sakūl | school |

### Follow the noun phrase

`ਕਿਤਾਬ ਮੇਜ਼ ਉੱਤੇ ਹੈ।` - `Kitāb mez utte hai.` means “The book is on the table.” The sentence begins with what we are locating and ends with the copula. Between them sits the whole place phrase. English “the” is supplied by context; it is not missing Punjabi vocabulary.

`ਅਮਨ ਕਮਰੇ ਵਿੱਚ ਹੈ।` - `Aman kamre vich hai.` means “Aman is in the room.” `ਮੇਰੇ ਕਮਰੇ ਵਿੱਚ` - `mere kamre vich` means “in my room”. The possessive also takes its oblique masculine form. A useful strategy is to build the phrase from the noun outward: room becomes oblique, its agreeing modifier follows, and the postposition closes the phrase.

`ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿੱਥੇ ਹੋ?` - `Tusī̃ kitthe ho?` means “Where are you?” `ਮੈਂ ਇੱਥੇ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ itthe hā̃.` answers “I am here.” These place words stand on their own; do not automatically add `ਵਿੱਚ` to “here”. Some everyday location expressions such as being at home appear without an explicit postposition: `ਮੈਂ ਘਰ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ ghar hā̃.`

### Common trap

`ਕਮਰੇ` can be a direct plural or an oblique singular. Count the rooms from the full construction. In `ਕਮਰੇ ਵਿੱਚ` the ending alone does not mean “in rooms”. Endings serve grammatical relationships as well as number.

### Practice

Locate the stated person or object.

1. The book is on the table.
2. Aman is in the room.
3. Where are you? (respectful)
4. I am here.

***Answers:***

1. `ਕਿਤਾਬ ਮੇਜ਼ ਉੱਤੇ ਹੈ।` - `Kitāb mez utte hai.`
2. `ਅਮਨ ਕਮਰੇ ਵਿੱਚ ਹੈ।` - `Aman kamre vich hai.`
3. `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿੱਥੇ ਹੋ?` - `Tusī̃ kitthe ho?`
4. `ਮੈਂ ਇੱਥੇ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ itthe hā̃.`

### Why these answers work

The first two answers place the relational word after its noun. Room visibly changes form; table does not show the same change. The question and its possible reply use complete place words. Their final copulas still agree with the person being located.

### Retrieve and recombine

Locate a book first on a table, then in your room. Ask a classmate where they are and give your own location. Explain why “my” changes form in the room phrase although you remain the owner.

## Lesson 7: Talk about a regular action

### Outcome and thinking point

Say what you do regularly. You can already place a person first and a verb at the end. Where would the drink go in “I drink tea”? Try putting it between the person and the action, then compare the masculine and feminine examples.

`ਮੈਂ ਚਾਹ ਪੀਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ chāh pī̃dā hā̃.` means “I drink tea”, said by a male speaker. `ਮੈਂ ਚਾਹ ਪੀਂਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ chāh pī̃dī hā̃.` is the corresponding female-speaker sentence. These describe a habit or a general practice, rather than explicitly presenting an action in progress at this moment.

### Everyday actions

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਪੀਣਾ | pīṇā | to drink |
| ਖਾਣਾ | khāṇā | to eat |
| ਪੜ੍ਹਨਾ | paṛhnā | to read, study |
| ਲਿਖਣਾ | likhṇā | to write |
| ਕਰਨਾ | karnā | to do |
| ਜਾਣਾ | jāṇā | to go |
| ਰੋਜ਼ | roz | every day |
| ਰੋਟੀ | roṭī | flatbread; a meal in context |

### Assemble an action sentence

Habitual forms should be learned with their stems rather than produced by blindly attaching letters. A common masculine singular ending is `-ਦਾ`: `ਕਰਦਾ` - `kardā`, `ਪੜ੍ਹਦਾ` - `paṛhdā`, and `ਜਾਂਦਾ` - `jā̃dā`. Stem shape and nasalisation can differ. Feminine singular forms end in `-ਦੀ`.

`ਉਹ ਰੋਜ਼ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੀ ਹੈ।` - `Uh roz paṛhdī hai.` means “She studies every day.” The pronoun does not say she by itself; the feminine verb form supplies that information. `ਅਮਨ ਪਾਣੀ ਪੀਂਦਾ ਹੈ।` - `Aman pāṇī pī̃dā hai.` describes Aman, here a male, drinking water habitually. We have not made water the agreement controller merely by placing it next to the verb.

`ਅਸੀਂ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਦੇ ਹਾਂ।` - `Asī̃ kamm karde hā̃.` means “We work”, for a masculine or mixed group. `ਕੰਮ ਕਰਨਾ` is literally to do work. This is a useful way to learn a verb together with a common noun partner, rather than translating every English verb as a single Punjabi word.

### Common trap

Do not choose the habitual ending from the gender of tea or water. In this pattern the subject determines agreement. Also avoid treating the dictionary infinitive as a finished present-tense verb: `ਪੀਣਾ` names the action but does not by itself mean “I drink”.

### Practice

Express habits with the indicated speaker.

1. I drink water. (male speaker)
2. I drink tea. (female speaker)
3. She studies every day.
4. We work. (mixed group)

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੈਂ ਪਾਣੀ ਪੀਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ pāṇī pī̃dā hā̃.`
2. `ਮੈਂ ਚਾਹ ਪੀਂਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ chāh pī̃dī hā̃.`
3. `ਉਹ ਰੋਜ਼ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੀ ਹੈ।` - `Uh roz paṛhdī hai.`
4. `ਅਸੀਂ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਦੇ ਹਾਂ।` - `Asī̃ kamm karde hā̃.`

### Why these answers work

Each answer keeps the action before the final auxiliary. The first two distinguish the speaker's grammatical agreement, while the third combines a frequency word with a habitual form. The fourth uses the plural masculine form appropriate to the specified mixed group.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give two habits for one imagined person: drinking something and studying. Then change the person from a man to a woman while keeping the objects identical. Say exactly which words need to change and why.

## Lesson 8: Ask about routines and deny a habit

### Outcome and thinking point

Find out what somebody regularly does and say what you do not do. The negative already appeared before a copula. With a longer verb phrase, should it go after the entire sentence or before the action? Test the examples for a consistent position.

`ਮੈਂ ਚਾਹ ਨਹੀਂ ਪੀਂਦਾ।` - `Maĩ chāh nahī̃ pī̃dā.` means “I do not drink tea”, for a male speaker. In a negative habitual statement the present auxiliary is commonly omitted. A fuller `ਮੈਂ ਚਾਹ ਨਹੀਂ ਪੀਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ` also makes the structure explicit, but the shorter model here is very useful in conversation.

### Questions for a daily routine

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਕਦੋਂ | kadõ | when |
| ਕਿੱਥੇ | kitthe | where |
| ਕੀ | kī | what |
| ਕਿਉਂ | kiũ | why |
| ਸਵੇਰੇ | savere | in the morning |
| ਸ਼ਾਮ ਨੂੰ | shām nū̃ | in the evening |
| ਕੰਮ | kamm | work |
| ਪੰਜਾਬੀ | panjābī | Punjabi |

### Build a question from an answer

Start with `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੇ ਹੋ।` - `Tusī̃ panjābī paṛhde ho.` meaning “You study Punjabi”, addressed respectfully to a man or a mixed group. Replace the subject of study with `ਕੀ`: `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕੀ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੇ ਹੋ?` - `Tusī̃ kī paṛhde ho?` The question word appears where the missing information belongs.

For a woman addressed respectfully, use `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕੀ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੇ ਹੋ?` only if following a local usage that levels agreement; the course's careful gender-agreeing model is `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕੀ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੀਆਂ ਹੋ?` - `Tusī̃ kī paṛhdīā̃ ho?`. Respectful feminine plural agreement is useful to recognise even when colloquial speakers vary. Practice prompts specify a male listener when using `ਪੜ੍ਹਦੇ`.

`ਉਹ ਕਿੱਥੇ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਦਾ ਹੈ?` - `Uh kitthe kamm kardā hai?` asks “Where does he work?” Notice that English needs “does”, but Punjabi simply keeps its habitual verb. `ਉਹ ਸਵੇਰੇ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਦਾ ਹੈ।` - `Uh savere kamm kardā hai.` says when, not where. A grammatically complete answer must also answer the actual information requested.

### Common trap

Do not add a Punjabi equivalent of English “do” merely to form a question. `ਕਰਨਾ` is a real action verb meaning do or make; it is not the universal question auxiliary of English. It belongs in “do work” because of the expression itself.

### Practice

Make a habitual question or negative statement.

1. I do not drink tea. (male speaker)
2. What do you study? (respectful male listener)
3. Where does he work?
4. She works in the morning.

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੈਂ ਚਾਹ ਨਹੀਂ ਪੀਂਦਾ।` - `Maĩ chāh nahī̃ pī̃dā.`
2. `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕੀ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੇ ਹੋ?` - `Tusī̃ kī paṛhde ho?`
3. `ਉਹ ਕਿੱਥੇ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਦਾ ਹੈ?` - `Uh kitthe kamm kardā hai?`
4. `ਉਹ ਸਵੇਰੇ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਦੀ ਹੈ।` - `Uh savere kamm kardī hai.`

### Why these answers work

The negative precedes the habitual action and omits the optional present auxiliary. The questions retain Punjabi verb structure without inserting an English-style do. The last answer changes the habitual ending to feminine while leaving the work noun and morning expression unchanged.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask somebody what they study, where they work and when they drink tea. Answer one question positively and one negatively for a clearly specified person. Make sure each answer supplies the kind of information the question requests.

## Lesson 9: Describe what is happening now

### Outcome and thinking point

Distinguish a regular activity from one happening now. If `ਮੈਂ ਪੜ੍ਹਦਾ ਹਾਂ` describes a habit, what extra material might show that reading is in progress? Compare the action stem with the separate progress word in the examples.

`ਮੈਂ ਪੜ੍ਹ ਰਿਹਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ paṛh rihā hā̃.` means “I am reading”, for a male speaker. `ਮੈਂ ਪੜ੍ਹ ਰਹੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ paṛh rahī hā̃.` is the female-speaker form. The progressive uses the verb stem plus an agreeing form of `ਰਿਹਾ` and the auxiliary. Do not retain the habitual `-ਦਾ` at the same time.

### Progress and context

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਹੁਣ | huṇ | now |
| ਅੱਜ | ajj | today |
| ਰਿਹਾ | rihā | progressive, masculine singular |
| ਰਹੀ | rahī | progressive, feminine singular |
| ਰਹੇ | rahe | progressive, masculine plural |
| ਸੁਣਨਾ | suṇnā | to listen, hear |
| ਬੋਲਣਾ | bolṇā | to speak |
| ਗਾਣਾ | gāṇā | song; to sing in other contexts |

### Compare two viewpoints

`ਅਮਨ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਪੜ੍ਹਦਾ ਹੈ।` - `Aman panjābī paṛhdā hai.` presents studying Punjabi as Aman's routine. `ਅਮਨ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਪੜ੍ਹ ਰਿਹਾ ਹੈ।` - `Aman panjābī paṛh rihā hai.` places us inside a current studying activity. Both can be true of the same person, but they answer different questions.

`ਉਹ ਚਾਹ ਪੀ ਰਹੀ ਹੈ।` - `Uh chāh pī rahī hai.` means “She is drinking tea.” The stem is `ਪੀ`, not the habitual form `ਪੀਂਦੀ`. `ਅਸੀਂ ਗਾਣਾ ਸੁਣ ਰਹੇ ਹਾਂ।` - `Asī̃ gāṇā suṇ rahe hā̃.` means “We are listening to a song”, for a masculine or mixed group. The progress element agrees with the listeners, not the song.

To ask what is happening, use `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕੀ ਕਰ ਰਹੇ ਹੋ?` - `Tusī̃ kī kar rahe ho?`, addressed respectfully to a man or a mixed group. Here `ਕਰ` is meaningful “doing”, and the question asks for an activity. The answer can replace the whole doing expression with reading, drinking or listening.

### Common trap

English “I am a student” and “I am reading” both contain am, but only the second describes an action in progress. Do not add `ਰਿਹਾ` to identities or stable descriptions simply because English has a form of be.

### Practice

Describe an action in progress.

1. I am reading. (male speaker)
2. I am speaking Punjabi. (female speaker)
3. She is drinking tea.
4. We are listening to a song. (mixed group)

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੈਂ ਪੜ੍ਹ ਰਿਹਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ paṛh rihā hā̃.`
2. `ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਬੋਲ ਰਹੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ panjābī bol rahī hā̃.`
3. `ਉਹ ਚਾਹ ਪੀ ਰਹੀ ਹੈ।` - `Uh chāh pī rahī hai.`
4. `ਅਸੀਂ ਗਾਣਾ ਸੁਣ ਰਹੇ ਹਾਂ।` - `Asī̃ gāṇā suṇ rahe hā̃.`

### Why these answers work

Each answer uses an action stem followed by a progressive form. The gender and number distinctions appear on that progressive element. The auxiliaries identify person, while the object does not control the agreement in these examples.

### Retrieve and recombine

Picture a classroom through a window. Describe one man reading, one woman speaking and a group listening. Then step away from the scene and describe one person's regular habit instead. Explain what changed in your viewpoint and verb phrase.

## Lesson 10: Go somewhere and come from somewhere

### Outcome and thinking point

Talk about movement, origin and direction. You know how to locate somebody in a room. Is a destination the same relationship as a place someone is already in? Compare bare destinations with explicit origin phrases rather than translating every English “to” as one word.

`ਮੈਂ ਸਕੂਲ ਜਾਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ sakūl jā̃dā hā̃.` means “I go to school”, for a male speaker. A familiar destination can appear directly before the movement verb. `ਮੈਂ ਸਕੂਲ ਤੋਂ ਆ ਰਿਹਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ sakūl tõ ā rihā hā̃.` means “I am coming from school.” The origin marker `ਤੋਂ` makes the direction of the relationship explicit.

### Movement words

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਆਉਣਾ | āuṇā | to come |
| ਜਾਣਾ | jāṇā | to go |
| ਤੋਂ | tõ | from; than in comparisons |
| ਵੱਲ | vall | towards |
| ਤੱਕ | takk | as far as, until |
| ਬਾਜ਼ਾਰ | bāzār | market |
| ਸ਼ਹਿਰ | shahir | city |
| ਅੰਮ੍ਰਿਤਸਰ | Amritsar | Amritsar |

### Build a short journey

`ਉਹ ਬਾਜ਼ਾਰ ਜਾ ਰਹੀ ਹੈ।` - `Uh bāzār jā rahī hai.` means “She is going to the market.” `ਅਸੀਂ ਘਰ ਆ ਰਹੇ ਹਾਂ।` - `Asī̃ ghar ā rahe hā̃.` means “We are coming home”, for a mixed group. Come and go depend on a viewpoint: the speaker, listener or an established destination can serve as the conversational centre. Do not reduce the choice to a rigid geographical rule.

`ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿੱਥੋਂ ਆ ਰਹੇ ਹੋ?` - `Tusī̃ kitthõ ā rahe ho?` asks “Where are you coming from?”, respectfully to a male listener. `ਕਿੱਥੋਂ` packages where plus origin. Contrast it with `ਕਿੱਥੇ`, which asks location or destination according to its verb. The nasal vowel in the origin question helps distinguish the forms.

`ਸਕੂਲ ਵੱਲ` - `sakūl vall` means “towards the school”. This points in a direction without necessarily claiming arrival. `ਬਾਜ਼ਾਰ ਤੱਕ` - `bāzār takk` sets the market as an endpoint. The verb and the relationship word together create the intended journey.

### Common trap

Do not insert `ਵਿੱਚ` after a destination just because the English place can also be a location. Going to a city and being in a city are different relationships. A destination may be bare, while an origin marked by `ਤੋਂ` cannot be interpreted simply as the place of the action.

### Practice

Describe the movement with the indicated subject.

1. She is going to the market.
2. I go to school. (male speaker)
3. We are coming home. (mixed group)
4. Where are you coming from? (respectful male listener)

***Answers:***

1. `ਉਹ ਬਾਜ਼ਾਰ ਜਾ ਰਹੀ ਹੈ।` - `Uh bāzār jā rahī hai.`
2. `ਮੈਂ ਸਕੂਲ ਜਾਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ sakūl jā̃dā hā̃.`
3. `ਅਸੀਂ ਘਰ ਆ ਰਹੇ ਹਾਂ।` - `Asī̃ ghar ā rahe hā̃.`
4. `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿੱਥੋਂ ਆ ਰਹੇ ਹੋ?` - `Tusī̃ kitthõ ā rahe ho?`

### Why these answers work

The first and third are current journeys; the second is a routine. The destination precedes the movement verb without an English-style preposition. The last question asks an origin and therefore uses the origin form of where rather than the ordinary location form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine leaving school for the market and later arriving home. Tell a listener your current journey, then describe where you are coming from. Add a routine sentence about school and keep it distinct from today's particular movement.

## Lesson 11: Give and receive with ਨੂੰ

### Outcome and thinking point

Say who receives something and identify a person as an object. English relies heavily on order to distinguish “Aman gives me a book” from “I give Aman a book”. What can a relationship marker contribute when several noun phrases appear before the Punjabi verb?

`ਅਮਨ ਮੈਨੂੰ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਦਿੰਦਾ ਹੈ।` - `Aman mainū̃ kitāb dindā hai.` means “Aman gives me a book”, with masculine Aman. `ਮੈਨੂੰ` is the recipient form “to me”. The thing given is the unmarked book; the recipient is separately identified.

### Recipients and objects

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਨੂੰ | nū̃ | to; marker for some objects |
| ਮੈਨੂੰ | mainū̃ | to me, me |
| ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ | tuhānū̃ | to you, respectful or plural |
| ਸਾਨੂੰ | sānū̃ | to us, us |
| ਉਸ ਨੂੰ | us nū̃ | to that person, him, her |
| ਦੇਣਾ | deṇā | to give |
| ਲੈਣਾ | laiṇā | to take |
| ਦੇਖਣਾ | dekhṇā | to see |

### Follow the participants

`ਮੈਂ ਅਮਨ ਨੂੰ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਦਿੰਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ Aman nū̃ kitāb dindī hā̃.` means “I give Aman a book”, for a female speaker. The habitual form agrees with the giver in this present pattern. The recipient's gender does not choose `ਦਿੰਦੀ`.

`ਮੈਂ ਉਸ ਨੂੰ ਦੇਖ ਰਿਹਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ us nū̃ dekh rihā hā̃.` means “I am looking at him or her”, for a male speaker. Here `ਨੂੰ` does not indicate a transfer recipient. It marks a definite person as the object. Punjabi object marking is sensitive to factors including animacy and specificity; “always add ਨੂੰ to every object” would be an inaccurate shortcut.

Compare an ordinary thing: `ਮੈਂ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਪੜ੍ਹ ਰਹੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ kitāb paṛh rahī hā̃.` means “I am reading a book”, for a female speaker. No `ਨੂੰ` is needed in this neutral example. Later, object marking will matter for completed-action agreement, so noticing it now prepares a useful distinction.

### Common trap

Do not build the standard recipient form by writing the direct pronoun followed by `ਨੂੰ` in every case. `ਮੈਂ` becomes `ਮੈਨੂੰ`; the compact form is the useful one to learn. Third-person `ਉਹ` also changes to `ਉਸ` before the marker.

### Practice

Keep giver, recipient and thing distinct.

1. Aman gives me a book. (male Aman)
2. I give Aman a book. (female speaker)
3. I am looking at that person. (male speaker)
4. Give the Punjabi phrase for “to us”.

***Answers:***

1. `ਅਮਨ ਮੈਨੂੰ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਦਿੰਦਾ ਹੈ।` - `Aman mainū̃ kitāb dindā hai.`
2. `ਮੈਂ ਅਮਨ ਨੂੰ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਦਿੰਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ Aman nū̃ kitāb dindī hā̃.`
3. `ਮੈਂ ਉਸ ਨੂੰ ਦੇਖ ਰਿਹਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ us nū̃ dekh rihā hā̃.`
4. `ਸਾਨੂੰ` - `sānū̃`

### Why these answers work

The first two use the same transfer pattern with different givers. The third marks a person who is being seen, illustrating another use of `ਨੂੰ`. The fourth is a phrase rather than a sentence because the prompt asks for only the recipient form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a book passing from a teacher to you, then from you to Aman. Name all three roles before saying each sentence. Finally describe seeing Aman, and explain why `ਨੂੰ` remains useful even when nothing is being given.

## Lesson 12: Express possession with ਕੋਲ

### Outcome and thinking point

Say that somebody has an object. English uses a verb “have”. Could Punjabi instead describe something as being with a person? Use the location pattern as a bridge, while remembering that the result expresses possession rather than literal physical closeness alone.

`ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਹੈ।` - `Mere kol kitāb hai.` means “I have a book.” The phrase `ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ` is a conventional possessor expression. The final linking word agrees with the possessed item. A book is singular, so the sentence ends with `ਹੈ` even though the possessor is the speaker.

### Possession phrases

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਕੋਲ | kol | near, with, in possession of |
| ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ | mere kol | I have, with me |
| ਤੁਹਾਡੇ ਕੋਲ | tuhāḍe kol | you have, with you |
| ਸਾਡੇ ਕੋਲ | sāḍe kol | we have, with us |
| ਉਸ ਕੋਲ | us kol | he or she has |
| ਪੈਸੇ | paise | money |
| ਸਮਾਂ | samā̃ | time |
| ਫ਼ੋਨ | fon | phone |

### Possession is not ownership alone

`ਤੁਹਾਡੇ ਕੋਲ ਸਮਾਂ ਹੈ?` - `Tuhāḍe kol samā̃ hai?` asks “Do you have time?” This is a yes-no question marked by intonation and punctuation; an initial `ਕੀ` can make the question explicit. `ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਸਮਾਂ ਨਹੀਂ ਹੈ।` - `Mere kol samā̃ nahī̃ hai.` says “I do not have time.” The pattern extends from objects to available resources.

`ਅਮਨ ਕੋਲ ਦੋ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਹਨ।` - `Aman kol do kitābā̃ han.` means “Aman has two books.” The number two and the plural book noun control plural existence. Compare ownership: `ਇਹ ਅਮਨ ਦੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਹੈ।` identifies a book as Aman's. A borrowed book may be in your possession without being your property, so the two patterns can convey different information.

For family relationships, Punjabi often uses a possessive noun phrase rather than blindly transferring this possession construction to every English “have”. `ਮੇਰੀ ਇੱਕ ਭੈਣ ਹੈ।` - `Merī ikk bhaiṇ hai.` means “I have one sister.” Here it is naturally framed as the existence of “my sister”. Learn such relational uses in their own contexts.

### Common trap

Do not say `ਮੈਂ ... ਹਾਂ` merely because English begins “I have”. The Punjabi subject of the existence claim is the thing or resource, while the possessor appears in the `ਕੋਲ` phrase. This is why money, books and time can select different agreement.

### Practice

Use the possession construction.

1. I have a phone.
2. I do not have time.
3. Aman has two books.
4. Do you have a book? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਫ਼ੋਨ ਹੈ।` - `Mere kol fon hai.`
2. `ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਸਮਾਂ ਨਹੀਂ ਹੈ।` - `Mere kol samā̃ nahī̃ hai.`
3. `ਅਮਨ ਕੋਲ ਦੋ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਹਨ।` - `Aman kol do kitābā̃ han.`
4. `ਕੀ ਤੁਹਾਡੇ ਕੋਲ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਹੈ?` - `Kī tuhāḍe kol kitāb hai?`

### Why these answers work

Possessor phrases remain separate from the singular or plural item being possessed. The third sentence therefore has plural `ਹਨ`; the other items are singular. Negation and yes-no marking attach to the whole existence claim without changing who the possessor is.

### Retrieve and recombine

At a library, ask whether somebody has a book and say whether you have time to read it. Then distinguish the book being with you from the book belonging to Aman. Explain what each sentence tells the listener.

## Lesson 13: Ask for something you need

### Outcome and thinking point

State a need and describe a preference without treating yourself as an ordinary action subject. English “I need water” begins with I. You already know a recipient form meaning “to me”. Could Punjabi frame the needed thing as necessary to that person?

`ਮੈਨੂੰ ਪਾਣੀ ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ਹੈ।` - `Mainū̃ pāṇī chāhīdā hai.` means “I need water” or, in an appropriate service context, “I would like water.” `ਮੈਨੂੰ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਚਾਹੀਦੀ ਹੈ।` - `Mainū̃ kitāb chāhīdī hai.` changes the necessity form to agree with a feminine book. The experiencer stays `ਮੈਨੂੰ` in both sentences.

### Needs and preferences

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਚਾਹੀਦਾ | chāhīdā | needed, masculine singular |
| ਚਾਹੀਦੀ | chāhīdī | needed, feminine singular |
| ਚਾਹੀਦੇ | chāhīde | needed, masculine plural |
| ਪਸੰਦ | pasand | liking, pleasing |
| ਮਦਦ | madad | help, feminine |
| ਟਿਕਟ | ṭikaṭ | ticket, feminine |
| ਖਾਣਾ | khāṇā | food |
| ਹੋਰ | hor | more, other |

### Work from the thing needed

`ਸਾਨੂੰ ਮਦਦ ਚਾਹੀਦੀ ਹੈ।` - `Sānū̃ madad chāhīdī hai.` means “We need help.” Although several people need it, help remains feminine singular. The recipient-like phrase identifies who has the need; it does not set the agreement of `ਚਾਹੀਦੀ`.

`ਮੈਨੂੰ ਚਾਹ ਪਸੰਦ ਹੈ।` - `Mainū̃ chāh pasand hai.` means “I like tea.” This is a related experiencer construction. It says something is pleasing to somebody, without requiring an English-style transitive like verb. `ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਕੀ ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ਹੈ?` - `Tuhānū̃ kī chāhīdā hai?` respectfully asks “What do you need?” The unknown thing uses the default masculine singular form in this general question.

The spelling resemblance between `ਚਾਹ` and `ਚਾਹੀਦਾ` should not lead you to think that every need sentence contains tea. They are different lexical items. Likewise, having a preference for tea does not necessarily mean wanting a cup right now. Context can distinguish a standing preference from a present request.

### Common trap

Do not make `ਚਾਹੀਦਾ` agree with the speaker's sex. A woman needing water still uses the masculine form because water is masculine. A man needing a book uses the feminine form. This is a clear contrast with the present habitual and progressive patterns.

### Practice

Express a need or preference.

1. I need water.
2. We need help.
3. I like tea.
4. What do you need? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੈਨੂੰ ਪਾਣੀ ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ਹੈ।` - `Mainū̃ pāṇī chāhīdā hai.`
2. `ਸਾਨੂੰ ਮਦਦ ਚਾਹੀਦੀ ਹੈ।` - `Sānū̃ madad chāhīdī hai.`
3. `ਮੈਨੂੰ ਚਾਹ ਪਸੰਦ ਹੈ।` - `Mainū̃ chāh pasand hai.`
4. `ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਕੀ ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ਹੈ?` - `Tuhānū̃ kī chāhīdā hai?`

### Why these answers work

Each answer marks the person experiencing the need or preference with a recipient form. Water takes masculine agreement; help takes feminine agreement. The preference expression uses `ਪਸੰਦ`, which is not a substitute for the necessity adjective. The general question uses an unspecified thing.

### Retrieve and recombine

At a station, state a need for a ticket, then imagine a classroom need for a book. Move from your own need to a group's need for help. Keep the thing needed separate from the person needing it as you choose agreement.

## Lesson 14: Make a respectful request

### Outcome and thinking point

Ask someone to perform a simple action. A statement about what someone usually does is not automatically a request. If the listener is an unfamiliar adult, which part of your wording should make the interpersonal relationship clear?

Punjabi has imperative forms appropriate to different relationships. This lesson uses polite plural forms as a practical default. `ਪਾਣੀ ਦਿਓ ਜੀ।` - `Pāṇī dio jī.` means “Please give water.” Context often supplies “me”; you can make it explicit with `ਮੈਨੂੰ`. `ਜੀ` is a respectful particle, not a universal grammatical replacement for please.

### Useful polite forms

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਦਿਓ | dio | give, polite |
| ਲਓ | lao | take, polite |
| ਆਓ | āo | come, polite |
| ਜਾਓ | jāo | go, polite |
| ਬੈਠੋ | baiṭho | sit, polite |
| ਸੁਣੋ | suṇo | listen, polite |
| ਬੋਲੋ | bolo | speak, polite |
| ਹੌਲੀ | haulī | slowly, softly |

### Build a helpful exchange

`ਇੱਥੇ ਬੈਠੋ ਜੀ।` - `Itthe baiṭho jī.` means “Please sit here.” `ਹੌਲੀ ਬੋਲੋ ਜੀ।` - `Haulī bolo jī.` asks “Please speak slowly.” The action remains near the end, and a manner word such as slowly comes before it. Neither sentence requires an explicit `ਤੁਸੀਂ`; the request is already directed to the listener.

`ਇਹ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਲਓ ਜੀ।` - `Ih kitāb lao jī.` means “Please take this book.” A host or teacher can use a polite imperative to offer something, so an imperative is not inherently harsh. Voice, relationship and context contribute to whether it sounds welcoming or forceful.

To ask for a negative action, Punjabi often uses `ਨਾ`: `ਇੱਥੇ ਨਾ ਬੈਠੋ।` - `Itthe nā baiṭho.` means “Do not sit here.” Contrast this with the ordinary negative `ਨਹੀਂ` in statements. This beginner contrast is useful, though authentic conversation includes additional negative and emphatic patterns.

### Common trap

Do not place the habitual auxiliary after every request. `ਬੈਠੋ` already functions as an imperative; `ਬੈਠੋ ਹੋ` is not produced by combining the polite pronoun's copula with a command. Also do not assume that adding `ਜੀ` makes an unreasonable or abrupt demand considerate in every context.

### Practice

Use a polite imperative.

1. Please sit here.
2. Please speak slowly.
3. Please take this book.
4. Do not sit here. (polite form)

***Answers:***

1. `ਇੱਥੇ ਬੈਠੋ ਜੀ।` - `Itthe baiṭho jī.`
2. `ਹੌਲੀ ਬੋਲੋ ਜੀ।` - `Haulī bolo jī.`
3. `ਇਹ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਲਓ ਜੀ।` - `Ih kitāb lao jī.`
4. `ਇੱਥੇ ਨਾ ਬੈਠੋ।` - `Itthe nā baiṭho.`

### Why these answers work

The polite verb form addresses the listener directly, so no subject pronoun or present copula is required. Here and slowly specify where and how. The negative request places `ਨਾ` before the imperative, preserving the polite ending while reversing the requested action.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine welcoming someone to a class: offer a book, ask them to sit, and ask them to speak slowly during an introduction. Then move the same respectful forms to a café request. Choose the object and action to fit the situation.

## Lesson 15: Count and ask how much

### Outcome and thinking point

Ask for a small quantity and check a price. A numeral can tell you that a noun is plural even when the noun's shape does not make this obvious. Predict whether “two rooms” should use the same noun form as “one room”.

`ਇੱਕ ਕਮਰਾ` - `ikk kamrā` is “one room”; `ਦੋ ਕਮਰੇ` - `do kamre` is “two rooms”. For books, `ਇੱਕ ਕਿਤਾਬ` contrasts with `ਦੋ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ`. Numbers and noun forms work together. Punjabi numbers are not all transparently assembled from a small English-like set of pieces, so learn the early numbers as whole words.

### Small quantities

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਇੱਕ | ikk | one |
| ਦੋ | do | two |
| ਤਿੰਨ | tinn | three |
| ਚਾਰ | chār | four |
| ਪੰਜ | panj | five |
| ਦਸ | das | ten |
| ਕਿੰਨਾ | kinnā | how much, masculine singular |
| ਕਿੰਨੀ | kinnī | how much, feminine singular |
| ਕਿੰਨੇ | kinne | how many, masculine plural |
| ਰੁਪਏ | rupaē | rupees |

### Ask about quantity and cost

`ਤੁਹਾਡੇ ਕੋਲ ਕਿੰਨੀਆਂ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਹਨ?` - `Tuhāḍe kol kinnīā̃ kitābā̃ han?` asks “How many books do you have?” The question word agrees with the feminine plural noun. `ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਤਿੰਨ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਹਨ।` - `Mere kol tinn kitābā̃ han.` supplies a number while keeping the possession pattern.

`ਇਹ ਕਿੰਨੇ ਰੁਪਏ ਦਾ ਹੈ?` - `Ih kinne rupaē dā hai?` asks “How many rupees is this?”, for a masculine singular item. The price phrase is linked with `ਦਾ`, which follows the item's grammatical gender. A feminine item can take `ਦੀ`. This is why a price question is more than a string containing a number and a currency word.

`ਦੋ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਦਿਓ ਜੀ।` - `Do kitābā̃ dio jī.` means “Please give two books.” The plural object does not change the imperative into an object-agreeing form. The polite `ਦਿਓ` addresses the person doing the giving, so it remains the form learned earlier.

### Common trap

Do not use `ਕਿੰਨਾ` unchanged before every noun. Countable feminine plural books select `ਕਿੰਨੀਆਂ`; masculine plural rupees select `ਕਿੰਨੇ`. Also do not assume that every noun ending in `-e` represents a plural, since the oblique singular can look similar.

### Practice

Count the items or ask their quantity.

1. I have three books.
2. Please give two books.
3. Ask how many books the listener has. (respectful)
4. These are four rooms.

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਤਿੰਨ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਹਨ।` - `Mere kol tinn kitābā̃ han.`
2. `ਦੋ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਦਿਓ ਜੀ।` - `Do kitābā̃ dio jī.`
3. `ਤੁਹਾਡੇ ਕੋਲ ਕਿੰਨੀਆਂ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਹਨ?` - `Tuhāḍe kol kinnīā̃ kitābā̃ han?`
4. `ਇਹ ਚਾਰ ਕਮਰੇ ਹਨ।` - `Ih chār kamre han.`

### Why these answers work

The number makes each noun phrase plural. Book and room use their own plural patterns. The question word follows book's feminine plural agreement, while the polite imperative remains a listener-directed form independent of how many books are requested.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask a shopkeeper for three books and ask a hotel worker about two rooms. Then state how many books you already have. Keep the difference between a request, a question and an existence statement audible in the final verb.

## Lesson 16: Place an event in time

### Outcome and thinking point

Say when a routine or current action happens and recognise a context-sensitive day word. English separates yesterday and tomorrow into different words. What happens if a Punjabi day expression can refer to either, with the verb and context resolving the choice?

`ਕੱਲ੍ਹ` - `kallh` can mean yesterday or tomorrow. It does not force a tense by itself. In later lessons, past and future forms will make the interpretation explicit. For now, avoid assuming that a vocabulary gloss gives one fixed calendar direction in every sentence.

### Time expressions

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਅੱਜ | ajj | today |
| ਕੱਲ੍ਹ | kallh | yesterday or tomorrow |
| ਹੁਣ | huṇ | now |
| ਬਾਅਦ ਵਿੱਚ | bāad vich | afterwards, later |
| ਪਹਿਲਾਂ | pahilā̃ | before, earlier |
| ਸਵੇਰੇ | savere | in the morning |
| ਸ਼ਾਮ ਨੂੰ | shām nū̃ | in the evening |
| ਰਾਤ ਨੂੰ | rāt nū̃ | at night |
| ਵਜੇ | vaje | o'clock |

### Build a schedule

`ਮੈਂ ਸਵੇਰੇ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ savere panjābī paṛhdī hā̃.` means “I study Punjabi in the morning”, for a female speaker. `ਉਹ ਸ਼ਾਮ ਨੂੰ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਦਾ ਹੈ।` - `Uh shām nū̃ kamm kardā hai.` means “He works in the evening.” The time expression can stand near the beginning, before the object and verb phrase.

`ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜ ਵਜੇ ਆਉਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ panj vaje āũdā hā̃.` means “I come at five o'clock”, for a male speaker, in a routine or scheduled context. Clock expressions with `ਵਜੇ` supply the time without requiring an added English-style at. `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਦੋਂ ਆਉਂਦੇ ਹੋ?` - `Tusī̃ kadõ āũde ho?` asks when a respectful male listener comes.

Time words can also be topics: “Today, I am studying Punjabi” sets today as the frame for the statement. Punjabi word order has flexibility for such emphasis. The beginner subject-time-object-verb pattern is a dependable starting point, not proof that every other order is ungrammatical.

### Common trap

Do not use a future translation of `ਕੱਲ੍ਹ` as evidence that a present habitual sentence has magically become an explicit future. Context can support scheduled readings, but the day word itself remains ambiguous. State the intended day clearly when arranging something consequential.

### Practice

Use the time expression in a full sentence.

1. I study Punjabi in the morning. (female speaker)
2. He works in the evening.
3. I come at five o'clock. (male speaker, routine)
4. When do you come? (respectful male listener)

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੈਂ ਸਵੇਰੇ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ savere panjābī paṛhdī hā̃.`
2. `ਉਹ ਸ਼ਾਮ ਨੂੰ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਦਾ ਹੈ।` - `Uh shām nū̃ kamm kardā hai.`
3. `ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜ ਵਜੇ ਆਉਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ panj vaje āũdā hā̃.`
4. `ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਦੋਂ ਆਉਂਦੇ ਹੋ?` - `Tusī̃ kadõ āũde ho?`

### Why these answers work

Time phrases precede the final verb phrase without replacing it. The habitual forms retain subject agreement. The clock expression uses the numeral plus `ਵਜੇ`; the question asks for an unspecified time with `ਕਦੋਂ` instead of a location question word.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give a morning study routine and an evening work routine. Ask a partner when they come to class, then answer with a clock time. Keep the person's identity constant while changing only the time information.

## Lesson 17: Say where someone was

### Outcome and thinking point

Move an identity or location into the past before learning completed actions. If “I am here” ends in a present linking word, can “I was here” be made by changing that final link alone? This is a smaller and more useful first step than learning every past form together.

`ਮੈਂ ਘਰ ਸੀ।` - `Maĩ ghar sī.` means “I was at home.” `ਉਹ ਕਮਰੇ ਵਿੱਚ ਸੀ।` - `Uh kamre vich sī.` means “He or she was in the room.” The past copula `ਸੀ` places a state in the past. It does not say that someone travelled home or entered the room.

### Past settings

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਸੀ | sī | was, past copula in these examples |
| ਸਨ | san | were, standard third-person plural |
| ਕੱਲ੍ਹ | kallh | yesterday in a past context |
| ਉਦੋਂ | udõ | then, at that time |
| ਇੱਥੇ | itthe | here |
| ਉੱਥੇ | utthe | there |
| ਬੰਦ | band | closed |
| ਖੁੱਲ੍ਹਾ | khullhā | open, masculine singular |

### State versus event

`ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਸਕੂਲ ਬੰਦ ਸੀ।` - `Kallh sakūl band sī.` means “The school was closed yesterday.” This reports a state. It does not identify who closed the school or the act of closing. `ਕਮਰੇ ਖੁੱਲ੍ਹੇ ਸਨ।` - `Kamre khullhe san.` means “The rooms were open.” The plural description and plural past copula belong together in this written-standard example.

`ਮੈਂ ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਬਿਮਾਰ ਸੀ।` - `Maĩ kallh bimār sī.` means “I was ill yesterday.” The adjective `ਬਿਮਾਰ` does not change for masculine and feminine subjects. A changing adjective would keep its appropriate agreement even though the copula had moved into the past.

Punjabi past copulas have regional and person-related variation. Conversational `ਸੀ` is widespread beyond singular uses, while written paradigms can distinguish forms such as `ਸਾਂ`, `ਸੋ` and `ਸਨ`. This lesson's production remains with first-person singular `ਸੀ` and clearly exemplified third-person plural `ਸਨ`, rather than claiming one short table represents every variety.

### Common trap

Do not add a completed movement form when the meaning is only location in the past. “I was home” and “I went home” answer different questions. Similarly, a closed shop is a state; somebody closing it is an event with participants.

### Practice

Describe a past state.

1. I was at home.
2. That person was in the room.
3. The school was closed yesterday.
4. The rooms were open.

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੈਂ ਘਰ ਸੀ।` - `Maĩ ghar sī.`
2. `ਉਹ ਕਮਰੇ ਵਿੱਚ ਸੀ।` - `Uh kamre vich sī.`
3. `ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਸਕੂਲ ਬੰਦ ਸੀ।` - `Kallh sakūl band sī.`
4. `ਕਮਰੇ ਖੁੱਲ੍ਹੇ ਸਨ।` - `Kamre khullhe san.`

### Why these answers work

The first three use the demonstrated singular-state past copula. The fourth makes the room noun, its changing adjective and the copula plural. Yesterday is resolved by the past context. None of the answers introduces a new actor who changes the state.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe your location yesterday, the state of a school and the state of several rooms. Then give the corresponding present statements. Explain which words carry time and which continue to express location or description.

## Lesson 18: Report going and coming as completed events

### Outcome and thinking point

Report a completed journey. You now distinguish a past state from an event. What new information does “Aman went home” provide that “Aman was home” does not? Identify the action before choosing its completed form.

`ਅਮਨ ਘਰ ਗਿਆ।` - `Aman ghar giā.` means “Aman went home”, for a male Aman. `ਉਹ ਘਰ ਗਈ।` - `Uh ghar gaī.` means “She went home.” The completed forms of go are not made by simply adding an ending to `ਜਾ`. Learn this common irregular family as a useful contrast.

### Completed movement forms

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਗਿਆ | giā | went, masculine singular |
| ਗਈ | gaī | went, feminine singular |
| ਗਏ | gae | went, masculine plural |
| ਆਇਆ | āiā | came, masculine singular |
| ਆਈ | āī | came, feminine singular |
| ਆਏ | āe | came, masculine plural |
| ਵਾਪਸ | vāpas | back |
| ਕੱਲ੍ਹ | kallh | yesterday in this context |

### See the agreement controller

`ਮੈਂ ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਬਾਜ਼ਾਰ ਗਿਆ।` - `Maĩ kallh bāzār giā.` means “I went to the market yesterday”, for a male speaker. A female speaker uses `ਗਈ`. Here there is no transferred or acted-on object that controls agreement. The subject of the movement supplies the gender and number.

`ਅਸੀਂ ਘਰ ਆਏ।` - `Asī̃ ghar āe.` means “We came home”, for a masculine or mixed group. `ਉਹ ਸਕੂਲ ਆਈ।` - `Uh sakūl āī.` means “She came to school.” A destination is not automatically a grammatical object simply because it appears before the verb. This distinction will prevent an important error in the next lesson.

You may encounter a past copula after a completed form, such as `ਗਿਆ ਸੀ`. Such combinations locate or frame completion in relation to a past context; they are not an obligatory extra word after every English simple past. The plain completed forms here are enough to report a short event when the time is understood.

### Common trap

Do not use `ਨੇ` after every past subject. The movement verbs in this lesson use ordinary subject agreement in the demonstrated construction. The special completed-transitive pattern comes next and applies because of the construction, not merely because something happened yesterday.

### Practice

Report the completed journey.

1. I went to the market yesterday. (male speaker)
2. She went home.
3. We came home. (mixed group)
4. She came to school.

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੈਂ ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਬਾਜ਼ਾਰ ਗਿਆ।` - `Maĩ kallh bāzār giā.`
2. `ਉਹ ਘਰ ਗਈ।` - `Uh ghar gaī.`
3. `ਅਸੀਂ ਘਰ ਆਏ।` - `Asī̃ ghar āe.`
4. `ਉਹ ਸਕੂਲ ਆਈ।` - `Uh sakūl āī.`

### Why these answers work

Go has an irregular completed stem, while come has the demonstrated vowel sequence. The forms agree with the moving person or people. Yesterday supplies a past reference in the first answer, and the completed form supports that reading of the day word.

### Retrieve and recombine

Tell a two-event journey for a woman: she went to the market and came home. Retell it for a mixed group. Compare both with a statement saying only that the woman was at home, without claiming any journey.

## Lesson 19: Notice agreement in completed transitive actions

### Outcome and thinking point

Report a completed action with a thing acted on. In the present pattern, the eater controls agreement. If a completed Punjabi clause marks the actor with `ਨੇ`, might the unmarked object become important instead? Compare bread and water while keeping Aman constant.

`ਅਮਨ ਨੇ ਰੋਟੀ ਖਾਧੀ।` - `Aman ne roṭī khādhī.` means “Aman ate the flatbread.” `ਅਮਨ ਨੇ ਪਾਣੀ ਪੀਤਾ।` - `Aman ne pāṇī pītā.` means “Aman drank the water.” The first completed form agrees with feminine `ਰੋਟੀ`; the second with masculine `ਪਾਣੀ`.

### Completed-action toolkit

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਨੇ | ne | actor marker in this completed pattern |
| ਖਾਧਾ | khādhā | ate, masculine singular |
| ਖਾਧੀ | khādhī | ate, feminine singular |
| ਪੀਤਾ | pītā | drank, masculine singular |
| ਪੀਤੀ | pītī | drank, feminine singular |
| ਪੜ੍ਹੀ | paṛhī | read, feminine singular completed |
| ਲਿਖੀ | likhī | wrote, feminine singular completed |
| ਚਿੱਠੀ | chiṭṭhī | letter, feminine |

### Work through the changed alignment

`ਅਮਨ ਨੇ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਪੜ੍ਹੀ।` - `Aman ne kitāb paṛhī.` means “Aman read the book.” The verb is feminine even if Aman is male. This is not an error in personal gender. It is a different agreement pattern in a completed transitive construction. Ask three questions: Is the action completed? Is there an actor performing it on an object? Is the object unmarked and available for agreement?

`ਅਮਨ ਨੇ ਚਿੱਠੀ ਲਿਖੀ।` - `Aman ne chiṭṭhī likhī.` transfers the same pattern to writing. With an object marked by `ਨੂੰ`, agreement may fall back to a default masculine singular form rather than the marked object's gender. That extension needs careful practice; the answer key stays with transparent unmarked objects.

First- and second-person subjects have their own standard and regional patterns concerning overt `ਨੇ`. Do not manufacture `ਮੈਂ ਨੇ` from an ordinary noun-plus-marker rule. This lesson deliberately uses a named actor so you can understand object agreement before handling pronoun-specific variation.

### Common trap

Do not choose the completed ending from the actor just because that was correct in present habits. Equally, do not put `ਨੇ` on an intransitive movement sentence. The preceding lesson's “Aman went” and this lesson's “Aman read a book” belong to different grammatical constructions.

### Practice

Use Aman as the actor in each completed sentence.

1. Aman ate the flatbread.
2. Aman drank the water.
3. Aman read the book.
4. Aman wrote the letter.

***Answers:***

1. `ਅਮਨ ਨੇ ਰੋਟੀ ਖਾਧੀ।` - `Aman ne roṭī khādhī.`
2. `ਅਮਨ ਨੇ ਪਾਣੀ ਪੀਤਾ।` - `Aman ne pāṇī pītā.`
3. `ਅਮਨ ਨੇ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਪੜ੍ਹੀ।` - `Aman ne kitāb paṛhī.`
4. `ਅਮਨ ਨੇ ਚਿੱਠੀ ਲਿਖੀ।` - `Aman ne chiṭṭhī likhī.`

### Why these answers work

The named actor receives the completed-transitive marker. Three objects are feminine and select feminine completed forms. Water is masculine and selects the masculine form. The spelling of eat and drink changes irregularly, so the answer cannot be generated from the habitual stem alone.

### Retrieve and recombine

Report one completed journey and one completed reading event for Aman. Explain why only one sentence uses the actor marker. Then replace the book with a letter and reading with writing while preserving the same agreement reasoning.

## Lesson 20: Say what will happen next

### Outcome and thinking point

Make a simple future statement. A day word alone can be ambiguous, but an explicitly future verb resolves the intended direction. If a male and a female speaker announce that they will go, which part of the future form should preserve the familiar gender distinction?

`ਮੈਂ ਜਾਵਾਂਗਾ।` - `Maĩ jāvā̃gā.` means “I will go”, for a male speaker. `ਮੈਂ ਜਾਵਾਂਗੀ।` - `Maĩ jāvā̃gī.` is the female-speaker form. The ending combines person-related material with a gender distinction. Learn these as complete future forms before trying to infer the entire paradigm.

### Useful future forms

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਜਾਵਾਂਗਾ | jāvā̃gā | I will go, masculine |
| ਜਾਵਾਂਗੀ | jāvā̃gī | I will go, feminine |
| ਜਾਵੇਗਾ | jāvegā | he will go |
| ਜਾਵੇਗੀ | jāvegī | she will go |
| ਆਵਾਂਗਾ | āvā̃gā | I will come, masculine |
| ਆਵਾਂਗੀ | āvā̃gī | I will come, feminine |
| ਕਰਾਂਗਾ | karā̃gā | I will do, masculine |
| ਕਰਾਂਗੀ | karā̃gī | I will do, feminine |

### Construct a plan

`ਮੈਂ ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਸਕੂਲ ਜਾਵਾਂਗੀ।` - `Maĩ kallh sakūl jāvā̃gī.` means “I will go to school tomorrow”, for a female speaker. The future form makes tomorrow the natural reading of `ਕੱਲ੍ਹ`. Compare the same day word with yesterday's completed `ਗਈ`: the calendar direction is supplied by the whole clause.

`ਉਹ ਸ਼ਾਮ ਨੂੰ ਆਵੇਗਾ।` - `Uh shām nū̃ āvegā.` means “He will come in the evening.” `ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜ ਵਜੇ ਆਵਾਂਗਾ।` - `Maĩ panj vaje āvā̃gā.` announces “I will come at five o'clock”, for a male speaker. Unlike the habitual arrival sentence, this explicitly commits the event to future time in the normal reading.

`ਮੈਂ ਅੱਜ ਕੰਮ ਨਹੀਂ ਕਰਾਂਗੀ।` - `Maĩ ajj kamm nahī̃ karā̃gī.` says “I will not work today”, for a female speaker. Negation still precedes the verb. There is no need to append the present copula after the completed future form; the future verb already carries the necessary finite information.

### Common trap

Do not use the first-person form for every pronoun. `ਜਾਵਾਂਗਾ` and `ਜਾਵੇਗਾ` are not interchangeable merely because both can describe a male. Person is part of the ending as well as gender. Also do not add `ਹਾਂ` by analogy with a present habitual sentence.

### Practice

Make a future statement.

1. I will go to school tomorrow. (female speaker)
2. He will come in the evening.
3. I will come at five o'clock. (male speaker)
4. I will not work today. (female speaker)

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੈਂ ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਸਕੂਲ ਜਾਵਾਂਗੀ।` - `Maĩ kallh sakūl jāvā̃gī.`
2. `ਉਹ ਸ਼ਾਮ ਨੂੰ ਆਵੇਗਾ।` - `Uh shām nū̃ āvegā.`
3. `ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜ ਵਜੇ ਆਵਾਂਗਾ।` - `Maĩ panj vaje āvā̃gā.`
4. `ਮੈਂ ਅੱਜ ਕੰਮ ਨਹੀਂ ਕਰਾਂਗੀ।` - `Maĩ ajj kamm nahī̃ karā̃gī.`

### Why these answers work

The future form identifies the subject's person and grammatical gender. Tomorrow is supported by the future verb in the first answer. The clock phrase does not require an extra postposition, and negation occupies its familiar preverbal position in the final sentence.

### Retrieve and recombine

Announce a school visit tomorrow and an arrival time. Then change the speaker from a woman to a man. Add one thing that person will not do, keeping the negative inside the same future sentence pattern.

## Lesson 21: Say what you can do and want to do

### Outcome and thinking point

Combine two actions into one verb phrase. English uses “can read” but “want to read”. Should Punjabi necessarily use the same form of read after both expressions? Compare a bare stem before ability with an infinitive before wanting.

`ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਪੜ੍ਹ ਸਕਦਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ panjābī paṛh sakdā hā̃.` means “I can read Punjabi”, for a male speaker. The main action is the stem `ਪੜ੍ਹ`; ability is carried by the agreeing `ਸਕਦਾ` plus auxiliary. A female speaker uses `ਸਕਦੀ`.

### Ability and intention

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਸਕਦਾ | sakdā | can, masculine singular |
| ਸਕਦੀ | sakdī | can, feminine singular |
| ਸਕਦੇ | sakde | can, masculine plural |
| ਚਾਹੁੰਦਾ | chāhundā | wants, masculine singular |
| ਚਾਹੁੰਦੀ | chāhundī | wants, feminine singular |
| ਸਿੱਖਣਾ | sikkhṇā | to learn |
| ਸਮਝਣਾ | samajhṇā | to understand |
| ਲਿਖਣਾ | likhṇā | to write |

### Compare the complements

`ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਸਿੱਖਣਾ ਚਾਹੁੰਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ panjābī sikkhṇā chāhundī hā̃.` means “I want to learn Punjabi”, for a female speaker. The learning action appears as an infinitive before the wanting expression. In `ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਲਿਖ ਸਕਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ panjābī likh sakdī hā̃.`, meaning “I can write Punjabi”, the main verb remains a bare stem before can.

`ਉਹ ਇੱਥੇ ਆ ਸਕਦਾ ਹੈ।` - `Uh itthe ā sakdā hai.` means “He can come here.” Depending on context, can may concern ability or practical possibility. If you intend permission, make the social situation clear rather than assuming every ability statement grants permission.

`ਮੈਂ ਹੁਣ ਨਹੀਂ ਆ ਸਕਦਾ।` - `Maĩ huṇ nahī̃ ā sakdā.` means “I cannot come now”, for a male speaker. This is a helpful refusal because it specifies a current limitation rather than a permanent inability. The present auxiliary can be omitted in this negative conversational form.

### Common trap

Do not say `ਪੜ੍ਹਨਾ ਸਕਦਾ` by copying the infinitive used before want. Different following verbs license different complement shapes. Conversely, a bare stem before wanting should not be assumed correct merely because it worked before ability.

### Practice

1. I can read Punjabi. (male speaker)
2. I can write Punjabi. (female speaker)
3. I want to learn Punjabi. (female speaker)
4. He can come here.

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਪੜ੍ਹ ਸਕਦਾ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ panjābī paṛh sakdā hā̃.`
2. `ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਲਿਖ ਸਕਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ panjābī likh sakdī hā̃.`
3. `ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਸਿੱਖਣਾ ਚਾਹੁੰਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ panjābī sikkhṇā chāhundī hā̃.`
4. `ਉਹ ਇੱਥੇ ਆ ਸਕਦਾ ਹੈ।` - `Uh itthe ā sakdā hai.`

### Why these answers work

Ability follows the bare action stem, while wanting follows an infinitive in the demonstrated construction. Gender agreement appears on the ability or wanting form, and person is shown by the final auxiliary. The language being learned is the object, not the controller of the wanting form.

### Retrieve and recombine

State one thing you can read, one thing you want to learn and one place you cannot come to now. Keep ability, desire and present availability distinct. Explain why the main action has a different shape in the desire sentence.

## Lesson 22: Join ideas and give a reason

### Outcome and thinking point

Connect two short clauses without losing the grammatical work inside each. If one person is working and another is studying, does adding “and” allow you to discard both verb endings? Build each clause first, then link them.

`ਅਮਨ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਦਾ ਹੈ ਅਤੇ ਉਹ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੀ ਹੈ।` - `Aman kamm kardā hai ate uh paṛhdī hai.` means “Aman works and she studies.” `ਅਤੇ` is a common written and; `ਤੇ` is frequent in everyday speech. The course uses both only when their register is clear, not as a claim that one belongs to a different language.

### Connections

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਅਤੇ | ate | and |
| ਤੇ | te | and, common conversational form |
| ਪਰ | par | but |
| ਕਿਉਂਕਿ | kiũki | because |
| ਇਸ ਲਈ | is laī | therefore, for this reason |
| ਵੀ | vī | also, too |
| ਸਿਰਫ਼ | siraf | only |
| ਅੱਜ | ajj | today |

### Keep the logical relationship visible

`ਮੈਂ ਆਵਾਂਗਾ ਪਰ ਉਹ ਨਹੀਂ ਆਵੇਗਾ।` - `Maĩ āvā̃gā par uh nahī̃ āvegā.` means “I will come, but he will not come”, for a male speaker. The two future forms reflect two subjects. But signals a contrast, not a change of tense or agreement.

`ਮੈਂ ਘਰ ਹਾਂ ਕਿਉਂਕਿ ਮੈਂ ਬਿਮਾਰ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ ghar hā̃ kiũki maĩ bimār hā̃.` means “I am at home because I am ill.” The reason clause follows because. You can often omit a repeated subject in natural speech, but keeping it explicit initially makes each clause easy to inspect.

`ਮੈਂ ਵੀ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ vī panjābī paṛhdī hā̃.` means “I also study Punjabi”, with the natural reading that the female speaker is another person studying it. The placement of `ਵੀ` influences what is being added. Put it near the element intended as “also”, rather than scattering it anywhere in the sentence.

### Common trap

Do not treat “because” and “therefore” as interchangeable labels. Because introduces a reason for another claim; therefore introduces the result drawn from a reason. A pair of clauses may both be grammatical while their connector presents the reasoning backwards.

### Practice

1. I will come, but he will not come. (male speaker)
2. I am home because I am ill.
3. I also study Punjabi. (female speaker)
4. Give the Punjabi word for “because”.

***Answers:***

1. `ਮੈਂ ਆਵਾਂਗਾ ਪਰ ਉਹ ਨਹੀਂ ਆਵੇਗਾ।` - `Maĩ āvā̃gā par uh nahī̃ āvegā.`
2. `ਮੈਂ ਘਰ ਹਾਂ ਕਿਉਂਕਿ ਮੈਂ ਬਿਮਾਰ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ ghar hā̃ kiũki maĩ bimār hā̃.`
3. `ਮੈਂ ਵੀ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਪੜ੍ਹਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ vī panjābī paṛhdī hā̃.`
4. `ਕਿਉਂਕਿ` - `kiũki`

### Why these answers work

The contrast sentence preserves separate future agreement for I and he. The reason sentence gives the illness as an explanation of the location. The addition particle follows the person being added to the group of learners. The final answer is a lexical retrieval requested as a phrase.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give a reason for being at home, contrast your plan with Aman's plan, and add yourself to a group of Punjabi learners. Before speaking, label the intended connection as reason, contrast or addition; then choose the connector that expresses it.

## Lesson 23: Compare and specify a choice

### Outcome and thinking point

Compare two familiar objects and select one. You know `ਤੋਂ` as from. Could a language reuse a starting-point relationship to mark the standard of comparison? Compare the two room sentences and find the object that serves as the reference point.

`ਇਹ ਕਮਰਾ ਉਸ ਕਮਰੇ ਤੋਂ ਵੱਡਾ ਹੈ।` - `Ih kamrā us kamre tõ vaḍḍā hai.` means “This room is larger than that room.” The reference room is followed by `ਤੋਂ`; the adjective describes the room being evaluated. No special English-style comparative suffix is added to `ਵੱਡਾ`.

### Choosing and comparing

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਵੱਡਾ | vaḍḍā | large |
| ਛੋਟਾ | chhoṭā | small |
| ਮਹਿੰਗਾ | mahingā | expensive |
| ਸਸਤਾ | sastā | inexpensive |
| ਜ਼ਿਆਦਾ | ziādā | more |
| ਘੱਟ | ghaṭṭ | less |
| ਸਭ ਤੋਂ | sabh tõ | most, of all |
| ਕਿਹੜਾ | kihṛā | which, masculine singular |
| ਕਿਹੜੀ | kihṛī | which, feminine singular |

### Follow the agreement

`ਇਹ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਉਸ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਤੋਂ ਸਸਤੀ ਹੈ।` - `Ih kitāb us kitāb tõ sastī hai.` means “This book is cheaper than that book.” The describing adjective is feminine because the book being described is feminine. The compared-against noun is inside its own `ਤੋਂ` phrase.

`ਇਹ ਸਭ ਤੋਂ ਵੱਡਾ ਕਮਰਾ ਹੈ।` - `Ih sabh tõ vaḍḍā kamrā hai.` means “This is the largest room.” The phrase `ਸਭ ਤੋਂ` extends the comparison to the relevant set. The set must be understood from context; this sentence need not claim the room is larger than every room on earth.

`ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਕਿਹੜੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਚਾਹੀਦੀ ਹੈ?` - `Tuhānū̃ kihṛī kitāb chāhīdī hai?` asks “Which book do you need?” Unlike a completely open what question, which often selects from an available or understood set. Here both the question word and the need form agree with book.

### Common trap

Do not make a comparison by placing `ਜ਼ਿਆਦਾ` before every adjective without attending to natural phrasing. The adjective plus `ਤੋਂ` construction already establishes the comparison in these examples. Also distinguish more objects from a greater degree of a property: quantity and comparison are related but not identical tasks.

### Practice

Compare or select the named item.

1. This room is larger than that room.
2. This book is cheaper than that book.
3. This is the largest room.
4. Which book do you need? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `ਇਹ ਕਮਰਾ ਉਸ ਕਮਰੇ ਤੋਂ ਵੱਡਾ ਹੈ।` - `Ih kamrā us kamre tõ vaḍḍā hai.`
2. `ਇਹ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਉਸ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਤੋਂ ਸਸਤੀ ਹੈ।` - `Ih kitāb us kitāb tõ sastī hai.`
3. `ਇਹ ਸਭ ਤੋਂ ਵੱਡਾ ਕਮਰਾ ਹੈ।` - `Ih sabh tõ vaḍḍā kamrā hai.`
4. `ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਕਿਹੜੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਚਾਹੀਦੀ ਹੈ?` - `Tuhānū̃ kihṛī kitāb chāhīdī hai?`

### Why these answers work

The standard of comparison follows an oblique noun phrase and precedes `ਤੋਂ`. Agreement belongs to the item being described. The superlative uses a relevant “all” set, while the final question combines a selecting question word with the earlier experiencer construction for needs.

Compare two rooms for size and two books for price. Select one book as the item you need. Explain why the second room appears as `ਕਮਰੇ`, but the adjective still describes the first room.

## Lesson 24: Manage a practical conversation

### Outcome and thinking point

Combine identification, need, location and repair in a short service exchange. You hear an answer but miss one word. Is it better to pretend to understand or to request a repeat using language you already control? Construct a useful repair before reading the exchange.

`ਦੁਬਾਰਾ ਬੋਲੋ ਜੀ।` - `Dubārā bolo jī.` means “Please say it again.” `ਹੌਲੀ ਬੋਲੋ ਜੀ।` asks for slower speech. These requests let you keep a conversation going without needing a long explanation of your language level. They are especially useful when directions or quantities matter.

### Service and repair words

| Gurmukhi | Romanisation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ਦੁਬਾਰਾ | dubārā | again |
| ਮਾਫ਼ ਕਰਨਾ | māf karnā | excuse me, pardon |
| ਧੰਨਵਾਦ | dhannavād | thank you |
| ਦੁਕਾਨ | dukān | shop |
| ਸਟੇਸ਼ਨ | saṭeshan | station |
| ਟਿਕਟ | ṭikaṭ | ticket |
| ਰਸਤਾ | rastā | way, route |
| ਸਹੀ | sahī | correct |

### Work through a short exchange

Customer: `ਮੈਨੂੰ ਇੱਕ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਚਾਹੀਦੀ ਹੈ।` - `Mainū̃ ikk kitāb chāhīdī hai.` means “I need one book.” Seller: `ਕਿਹੜੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ?` - `Kihṛī kitāb?` asks “Which book?” A short noun-phrase question is natural because the previous sentence already supplies the need frame.

Customer: `ਮੈਨੂੰ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਦੀ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਚਾਹੀਦੀ ਹੈ।` - `Mainū̃ panjābī dī kitāb chāhīdī hai.` means “I need a Punjabi book”, here a book for or about the language in the shop context. Seller: `ਇਹ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਲਓ ਜੀ।` - `Ih kitāb lao jī.` offers “Please take this book.” The customer can then ask a price or give thanks.

At a station, the same need construction takes a ticket instead of a book. A place question can ask `ਸਟੇਸ਼ਨ ਕਿੱਥੇ ਹੈ?` - `Saṭeshan kitthe hai?`, “Where is the station?” If the reply is too fast, request a repeat and identify the location phrase you did understand. A conversation is not a test of reproducing one fixed dialogue; it is a sequence of small information problems.

### Common trap

Do not translate every English sentence in a dialogue into a full Punjabi clause when context naturally supports a short reply. Conversely, do not use a fragment if it leaves a crucial quantity, destination or negation unclear. Brevity is helpful only when the missing material is recoverable.

### Practice

Produce the requested turn in the conversation.

1. Please say it again.
2. I need one ticket.
3. Where is the station?
4. Please take this book.

***Answers:***

1. `ਦੁਬਾਰਾ ਬੋਲੋ ਜੀ।` - `Dubārā bolo jī.`
2. `ਮੈਨੂੰ ਇੱਕ ਟਿਕਟ ਚਾਹੀਦੀ ਹੈ।` - `Mainū̃ ikk ṭikaṭ chāhīdī hai.`
3. `ਸਟੇਸ਼ਨ ਕਿੱਥੇ ਹੈ?` - `Saṭeshan kitthe hai?`
4. `ਇਹ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਲਓ ਜੀ।` - `Ih kitāb lao jī.`

### Why these answers work

The repair and offer use polite imperatives. Ticket takes feminine agreement in the need construction. The station question seeks location and keeps the singular copula. None of the turns needs an English-style do auxiliary or an extra word for an indefinite article.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create a four-turn exchange at a bookshop and another at a station. Include one need, one information question, one repair request and one closing thanks. Change the content while preserving the purpose of each turn.

4. I want to learn Punjabi. (female speaker)

***Answers:***

1. `ਅਮਨ ਹੁਣ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਪੜ੍ਹ ਰਿਹਾ ਹੈ।` - `Aman huṇ panjābī paṛh rihā hai.`
2. `ਅਮਨ ਨੇ ਕਿਤਾਬ ਪੜ੍ਹੀ।` - `Aman ne kitāb paṛhī.`
3. `ਉਹ ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਸਕੂਲ ਜਾਵੇਗਾ।` - `Uh kallh sakūl jāvegā.`
4. `ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਸਿੱਖਣਾ ਚਾਹੁੰਦੀ ਹਾਂ।` - `Maĩ panjābī sikkhṇā chāhundī hā̃.`

### Why these answers work

The progressive agrees with Aman, while the completed reading form agrees with the unmarked feminine book. The future resolves tomorrow and encodes third-person masculine agreement. Wanting takes an infinitive and then agrees with the female speaker in the final answer.

### Retrieve and recombine

Tell a six-sentence story about a learner: identity, daily routine, current activity, past location, completed event and future plan. Ask two questions about the story, including one respectful request for clarification. Revisit any pattern whose agreement you cannot explain, and seek fluent-speaker feedback on pronunciation and natural regional phrasing.
