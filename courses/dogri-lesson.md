# Dogri: A pattern-first foundation

Editorial status: original AI-assisted draft; fluent-speaker review pending.

This is a deliberately small conversational foundation in the Jammu-area teaching variety illustrated by NCERT and CIIL. Dogri is a language in its own right. Similarities with Punjabi or Hindi do not make their conjugations interchangeable. Regional speech and writing vary. We use Devanagari, acknowledge the historical Dogre/Takri tradition and other writing practices, and do not present one classroom model as every speaker's usage.

The lessons use a consistent, small selection of spellings from the instructional sources. You may also encounter forms such as मिगी beside मिकी, तुंदा beside थौआड़ा, and ने beside न. Here मिकी, थौआड़ा and ने form the main learning set. These are not instructions to correct a community's own usage. For home we retain the conversational कर and its location form करै used in the sentence source; other materials write घर. We teach the selected forms together rather than automatically applying another language's spelling or pronunciation rules.

Romanisation is a reading aid, not a complete phonetic transcription: ā ī ū indicate long vowels, ṭ ḍ ṇ ṛ retroflex consonants, doubled letters longer consonants, and a combining tilde a nasal vowel. We write ch as in English "chair" and chh for its aspirated counterpart. The short location word च is written ch too. ऐ is represented ai and ए e when the source distinguishes their spellings. Long e and o are written without macrons. Dogri has meaningful tone contrasts; the source-based spelling aid does not mark every tone. Do not pronounce written aspirates by mechanically importing Hindi values. CIIL's primer explains that historical aspirate spellings interact with tone. A fluent speaker or reliable recording is needed to settle the sound of a particular word.

This course builds useful statements, questions and short exchanges from verified patterns. It does not offer a complete conjugation system: masculine first-person habits, restricted third-person past states and selected intentions are labelled as such. Feminine and respectful forms appear where evidence supports them. Native-speaker review and audio work remain important next steps.

### References and review notes

The grammatical inventory was checked against [Sunil Kumar's CIIL-authored Dogri POS study](https://www.languageinindia.com/jan2018/sunilkumardogritagsetfinal.pdf), the [CIIL–NCERT Dogri primer](https://ciil.gov.in/primers/Dogri_Primer.pdf), and NCERT's *Bhasha Sangam*, volume 04, read in a [public document copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/704019828/04-Dogri). The latter establishes connected everyday usage; the former sources support word classes, case, agreement and script cautions. The teaching explanations, lesson organisation, practice and recombinations here are original, rather than a reproduction of NCERT's hundred-sentence sequence. Some source renderings have inconsistent orthography; uncertain broad paradigms have been excluded.

## Lesson 1: A name is a complete message

**Outcome:** Introduce a name with a short sentence ending in the linking verb.

### Think first

In "my name Asha is", which part supplies the new information? Can you keep the surrounding words and change just the name?

### Build the pattern

Begin with a message you can immediately reuse: मेरा नां आशा ऐ, merā nā̃ āshā ai, "My name is Asha." The order places the name before the linking word ऐ. English puts "is" before the name; Dogri puts this linking verb at the end. Treat the final word as part of the sentence rather than an optional sound.

The piece मेरा नां means "my name". For now learn it as a unit. You are naming the label by which somebody is called, not describing that person's age, occupation or gender. Consequently a woman can use मेरा नां too. The shape of मेरा belongs with the noun नां; it is not a signal that the speaker is a man. Later lessons separate the ownership words and show their agreement.

Names can be inserted without translating them. An English speaker called Anita does not need to choose a Dogri identity or replace her name with a regional one. In the examples, आशा and रवि are simply convenient names. You may substitute your own established spelling when you know it.

Start reading from the left, noticing the horizontal headline of Devanagari letters. A vowel sign may appear around its consonant, so do not assume that every visible mark is a separate syllable. In नां the vowel is nasal: the reading aid nā̃ reminds you to let air pass through the nose. It is different from adding a clearly pronounced final English n. Tone requires listening as well; this reading aid does not resolve all Dogri pitch contrasts.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| मेरा | merā | my, with the noun used here |
| नां | nā̃ | name |
| ऐ | ai | is, third-person singular |
| आशा | āshā | Asha |
| रवि | ravi | Ravi |
| नीना | nīnā | Nina |

### Worked examples

मेरा नां नीना ऐ, merā nā̃ nīnā ai, means "My name is Nina." Only the inserted name has changed. The surrounding structure remains intact.

मेरा नां रवि ऐ, merā nā̃ ravi ai, provides the same information for Ravi. Read the four pieces as one message, with a small natural fall at the end. नां रवि alone would identify a name in a list, but the full pattern makes a statement.

### Common trap

Do not replace ऐ with Hindi है or English "is" simply because they have a comparable function. Remember the actual Dogri ending.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Say: My name is Asha.
2. Say: My name is Nina.
3. Supply the Dogri phrase "my name".
4. Complete with the linking word: मेरा नां रवि __.

***Answers:***

1. `मेरा नां आशा ऐ।` - `merā nā̃ āshā ai.`.
2. `मेरा नां नीना ऐ।` - `merā nā̃ nīnā ai.`.
3. `मेरा नां` - `merā nā̃`.
4. `ऐ` - `ai`.

### Why these answers work

The first two answers change the information slot while preserving the frame. The third extracts the ownership phrase. The fourth supplies the final link; it does not require a new name or another pronoun.

### Retrieve and recombine

Cover the examples and introduce yourself once. Then introduce an imaginary speaker with a different name. Check that both sentences still end with the linking word.

## Lesson 2: Point to one thing and say what it is

**Outcome:** Identify one visible object using एह् and a final ऐ.

### Think first

If एह् means "this", what would be the simplest order for "This is a school"? Does English word order help or get in the way?

### Build the pattern

The basic identification frame is एह् + identifying noun + ऐ. The first word directs attention to something near the speaker or already being indicated. The noun tells the listener what that thing is. The final ऐ makes the link. एह् स्कूल ऐ, eh skūl ai, identifies a building as a school.

You do not need a separate word corresponding to every English "a" or "the". English often requires an article before a singular count noun. This Dogri frame can identify a thing with the noun alone. इक, introduced below, specifies "one" when counting matters; it is not a compulsory decoration before every noun.

The same nearby demonstrative can stand before different nouns. In this first set, you are not deriving a complete gender table. You are learning an attested pointing form and keeping the predicate singular. Words borrowed into Dogri, such as स्कूल, participate in Dogri sentences; their history does not make the whole sentence English.

Devanagari ह् in एह् is written with a sign suppressing its inherent vowel. Our practical reading aid eh is deliberately modest: it is not an instruction to add an extra ha syllable. The detailed interaction of h spellings and tone needs spoken modelling. Reading the script accurately and reproducing tone are related tasks, but they are not identical.

Make each identification meaningful. Pointing at a photograph of a river and saying its Dogri name is a genuine act of identifying. Merely reciting all the nouns in order does not practise choosing the right category for what you see.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| एह् | eh | this |
| स्कूल | skūl | school |
| बाग | bāg | garden |
| नदी | nadī | river |
| जंगल | jangal | forest |
| इक | ik | one |

### Worked examples

एह् बाग ऐ, eh bāg ai, means "This is a garden." The speaker points; the listener learns the category.

एह् नदी ऐ, eh nadī ai, means "This is a river." The linking form remains ऐ with this single referent. एह् जंगल ऐ, eh jangal ai, identifies a forest. You are not claiming that a river and a forest share grammatical gender, only observing the same singular linking form in these sentences.

### Common trap

Do not insert English articles into Dogri or force इक into every identification. Use it when you need to express the number one.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Point out: This is a river.
2. Point out: This is a garden.
3. Point out: This is a forest.
4. Write the Dogri word for one.

***Answers:***

1. `एह् नदी ऐ।` - `eh nadī ai.`.
2. `एह् बाग ऐ।` - `eh bāg ai.`.
3. `एह् जंगल ऐ।` - `eh jangal ai.`.
4. `इक` - `ik`.

### Why these answers work

All three statements put the identifying noun between the demonstrative and the linking verb. Their English translations need an article, but their Dogri forms do not. The last answer names a number rather than a linking word.

### Retrieve and recombine

Find three pictures and identify them without reading a sentence. Change their order and try again. Keep the final verb even when the noun becomes familiar.

## Lesson 3: Here and there: existence before description

**Outcome:** Say that a thing exists in a place, and distinguish one from several.

### Think first

Compare "This is a garden" with "There is a garden here". Are you naming a visible thing, or announcing what can be found at a location?

### Build the pattern

Location plus a noun plus the linking verb can announce what exists somewhere. इत्थे इक बाग ऐ, itthe ik bāg ai, means "There is one garden here." The location comes first, followed by what is present. English uses a special "there is" construction, but no dummy English-style subject needs to be translated into Dogri.

उत्थे points to "there", a location away from the speaker or already established in the conversation. It is not the same as the English dummy "there" in "there is". In उत्थे इक स्कूल ऐ the word genuinely supplies a location. A useful check is to ask whether you could point in its direction.

For several gardens, use बाग ने, bāg ne, in this existence pattern. The singular and plural of this noun look the same in the direct form used here; the surrounding words and verb clarify the number. बड़े बाग ने means "there are big gardens" in the relevant context. Later you will learn why बड़े differs from बड़ा.

This lesson restricts plural production to nouns and forms actually introduced. It does not claim that every Dogri plural is unchanged. Some nouns visibly change, and postpositions can trigger another form even when a direct plural does not. Learning a noun with a small sentence avoids the misleading belief that one spelling answers every grammatical need.

A location statement can also organise a simple map description. First announce the place, then name one thing in it. Let the listener finish picturing that item before adding another sentence. Clear reference matters more than accumulating a long list.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| इत्थे | itthe | here |
| उत्थे | utthe | there |
| इक | ik | one |
| बाग | bāg | garden; gardens in this direct form |
| ने | ne | are, plural linking form |
| बड़े | baṛe | big, masculine plural here |

### Worked examples

इत्थे इक स्कूल ऐ, itthe ik skūl ai, introduces a school at this location. उत्थे इक बाग ऐ, utthe ik bāg ai, shifts attention elsewhere.

उत्थे बड़े बाग ने, utthe baṛe bāg ne, announces several big gardens. The change from ऐ to ने matters: the sentence no longer refers to a single garden. It does not describe where the speaker personally lives.

### Common trap

Do not use the third-person plural ने as a universal equivalent of English "are". The respectful second-person ending will be ओ in a later pattern.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Say: Here there is one garden.
2. Say: There there is one school.
3. Say: There are big gardens there.
4. Supply the plural link in बाग __.

***Answers:***

1. `इत्थे इक बाग ऐ।` - `itthe ik bāg ai.`.
2. `उत्थे इक स्कूल ऐ।` - `utthe ik skūl ai.`.
3. `उत्थे बड़े बाग ने।` - `utthe baṛe bāg ne.`.
4. `ने` - `ne`.

### Why these answers work

The first two locate a singular item and therefore use ऐ. The third announces a plural set and uses ने. The last answer retrieves the plural ending alone; it is not the word for the location "there".

### Retrieve and recombine

Sketch a two-place map. Put one garden near you and several gardens farther away. Describe both areas from memory, then move the labels and change only the location words.

## Lesson 4: Ask for the missing name

**Outcome:** Turn a known identity frame into a question with के.

### Think first

In "my name Ravi is", replace Ravi with "what". Which part of the sentence needs to move?

### Build the pattern

A question can keep the same information structure as its answer. In मेरा नां के ऐ? the word के occupies the unknown name slot. More naturally, when asking somebody else's name you use थौआड़ा नां के ऐ?, thoāṛā nā̃ ke ai, "What is your name?" This lesson uses थौआड़ा as the respectful ownership word with नां.

The word के asks for information. It is not the linking verb, and it does not create an English-style reversal of subject and verb. The link remains at the end. Once the listener supplies the missing name, the ordinary statement frame returns. This connection between a question and its answer makes new sentences easier to build.

Keep social choice separate from sentence mechanics. The respectful form is a useful default with an unfamiliar adult, a teacher, or somebody whose preferences you do not yet know. It does not mean that all friends and families use it with one another. Familiar forms appear in regional conversations, but you need not imitate them before you can judge their social fit.

A speaker can answer with just a name when the question is clear. In practice here, use the full sentence because it makes the frame visible. Outside an exercise, a brief answer is not grammatically defective merely because it is brief. The listener's question supplies the missing context.

When reading, distinguish the nasal vowel of नां from the final consonant in a word such as नाटक. Our reading aid keeps the tilde for the nasal vowel rather than using an arbitrary letter n everywhere. This helps preserve a contrast that ordinary English spelling might obscure.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| के | ke | what |
| थौआड़ा | thoāṛā | your, respectful, with this noun |
| नां | nā̃ | name |
| मेरा | merā | my, with this noun |
| कुसुम | kusum | Kusum |
| अमित | amit | Amit |

### Worked examples

थौआड़ा नां के ऐ?, thoāṛā nā̃ ke ai, asks the listener for a name. मेरा नां कुसुम ऐ, merā nā̃ kusum ai, supplies one.

मेरा नां अमित ऐ, merā nā̃ amit ai, has the same structure with different information. If you already know the person is Amit, repeating the question would serve a different purpose, such as checking what you heard. Intonation and context help distinguish a genuine request from confirmation.

### Common trap

Do not answer a name question by repeating थौआड़ा. The question says "your" from the questioner’s viewpoint; the answer normally says "my" from the respondent’s viewpoint.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Ask respectfully: What is your name?
2. Answer as Kusum with a full sentence.
3. Answer as Amit with a full sentence.
4. Write the question word for what.

***Answers:***

1. `थौआड़ा नां के ऐ?` - `thoāṛā nā̃ ke ai?`.
2. `मेरा नां कुसुम ऐ।` - `merā nā̃ kusum ai.`.
3. `मेरा नां अमित ऐ।` - `merā nā̃ amit ai.`.
4. `के` - `ke`.

### Why these answers work

The question places के in the unknown-information slot. The answers change perspective from your to my and replace the question word with a name. The final answer isolates the question word so it can be reused independently.

### Retrieve and recombine

Write two name cards. Ask the same respectful question for each card, then answer as that person. Cover the cards and recall both names in complete sentences.

## Lesson 5: My, your and our follow the noun

**Outcome:** Choose ownership forms according to the thing or person described.

### Think first

Why might a male speaker say मेरी माँ while a female speaker says मेरा नां? Is ownership agreement describing the owner or the following noun?

### Build the pattern

Dogri ownership words change with the grammatical features of the noun they accompany. मेरा goes with नां in our examples, while मेरी goes with माँ. The speaker's own gender does not determine this difference. The phrase names whose mother or whose name is being discussed; its agreement follows the possessed noun.

The same useful contrast appears in साड़ा स्कूल and साड़ी क्लास: "our school" and "our class". This is an ownership relationship, not a statement that the speaker owns a building as property. English "my school" likewise usually means the school one attends. Keep the everyday relationship in mind when translating.

You will also encounter respectful थौआड़ा and थौआड़ी. Here the ending changes with the following noun, while the respectful viewpoint remains stable. Learning the pairs side by side is more useful than memorising one English translation and hoping the right ending appears automatically.

These are direct forms in a limited set of noun phrases. A phrase inside a larger postpositional construction may require a different form. For example, a later lesson uses मेरे rather than मेरा before a case-marked school phrase. Do not conclude from today's two-way contrast that there are only two possible endings.

A practical way to make the choice is to begin with the noun and then attach ownership. First choose माँ, then मेरी. First choose स्कूल, then साड़ा. This reverses the common beginner habit of translating English words from left to right before knowing the noun. It also prevents the owner's identity from distracting you from the actual agreement controller.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| मेरा | merā | my, masculine direct singular here |
| मेरी | merī | my, feminine singular here |
| साड़ा | sāṛā | our, masculine direct singular here |
| साड़ी | sāṛī | our, feminine singular here |
| माँ | mā̃ | mother |
| क्लास | klās | class |

### Worked examples

मेरा स्कूल, merā skūl, and मेरी क्लास, merī klās, belong to the same speaker. The nouns cause the different endings.

एह् साड़ी क्लास ऐ, eh sāṛī klās ai, means "This is our class." एह् थौआड़ा स्कूल ऐ, eh thoāṛā skūl ai, identifies the listener's school respectfully. The copula links the whole ownership phrase to the thing being indicated.

### Common trap

Do not change मेरा to मेरी merely because the owner is female. Choose the form using the noun inside the ownership phrase.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Write: my mother.
2. Write: our school.
3. Say: This is our class.
4. Say respectfully: This is your school.

***Answers:***

1. `मेरी माँ` - `merī mā̃`.
2. `साड़ा स्कूल` - `sāṛā skūl`.
3. `एह् साड़ी क्लास ऐ।` - `eh sāṛī klās ai.`.
4. `एह् थौआड़ा स्कूल ऐ।` - `eh thoāṛā skūl ai.`.

### Why these answers work

Mother and class take feminine ownership forms in the phrases taught here. School takes the masculine form. The two full statements preserve the identification frame from Lesson 2, showing that a noun phrase can grow without changing the position of the final verb.

### Retrieve and recombine

Choose one masculine and one feminine noun from this lesson. Attach my, then our. Say the resulting four phrases without thinking about whether the imaginary owner is a man or a woman.

## Lesson 6: In a place: the small word च

**Outcome:** Use a location phrase and notice the noun form before its postposition.

### Think first

English puts "in" before "the school". If च comes after its noun, where will it appear in a Dogri phrase?

### Build the pattern

The word च follows the place it relates to the sentence. स्कूले च, skūle ch, means "in the school"; बागे च, bāge ch, means "in the garden". Because it follows the noun, grammarians call it a postposition. The label is less important than the practical order: place first, relation second.

Notice स्कूल beside स्कूले च and बाग beside बागे च. A noun can have an oblique form before a postposition. Do not merely attach च to whichever dictionary spelling you happen to remember. This lesson gives each useful phrase intact; broader noun classes require more evidence than these two examples alone.

करै च is the selected conversational phrase for "in the home". The source's spellings for home vary, so we consistently learn this unit instead of pretending every spelling represents an identical sound. The English translation depends on context: a statement about where something is may naturally use "at home".

A location phrase can precede a statement of existence. स्कूले च इक बाग ऐ says that there is a garden in the school grounds. Here "in the school" describes the institution or compound rather than requiring a garden inside a classroom. Translating relationships requires ordinary real-world interpretation as well as grammar.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| स्कूल | skūl | school, direct form |
| स्कूले च | skūle ch | in the school |
| बाग | bāg | garden, direct form |
| बागे च | bāge ch | in the garden |
| करै च | karai ch | at home; in the house |
| च | ch | in, postposition |

### Worked examples

बागे च इक स्कूल ऐ, bāge ch ik skūl ai, imagines a school situated in a garden. Reverse the two nouns and the geography changes: स्कूले च इक बाग ऐ, skūle ch ik bāg ai, places the garden within school grounds.

करै च इक बिल्ली ऐ, karai ch ik billī ai, says there is a cat at home. बिल्ली, billī, is the new noun "cat".

### Common trap

Do not reverse the whole sentence just because च follows its noun. Only the local noun-plus-postposition relationship needs this ordering.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Write: in the garden.
2. Write: in the school.
3. Say: There is a garden in the school grounds.
4. Say: There is a cat at home.

***Answers:***

1. `बागे च` - `bāge ch`.
2. `स्कूले च` - `skūle ch`.
3. `स्कूले च इक बाग ऐ।` - `skūle ch ik bāg ai.`.
4. `करै च इक बिल्ली ऐ।` - `karai ch ik billī ai.`.

### Why these answers work

The first two answers preserve the supplied oblique forms. The final statements use the place phrase as a frame for existence. The cat remains singular, so its sentence ends with ऐ just like the sentence about one garden.

### Retrieve and recombine

Draw a school inside a garden, then a garden inside a school. Describe both pictures. Cover the noun forms and reconstruct the two location phrases before making the statements.

## Lesson 7: Whose name? Connecting two nouns

**Outcome:** Build a possessor phrase with दा and keep its agreement target clear.

### Think first

In "my mother’s name", is the final thing being named the mother or the name? Which noun should control the linking ownership word?

### Build the pattern

The connector दा links a possessor to a masculine noun such as नां. मेरी माँ दा नां means "my mother's name". The two ownership choices do different jobs: मेरी belongs with माँ, while दा belongs with नां. The phrase is not inconsistent simply because it contains both feminine and masculine endings.

The resulting noun phrase can go directly into the name frame. मेरी माँ दा नां नीना ऐ identifies the name of the speaker's mother. You have enlarged one part of the sentence without altering the final linking verb.

With school, the phrase is मेरे स्कूले दा नां. The possessor itself stands before a postposition, so स्कूल appears as स्कूले and the preceding ownership form is मेरे. This shows why learning only मेरा and मेरी was not enough for every context. Track both relationships: the inner possessor phrase has its own case shape, while दा links that phrase to the outer noun.

Do not generalise this one example into a full noun declension chart. Use the secure phrases to understand how the construction works. New nouns should be learned with an attested oblique form when their shape is uncertain. Accurate small constructions are more productive than guessed large tables.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| दा | dā | of, masculine agreement here |
| मेरी माँ दा नां | merī mā̃ dā nā̃ | my mother’s name |
| मेरे | mere | my in this oblique phrase |
| स्कूले | skūle | school, oblique form |
| मेरे स्कूले दा नां | mere skūle dā nā̃ | my school’s name |
| दी | dī | feminine genitive form, for later use |

### Worked examples

मेरी माँ दा नां आशा ऐ, merī mā̃ dā nā̃ āshā ai, names Asha as the speaker's mother.

मेरे स्कूले दा नां के ऐ?, mere skūle dā nā̃ ke ai, asks "What is my school's name?" This would suit a guessing game rather than an ordinary introduction. Grammar alone does not decide whether a question makes sense in its social situation.

### Common trap

Do not choose दी merely because the possessor is a mother. The outer noun नां controls दा in the phrase used here.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Write: my mother’s name.
2. Write: my school’s name.
3. Say: My mother’s name is Nina.
4. Complete: मेरी माँ __ नां.

***Answers:***

1. `मेरी माँ दा नां` - `merī mā̃ dā nā̃`.
2. `मेरे स्कूले दा नां` - `mere skūle dā nā̃`.
3. `मेरी माँ दा नां नीना ऐ।` - `merī mā̃ dā nā̃ nīnā ai.`.
4. `दा` - `dā`.

### Why these answers work

The name noun calls for दा. Mother still keeps मेरी inside the possessor phrase. School appears in its oblique form because it comes before the postposition. The complete sentence places the person’s name before ऐ.

### Retrieve and recombine

Build "my mother’s name" from the inside out: mother, my mother, my mother’s name. Then add a fictional name and the linking verb. Explain the two different agreement choices aloud.

## Lesson 8: Big garden, big gardens, big class

**Outcome:** Make a small adjective agree with the noun it describes.

### Think first

Compare बड़ा बाग, बड़े बाग and बड़ी क्लास. Which change points to number, and which points to gender?

### Build the pattern

The adjective बड़ा changes its ending in the combinations introduced here. बड़ा बाग is "a big garden"; बड़े बाग is "big gardens"; बड़ी क्लास is "a big class". Keep the noun and adjective together while learning. English "big" remains unchanged, so word-for-word translation conceals an important Dogri pattern.

The linking verb gives additional information. एह् बड़ा बाग ऐ describes one garden. इत्थे बड़े बाग ने describes several. Both the adjective and the verb help the listener recover the number. Agreement is therefore not an isolated spelling exercise; it makes reference clearer.

An adjective can belong inside an identifying noun phrase. You do not need to invent an extra word meaning "which is" every time you say "big garden". Place the description next to the noun and let the whole phrase fill the slot you already know.

This lesson presents a changing adjective, not a claim that all Dogri adjectives share its pattern. The CIIL word-class source also lists adjectives whose form does not visibly identify the noun's gender. Memorising the behaviour of a useful adjective prevents a false universal rule.

The feminine example बड़ी क्लास can mean a large class as a group or a higher class in a suitable school context. Here we mean a large group. Context restricts meaning even when the grammar is identical.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| बड़ा | baṛā | big, masculine singular direct |
| बड़े | baṛe | big, masculine plural direct |
| बड़ी | baṛī | big, feminine singular |
| बाग | bāg | garden; gardens here |
| क्लास | klās | class, feminine |
| स्कूल | skūl | school, masculine here |

### Worked examples

एह् बड़ा स्कूल ऐ, eh baṛā skūl ai, identifies a large school. एह् बड़ी क्लास ऐ, eh baṛī klās ai, identifies a large class.

उत्थे बड़े बाग ने, utthe baṛe bāg ne, describes several gardens there. Compare the singular बड़े-looking oblique phrases you may meet later: an ending can have different jobs, so always inspect the complete phrase.

### Common trap

Do not assume every word ending in e is plural in every environment. Here the direct plural is taught alongside its plural linking verb; case-marked phrases require separate attention.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Write: a big garden.
2. Write: big gardens.
3. Say: This is a big class.
4. Say: This is a big school.

***Answers:***

1. `बड़ा बाग` - `baṛā bāg`.
2. `बड़े बाग` - `baṛe bāg`.
3. `एह् बड़ी क्लास ऐ।` - `eh baṛī klās ai.`.
4. `एह् बड़ा स्कूल ऐ।` - `eh baṛā skūl ai.`.

### Why these answers work

The adjective follows the gender and number of its noun in each supplied combination. The two statements are singular even though their nouns belong to different genders, so both retain ऐ. The plural phrase needs no sentence-ending verb because only a phrase was requested.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine a small map with one large school and several large gardens. Describe it twice, first as full statements and then as labels. Identify the words that carry number information.

## Lesson 9: Which one and where?

**Outcome:** Choose an information question that matches the answer you need.

### Think first

Would a person answer "which river?" with a location, a river’s name, or a number? How is that different from answering "where?"?

### Build the pattern

केड़ा asks "which" with a masculine noun in the direct singular examples here; केड़ी accompanies a feminine noun such as नदी. The question selects an item from a relevant set. केड़ा स्कूल asks which school, not how many schools or where a school is situated.

कुत्थे asks for a location. A location answer can be as short as इत्थे or उत्थे when the thing under discussion is already clear. In a fuller existence exchange, बाग कुत्थे ऐ? asks "Where is the garden?" The question word occupies the position of the missing location before the final link.

Do not treat all English question words as synonyms for "what". A question about which river expects identification; a question about where a river is expects a place. The grammatical shape of an answer is a useful clue to what the questioner wants.

The choice between केड़ा and केड़ी illustrates agreement without requiring a complete interrogative paradigm. We deliberately practise only direct singular nouns already introduced. A question inside a postpositional phrase may have another form, so preserve such phrases as whole units when you encounter them.

To build a useful exchange, decide on the answer before you ask the question. If the intended answer is a school name, choose a name or selection question. If the intended answer is "there", choose a location question. This planning makes the language an information exchange rather than a string of question-shaped sounds.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| केड़ा | keṛā | which, masculine singular here |
| केड़ी | keṛī | which, feminine singular here |
| कुत्थे | kutthe | where |
| नदी | nadī | river, feminine |
| तवी | tavī | Tawi, river name |
| उत्थे | utthe | there |

### Worked examples

स्कूल कुत्थे ऐ?, skūl kutthe ai, asks where the school is. उत्थे, utthe, is a possible brief answer when pointing resolves the place.

केड़ी नदी?, keṛī nadī, asks which river. तवी नदी, tavī nadī, identifies one. Neither short expression needs to pretend to be a complete standalone sentence when it is part of a clear exchange.

### Common trap

Do not answer a where-question merely with a category label such as "school". Give a location or use a fuller phrase that actually locates it.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

2. Ask: Where is the school?
3. Write the phrase: which river?
4. Write the phrase: which school?

***Answers:***

1. `बाग कुत्थे ऐ?` - `bāg kutthe ai?`.
2. `स्कूल कुत्थे ऐ?` - `skūl kutthe ai?`.
3. `केड़ी नदी?` - `keṛī nadī?`.
4. `केड़ा स्कूल?` - `keṛā skūl?`.

The first two questions keep the final singular link and use कुत्थे for the missing location. The last two are selection phrases: their question words agree with river and school respectively. They can be used naturally after a speaker mentions an unspecified river or school.

## Lesson 10: Numbers tell you what to count

**Outcome:** Count a small set and keep counting separate from articles and descriptions.

### Think first

If a garden is big, does that tell you whether there is one garden or more? What does इक add that बड़ा cannot?

### Build the pattern

A number and an adjective answer different questions. इक gives a quantity; बड़ा gives a description. You can combine them in इक बड़ा बाग, "one big garden". The number makes the singular reference explicit, while the adjective describes that one item.

This lesson introduces a small counting set from the Dogri primer: इक, दो, त्रै, चार, पंज, छे. Say them with their Dogri forms rather than silently replacing the unfamiliar third item with the Hindi word. Similarity elsewhere in the list does not make the languages' number systems identical.

For our productive noun practice, use the direct plural बाग. दो बाग and त्रै बाग allow a count without an additional visible plural ending on this particular noun. In full existence statements, the plural linking verb ने confirms that you are describing more than one item.

Count actual marks or objects, changing the order between attempts. Memorising a sequence lets you recite the next number, but recognising a quantity requires selecting its label independently. Both skills matter when somebody gives a room number, a count, or a short list.

The reading aid त्रै = trai records the consonant cluster and vowel spelling in this source-based set. Do not simplify every cluster into one consonant because it feels easier. The exercise is restricted to the first six numbers; more complex forms can be added with reliable pronunciation support later.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| इक | ik | one |
| दो | do | two |
| त्रै | trai | three |
| चार | chār | four |
| पंज | panj | five |
| छे | chhe | six |

### Worked examples

इत्थे दो बाग ने, itthe do bāg ne, means "There are two gardens here." उत्थे त्रै बाग ने, utthe trai bāg ne, describes three gardens elsewhere.

एह् इक बड़ा स्कूल ऐ, eh ik baṛā skūl ai, means "This is one big school." The count, description and noun all belong inside the identification frame. The linking verb remains singular.

### Common trap

Do not use a plural verb after इक merely because the location contains other things too. Agreement follows the noun phrase in the statement you are actually making.

### Practice

## Lesson 11: Liking starts with the experiencer

**Outcome:** Use मिकी with a thing you like and a matching लगदा or लगदी.

### Think first

English begins "I like...". In a pattern closer to "to me this seems good", which expression represents the person having the experience?

### Build the pattern

मिकी names the experiencer, approximately "to me" in this construction. मिकी केला चंगा लगदा ऐ means "I like banana." The banana is presented as pleasing to the speaker. This organisation differs from English "I like", so simply putting मैं in the experiencer slot would miss the Dogri pattern being taught.

The ending in लगदा belongs with the masculine thing liked. With feminine खिचड़ी, the corresponding combination is चंगी लगदी. The descriptive word and the verb both show the contrast. The speaker's gender is not responsible: the same person can like a masculine noun and a feminine noun.

The sentence source contains several spellings of the experiencer, including मिकी and मिगी. This course selects मिकी consistently. Recognise the other forms when you hear or read them, but do not mix spellings unpredictably inside your own small practice set.

Food preferences are personal information, not assumptions about a region or community. You can practise with an imaginary speaker if a named food is unfamiliar or unsuitable. The learning point is the relationship between experiencer and liked item, not persuading anybody to share a particular taste.

For now, keep the two noun-plus-predicate pairs intact. Later lessons introduce activity preferences through a second useful construction with पसंद. Having more than one way to talk about liking does not mean their words can be swapped at random.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| मिकी | mikī | to me, experiencer form here |
| केला | kelā | banana, masculine |
| खिचड़ी | khichṛī | khichdi, feminine |
| चंगा | changā | good, masculine here |
| चंगी | changī | good, feminine here |
| लगदी | lagdī | seems; feminine form here |

### Worked examples

मिकी खिचड़ी चंगी लगदी ऐ, mikī khichṛī changī lagdī ai, reports a preference for khichdi. मिकी केला चंगा लगदा ऐ, mikī kelā changā lagdā ai, uses the masculine pair instead.

रवि गी खिचड़ी चंगी लगदी ऐ, ravi gī khichṛī changī lagdī ai, says Ravi likes khichdi. गी follows the name to mark that experiencer. The food still controls the feminine words.

### Common trap

Do not choose लगदा or लगदी from the gender of Ravi, Asha or the speaker. In these sentences, attend to the liked noun.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Write the experiencer form "to me" used here.
2. Complete: खिचड़ी चंगी __ ऐ.

## Lesson 12: Activities can be liked too

**Outcome:** Express an activity preference with an infinitive phrase and पसंद.

### Think first

What is the difference between "I play" and "I like playing"? Could somebody like an activity without doing it right now?

### Build the pattern

The frame मिकी + activity phrase + पसंद ऐ expresses a preference. खेलना is "to play" or "playing" when an activity is named. मिकी खेलना पसंद ऐ therefore talks about liking the activity, not performing it at this exact moment.

You can specify the activity by placing its object or complement before the infinitive: नाटक खेलना, "to perform a play", or फुटबाल खेलना, "to play football". The complete activity phrase fits into the preference slot. This is an early example of a larger phrase doing the job that a single noun can do elsewhere.

The infinitive ending ना here does not make the sentence a first-person habit. Context and the other words matter. In the preference construction, the infinitive is followed by पसंद and the linking verb. Later, a limited habit pattern will use superficially similar forms with a different sentence structure.

पढ़ना names reading or studying. Which English translation fits depends on its object and setting. A language-learning discussion may favour "studying"; a book discussion may favour "reading". There is no need to force a single English word into every situation.

Ask about the same preference with तुस्सेंगी, the respectful experiencer form used here. The question तुस्सेंगी खेलना पसंद ऐ? does not require an English auxiliary corresponding to "do". The structure remains recognisable; the listener and the questioning intonation create the information request.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| पसंद | pasand | liked; preference |
| खेलना | khelnā | to play |
| पढ़ना | paṛhnā | to read; study |
| नाटक | nāṭak | play; drama |
| फुटबाल | phuṭbāl | football |
| तुस्सेंगी | tussẽgī | to you, respectful experiencer |

### Worked examples

मिकी नाटक खेलना पसंद ऐ, mikī nāṭak khelnā pasand ai, means "I like performing plays." मिकी पढ़ना पसंद ऐ, mikī paṛhnā pasand ai, expresses a reading or studying preference.

तुस्सेंगी नाटक खेलना पसंद ऐ?, tussẽgī nāṭak khelnā pasand ai, politely asks the listener about that activity. The respectful form belongs to the experiencer, while the rest of the preference phrase stays intact.

Do not translate the English helper "do" into a separate Dogri word in every question. This preference question works without it.

## Lesson 13: Saying no without losing the topic

**Outcome:** Recognise and use negation in the limited liking pattern.

### Think first

If someone says an activity is pleasing and you insert a negative before the liking verb, which part of the claim is being denied?

### Build the pattern

नेईं is a common Dogri negative form. The connected teaching source also writes pronunciation-oriented variants such as नैइ. This lesson uses नेईं, the spelling supported by the CIIL word-class discussion, rather than treating the variants as different English meanings.

Our productive negative frame is deliberately narrow: मिकी + item + चंगा or चंगी + नेईं + लगदा or लगदी. It says the item does not appeal to the speaker. The negative precedes the liking verb. In this colloquial frame the final linking verb may be absent, as it is in the connected source's negative preference example.

For केला use the masculine pair चंगा लगदा; for खिचड़ी use feminine चंगी लगदी. Negation does not remove the agreement relationship. It changes the truth of the preference, not the grammatical gender of the food.

Do not assume that every Dogri negative construction has exactly this placement or the same omitted final verb. Commands, past events and other sentence types need their own evidence. A small complete negative pattern is still useful: it lets you politely correct an assumption about what you enjoy.

You can also use नेईं as a brief negative answer when context supplies the question. A fuller answer is often kinder and clearer than an isolated rejection because it identifies the claim you are correcting. Tone of voice and setting matter; a textbook cannot prescribe one interpersonal effect for every utterance.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| नेईं | neī̃ | no; not |
| चंगा लगदा | changā lagdā | seems good, masculine |
| चंगी लगदी | changī lagdī | seems good, feminine |
| मिकी | mikī | to me |
| केला | kelā | banana |
| खिचड़ी | khichṛī | khichdi |

### Worked examples

मिकी केला चंगा नेईं लगदा, mikī kelā changā neī̃ lagdā, says the speaker does not like banana. मिकी खिचड़ी चंगी नेईं लगदी, mikī khichṛī changī neī̃ lagdī, makes the corresponding statement about khichdi.

The second sentence does not say khichdi is objectively bad. It places the experience with the speaker. That distinction is useful when discussing food somebody else enjoys.

### Common trap

Do not move the negative to the beginning and leave the listener to guess what is being denied. Practise the complete supplied frame before improvising other negative sentence types.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Write the negative word taught here.
2. Negate: मिकी केला चंगा लगदा ऐ.
3. Negate the speaker’s liking for khichdi.
4. Complete: खिचड़ी चंगी नेईं __.

***Answers:***

## Lesson 14: A masculine speaker describes a habit

**Outcome:** Use a limited first-person present pattern without confusing it with an infinitive.

### Think first

Why is पढ़ना an activity name after a preference expression, but part of a habit statement in मैं पढ़ना आं?

### Build the pattern

In the selected colloquial pattern, a masculine speaker can say मैं पढ़ना आं, maĩ paṛhnā ā̃, "I read" or "I study". The subject मैं and final आं organise the form as a first-person statement. It is not merely the activity label पढ़ना standing alone.

The connected source gives this type of first-person habitual form alongside other regional or stylistic realisations. We use a consistent small set: पढ़ना आं, खेलना आं and जन्ना आं. Do not build an unlimited conjugation system by attaching ना to every verb root. Dogri has meaningful stem differences, and the resemblance to an infinitive can be misleading.

These examples are explicitly for a masculine singular speaker. A learner using feminine agreement should recognise the structure and practise it as a character's statement; this lesson does not silently claim that the masculine form is universal. The later wanting and intention lessons include supported feminine contrasts.

A habit can describe what somebody does generally, not necessarily what is happening now. Adding an ordinary activity object gives a more specific habit: मैं फुटबाल खेलना आं. A school location similarly narrows a studying statement: मैं स्कूले च पढ़ना आं.

Read the final आं with a nasal vowel. Omitting it loses the first-person auxiliary in the pattern being practised. The source's transcription may spell this aan; our tilde makes clear that the learning target is nasalisation rather than an extra full n consonant.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| मैं | maĩ | I |
| आं | ā̃ | first-person auxiliary here |
| पढ़ना आं | paṛhnā ā̃ | read/study, masculine I-pattern |
| खेलना आं | khelnā ā̃ | play, masculine I-pattern |
| जन्ना आं | jannā ā̃ | go, masculine I-pattern |
| स्कूले च | skūle ch | in school |

### Worked examples

मैं स्कूले च पढ़ना आं, maĩ skūle ch paṛhnā ā̃, says a masculine speaker studies at school. मैं नाटक खेलना आं, maĩ nāṭak khelnā ā̃, says he performs plays.

Compare मिकी नाटक खेलना पसंद ऐ: the activity is liked, but no habit has been asserted. The shared activity words are surrounded by different grammatical frames.

### Common trap

Do not use मिकी as the subject of this habitual pattern. It was an experiencer form for liking, while the habit statement here begins with मैं.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. As a masculine speaker, say: I study.
2. As a masculine speaker, say: I play football.
3. As a masculine speaker, say: I study at school.
4. Supply the final word: मैं पढ़ना __.

***Answers:***

The answers use the masculine first-person pattern with मैं and आं. The object or location sits before the habitual expression. None of these answers contains पसंद, so they assert an activity or habit rather than merely a preference.

## Lesson 15: Respectful you has its own ending

**Outcome:** Ask about the listener’s habits using तुस and ओ.

### Think first

English uses "you" for many relationships. If you select respectful तुस, should you keep the first-person ending आं?

### Build the pattern

For respectful address in this lesson, use तुस, tus, with the auxiliary ओ, o. The small habitual set is तुस पढ़ने ओ, तुस खेलदे ओ and तुस जंदे ओ. Learn these as corresponding frames rather than predicting every verb from one ending.

The first-person source pattern and the respectful listener pattern need not look perfectly symmetrical. मैं पढ़ना आं contrasts with तुस पढ़ने ओ, while the selected play and go forms show दे. This is precisely why copying a Hindi or Punjabi paradigm would be unreliable. Attend to the actual forms supplied for each person.

A statement can become a question through intonation and punctuation when the speaker is asking for confirmation. तुस फुटबाल खेलदे ओ? asks whether the listener plays football. No English-style "do" is required, and the final auxiliary remains at the end.

For an open question, insert the missing information word or phrase. तुस कुत्थे जंदे ओ? asks where the listener goes. The question is about a destination or regular movement; it does not automatically ask where the person currently is.

Respectful address is a practical default here, not a claim about every family's speech. A learner can maintain this respectful set consistently while listening for the forms their conversation partner prefers. Consistency makes it easier to notice when a social choice is affecting a verb.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| तुस | tus | you, respectful here |
| ओ | o | respectful second-person auxiliary |

### Think first

### Build the pattern

A third-person habitual statement puts the actor before the activity. In the singular frame, माँ रोटी बनांदी ऐ says "Mother prepares food." ### Build the pattern

## Lesson 18: Ability is different from habit

**Outcome:** Say that somebody can perform an action using a supported ability construction.

### Think first

Could a learner be able to perform a play but never join a performance? What must you add to distinguish ability from a regular habit?

### Build the pattern

The ability construction uses an action form before सकना, सकने or सकदे in the selected person patterns. For a masculine singular speaker, मैं नाटक खेली सकना आं means "I can perform a play." The action appears as खेली rather than the infinitive खेलना. Learn this combination as a whole.

With we, the source-supported set is अस्स नाटक खेली सकने आं. With respectful you, तुस डोगरी बोली सकदे ओ? asks whether the listener can speak Dogri. These forms give a useful three-way contrast without pretending that every verb and every person can be generated from a single suffix.

बोली is the action form used before the ability expression for speaking. The language name fills its object slot. Replace a language only when you know its appropriate local name; no new grammar is needed merely because the speaker changes from Dogri to another language.

Ability is not identical to desire, permission or certainty. Saying you can do something does not automatically say you want to do it, have permission to do it, or promise to do it tomorrow. Those meanings may interact in real conversation, but this lesson's practice focuses on capacity.

Do not interpret the final vowel in खेली or बोली as feminine agreement with the speaker in this construction. A masculine speaker uses the same supplied action form before the masculine ability expression. The relationship belongs to the construction rather than a simple noun-gender rule.

## Lesson 19: Direct someone’s attention

**Outcome:** Use two verified directive forms in a calm classroom or shared-task exchange.

### Think first

How is "Look here" different from "You look here"? Does a direction need to state its addressee explicitly?

### Build the pattern

The CIIL primer uses दिक्खो for "look" and ध्यान देओ for "pay attention" in instructions. These are directive forms aimed at an addressee, rather than statements describing a habit. A subject pronoun is often unnecessary because the person being addressed is already clear.

You can add a location to the first form: इत्थे दिक्खो, "Look here", or उत्थे दिक्खो, "Look there". This small pair is useful when sharing a map, checking a page or pointing out an object. The final word is the instruction itself, not a copula.

ध्यान कन्नै means "with attention" or "carefully" in the primer's instruction. Placed before दिक्खो, it describes how to look. The word कन्नै expresses an accompanying relation here; it is not the same as the location postposition च. This gives you a practical new relation without a large abstract inventory.

A directive's social effect depends on relationship, situation and delivery. These forms occur in instructional language and can be used calmly during a shared task. Do not assume that adding an English "please" anywhere creates a natural Dogri request. This lesson does not claim to cover all honorific request strategies or negative commands.

The forms are verified directly from a rendered primer page because text extraction confused several Devanagari letters. That matters for learning: दिक्खो is the intended word, not the faulty OCR sequence. Work from legible script and reliable speech rather than treating every extracted character as authoritative.

## Lesson 20: And, but and also connect meanings

**Outcome:** Join short messages while controlling the scope of a connector.

### Think first

What changes if you replace "and" with "but" between two preferences? Are the basic facts different, or the relationship between them?

### Build the pattern

ते can join nouns or full clauses. माँ ते पेयो forms a single coordinated noun phrase. Two complete statements joined with ते retain their own content while presenting it together. Decide whether you are connecting people, activities or whole claims before assembling a long sentence.

पर introduces a contrast. If one person likes an activity and another prefers something else, पर signals the difference the speaker wants the listener to notice. The connector does not negate either clause by itself. Actual negation still needs its own negative word.

भी adds "also" or "too". Its position helps identify what is being added to an existing comparison. मिकी भी पढ़ना पसंद ऐ naturally adds the speaker to people who like reading. Placing the added activity elsewhere in a different context can shift what "also" contributes, so do not assume one English paraphrase fits every position.

A useful speaking strategy is to build two accurate short sentences first, then select the relation between them. This prevents the connector from becoming a substitute for grammatical structure. If each clause requires an auxiliary, joining them does not automatically allow you to discard every final verb.

The sources also show other uses of ते, including topic-related uses. Here we focus on straightforward coordination, while recognising that the same written form can have more than one discourse job. A learner should infer the relation from the whole sentence, not from a single dictionary gloss.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| ते | te | and, coordination here |
| पर | par | but |
| भी | bhī | also; too |
| माँ ते पेयो | mā̃ te peyo | mother and father |
| नाटक ते फुटबाल | nāṭak te phuṭbāl | drama and football |
| मिकी भी | mikī bhī | to me too; I also, in liking |

### Worked examples

मिकी भी नाटक खेलना पसंद ऐ, mikī bhī nāṭak khelnā pasand ai, adds the speaker to others who like performing plays.

इत्थे इक बाग ऐ, पर उत्थे दो बाग ने, itthe ik bāg ai, par utthe do bāg ne, contrasts the quantities at two places. Both clauses remain affirmative. The contrast concerns one garden versus two, not presence versus absence.

### Common trap

Do not translate पर as a negative word. It connects contrasting information; it does not itself mean "not".

### Practice

## Lesson 21: Time frames make a habit more precise

**Outcome:** Add a day-part or recurring-time phrase without rebuilding the basic clause.

### Think first

If "we play football" is already complete, what does "in the morning" add? Does it turn the sentence into a new person or a different activity?

### Build the pattern

सवेरे supplies "in the morning" as a time frame. अस्स सवेरे फुटबाल खेलदे आं says the group plays football in the morning. The pronoun, action and auxiliary remain recognisable; the new phrase tells the listener when the habit occurs.

रोज़ means "daily" or "every day". It describes frequency rather than a point on the clock. सवेरे and रोज़ therefore answer different questions. Someone may do an activity every day at varying times, or do it in the morning only on selected days.

A habitual form with today may describe the day's scheduled or characteristic activity, but it should not be presented as a complete progressive system. This course avoids claiming that every present meaning has one identical form.

For productive practice, use the clear repeated-time frames सवेरे, रोज़ and छुट्टियें च. The other time words can be recognised as vocabulary. This separation gives learners useful control while keeping uncertain tense and aspect interpretations outside the answer keys.

## Lesson 22: Wanting: a supported feminine and masculine contrast

**Outcome:** Express a wish with an infinitive and चाना or चानी.

### Think first

Does wanting to see a garden imply that you can see it or that you already saw it? Which separate claim are you making?

### Build the pattern

The infinitive names a desired action before the wanting expression. For a masculine singular speaker, मैं बाग दिक्खना चाना आं means "I want to see the garden." A feminine singular speaker uses चानी in the same supplied frame. The desired action दिक्खना remains unchanged.

This is a useful contrast between the action being named and the speaker-sensitive form expressing desire. Do not change every word ending in ā when the speaker is feminine. In this frame, the infinitive continues to name the activity, while चाना changes to चानी.

The connected source includes more than one colloquial realisation of the final auxiliary. Here we consistently use आं with the first-person wanting pattern that is explicitly illustrated with both gender forms. This limited choice is not a claim that all other community forms are wrong.

A wish is not a promise or a report of present action. The garden may be far away, closed, or merely imagined. Wanting to see it leaves those questions unresolved. Keeping these meanings separate will help you interpret future intention and past-state lessons without conflating them.

The infinitive बनना names becoming. With लेखक it creates a goal, "to become a writer". A goal can be practised in a fictional character's voice; the activity is useful even if the learner's own ambitions are different. We avoid occupational gender charts beyond the noun supplied here.

| दिक्खना | dikkhnā | to see |
| चाना | chānā | want, masculine speaker here |
| चानी | chānī | want, feminine speaker here |
| बनना | bannā | to become |
| लेखक | lekhak | writer |
| जाना | jānā | to go, infinitive |

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. As a feminine speaker, say: I want to see the garden.
2. As a masculine speaker, say: I want to see the school.
3. As a feminine speaker, say: I want to perform a play.
4. Complete for a feminine speaker: मैं दिक्खना __ आं.

***Answers:***

1. `मैं बाग दिक्खना चानी आं।` - `maĩ bāg dikkhnā chānī ā̃.`.
2. `मैं स्कूल दिक्खना चाना आं।` - `maĩ skūl dikkhnā chānā ā̃.`.
3. `मैं नाटक खेलना चानी आं।` - `maĩ nāṭak khelnā chānī ā̃.`.
4. `चानी` - `chānī`.

### Why these answers work

The desired activity is expressed with the infinitive before the wanting word. Only the supplied speaker-sensitive contrast changes between masculine चाना and feminine चानी. The final auxiliary remains आं in this consistent teaching frame.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create two characters with one shared goal. State it in each character’s voice using the appropriate form. Then change the desired action for one character while preserving the rest of the structure.

## Lesson 23: A plan to go somewhere

**Outcome:** Use the limited intention frame जाने आला or जाने आली.

### Think first

Compare "I want to go" with "I am going to go" as a plan. Is a wish the same thing as an intended future action?

### Build the pattern

The connected teaching source uses जाने आला and जाने आली with a first-person auxiliary for a planned future journey. मैं जम्मू जाने आला आं is the masculine speaker's intention; मैं जम्मू जाने आली आं is the feminine counterpart. Both mean the speaker plans to go to Jammu.

This lesson teaches one attested future-oriented construction rather than a full synthetic future tense. It is a practical way to talk about a plan, but it does not supply every form needed for predictions, promises or conditional futures. Its usefulness does not depend on pretending it is universal.

Notice जाने before आला or आली. It differs from the simple infinitive जाना in the preceding lesson. Preserve the whole construction when learning it. Replacing जाने with whichever infinitive form comes to mind would hide the actual structure.

A destination can be a place name, so the exercise avoids uncertain new case forms. जम्मू and कश्मीर are used as familiar destination labels. A learner can make plans in an imagined context without suggesting travel advice, a timetable or any claim about access.

या offers alternatives. A person may plan to visit one place or another without having decided which. The connector preserves that uncertainty rather than turning the statement into a confirmed itinerary. Distinguish the grammatical expression of intention from the real-world certainty of a trip.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| जाने | jāne | go, form in this construction |
| आला | ālā | masculine intention element here |
| आली | ālī | feminine intention element here |
| जम्मू | jammū | Jammu |
| कश्मीर | kashmīr | Kashmir |
| या | yā | or |

### Worked examples

मैं जम्मू जाने आली आं, maĩ jammū jāne ālī ā̃, expresses a feminine speaker's travel plan. मैं कश्मीर जाने आला आं, maĩ kashmīr jāne ālā ā̃, expresses a masculine speaker's plan.

मैं जम्मू या कश्मीर जाने आली आं, maĩ jammū yā kashmīr jāne ālī ā̃, leaves the destination choice open. It still uses the same feminine intention frame.

### Common trap

Do not describe this lesson as a complete Dogri future conjugation. It supplies a limited planned-movement construction with two verified speaker forms.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. As a masculine speaker, say: I plan to go to Jammu.
2. As a feminine speaker, say: I plan to go to Kashmir.
3. Write: Jammu or Kashmir.
4. Complete for a feminine speaker: मैं जम्मू जाने __ आं.

***Answers:***

1. `मैं जम्मू जाने आला आं।` - `maĩ jammū jāne ālā ā̃.`.
2. `मैं कश्मीर जाने आली आं।` - `maĩ kashmīr jāne ālī ā̃.`.
3. `जम्मू या कश्मीर` - `jammū yā kashmīr`.
4. `आली` - `ālī`.

### Why these answers work

The destination precedes जाने. The intention element agrees with the speaker in the contrast taught here, while the final auxiliary stays आं. या connects alternatives and does not mean both destinations are definitely included.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give one character a wish and another a plan involving the same place. State the plan using this lesson and describe the wish using the preceding lesson’s learned action vocabulary. Explain the difference in commitment.

## Lesson 24: There was one: a narrow past-state contrast

**Outcome:** Recognise and produce a restricted masculine singular existence statement with हा.

### Think first

How would the information change if a place had one garden before but has two now? Which ending can mark the earlier state?

### Build the pattern

The connected source supplies हा in a masculine singular past existence statement about a parrot at home. We use that evidence to build a narrow contrast: इत्थे इक बाग हा, "There was one garden here", beside इत्थे दो बाग ने, "There are two gardens here."

This lesson is about a state or presence in an earlier period. It does not teach the past tense of every action. Saying a garden was there does not explain who built it, when it disappeared, or what happened to it. Those event claims require additional grammar and evidence.

Keep production to the masculine singular nouns बाग and तोता with हा. We do not infer a feminine past form by copying Hindi थी or invent a plural paradigm from one attested example. Restricting the table prevents a plausible-looking but unsupported generalisation.

A clear surrounding story can establish that one statement is past and the other current. English translations make the time contrast explicit with "was" versus "are". In Dogri, the selected linking forms carry that contrast. You can also contrast one place's former state with another place's present state when the discourse makes the comparison meaningful.

Recall that a location-first sentence announces existence, not ownership in a legal sense. "There was a parrot at home" can naturally suggest that a household kept one, but the construction itself states presence. Do not translate it into a universal possessive verb covering every kind of ownership.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| हा | hā | was, masculine singular state here |
| तोता | totā | parrot, masculine |
| बाग | bāg | garden, masculine |
| करै च | karai ch | at home |
| ऐ | ai | is, current singular state |
| ने | ne | are, current plural state |

### Worked examples

उत्थे इक बाग हा, utthe ik bāg hā, says a garden existed there earlier. उत्थे इक बाग ऐ, utthe ik bāg ai, makes the corresponding current claim.

बागे च इक तोता हा, bāge ch ik totā hā, locates a parrot in the garden in an earlier scene. The sentence does not claim the bird flew, arrived or departed; it simply places it there.

### Common trap

Do not turn हा into a universal ending for "did". It links a restricted masculine singular state or existence claim, rather than expressing every possible past event.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Say: There was one garden here.
2. Say: There was a parrot in the garden.
3. Make current: उत्थे इक बाग हा.
4. Supply the restricted past link: इक तोता __.

***Answers:***

1. `इत्थे इक बाग हा।` - `itthe ik bāg hā.`.
2. `बागे च इक तोता हा।` - `bāge ch ik totā hā.`.
3. `उत्थे इक बाग ऐ।` - `utthe ik bāg ai.`.
4. `हा` - `hā`.

### Why these answers work

The past examples use the verified masculine singular हा. The current example restores ऐ. Location phrases remain unchanged when the time reference changes. The final item retrieves the linking form without adding an unsupported action verb.

### Retrieve and recombine

Draw a before-and-now map with one garden in the earlier picture and two in the present picture. Describe each separately, then explain why the two statements can both be true.

## Lesson 25: A small conversation from real choices

**Outcome:** Combine the foundation patterns while keeping their meanings and limits distinct.

### Think first

When preparing a conversation, should you start with every grammar form you know or with the information two people need to exchange?

### Build the pattern

Begin with a shared situation. Two learners meet at a school, introduce themselves, discuss an activity and inspect a nearby garden. Each turn should answer a real question or move the conversation toward a clear purpose. A collection of unrelated correct sentences is not yet a coherent exchange.

Use a name question to establish identity. Use a preference question to discover an interest. Use an ability question to check capacity. These are different information needs: liking drama does not prove somebody can perform a play, and ability does not guarantee a current habit.

A location question can lead to a brief answer followed by a directive: identify where the garden is, then ask the partner to look there. Keep the final forms distinct. The question and existence statement use a linking verb; the looking instruction ends with दिक्खो.

The course's small verb inventory is a deliberate boundary. If your conversation needs a new past event or an unfamiliar honorific request, record the communicative need and seek a reliable example. Do not patch the gap with another language and call the result Dogri. Recombining verified material is productive; fabricating a paradigm is not.

Review also requires honest self-assessment. Can you choose the right person, distinguish one from several, preserve an oblique location phrase and explain the difference between preference and ability? These concrete skills are better evidence of progress than a vague claim of fluency. Fluent-speaker feedback remains necessary for tone, idiomatic choices and broader regional usage.

### Useful words and forms

| Dogri | Reading aid | Meaning or job |
|---|---|---|
| थौआड़ा नां | thoāṛā nā̃ | your name, respectful |
| तुस्सेंगी | tussẽgī | to you, respectful experiencer |
| पसंद ऐ | pasand ai | is liked, in preference frame |
| सकदे ओ | sakde o | can, respectful you-frame |
| कुत्थे | kutthe | where |
| दिक्खो | dikkho | look, directive |

### Worked examples

A learner asks तुस्सेंगी नाटक खेलना पसंद ऐ?, tussẽgī nāṭak khelnā pasand ai, about an interest. Another asks तुस नाटक खेली सकदे ओ?, tus nāṭak khelī sakde o, about ability. The similar activity vocabulary serves different questions.

बाग कुत्थे ऐ?, bāg kutthe ai, can be answered with उत्थे, utthe. A following उत्थे दिक्खो, utthe dikkho, directs attention to that shared location.

### Common trap

Do not reward yourself for speed if it makes every sentence use the same auxiliary. Check viewpoint and construction before speaking faster.

### Practice

Produce the short Dogri answer requested. Use the forms taught here.

1. Ask respectfully about a reading preference.
2. Ask respectfully whether the listener can speak Dogri.
3. Say there are three gardens here.
4. Direct attention: Look there carefully.

***Answers:***

1. `तुस्सेंगी पढ़ना पसंद ऐ?` - `tussẽgī paṛhnā pasand ai?`.
2. `तुस डोगरी बोली सकदे ओ?` - `tus ḍogrī bolī sakde o?`.
3. `इत्थे त्रै बाग ने।` - `itthe trai bāg ne.`.
4. `उत्थे ध्यान कन्नै दिक्खो।` - `utthe dhyān kannai dikkho.`.

### Why these answers work

The preference question uses a respectful experiencer, while ability uses the respectful actor तुस. The existence statement has a plural count and therefore ने. The last answer is a directive and needs neither ऐ nor ओ at the end.

### Retrieve and recombine

Build a six-turn exchange containing an introduction, a preference, an ability question and a location. Change the characters and activity, then repeat without the text. Mark any new form you need for later source-supported study.
