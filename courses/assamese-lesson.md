# Assamese: A pattern-first foundation

This course uses modern standard Assamese in Assamese script, preserving ৰ and ৱ. Romanisation is a readable pronunciation aid: x marks the Assamese velar fricative, s represents the usual standard pronunciation of চ and ছ in the words used here, and a tilde marks nasalisation. ṭ and ṛh help relate forms to spelling and do not assert a full Hindi-style dental-retroflex contrast in Assamese speech. Vowel quality varies by context; the aid is not an IPA transcription. Assamese মই means I, আমি means we, and আপুনি is respectful you. The course explicitly maintains these distinctions from Bengali. It concentrates on everyday standard patterns, with social register and regional pronunciation subject to review.

### References and review notes

[Assamese grammar overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_grammar) was consulted to check the pronoun inventory, agent marking, classifier distinctions, and negative-prefix overview. Explanations and exercises are original. The material avoids claiming that compact aspect forms have only one English tense equivalent. A fluent Assamese reviewer should prioritise nasalised endings, imperative pronunciation, classifier pragmatics, and regional variation in negative-vowel harmony.

## Lesson 1: Start with identity and Assamese sounds

### Outcome

Introduce yourself and a respected person with a short present identity sentence.

### Think before reading

If a language can identify somebody without a spoken “is,” what two pieces must the sentence still contain?

### Build the pattern

An ordinary Assamese identity statement can put a person directly beside a role: মই শিক্ষক (moi xikkhok), I am a teacher. There is no need to insert a word copied from English am. আপুনি (apuni) is respectful you, and তেওঁ (teõ) is a respectful he or she. These are Assamese forms. In particular, আমি (ami) means we here, not Bengali I.

The letter ৰ is Assamese r, while ৱ represents a w-like sound. Our x represents the characteristic Assamese velar fricative, made with friction toward the back of the mouth. It is not English ks. In শিক্ষক, xikkhok is a practical pronunciation guide. The tilde marks nasalisation in তেওঁ. Keep vowels fairly steady rather than turning them into English diphthongs. Regional pronunciation varies; the script remains the primary written form.

A role can refer to a person of any gender when the chosen noun permits it. The pronoun তেওঁ does not force a male or female interpretation. Later lessons introduce gendered ordinary third-person pronouns without confusing them with respectful reference.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| মই শিক্ষক। | Moi xikkhok. | I am a teacher. |
| আপুনি শিক্ষক। | Apuni xikkhok. | You are a teacher. (respectful) |
| তেওঁ শিক্ষক। | Teõ xikkhok. | He or she is a teacher. (respectful) |
| আমি বন্ধু। | Ami bondhu. | We are friends. |
| মই ছাত্ৰ। | Moi satro. | I am a male student. |
| মই ৰীনা। | Moi Rina. | I am Rina. |

### Common trap

Do not import Bengali আমি meaning I or আপনি as the respectful pronoun. Assamese uses মই for I and আপুনি for respectful you, even though the scripts look closely related.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: He or she is a teacher. (respectful)
2. Say: We are friends.
3. Say: I am a male student.
4. Say: I am Rina.
5. Say: I am a doctor.
6. Say: He or she is Rina. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `তেওঁ শিক্ষক।` - `Teõ xikkhok.`
2. `আমি বন্ধু।` - `Ami bondhu.`
3. `মই ছাত্ৰ।` - `Moi satro.`
4. `মই ৰীনা।` - `Moi Rina.`
5. `মই চিকিৎসক।` - `Moi sikitsok.`
6. `তেওঁ ৰীনা।` - `Teõ Rina.`

### Why these answers work

The answers contain a subject and identifying expression with no extra present copula. আমি remains a plural speaker group. The final two answers transfer the same frame from educational introductions to a workplace role and a personal name.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce yourself by name, then by role. Introduce a respected colleague using তেওঁ. Say aloud what Assamese আমি means before moving to the next lesson.


## Lesson 2: Ask who, what, and a name

### Outcome

Ask for a name or identification without English-style inversion.

### Think before reading

Why does a person question use কোন while a question about an object uses কি?

### Build the pattern

কোন (kon) asks who, and কি (ki) asks what. আপুনি কোন asks who you are. In a friendly introduction, আপোনাৰ নাম কি is often less abrupt because it asks for a name rather than demanding identification. আপোনাৰ (aponar) means your respectfully; its possessive structure will be explored later.

এইটো (eiṭo) points to this thing and সেইটো (xeiṭo) to that thing. এইটো কি asks what this is. The thing remains first and the unknown category comes after it. কিতাপ (kitap) means book, কলম (kolom) pen, চাহ (sah) tea, and পানী (pani) water. The s in sah represents the ordinary standard Assamese pronunciation of চ here, not the Bengali affricate in tea.

A short answer such as এইটো কিতাপ classifies an object. Once you want to count it or identify a particular book, Assamese classifiers become important. For this first exchange, focus on asking which category the unknown thing belongs to.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| এইটো কি? | Eiṭo ki? | What is this? |
| সেইটো কি? | Xeiṭo ki? | What is that? |
| আপুনি কোন? | Apuni kon? | Who are you? (respectful) |
| আপোনাৰ নাম কি? | Aponar nam ki? | What is your name? (respectful) |
| এইটো কিতাপ। | Eiṭo kitap. | This is a book. |
| সেইটো কলম। | Xeiṭo kolom. | That is a pen. |

### Common trap

Do not interpret every চ as Bengali ch in this pronunciation guide. Also avoid using thing demonstratives as a default respectful way to introduce an adult.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Who are you? (respectful)
2. Say: What is your name? (respectful)
3. Say: This is a book.
4. Say: That is a pen.
5. Say: This is tea.
6. Say: That is water.

***Answers:***

1. `আপুনি কোন?` - `Apuni kon?`
2. `আপোনাৰ নাম কি?` - `Aponar nam ki?`
3. `এইটো কিতাপ।` - `Eiṭo kitap.`
4. `সেইটো কলম।` - `Xeiṭo kolom.`
5. `এইটো চাহ।` - `Eiṭo sah.`
6. `সেইটো পানী।` - `Xeiṭo pani.`

### Why these answers work

The question word replaces the unknown identity or category. A name question uses the possessive আপোনাৰ before নাম. The last two answers extend the object-identification frame to food and drink vocabulary without adding an English copula.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask what two desk objects are, then answer. Change to an introduction and ask a respectful listener for a name. Explain why the person and object questions differ.


## Lesson 3: Deny an identity

### Outcome

Use the negative identity predicate and distinguish it from an action negative.

### Think before reading

Does saying “I am not a teacher” deny an action or a classification?

### Build the pattern

নহয় (nohoy) is a negative identity predicate in an ordinary third-person or respectful statement: তেওঁ শিক্ষক নহয়. First-person মই and আমি use নহওঁ (nohoõ) in the person-matching pattern taught here: মই শিক্ষক নহওঁ. Familiar তুমি uses নহোৱা (nohowa). These forms belong to the identity construction; they are not interchangeable with every kind of negative in Assamese.

A thing can be reclassified with এইটো কিতাপ নহয়, this is not a book. The order remains demonstrative, category, negative predicate. A correction can then supply the right category in a separate positive sentence. Our exercise keys keep only one sentence per answer, so the classification and correction remain easy to inspect.

হয় (hoy) can function as yes in an exchange, and নহয় as no when rejecting a claim. A standalone response depends on what question was asked. Do not assume English yes and no always map neatly to the same Assamese word regardless of the underlying predicate.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| মই শিক্ষক নহওঁ। | Moi xikkhok nohoõ. | I am not a teacher. |
| তেওঁ শিক্ষক নহয়। | Teõ xikkhok nohoy. | He or she is not a teacher. (respectful) |
| আপুনি চিকিৎসক নহয়। | Apuni sikitsok nohoy. | You are not a doctor. (respectful) |
| আমি শিক্ষক নহওঁ। | Ami xikkhok nohoõ. | We are not teachers. |
| এইটো কিতাপ নহয়। | Eiṭo kitap nohoy. | This is not a book. |
| সেইটো চাহ নহয়। | Xeiṭo sah nohoy. | That is not tea. |

### Common trap

Do not add নহয় after every action verb. Assamese action negation has its own prefix pattern. First establish whether you are rejecting a classification or denying an activity.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: You are not a doctor. (respectful)
2. Say: We are not teachers.
3. Say: This is not a book.
4. Say: That is not tea.
5. Say: This is not a pen.
6. Say: That is not water.

***Answers:***

1. `আপুনি চিকিৎসক নহয়।` - `Apuni sikitsok nohoy.`
2. `আমি শিক্ষক নহওঁ।` - `Ami xikkhok nohoõ.`
3. `এইটো কিতাপ নহয়।` - `Eiṭo kitap nohoy.`
4. `সেইটো চাহ নহয়।` - `Xeiṭo sah nohoy.`
5. `এইটো কলম নহয়।` - `Eiṭo kolom nohoy.`
6. `সেইটো পানী নহয়।` - `Xeiṭo pani nohoy.`

### Why these answers work

The first-person answers use নহওঁ, while respectful and object subjects use নহয়. The two new object corrections show that changing the category noun leaves the third-person negative predicate unchanged.

### Retrieve and recombine

Correct two false claims about your occupation and two false labels on objects. Before choosing the negative, identify whether the subject is first person, respectful, or a thing.


## Lesson 4: Locate someone here or there

### Outcome

State presence and ask where someone is using the correct personal form.

### Think before reading

Why does a location need a presence predicate although a basic identity can omit a positive copula?

### Build the pattern

ইয়াত (iyat) means here, তাত (tat) there, and ক’ত (kot) where. মই ইয়াত আছোঁ means I am here. The presence verb আছোঁ (asõ) matches the first person; আছে (ase) is used with আপুনি and তেওঁ as well as ordinary third-person subjects. Do not import Bengali honorific আছেন into Assamese.

ক’ত replaces the known location in আপুনি ক’ত আছে. There is no movement of the verb to the front. The neutral sequence remains person, location, presence. আমি ইয়াত আছোঁ uses the same first-person form for a group of speakers.

The standard spelling আছে contains a consonant whose romanisation here is s, reflecting its usual sound in standard Assamese. Nasalisation distinguishes the first-person form আছোঁ in both script and our romanisation. Read a respectful location sentence and a first-person location sentence together so that this small but consequential difference stays audible.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| মই ইয়াত আছোঁ। | Moi iyat asõ. | I am here. |
| তেওঁ তাত আছে। | Teõ tat ase. | He or she is there. (respectful) |
| আপুনি ক’ত আছে? | Apuni kot ase? | Where are you? (respectful) |
| আমি ইয়াত আছোঁ। | Ami iyat asõ. | We are here. |
| মই তাত আছোঁ। | Moi tat asõ. | I am there. |
| আপুনি ইয়াত আছে। | Apuni iyat ase. | You are here. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not replace Assamese আছে with Bengali আছেন to show respect. Respect is compatible with Assamese আপুনি আছে in the standard pattern taught here.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Where are you? (respectful)
2. Say: We are here.
3. Say: I am there.
4. Say: You are here. (respectful)
5. Say: A book is here.
6. Say: A pen is there.

***Answers:***

1. `আপুনি ক’ত আছে?` - `Apuni kot ase?`
2. `আমি ইয়াত আছোঁ।` - `Ami iyat asõ.`
3. `মই তাত আছোঁ।` - `Moi tat asõ.`
4. `আপুনি ইয়াত আছে।` - `Apuni iyat ase.`
5. `কিতাপ ইয়াত আছে।` - `Kitap iyat ase.`
6. `কলম তাত আছে।` - `Kolom tat ase.`

### Why these answers work

The location stands before the final predicate. Both মই and আমি choose আছোঁ. The last two answers locate objects with আছে, which also appears with a respectful subject but is not an exclusively honorific form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine a phone call between two rooms. Locate yourself, ask where the listener is, then locate a book. Keep here and there anchored to the imagined speaker.


## Lesson 5: Read, work, and drink

### Outcome

Construct a routine with an object before a person-marked verb.

### Think before reading

In a sentence meaning “I read books,” which word tells the listener that the speaker is doing the reading?

### Build the pattern

মই কিতাপ পঢ়োঁ (moi kitap poṛhõ) places the object before the final verb. পঢ়োঁ is first-person read; respectful আপুনি and তেওঁ take পঢ়ে (poṛhe). কাম কৰোঁ (kam korõ) means I work, literally do work. The respectful counterpart is কাম কৰে (kam kore).

খাওঁ (khaõ) means I eat or consume, and খায় (khay) is the respectful or third-person form. Assamese uses this verb naturally for tea and water, so মই চাহ খাওঁ means I drink tea. Translating it literally as I eat tea obscures the normal Assamese meaning.

These forms describe habits and general actions. They do not by themselves force the interpretation that the action is occurring right now. The object stays separate from the verb; person marking belongs to the verb. Nasalisation in কৰোঁ and খাওঁ is meaningful and should not be stripped from a typed romanised answer. A group subject আমি takes the same first-person pattern.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| মই কিতাপ পঢ়োঁ। | Moi kitap poṛhõ. | I read books. |
| আপুনি কিতাপ পঢ়ে। | Apuni kitap poṛhe. | You read books. (respectful) |
| মই চাহ খাওঁ। | Moi sah khaõ. | I drink tea. |
| তেওঁ চাহ খায়। | Teõ sah khay. | He or she drinks tea. (respectful) |
| মই কাম কৰোঁ। | Moi kam korõ. | I work. |
| আপুনি কাম কৰে। | Apuni kam kore. | You work. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not add a Bengali -en ending to the respectful Assamese verb. আপুনি কৰে and আপুনি পঢ়ে are the forms taught here. Also preserve the nasalised first-person ending.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I drink tea.
2. Say: He or she drinks tea. (respectful)
3. Say: I work.
4. Say: You work. (respectful)
5. Say: I drink water.
6. Say: We work.

***Answers:***

1. `মই চাহ খাওঁ।` - `Moi sah khaõ.`
2. `তেওঁ চাহ খায়।` - `Teõ sah khay.`
3. `মই কাম কৰোঁ।` - `Moi kam korõ.`
4. `আপুনি কাম কৰে।` - `Apuni kam kore.`
5. `মই পানী খাওঁ।` - `Moi pani khaõ.`
6. `আমি কাম কৰোঁ।` - `Ami kam korõ.`

### Why these answers work

The answers maintain object-before-verb order and choose the appropriate first-person or respectful form. The new water and group-work sentences reuse the same morphology rather than introducing a separate drinking paradigm.

### Retrieve and recombine

Name two routines of your own. Attribute the same routines to a respected person and then to your group. Identify the ending changes before adding any new vocabulary.


## Lesson 6: Negate routines with a prefix

### Outcome

Deny an ordinary action and hear how the negative vowel adapts to the verb.

### Think before reading

If the negative element comes before the verb stem, should you place a separate না after the whole sentence?

### Build the pattern

Assamese simple-action negation commonly prefixes a negative element to the verb. কৰোঁ becomes নকৰোঁ (nokorõ), I do not do; পঢ়োঁ becomes নপঢ়োঁ (nopoṛhõ), I do not read. The negative vowel is influenced by the following stem. This is a different pattern from Bengali sentence-final না.

খাওঁ becomes নাখাওঁ (nakhaõ), I do not eat or drink. Alternative vowel-harmonised pronunciations and spellings occur, but this course keeps the na form with খা stable. Respectful খায় becomes নাখায় (nakhay). The person pattern remains inside the negative word, so first-person nasalisation does not disappear.

Build the affirmative first, identify its verb, then replace it with the appropriate prefixed negative form. মই চাহ নাখাওঁ describes a negative routine; it does not use the identity predicate নহওঁ. A listener still hears the same subject and object, with the action now denied. This separation of identity and action negation is essential for avoiding a misleading single-word translation of English not.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| মই কাম নকৰোঁ। | Moi kam nokorõ. | I do not work. |
| আপুনি কাম নকৰে। | Apuni kam nokore. | You do not work. (respectful) |
| মই কিতাপ নপঢ়োঁ। | Moi kitap nopoṛhõ. | I do not read books. |
| তেওঁ কিতাপ নপঢ়ে। | Teõ kitap nopoṛhe. | He or she does not read books. (respectful) |
| মই চাহ নাখাওঁ। | Moi sah nakhaõ. | I do not drink tea. |
| তেওঁ চাহ নাখায়। | Teõ sah nakhay. | He or she does not drink tea. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not copy Bengali খাই না into Assamese. In these routine patterns the negative belongs before the Assamese verb stem, while its person ending remains present.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I do not read books.
2. Say: He or she does not read books. (respectful)
3. Say: I do not drink tea.
4. Say: He or she does not drink tea. (respectful)
5. Say: We do not work.
6. Say: I do not drink water.

***Answers:***

1. `মই কিতাপ নপঢ়োঁ।` - `Moi kitap nopoṛhõ.`
2. `তেওঁ কিতাপ নপঢ়ে।` - `Teõ kitap nopoṛhe.`
3. `মই চাহ নাখাওঁ।` - `Moi sah nakhaõ.`
4. `তেওঁ চাহ নাখায়।` - `Teõ sah nakhay.`
5. `আমি কাম নকৰোঁ।` - `Ami kam nokorõ.`
6. `মই পানী নাখাওঁ।` - `Moi pani nakhaõ.`

### Why these answers work

The negative prefix combines with each verb rather than standing at the end as a separate particle. The last two answers transfer the negative to first-person plural and a new drink while preserving the appropriate person ending.

### Retrieve and recombine

State one routine positively and then deny it. Repeat with a different verb whose negative vowel differs. Explain why identity negation from Lesson 3 would not fit.


## Lesson 7: Ask about an action

### Outcome

Distinguish a yes-or-no question from a request for missing content.

### Think before reading

Would “Do you read?” and “What do you read?” accept the same kind of reply?

### Build the pattern

A neutral written yes-or-no question can place নে (ne) at the end: আপুনি চাহ খায় নে. The statement's subject, object, and verb remain in their familiar order. The final particle asks whether the proposition is true. It does not replace the verb ending.

কি (ki) asks for missing content inside the clause: আপুনি কি পঢ়ে asks what you read. The unknown object appears before the verb where a named object would appear. The same spelling কি is also encountered as a question particle in other question patterns; this lesson uses final নে for transparent yes-or-no questions and কি for what.

কেতিয়া (ketiya) asks when, and কিয় (kiyo) asks why. আপুনি কেতিয়া কাম কৰে asks about a routine's time. Asking why can sound challenging in some situations, so use it when the conversational context supports a reason question. Word order alone does not communicate the friendliness of your intention.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| আপুনি চাহ খায় নে? | Apuni sah khay ne? | Do you drink tea? (respectful) |
| আপুনি কি পঢ়ে? | Apuni ki poṛhe? | What do you read? (respectful) |
| আপুনি কিতাপ পঢ়ে নে? | Apuni kitap poṛhe ne? | Do you read books? (respectful) |
| আপুনি কেতিয়া কাম কৰে? | Apuni ketiya kam kore? | When do you work? (respectful) |
| আপুনি কি খায়? | Apuni ki khay? | What do you eat or drink? (respectful) |
| আপুনি কিয় পঢ়ে? | Apuni kiyo poṛhe? | Why do you read? (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not answer a what-question only with yes if the listener is asking for the missing object. Identify the information gap before choosing your reply.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Do you read books? (respectful)
2. Say: When do you work? (respectful)
3. Say: What do you eat or drink? (respectful)
4. Say: Why do you read? (respectful)
5. Say: Do you drink water? (respectful)
6. Say: Do you work? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `আপুনি কিতাপ পঢ়ে নে?` - `Apuni kitap poṛhe ne?`
2. `আপুনি কেতিয়া কাম কৰে?` - `Apuni ketiya kam kore?`
3. `আপুনি কি খায়?` - `Apuni ki khay?`
4. `আপুনি কিয় পঢ়ে?` - `Apuni kiyo poṛhe?`
5. `আপুনি পানী খায় নে?` - `Apuni pani khay ne?`
6. `আপুনি কাম কৰে নে?` - `Apuni kam kore ne?`

### Why these answers work

Final নে marks a question about the whole assertion, whereas কি, কেতিয়া, and কিয় replace an unknown object, time, or reason. The last two questions transfer the distinction to water and a work routine.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask a respectful listener whether they read, what they read, and when they read. Imagine what sort of answer each question invites, even before composing the answer.


## Lesson 8: Address friends and refer to others

### Outcome

Keep familiar second-person forms separate from respectful forms and recognise gendered ordinary pronouns.

### Think before reading

Does the respectful pronoun তেওঁ work exactly like the ordinary pair সি and তাই?

### Build the pattern

তুমি (tumi) is familiar you, with verbs such as কৰা (kora), do, পঢ়া (poṛha), read, and খোৱা (khowa), eat or drink. The shift from আপুনি to তুমি therefore changes the verb form. It is a relationship choice rather than a mechanical translation of English you.

Ordinary সি (xi) refers to he, and তাই (tai) to she in the distal third-person set. This lexical gender distinction is real in Assamese, unlike Bengali's ordinary সে. The verb itself does not acquire a male or female ending: সি পঢ়ে and তাই পঢ়ে share পঢ়ে. Respectful তেওঁ can refer to either gender.

The very familiar তই is not used productively in this course because it carries a different social register and verb paradigm. Familiarity varies by household and community. Do not decide a person's pronoun solely from apparent age. In a new interaction, respectful আপুনি is the safer grammatical starting point until another convention is established.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| তুমি কাম কৰা। | Tumi kam kora. | You work. (familiar) |
| তুমি কিতাপ পঢ়া। | Tumi kitap poṛha. | You read books. (familiar) |
| তুমি চাহ খোৱা। | Tumi sah khowa. | You drink tea. (familiar) |
| সি কিতাপ পঢ়ে। | Xi kitap poṛhe. | He reads books. (ordinary) |
| তাই কিতাপ পঢ়ে। | Tai kitap poṛhe. | She reads books. (ordinary) |
| তুমি কাম নকৰা। | Tumi kam nokora. | You do not work. (familiar) |

### Common trap

Do not claim Assamese lacks all gender distinctions merely because verbs do not agree for gender. সি and তাই provide a lexical contrast, while তেওঁ is respectful without that contrast.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: You drink tea. (familiar)
2. Say: He reads books. (ordinary)
3. Say: She reads books. (ordinary)
4. Say: You do not work. (familiar)
5. Say: She drinks tea. (ordinary)
6. Say: He or she works. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `তুমি চাহ খোৱা।` - `Tumi sah khowa.`
2. `সি কিতাপ পঢ়ে।` - `Xi kitap poṛhe.`
3. `তাই কিতাপ পঢ়ে।` - `Tai kitap poṛhe.`
4. `তুমি কাম নকৰা।` - `Tumi kam nokora.`
5. `তাই চাহ খায়।` - `Tai sah khay.`
6. `তেওঁ কাম কৰে।` - `Teõ kam kore.`

### Why these answers work

তুমি selects familiar verbs, and সি or তাই select the same ordinary third-person verb shape. The new negative friend sentence retains the familiar ending inside নকৰা. The last statement switches back to respectful reference without inventing a Bengali ending.

### Retrieve and recombine

Recast one routine as a statement to a friend, about a man, about a woman, and about a respected person. Say which distinction is in the pronoun and which is in the verb.


## Lesson 9: Describe an ongoing action

### Outcome

Use an action-link form followed by the presence auxiliary to describe what is happening.

### Think before reading

In কৰি আছোঁ, which piece names the activity and which piece carries first-person agreement?

### Build the pattern

কৰি আছোঁ (kori asõ) means I am doing. The first element is a linking form of the action; the final presence auxiliary carries person. মই কাম কৰি আছোঁ therefore presents work as unfolding. Respectful আপুনি uses কৰি আছে. This analytic construction makes the distinction between activity and auxiliary especially visible.

পঢ়ি আছোঁ (poṛhi asõ) means I am reading, and খাই আছোঁ (khai asõ) means I am eating or drinking. Do not replace the linking form with a complete simple-present verb: কৰোঁ আছোঁ would pile two finite forms together in the model used here.

এতিয়া (etiya) means now. Adding it near the beginning gives মই এতিয়া কাম কৰি আছোঁ. The time word supports the interpretation but does not create the grammar. In connected speech you will also hear compact aspect forms whose meanings depend on context. This foundation first establishes the explicit action-plus-auxiliary pattern so you can analyse what each piece contributes.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| মই কাম কৰি আছোঁ। | Moi kam kori asõ. | I am working. |
| আপুনি কাম কৰি আছে। | Apuni kam kori ase. | You are working. (respectful) |
| মই কিতাপ পঢ়ি আছোঁ। | Moi kitap poṛhi asõ. | I am reading a book. |
| তেওঁ চাহ খাই আছে। | Teõ sah khai ase. | He or she is drinking tea. (respectful) |
| মই এতিয়া কাম কৰি আছোঁ। | Moi etiya kam kori asõ. | I am working now. |
| তুমি কিতাপ পঢ়ি আছা। | Tumi kitap poṛhi asa. | You are reading a book. (familiar) |

### Common trap

Do not use the full first-person routine form কৰোঁ before আছোঁ. The preceding action must be the linking form কৰি in this construction.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I am reading a book.
2. Say: He or she is drinking tea. (respectful)
3. Say: I am working now.
4. Say: You are reading a book. (familiar)
5. Say: We are working.
6. Say: She is reading a book. (ordinary)

***Answers:***

1. `মই কিতাপ পঢ়ি আছোঁ।` - `Moi kitap poṛhi asõ.`
2. `তেওঁ চাহ খাই আছে।` - `Teõ sah khai ase.`
3. `মই এতিয়া কাম কৰি আছোঁ।` - `Moi etiya kam kori asõ.`
4. `তুমি কিতাপ পঢ়ি আছা।` - `Tumi kitap poṛhi asa.`
5. `আমি কাম কৰি আছোঁ।` - `Ami kam kori asõ.`
6. `তাই কিতাপ পঢ়ি আছে।` - `Tai kitap poṛhi ase.`

### Why these answers work

The first verb remains nonfinite while the final auxiliary chooses আছোঁ or আছে. The final two answers transfer the ongoing pattern to a group and to a third-person woman without adding gender agreement to the auxiliary.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine a quiet classroom. Describe yourself reading, a respected visitor drinking tea, and the group working. Remove এতিয়া and check whether the ongoing construction remains intact.


## Lesson 10: Name owners and relationships

### Outcome

Use possessive forms before a noun and ask whose something is.

### Think before reading

Why does মোৰ replace মই in “my book” even though both refer to the speaker?

### Build the pattern

মোৰ (mor) means my, আমাৰ (amar) our, তোমাৰ (tomar) your familiar, and আপোনাৰ (aponar) your respectful. These forms precede the noun: মোৰ কিতাপ. They express a relationship between an owner or associated person and the following thing.

মোৰ নাম ৰীনা identifies a name, while মোৰ কিতাপ ইয়াত আছে locates a possessed thing. The possessive phrase remains intact whether it is the whole identifying expression or the subject of a longer location clause. কাৰ (kar) asks whose: এইটো কাৰ কিতাপ. Replace only the unknown owner rather than reorganising the whole English question word by word.

Not every relationship is literal ownership. মোৰ বন্ধু means my friend, without implying that people can be owned. Assamese kinship terms have additional possessive patterns and conventional forms that deserve their own treatment; do not mechanically extend this introductory noun pattern to every family term. Here the examples stay with names, friends, and objects whose forms are straightforward.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| এইটো মোৰ কিতাপ। | Eiṭo mor kitap. | This is my book. |
| সেইটো আপোনাৰ কলম। | Xeiṭo aponar kolom. | That is your pen. (respectful) |
| এইটো কাৰ কিতাপ? | Eiṭo kar kitap? | Whose book is this? |
| মোৰ নাম ৰীনা। | Mor nam Rina. | My name is Rina. |
| মোৰ কিতাপ ইয়াত আছে। | Mor kitap iyat ase. | My book is here. |
| সেইটো তোমাৰ কলম। | Xeiṭo tomar kolom. | That is your pen. (familiar) |

### Common trap

Do not confuse Assamese আমাৰ, our, with Bengali আমার, my. The similar appearance conceals a different person-number meaning across the two languages.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Whose book is this?
2. Say: My name is Rina.
3. Say: My book is here.
4. Say: That is your pen. (familiar)
5. Say: This is our book.
6. Say: He or she is my friend. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `এইটো কাৰ কিতাপ?` - `Eiṭo kar kitap?`
2. `মোৰ নাম ৰীনা।` - `Mor nam Rina.`
3. `মোৰ কিতাপ ইয়াত আছে।` - `Mor kitap iyat ase.`
4. `সেইটো তোমাৰ কলম।` - `Xeiṭo tomar kolom.`
5. `এইটো আমাৰ কিতাপ।` - `Eiṭo amar kitap.`
6. `তেওঁ মোৰ বন্ধু।` - `Teõ mor bondhu.`

### Why these answers work

The owner form precedes the noun, while the unknown owner is expressed by কাৰ. The last two answers transfer the pattern to collective ownership and a respectful friend, keeping the identity and location constructions distinct.

### Retrieve and recombine

Assign three objects to yourself, your group, and the listener. Ask whose one is, then answer. State explicitly whether the owner is one speaker or a group.


## Lesson 11: Have and lack resources

### Outcome

Express possession and absence using a genitive owner and an existence predicate.

### Think before reading

What does মোৰ কিতাপ আছে assert beyond merely saying মোৰ কিতাপ?

### Build the pattern

মোৰ কিতাপ আছে (mor kitap ase) means I have a book or books. The structure uses the owner in the genitive, the available thing, and আছে. The English subject I is therefore represented by মোৰ, not মই, in this possession construction.

নাই (nai) expresses absence: মোৰ কলম নাই, I have no pen. ইয়াত পানী নাই means there is no water here, with no owner at all. A negative existence sentence does not need the routine-action prefix placed before আছে in this elementary pattern.

সময় (xomoy) means time and টকা (ṭoka) money or a currency unit. মোৰ সময় আছে and মোৰ টকা নাই extend the same structure to resources that are not counted as simple objects. আপোনাৰ সময় আছে নে asks a respectful listener whether they have time. The respect belongs to আপোনাৰ; the final আছে predicates the existence of time rather than directly locating the person. This reasoning prevents false agreement changes based on the English translation.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| মোৰ কিতাপ আছে। | Mor kitap ase. | I have a book. |
| মোৰ কলম নাই। | Mor kolom nai. | I have no pen. |
| আপোনাৰ সময় আছে নে? | Aponar xomoy ase ne? | Do you have time? (respectful) |
| আমাৰ পানী আছে। | Amar pani ase. | We have water. |
| ইয়াত পানী নাই। | Iyat pani nai. | There is no water here. |
| আপোনাৰ কিতাপ আছে। | Aponar kitap ase. | You have a book. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not force a first-person verb ending into মোৰ কিতাপ আছে. The construction asserts the availability of books, and the owner is expressed separately in the genitive.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Do you have time? (respectful)
2. Say: We have water.
3. Say: There is no water here.
4. Say: You have a book. (respectful)
5. Say: We have time.
6. Say: I have no money.

***Answers:***

1. `আপোনাৰ সময় আছে নে?` - `Aponar xomoy ase ne?`
2. `আমাৰ পানী আছে।` - `Amar pani ase.`
3. `ইয়াত পানী নাই।` - `Iyat pani nai.`
4. `আপোনাৰ কিতাপ আছে।` - `Aponar kitap ase.`
5. `আমাৰ সময় আছে।` - `Amar xomoy ase.`
6. `মোৰ টকা নাই।` - `Mor ṭoka nai.`

### Why these answers work

The genitive owner comes first and নাই replaces positive existence to express absence. The final two answers extend the same pattern to group time and money, demonstrating that a possession construction is not limited to physical belongings.

### Retrieve and recombine

Before an imagined lesson, say what you have and lack. Ask whether the listener has time. Compare a possession sentence with a location sentence about the same object.


## Lesson 12: Use Assamese classifiers

### Outcome

Count books and pens and refer back to particular objects with suitable classifiers.

### Think before reading

Why is a book কিতাপখন while a pen can be কলমডাল?

### Build the pattern

Assamese has a rich classifier system. খন (khon) is common with books and other flat or extended objects; ডাল (dal) is useful with a pen and other long objects. কিতাপখন identifies the book, and কলমডাল identifies the pen. Classifiers contribute a conventional view of the object, not merely an English article.

Counting can put the numeral and classifier before the noun: এখন কিতাপ (ekhon kitap), one book; দুখন কিতাপ (dukhon kitap), two books; এডাল কলম (edal kolom), one pen; দুডাল কলম (dudal kolom), two pens. Here, এখন is not the Bengali word meaning now. In this Assamese course, এতিয়া means now.

The table gives a small set of combinations rather than claiming one classifier works for everything. A numeral-classifier expression and a noun-final classifier have related but different discourse uses. First introduce a counted object, then use the noun-final form when referring back to it. Human classifiers and their respect implications need separate attention in the next lesson.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| এইটো এখন কিতাপ। | Eiṭo ekhon kitap. | This is a book. |
| কিতাপখন ইয়াত আছে। | Kitapkhon iyat ase. | The book is here. |
| মোৰ দুখন কিতাপ আছে। | Mor dukhon kitap ase. | I have two books. |
| মোৰ এডাল কলম আছে। | Mor edal kolom ase. | I have a pen. |
| মোৰ দুডাল কলম আছে। | Mor dudal kolom ase. | I have two pens. |
| কলমডাল মোৰ। | Kolomdal mor. | The pen is mine. |

### Common trap

Do not translate Bengali একটা বই mechanically into Assamese. The selected book classifier here is খন. Also keep Assamese এখন, one with this classifier, distinct from Bengali এখন, now.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I have two books.
2. Say: I have a pen.
3. Say: I have two pens.
4. Say: The pen is mine.
5. Say: The book is yours. (respectful)
6. Say: The pen is there.

***Answers:***

1. `মোৰ দুখন কিতাপ আছে।` - `Mor dukhon kitap ase.`
2. `মোৰ এডাল কলম আছে।` - `Mor edal kolom ase.`
3. `মোৰ দুডাল কলম আছে।` - `Mor dudal kolom ase.`
4. `কলমডাল মোৰ।` - `Kolomdal mor.`
5. `কিতাপখন আপোনাৰ।` - `Kitapkhon aponar.`
6. `কলমডাল তাত আছে।` - `Kolomdal tat ase.`

### Why these answers work

The count precedes the noun in the counted phrases, while the classifier follows the noun in reference forms. The last two answers apply familiar possession and location grammar to newly specific noun phrases.

### Retrieve and recombine

Picture two books and one pen. Introduce the quantities, then locate the particular pen. Explain why changing the object may require changing the classifier.


## Lesson 13: People, respect, and agent marking

### Outcome

Recognise human classifiers and the agent ending in a named subject’s action sentence.

### Think before reading

Why can ৰীনা be unchanged in an identity but appear as ৰীনাই before a transitive action?

### Build the pattern

Assamese marks many noun agents with an ending -এ or -ই in transitive clauses. ৰীনাই কিতাপ পঢ়ে means Rina reads books. The agent ending identifies the person doing the reading. This is not a feminine ending; it arises from the noun's role in the clause. Simple pronouns such as মই and তেওঁ do not take this ending in the same way.

Human classifiers also encode social distinctions. এজন শিক্ষক identifies one male teacher; এগৰাকী শিক্ষক provides a respectful human classifier usable across genders. This course uses গৰাকী in respectful introductions rather than treating the general thing classifier as socially neutral for adults. The noun-final শিক্ষকগৰাকী identifies the teacher respectfully.

In শিক্ষকগৰাকীয়ে কিতাপ পঢ়ে, the classifier-bearing noun also receives the agent ending. Read it as teacher, respectful classifier, agent ending, then object and verb. This layered structure is characteristic Assamese grammar and must not be replaced with a Bengali-style unmarked noun just because the English translation is the same.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| তেওঁ এগৰাকী শিক্ষক। | Teõ egoraki xikkhok. | He or she is a teacher. (respectful classifier) |
| ৰীনাই কিতাপ পঢ়ে। | Rinai kitap poṛhe. | Rina reads books. |
| শিক্ষকগৰাকীয়ে কিতাপ পঢ়ে। | Xikkhokgorakiye kitap poṛhe. | The teacher reads books. (respectful) |
| ৰীনা শিক্ষক। | Rina xikkhok. | Rina is a teacher. |
| ৰীনাই চাহ খায়। | Rinai sah khay. | Rina drinks tea. |
| তেওঁ এজন শিক্ষক। | Teõ ejon xikkhok. | He is a male teacher. |

### Common trap

Do not generalise an agent ending to every pronoun or every clause. The examples contrast noun agents of reading with a simple identity and with unmarked simple pronouns.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: The teacher reads books. (respectful)
2. Say: Rina is a teacher.
3. Say: Rina drinks tea.
4. Say: He is a male teacher.
5. Say: Rina works.
6. Say: The teacher drinks tea. (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `শিক্ষকগৰাকীয়ে কিতাপ পঢ়ে।` - `Xikkhokgorakiye kitap poṛhe.`
2. `ৰীনা শিক্ষক।` - `Rina xikkhok.`
3. `ৰীনাই চাহ খায়।` - `Rinai sah khay.`
4. `তেওঁ এজন শিক্ষক।` - `Teõ ejon xikkhok.`
5. `ৰীনাই কাম কৰে।` - `Rinai kam kore.`
6. `শিক্ষকগৰাকীয়ে চাহ খায়।` - `Xikkhokgorakiye sah khay.`

### Why these answers work

Named and classifier-bearing subjects receive the agent form in the transitive reading and work examples as shown. Respectful classifiers refer to the person without creating gender agreement on the verb. The new name and teacher examples make the noun-role contrast visible.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce a teacher with a respectful classifier, then say that the teacher reads. Point out the extra agent ending in the action sentence and explain why it was absent from the introduction.


## Lesson 14: Describe places precisely

### Outcome

Use locative endings and spatial postpositions to locate familiar objects.

### Think before reading

Where does ওপৰত go relative to the table that supports the object?

### Build the pattern

ঘৰত (ghorot) means at home or in the house, with the locative ending -ত. A longer spatial expression uses a genitive reference noun before a postposition: মেজৰ ওপৰত (mejor oporot), on the table. মেজৰ তলত (mejor tolot) is under the table, and ঘৰৰ ভিতৰত (ghoror bhitorot) is inside the house.

The order is reference object followed by spatial relation, unlike English on the table. Keep the genitive ৰ on the reference noun in the models here. The complete location block replaces ইয়াত in a sentence you already know: কিতাপখন মেজৰ ওপৰত আছে.

মেজ (mej) is table and ঘৰ (ghor) house or home. These familiar words can appear with different endings depending on whether they name a place directly or anchor a spatial relation. ঘৰত directly locates someone at home; ঘৰৰ ভিতৰত explicitly highlights the inside. The difference is precision, not a requirement to translate every English in with a separate word.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |

## Lesson 15: Move toward and come from

### Outcome

### Think before reading

How can ঘৰত, ঘৰলৈ, and ঘৰৰ পৰা describe three different relationships to the same house?

### Build the pattern

ঘৰলৈ (ghoroloi) means toward or to home, while ঘৰৰ পৰা (ghoror pora) means from home. The source uses a genitive form followed by পৰা. The destination ending -লৈ attaches to the place expression. Neither should be confused with the static location ঘৰত.

যাওঁ (jaõ) is first-person go, যায় (jay) respectful or third-person goes; আহোঁ (ahõ) is first-person come, and আহে (ahe) respectful or third-person comes. মই ঘৰলৈ যাওঁ is a routine or general movement statement. মই ঘৰৰ পৰা আহোঁ identifies a source. These are Assamese verb shapes, not Bengali যাই and আসি.

ক’লৈ (koloi) asks where to, while ক’ৰ পৰা (kor pora) asks where from. The question word receives the same kind of relationship marking as a named place. This makes questions predictable: replace the known destination or source with its corresponding interrogative expression, and leave the final movement verb intact.

### Build the pattern

## Lesson 17: Describe size, quality, and comparison

### Outcome

Describe an object without adjective gender agreement and compare two things.

### Think before reading

If a good book becomes two good books, which adjective ending must change?

### Build the pattern

Ordinary adjectives such as ভাল (bhal), good, ডাঙৰ (daṅor), big, সৰু (xoru), small, and নতুন (notun), new, do not gain masculine or feminine endings in these sentences. কিতাপখন নতুন is a complete present description. A noun phrase can put the adjective before the noun: এখন নতুন কিতাপ.

বৰ (bor) can intensify a quality, as in বৰ ভাল, very good. Comparison can use a genitive standard followed by তকৈ (tokoi): এইখন সেইখনতকৈ ডাঙৰ, this one is bigger than that one. The comparison standard is marked as a unit; the adjective itself does not acquire an English-style -er.

Do not extend খন-based demonstratives indiscriminately to every object. The classifier still contributes the conventional noun category even when the noun is omitted.

The past verb supplies event time while কালি adds the day. First-person groups share the first-person past pattern. The final two answers transfer the completed-event report to coming and to a particular book.

## Lesson 20: A completed result and its negative

### Outcome

### Think before reading

### Build the pattern

কৰিছোঁ (korisõ) is a compact aspect form often used for have done in a completion context. পঢ়িছোঁ (poṛhisõ) similarly gives have read, and খাইছোঁ (khaisõ) have eaten or drunk. 3. `মই কিতাপখন পঢ়া নাই।` - `Moi kitapkhon poṛha nai.`
4. `তেওঁ চাহ খোৱা নাই।` - `Teõ sah khowa nai.`
5. `আমি কাম কৰা নাই।` - `Ami kam kora nai.`
6. `মই এতিয়াও কিতাপখন পঢ়া নাই।` - `Moi etiyaw kitapkhon poṛha nai.`

### Why these answers work

The affirmative answers use compact completion forms. Negative completion uses a nonfinite action form followed by নাই. The final two answers recombine the negative with a speaker group and with yet, preserving the distinction from a negative routine.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine a reading checklist. Say which book you have read and which task you have not done yet. Compare a negative routine with a negative completed event.


## Lesson 21: Plan tomorrow

### Outcome

Use future forms and keep their negative prefix separate from past completion.

### Think before reading

Why does a future plan use কৰিম rather than the routine কৰোঁ?

### Build the pattern

কৰিম (korim) is I or we will do. Respectful আপুনি and তেওঁ use কৰিব (koribo), while familiar তুমি uses কৰিবা (koriba). The written respectful form ends in ব but the romanisation includes its pronounced final vowel. This is Assamese morphology, not Bengali first-person করব.

যাম (jam) means I will go, আহিম (ahim) I will come, খাম (kham) I will eat or drink, and পঢ়িম (poṛhim) I will read. কাইলৈ (kailoi) unambiguously means tomorrow. মই কাইলৈ আহিম therefore makes a clear upcoming plan.

The future negative uses the familiar prefix strategy: নকৰিম (nokorim), will not do, নাযাম (najam), will not go, and নাখাম (nakham), will not eat or drink. It is distinct from the completed-event construction কৰা নাই. Choose the event's time first, then make the chosen form negative. A question about a plan can add final নে while preserving the future verb.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| মই কাইলৈ আহিম। | Moi kailoi ahim. | I will come tomorrow. |
| আপুনি কেতিয়া আহিব? | Apuni ketiya ahibo? | When will you come? (respectful) |
| মই কিতাপখন পঢ়িম। | Moi kitapkhon poṛhim. | I will read the book. |
| মই চাহ নাখাম। | Moi sah nakham. | I will not drink tea. |
| তেওঁ কাম কৰিব। | Teõ kam koribo. | He or she will work. (respectful) |
| মই ঘৰলৈ যাম। | Moi ghoroloi jam. | I will go home. |

### Common trap

Do not use Bengali -bo as the first-person Assamese future ending. Assamese কৰিম and যাম are the first-person models here; respectful কৰিব and যাব follow their own pattern.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I will read the book.
2. Say: I will not drink tea.
3. Say: He or she will work. (respectful)
4. Say: I will go home.
5. Say: We will work tomorrow.
6. Say: Will you come here? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `মই কিতাপখন পঢ়িম।` - `Moi kitapkhon poṛhim.`
2. `মই চাহ নাখাম।` - `Moi sah nakham.`
3. `তেওঁ কাম কৰিব।` - `Teõ kam koribo.`
4. `মই ঘৰলৈ যাম।` - `Moi ghoroloi jam.`
5. `আমি কাইলৈ কাম কৰিম।` - `Ami kailoi kam korim.`
6. `আপুনি ইয়ালৈ আহিব নে?` - `Apuni iyaloi ahibo ne?`

### Why these answers work

The future ending matches the subject and the negative is prefixed to the future stem. Tomorrow is a separate time expression. The final two answers transfer a plan to a group and ask a respectful listener about a future arrival.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give two plans for tomorrow and one thing you will not do. Ask whether the listener will come. Recover the correct first-person and respectful future forms before speaking.


## Lesson 22: Ability and desire

### Outcome

Combine a nonfinite action with ability or desire while keeping only the final verb finite.

### Think before reading

In পঢ়িব পাৰোঁ, which word expresses the action and which expresses the ability?

### Build the pattern

পঢ়িব পাৰোঁ (poṛhibo parõ) means I can read. The first verb appears in an infinitival form and the final পাৰোঁ carries first-person ability. Respectful পাৰে (pare) gives আপুনি পঢ়িব পাৰে. The written infinitive can look like a respectful future form, so its grammatical role must be read from the whole construction.

কৰিব (koribo), do; যাব (jabo), go; আহিব (ahibo), come; and ক’ব (kobo), say or speak, combine with this ability pattern. মই অসমীয়া ক’ব পাৰোঁ says I can speak Assamese. It is a statement of ability at an unspecified level, not a promise of fluency.

বিচাৰোঁ (bisarõ), want or seek, can combine with a desired action: মই ঘৰলৈ যাব বিচাৰোঁ. With a thing, মই চাহ বিচাৰোঁ means I want tea. নোৱাৰোঁ (nowarõ) is cannot, as in মই আহিব নোৱাৰোঁ. This frequent negative ability form should be learned directly rather than guessed from the affirmative by a naive prefix rule.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| মই অসমীয়া ক’ব পাৰোঁ। | Moi oxomiya kobo parõ. | I can speak Assamese. |
| আপুনি অসমীয়া ক’ব পাৰে। | Apuni oxomiya kobo pare. | You can speak Assamese. (respectful) |
| মই কিতাপখন পঢ়িব পাৰোঁ। | Moi kitapkhon poṛhibo parõ. | I can read the book. |
| মই আহিব নোৱাৰোঁ। | Moi ahibo nowarõ. | I cannot come. |
| মই ঘৰলৈ যাব বিচাৰোঁ। | Moi ghoroloi jabo bisarõ. | I want to go home. |
| মই চাহ বিচাৰোঁ। | Moi sah bisarõ. | I want tea. |

### Common trap

Do not conjugate both verbs as independent first-person predicates. Also learn নোৱাৰোঁ as the ordinary inability form rather than inventing a negative by analogy without checking it.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I can read the book.
2. Say: I cannot come.
3. Say: I want to go home.
4. Say: I want tea.
5. Say: We can work.
6. Say: I want to go to school.

***Answers:***

1. `মই কিতাপখন পঢ়িব পাৰোঁ।` - `Moi kitapkhon poṛhibo parõ.`
2. `মই আহিব নোৱাৰোঁ।` - `Moi ahibo nowarõ.`
3. `মই ঘৰলৈ যাব বিচাৰোঁ।` - `Moi ghoroloi jabo bisarõ.`
4. `মই চাহ বিচাৰোঁ।` - `Moi sah bisarõ.`
5. `আমি কাম কৰিব পাৰোঁ।` - `Ami kam koribo parõ.`
6. `মই বিদ্যালয়লৈ যাব বিচাৰোঁ।` - `Moi bidyaloyoloi jabo bisarõ.`

### Why these answers work

The activity precedes the final ability or desire verb. The first-person ending belongs to পাৰোঁ, বিচাৰোঁ, or নোৱাৰোঁ. The final two answers extend ability to a group and desire to a school destination.

### Retrieve and recombine

State one thing you can do, one you cannot do, and one you want to do. Explain how the same infinitive can serve different meanings depending on the final verb.


## Lesson 23: Needs, liking, and reasons

### Outcome

Distinguish a need from a preference and connect a short reason clause.

### Think before reading

Why can English “I need water” use a different Assamese person form from “I like tea”?

### Build the pattern

মোক পানী লাগে (mok pani lage) expresses I need water. The person appears as মোক, the object or recipient form. মোক এখন কিতাপ লাগে is a practical request-like statement of need. Do not force every English subject I to become মই in every Assamese construction.

মই চাহ ভাল পাওঁ (moi sah bhal paõ) means I like tea. Here the ordinary subject মই combines with the expression ভাল পাওঁ, like or love depending on its object and context. Respectful আপুনি uses ভাল পায় (bhal pay). Liking, wanting, and needing are distinct meanings with distinct patterns.

কাৰণ (karon), because, introduces a reason. মই নাযাম, কাৰণ মোৰ কাম আছে means I will not go because I have work. The reason clause uses the possession frame, while the main clause uses a future negative. Joining them does not require matching pronoun case or tense across the two clauses. Each clause retains the grammar demanded by its own meaning.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| মোক পানী লাগে। | Mok pani lage. | I need water. |
| মই চাহ ভাল পাওঁ। | Moi sah bhal paõ. | I like tea. |
| মোক এখন কিতাপ লাগে। | Mok ekhon kitap lage. | I need a book. |
| আপুনি চাহ ভাল পায় নে? | Apuni sah bhal pay ne? | Do you like tea? (respectful) |
| মই কিতাপখন ভাল পাওঁ। | Moi kitapkhon bhal paõ. | I like the book. |
| মই নাযাম, কাৰণ মোৰ কাম আছে। | Moi najam, karon mor kam ase. | I will not go because I have work. |

### Common trap

Do not collapse লাগে, বিচাৰোঁ, and ভাল পাওঁ into one universal want verb. Also do not replace মোক with মই inside the need construction taught here.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I need a book.
2. Say: Do you like tea? (respectful)
3. Say: I like the book.
4. Say: I will not go because I have work.
5. Say: We need water.
6. Say: I cannot come because I have work.

***Answers:***

1. `মোক এখন কিতাপ লাগে।` - `Mok ekhon kitap lage.`
2. `আপুনি চাহ ভাল পায় নে?` - `Apuni sah bhal pay ne?`
3. `মই কিতাপখন ভাল পাওঁ।` - `Moi kitapkhon bhal paõ.`
4. `মই নাযাম, কাৰণ মোৰ কাম আছে।` - `Moi najam, karon mor kam ase.`
5. `আমাক পানী লাগে।` - `Amak pani lage.`
6. `মই আহিব নোৱাৰোঁ, কাৰণ মোৰ কাম আছে।` - `Moi ahibo nowarõ, karon mor kam ase.`

### Why these answers work

The need answers use মোক or আমাক for the experiencer; liking uses an ordinary subject and the appropriate finite পাওঁ or পায়. The final two answers transfer need to a group and connect a reason using a familiar possession clause.

### Retrieve and recombine

Plan refreshments for a study session. Say what you like, what the group needs, and why you cannot go somewhere. Identify the person form in each construction.


## Lesson 24: Shopping and conversation repair

### Outcome

Ask a price, request a quantity, and ask a speaker to repeat or slow down.

### Think before reading

How can the same respectful request grammar help both a purchase and a misunderstanding?

### Build the pattern

দাম (dam) means price and কিমান (kiman) asks how much or how many. কিতাপখনৰ দাম কিমান asks the price of the book. The genitive ending follows the classifier, giving কিতাপখনৰ. পঞ্চাশ টকা (ponchas ṭoka) is fifty rupees in the Indian shopping context assumed here.

মোক দুডাল কলম দিয়ক combines a recipient, a counted object phrase, and the respectful imperative. The count and classifier are not optional English plural decorations: they form the normal Assamese quantity expression used in this model.

আকৌ কওক (akou kowk) asks someone to say it again. অলপ লাহে লাহে কওক asks them to speak a little slowly. বুজি পোৱা নাই, have not understood, is a useful completion-negative repair expression. These phrases allow you to ask for more information instead of pretending to understand an amount. A classroom course cannot guarantee recognition of every spoken number or regional pronunciation, so asking again is part of competent beginner interaction.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| কিতাপখনৰ দাম কিমান? | Kitapkhonor dam kiman? | What is the price of the book? |
| দাম পঞ্চাশ টকা। | Dam ponchas ṭoka. | The price is fifty rupees. |
| মোক দুডাল কলম দিয়ক। | Mok dudal kolom diyok. | Give me two pens. (respectful) |
| মই কিতাপখন বিচাৰোঁ। | Moi kitapkhon bisarõ. | I want the book. |
| মই বুজি পোৱা নাই। | Moi buji powa nai. | I have not understood. |
| আকৌ কওক। | Akou kowk. | Say it again. (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not silently guess a price when you miss a number. Linguistically, keep the genitive after the classifier in কিতাপখনৰ rather than inserting it between the noun and classifier.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: Give me two pens. (respectful)
2. Say: I want the book.
3. Say: I have not understood.
4. Say: Say it again. (respectful)
5. Say: Speak a little slowly. (respectful)
6. Say: What is the price of the pen?

***Answers:***

1. `মোক দুডাল কলম দিয়ক।` - `Mok dudal kolom diyok.`
2. `মই কিতাপখন বিচাৰোঁ।` - `Moi kitapkhon bisarõ.`
3. `মই বুজি পোৱা নাই।` - `Moi buji powa nai.`
4. `আকৌ কওক।` - `Akou kowk.`
5. `অলপ লাহে লাহে কওক।` - `Olop lahe lahe kowk.`
6. `কলমডালৰ দাম কিমান?` - `Kolomdalor dam kiman?`

### Why these answers work

The price question uses কিমান for the missing value. The purchase request keeps the numeral-classifier block intact. The final two repair answers reuse respectful imperative grammar with an adverbial phrase and apply the price frame to a pen.

### Retrieve and recombine

Role-play buying a book: ask its price, request it, and ask for a repetition. Then switch to two pens and rebuild the quantity phrase with the appropriate classifier.


## Lesson 25: A coherent study visit

### Outcome

Combine personal information, a routine, a result, and a plan while selecting each construction independently.

### Think before reading

Can you classify each intended sentence before translating: identity, location, habit, completed event, need, or plan?

### Build the pattern

Start a short visit with মোৰ নাম ৰীনা. Locate yourself with মই ঘৰত আছোঁ, then explain a routine using a simple present verb. A completed reading result uses a compact completion form or the relevant past form; an upcoming visit uses the future. A coherent paragraph does not need every sentence to share the same tense.

অসমীয়া requires special care if you know Bengali. I is মই, we is আমি, respectful you is আপুনি, and tomorrow is কাইলৈ. A shared-looking script is not permission to carry over Bengali negative placement, honorific endings, or classifier choices. You have now seen Assamese patterns in their own contexts, including agent marking on noun subjects.

For an action sequence, the linking form can precede a final future verb: মই চাহ খাই কাম কৰিম, I will work after drinking tea. The first action is not independently marked as past; it is simply prior to the future work. Keep the same understood subject in these initial sequences. A longer narrative or a change of actor calls for more explicit clause organisation and further study.

### Examples to take apart

| Native form | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| মোৰ নাম ৰীনা। | Mor nam Rina. | My name is Rina. |
| মই ঘৰত আছোঁ। | Moi ghorot asõ. | I am at home. |
| মই প্ৰতিদিনে কিতাপ পঢ়োঁ। | Moi protidine kitap poṛhõ. | I read books every day. |
| মই কিতাপখন পঢ়িছোঁ। | Moi kitapkhon poṛhisõ. | I have read the book. |
| মই কাইলৈ কাম কৰিম। | Moi kailoi kam korim. | I will work tomorrow. |
| আপুনি কাইলৈ কি কৰিব? | Apuni kailoi ki koribo? | What will you do tomorrow? (respectful) |

### Common trap

Do not interpret the linking খাই as proof that the drinking happened in the past. The final future verb places the planned sequence in future time.

### Practice

Build each sentence in the course variety. Say the whole sentence before opening the key.

1. Say: I read books every day.
2. Say: I have read the book.
3. Say: I will work tomorrow.
4. Say: What will you do tomorrow? (respectful)
5. Say: I need a new book.
6. Say: I will work after drinking tea.

***Answers:***

1. `মই প্ৰতিদিনে কিতাপ পঢ়োঁ।` - `Moi protidine kitap poṛhõ.`
2. `মই কিতাপখন পঢ়িছোঁ।` - `Moi kitapkhon poṛhisõ.`
3. `মই কাইলৈ কাম কৰিম।` - `Moi kailoi kam korim.`
4. `আপুনি কাইলৈ কি কৰিব?` - `Apuni kailoi ki koribo?`
5. `মোক এখন নতুন কিতাপ লাগে।` - `Mok ekhon notun kitap lage.`
6. `মই চাহ খাই কাম কৰিম।` - `Moi sah khai kam korim.`

### Why these answers work

The mixed answers select naming, routine, completion, future, respectful question, and action-sequence patterns. The final two prompts transfer need and sequence into a practical study plan rather than introducing a new tense table.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create a six-sentence study-visit dialogue. Include an introduction, location, routine, completed action, future plan, and respectful question. Then identify one Assamese feature that would be lost by substituting a Bengali form.
