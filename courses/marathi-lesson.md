# Marathi: A pattern-first foundation

This course uses contemporary standard Marathi in Devanagari, with mostly neutral everyday expressions and standard written endings. Colloquial variants are identified where they help recognition. Romanisation is a reading aid: ā ī ū mark length, ṭ ḍ ṇ ḷ mark retroflex consonants, ś is sh, and h after a stop marks aspiration. It is not a replacement for Marathi spelling or audio.

### References and review notes

The distinction between simple habitual and explicit progressive forms, and the completed transitive agreement contrast, were checked against Dhongde and Wali’s [Marathi reference grammar](https://archive.org/stream/marathi_20210812/Marathi_djvu.txt). The explanations, situations and exercises here are original. Regional speech, colloquial neuter spellings and register deserve fluent-speaker review; the course does not claim that one written pattern exhausts spoken Marathi.

## Lesson 1: Identity with आहे

**Outcome:** Introduce yourself and identify another person without translating English articles.

### Think before reading

English changes am to is. If मी means I and तो means he, must Marathi change आहे between them?

### Build the pattern

Marathi gives you an economical first sentence: a person, an identity, and आहे (āhe). Both मी विद्यार्थी आहे and तो विद्यार्थी आहे use the same final word. English needs different forms of be; this particular Marathi contrast does not. A profession or identity normally needs no separate word corresponding to English a. Put the identity before the final verb. This is a useful neutral order, although conversation can move familiar information around for emphasis.

The table deliberately begins with विद्यार्थी, which can describe a student without teaching an adjective ending yet. मी does not tell you the speaker’s gender. The identity word may have a feminine counterpart, as शिक्षक and शिक्षिका do, but the present आहे is unchanged. Do not assume that every job has to be replaced by a special feminine word in every speaker’s usage.

Say मी slowly as one long vowel. In the romanisation, ā, ī and ū represent long vowels; ṭ, ḍ, ṇ and ḷ indicate retroflex consonants, made with the tongue farther back than their dental counterparts. Marathi ल and ळ are distinct. Aspirated consonants such as ख have an audible breath release; kh is not the English sound in school. Start by recognising these distinctions in the actual words you use, rather than reciting an alphabet before speaking.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मी विद्यार्थी आहे. | Mī vidyārthī āhe. | I am a student. |
| तो शिक्षक आहे. | To śikṣak āhe. | He is a teacher. |
| ती शिक्षिका आहे. | Tī śikṣikā āhe. | She is a teacher. |
| मी भारतीय आहे. | Mī bhāratīya āhe. | I am Indian. |
| नमस्कार. | Namaskār. | Hello, a respectful greeting. |
| ती विद्यार्थी आहे. | Tī vidyārthī āhe. | She is a student. |

### Common trap

Do not attach an English article to विद्यार्थी, or make आहे masculine and feminine. Gender distinctions elsewhere in Marathi are real, but they do not license changing every word in an identity sentence.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say that you are a student.
2. Identify a man as a teacher.
3. Identify a woman as a teacher, using शिक्षिका.
4. Say that you are Indian.

***Answers:***

1. `मी विद्यार्थी आहे.` - `Mī vidyārthī āhe.`
2. `तो शिक्षक आहे.` - `To śikṣak āhe.`
3. `ती शिक्षिका आहे.` - `Tī śikṣikā āhe.`
4. `मी भारतीय आहे.` - `Mī bhāratīya āhe.`

### Why these answers work

The first and fourth answers share मी and आहे while changing the identity. The middle answers change both the reference pronoun and the selected profession word. None needs a word for a. Read the final verb after the identity instead of inserting it immediately after the pronoun.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce an imaginary student, then identify yourself. Keep the difference between speaking about someone and speaking as that person. Before the next lesson, retrieve मी, तो, ती and आहे without looking.


## Lesson 2: Respectful you and two kinds of we

**Outcome:** Choose a suitable addressee form and include or exclude your listener deliberately.

### Think before reading

You invite your listener into a group. Would a word meaning we but excluding that listener communicate the invitation correctly?

### Build the pattern

Marathi separates आम्ही (āmhī), we excluding the addressee, from आपण (āpaṇ), an inclusive we in the use taught here. This distinction is not about the number of people alone. Imagine two organisers speaking to a guest: आम्ही तयार आहोत reports the organisers' readiness; आपण तयार आहोत places speaker and guest inside the same ready group.

तू is a familiar singular you, while तुम्ही serves both plural you and respectful singular you. Use तुम्ही with a new adult acquaintance unless the relationship suggests otherwise. Its associated present copula is आहात. Familiar तू takes आहेस, and both first-person plural forms in this lesson take आहोत. Learn the short combinations as meaningful pairs, rather than swapping pronouns while leaving the verb untouched.

आपण also appears as an especially respectful you in some contexts and as a reflexive elsewhere. Those are separate uses; they do not erase the inclusive contrast practised here. When hearing an isolated आपण sentence, identify who is speaking to whom before assigning an English pronoun. The adjective तयार, ready, is helpful because it stays unchanged across the examples. That lets you hear person and respect in the copula itself. A question can be indicated by intonation, but the next lesson supplies an explicit question particle so that you need not rely on written punctuation alone.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| तुम्ही तयार आहात. | Tumhī tayār āhāt. | You are ready, respectfully or to several people. |
| तू तयार आहेस. | Tū tayār āhes. | You are ready, to a familiar individual. |
| आम्ही तयार आहोत. | Āmhī tayār āhot. | We are ready, excluding the listener. |
| आपण तयार आहोत. | Āpaṇ tayār āhot. | We are ready, including the listener. |
| ते तयार आहेत. | Te tayār āhet. | They are ready. |
| मी तयार आहे. | Mī tayār āhe. | I am ready. |

### Common trap

Using आम्ही in a shared invitation can unintentionally leave the listener outside the plan. Also, respectful singular तुम्ही does not take the singular familiar आहेस merely because only one person is present.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Tell a new adult acquaintance that they are ready.
2. Tell one close friend that they are ready.
3. Say your group is ready, excluding the listener.
4. Say everyone including your listener is ready.

***Answers:***

1. `तुम्ही तयार आहात.` - `Tumhī tayār āhāt.`
2. `तू तयार आहेस.` - `Tū tayār āhes.`
3. `आम्ही तयार आहोत.` - `Āmhī tayār āhot.`
4. `आपण तयार आहोत.` - `Āpaṇ tayār āhot.`

### Why these answers work

The first two answers show social relationship through तुम्ही आहात and तू आहेस. The last two retain आहोत because both are first-person plural, but the pronoun changes the group’s membership. तयार contributes the same description throughout, so it cannot tell you who belongs to the group.

### Retrieve and recombine

Picture a family speaking to a taxi driver. Say that the family is ready without including the driver, then imagine the driver joining a shared activity and rebuild the inclusive statement.


## Lesson 3: Yes, no and a genuine question

**Outcome:** Ask whether a statement is true and deny a present state.

### Think before reading

You already know तुम्ही तयार आहात. Where could a question signal go without rebuilding the sentence in English order?

### Build the pattern

Add का (kā) to the end of a statement to ask a straightforward yes-or-no question. तुम्ही तयार आहात का keeps the person and predicate in their normal positions. The particle asks the listener to confirm the proposition. It does not require an equivalent of English do, and there is no inversion of subject and verb.

For the simple present negative taught here, नाही (nāhī) replaces the affirmative copula. मी तयार नाही means I am not ready. It is not made by adding नाही after a complete आहे statement. Third-person ती तयार नाही works with the same negative form. In careful speech, additional person marking can occur with some subjects, but the short examples here provide common, usable denials without introducing that full paradigm.

हो means yes and नाही can also stand alone for no. A complete answer is often clearer than a bare yes or no, especially if the question itself contains a negative. If someone asks whether a teacher is present, identifying the person and denying readiness are different answers, even if both begin with नाही. Listen for the proposition being checked.

Be careful with का: in other positions it can mean why. Sentence-final का after a complete statement is the yes-or-no pattern at this stage. The distinction shows why a word’s location matters alongside its dictionary meaning. The practice keeps questions affirmative so that the intended response is unambiguous.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| तुम्ही तयार आहात का? | Tumhī tayār āhāt kā? | Are you ready, respectfully? |
| मी तयार नाही. | Mī tayār nāhī. | I am not ready. |
| ती शिक्षिका आहे का? | Tī śikṣikā āhe kā? | Is she a teacher? |
| तो तयार नाही. | To tayār nāhī. | He is not ready. |
| हो. | Ho. | Yes. |
| नाही. | Nāhī. | No. |

### Common trap

Avoid English question order and an invented do-support verb. Also distinguish a refusal from an inability: saying you are not ready describes a state, while saying no to an invitation can perform a different social action.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask a stranger whether they are ready.
2. Say you are not ready.
3. Ask whether a woman is a teacher, using शिक्षिका.
4. Say that he is not ready.

***Answers:***

1. `तुम्ही तयार आहात का?` - `Tumhī tayār āhāt kā?`
2. `मी तयार नाही.` - `Mī tayār nāhī.`
3. `ती शिक्षिका आहे का?` - `Tī śikṣikā āhe kā?`
4. `तो तयार नाही.` - `To tayār nāhī.`

### Why these answers work

Questions preserve the affirmative copula and add का. The denials use नाही in place of आहे. The pronouns stay at the beginning because nothing about asking or denying changes who is being discussed. A question mark alone is not the new grammatical component; का is.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask whether a student is ready, answer negatively, then change only the person. Decide which form of you is suitable if you ask the same question at a reception desk.


## Lesson 4: This, that and three noun genders

**Outcome:** Point to a person or object and match a basic description.

### Think before reading

घर means house and मुलगी means girl. Should this have the same form before both just because English uses one word?

### Build the pattern

Marathi nouns belong to masculine, feminine or neuter genders. Natural gender helps with many people words, but it does not predict the gender of every object. Learn घर, house, as neuter, मुलगा, boy, as masculine, and मुलगी, girl, as feminine. These choices affect words around the noun.

For a nearby singular referent, use हा with masculine nouns, ही with feminine nouns and हे with neuter nouns. Thus हा मुलगा and हे घर are different noun phrases before you add any sentence verb. The near forms are not simply alternate spellings of one unchanging word. With a distant singular referent, तो, ती and ते provide a corresponding contrast. The same forms can refer back to people or things already mentioned; their job depends on context.

The adjective मोठा changes with gender in these singular examples: मोठा, मोठी, मोठे. The house is neuter, so हे घर मोठे आहे. Marathi also has adjectives that remain unchanged, including तयार from earlier lessons. A rule for the changing adjective class is not a command to alter every describing word.

Written Marathi often uses an explicit final vowel such as मोठे; conversational spelling may show मोठं for a colloquial neuter pronunciation. The main examples retain standard written forms. Recognising the everyday variant is useful, but mixing the two spelling styles at random makes it harder to see the pattern. Choose the noun first, then let its gender guide both this and the adjective.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| हे घर मोठे आहे. | He ghar moṭhe āhe. | This house is big. |
| ही मुलगी तयार आहे. | Hī mulgī tayār āhe. | This girl is ready. |
| हा मुलगा तयार आहे. | Hā mulgā tayār āhe. | This boy is ready. |
| ते घर मोठे आहे. | Te ghar moṭhe āhe. | That house is big. |
| ही शाळा मोठी आहे. | Hī śāḷā moṭhī āhe. | This school is big. |
| हा रस्ता मोठा आहे. | Hā rastā moṭhā āhe. | This road is big. |

### Common trap

The form ते can be neuter singular that or a plural reference in another context. Do not label every occurrence they. Read the noun and the copula together before deciding its number.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Describe this house as big.
2. Say this girl is ready.
3. Say this boy is ready.
4. Describe that house as big.

***Answers:***

1. `हे घर मोठे आहे.` - `He ghar moṭhe āhe.`
2. `ही मुलगी तयार आहे.` - `Hī mulgī tayār āhe.`
3. `हा मुलगा तयार आहे.` - `Hā mulgā tayār āhe.`
4. `ते घर मोठे आहे.` - `Te ghar moṭhe āhe.`

### Why these answers work

घर triggers neuter हे or ते and मोठे. मुलगी selects ही; मुलगा selects हा. तयार stays fixed in both descriptions. The copula remains आहे because all four referents are singular. The first and fourth differ in distance, not in the house’s grammatical gender.

### Retrieve and recombine

Point mentally to a school, a road and a house. Choose the correct this before speaking the rest of each description, then make the house distant without changing its gender.


## Lesson 5: Who, what and where

**Outcome:** Ask for a missing person, identity or location.

### Think before reading

A statement says ती इथे आहे. If you do not know the location, which part should a question word replace?

### Build the pattern

Information questions contain a word that stands for missing information. कोण (koṇ) asks who, काय (kāy) asks what, and कुठे (kuṭhe) asks where. In ती कुठे आहे, the unknown location occupies the position where इथे, here, could have appeared. You need not pull every question word to the sentence beginning as English often does.

इथे and तिथे mean here and there. They are useful location expressions that do not require a separate word for at. मी इथे आहे is already complete. With nouns, Marathi uses endings and postpositions, which come later; avoid constructing a general location rule from these two adverbs alone.

तुम्ही कोण आहात can ask who someone is, but a direct identity demand may sound abrupt when introducing yourself socially. The grammar is useful for identifying a speaker on a call or clarifying a role. A respectful pronoun makes the form courteous but does not automatically make every question tactful. For casual introductions, offering your own name first is often easier.

हे काय आहे asks what this is. The neuter हे is a practical choice when the object’s identity has not yet been established. After naming it, use the noun’s actual gender for later description. Compare a location question about a known person with an identity question about an unknown object. Both contain an information gap, but their possible answers belong to different categories. A useful listener checks the type of information requested before beginning a response.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ती कुठे आहे? | Tī kuṭhe āhe? | Where is she? |
| हे काय आहे? | He kāy āhe? | What is this? |
| तुम्ही कोण आहात? | Tumhī koṇ āhāt? | Who are you, respectfully? |
| मी इथे आहे. | Mī ithe āhe. | I am here. |
| तो तिथे आहे. | To tithe āhe. | He is there. |
| शाळा कुठे आहे? | Śāḷā kuṭhe āhe? | Where is the school? |

### Common trap

Do not add sentence-final का automatically to every question. कोण, काय and कुठे already identify the missing information. A yes-or-no question and a where question ask the listener to do different things.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask where she is.
2. Ask what this is.
3. Ask who an adult stranger is, using respectful you.
4. Say that you are here.

***Answers:***

1. `ती कुठे आहे?` - `Tī kuṭhe āhe?`
2. `हे काय आहे?` - `He kāy āhe?`
3. `तुम्ही कोण आहात?` - `Tumhī koṇ āhāt?`
4. `मी इथे आहे.` - `Mī ithe āhe.`

### Why these answers work

The first answer replaces a place with कुठे. The second asks for an object’s identity using काय. The third keeps respectful आहात with तुम्ही. The fourth supplies an actual location, इथे, so it is a statement rather than a question with a missing slot.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine finding an unfamiliar object outside a school. Ask what the object is and where the school is. Keep the two question words distinct even though the situation is shared.


## Lesson 6: Everyday actions and the speaker contrast

**Outcome:** Build simple habitual sentences with a small set of verbs.

### Think before reading

A woman says I go. You have seen masculine जातो. Would simply replacing I with she teach the correct form for the woman speaking?

### Build the pattern

Dictionary forms such as जाणे, to go, and वाचणे, to read, name actions. A complete statement normally needs an appropriate finite form. For a male speaker, मी जातो means I go; for a female speaker, मी जाते. Third-person feminine uses ती जाते, while masculine तो जातो. These are Marathi patterns, not the Hindi pattern with a feminine ending in ī plus a present auxiliary.

The first-person feminine form is therefore especially worth hearing carefully. In this lesson, the forms in -तो and -ते express ordinary habits or general actions. No separate आहे is needed in the simple examples. Later, a different combination makes an action explicitly ongoing.

Keep a bare object before the action: मी पुस्तक वाचतो, I read a book. पुस्तक is neuter, but the habitual form here reflects the male speaker, not the book’s gender. This gives you a useful contrast with adjective agreement: a description of a book follows the book, whereas the ordinary active habitual predicate follows the acting subject.

The table introduces reading, going and doing work. काम करणे literally combines work with do and is a normal way to say work. Do not assume that every English single-word verb must correspond to one Marathi word. The object and the light verb can form a natural unit. रोज, every day, can be added to make the habitual interpretation explicit without changing the verb ending.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मी पुस्तक वाचतो. | Mī pustak vāchto. | I read a book, male speaker. |
| मी पुस्तक वाचते. | Mī pustak vāchte. | I read a book, female speaker. |
| तो रोज जातो. | To roj jāto. | He goes every day. |
| ती काम करते. | Tī kām karte. | She works. |
| मी काम करतो. | Mī kām karto. | I work, male speaker. |
| ती पुस्तक वाचते. | Tī pustak vāchte. | She reads a book. |

### Common trap

Do not use Hindi feminine जाती for Marathi I go or she goes. Marathi जाते is the target here. Also, the object’s neuter gender does not replace subject agreement in these habitual action sentences.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you read a book, as a male speaker.
2. Say you read a book, as a female speaker.
3. Say he goes every day.
4. Say she works.

***Answers:***

1. `मी पुस्तक वाचतो.` - `Mī pustak vāchto.`
2. `मी पुस्तक वाचते.` - `Mī pustak vāchte.`
3. `तो रोज जातो.` - `To roj jāto.`
4. `ती काम करते.` - `Tī kām karte.`

### Why these answers work

The first two keep पुस्तक constant while the speaker’s form changes. रोज locates the third action in a repeated routine. काम करते is a feminine-subject predicate, with काम serving as part of the activity expression. None of the four requires an extra आहे after the habitual form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Choose whether you are speaking as yourself or as an imagined person, then describe a daily reading habit. Switch the subject to a woman without treating the book as the agreement controller.


## Lesson 7: Drinking, eating and declining

**Outcome:** Describe food habits and negate an action.

### Think before reading

You can deny a state with नाही. In I do not drink tea, should the positive पितो remain unchanged immediately before नाही?

### Build the pattern

Habitual negation uses a non-agreeing action form followed by नाही in the basic pattern taught here. Compare मी चहा पितो, I drink tea as a male speaker, with मी चहा पीत नाही, I do not drink tea. The negative does not preserve the positive masculine -तो ending. A female speaker can use the same short मी चहा पीत नाही.

खाणे, to eat, gives खातो in the male first-person habitual and खात नाही in the negative. वाचणे gives वाचतो and वाचत नाही. The negative construction is therefore recognisable across several verbs, but spelling and stems still have to be learned with each verb. पिणे has पीत in this construction; mechanically replacing a final letter is not a complete conjugation method.

Place the food or drink before the verb. चहा means tea, पाणी means water, भात means cooked rice, and दूध means milk. These nouns have genders, but that does not change the lesson’s short first-person negative. English often inserts an indefinite quantity word such as some; a bare Marathi food noun can be enough when quantity is not the point.

A food preference is different from a refusal at this moment. मी चहा पीत नाही normally describes not drinking tea, often as a general habit. When someone offers tea, it may communicate a broader reason for declining. If you mean only that you do not want tea now, the wanting construction in a later lesson will let you be more precise.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मी चहा पीत नाही. | Mī chahā pīt nāhī. | I do not drink tea. |
| मी भात खात नाही. | Mī bhāt khāt nāhī. | I do not eat rice. |
| ती दूध पिते. | Tī dūdh pite. | She drinks milk. |
| तो पाणी पितो. | To pāṇī pito. | He drinks water. |
| मी भात खातो. | Mī bhāt khāto. | I eat rice, male speaker. |
| मी पुस्तक वाचत नाही. | Mī pustak vāchat nāhī. | I do not read a book. |

### Common trap

Avoid adding नाही to an unchanged पितो or खातो as though it were English not. Also, a bare food noun does not automatically mean one serving; use a quantity when the number of items matters.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you do not drink tea.
2. Say you do not eat rice.
3. Say she drinks milk.
4. Say he drinks water.

***Answers:***

1. `मी चहा पीत नाही.` - `Mī chahā pīt nāhī.`
2. `मी भात खात नाही.` - `Mī bhāt khāt nāhī.`
3. `ती दूध पिते.` - `Tī dūdh pite.`
4. `तो पाणी पितो.` - `To pāṇī pito.`

### Why these answers work

पीत नाही and खात नाही are complete negative predicates. The affirmative forms पिते and पितो return to subject-sensitive habitual endings. The food words occupy the object position, so replacing milk with water changes the activity’s object without changing who performs it.

### Retrieve and recombine

At an imaginary breakfast, describe one habit and one thing you do not consume. Then interpret whether your negative sounds like a general dietary habit or only a temporary refusal.


## Lesson 8: Location endings and places in town

**Outcome:** Locate someone at home, in a village or in a shop.

### Think before reading

If घर is house, what extra information does घरात contribute that the plain noun cannot supply?

### Build the pattern

Marathi frequently attaches location information to the noun. घरात means in the house; गावात means in the village. Learn these as noun-plus-location combinations and compare them with the bare nouns घर and गाव. The ending does a job that English often assigns to a separate preposition before the noun.

Not every noun adds the same visible string to an unchanged dictionary form. शाळा becomes शाळेत, at or in school, and दुकान becomes दुकानात, in the shop. The stem can adjust. Starting with a small set of actual forms is safer than inventing one universal spelling rule. घर also appears as घरी for home as a place, especially in ordinary expressions such as मी घरी आहे. घरी and घरात overlap but need not emphasise exactly the same spatial relationship.

With these location expressions, the familiar copula is enough: दुकान गावात आहे, the shop is in the village. You can place a person or an object in the same slot. To ask for the location, replace the location expression with कुठे; do not append a second locative ending to कुठे itself.

The distinction between place and motion matters. This lesson says where someone is, not where they are travelling. Later movement sentences can use some of the same forms, but you should not assume that all English at, in and to expressions have one Marathi equivalent. Identify the relationship before choosing the ending. This prevents a vocabulary list from hiding the grammar that makes directions understandable.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मी घरी आहे. | Mī gharī āhe. | I am at home. |
| ती शाळेत आहे. | Tī śāḷet āhe. | She is at school. |
| तो दुकानात आहे. | To dukānāt āhe. | He is in the shop. |
| दुकान गावात आहे. | Dukān gāvāt āhe. | The shop is in the village. |
| पुस्तक घरात आहे. | Pustak gharāt āhe. | The book is in the house. |
| गाव कुठे आहे? | Gāv kuṭhe āhe? | Where is the village? |

### Common trap

Do not translate in with a free-standing English-style word placed before every noun. Marathi’s attached forms include stem changes, so शाळेत needs to be learned alongside शाळा, not guessed from घरात.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you are at home, using घरी.
2. Say she is at school.
3. Say he is in the shop.
4. Say the shop is in the village.

***Answers:***

1. `मी घरी आहे.` - `Mī gharī āhe.`
2. `ती शाळेत आहे.` - `Tī śāḷet āhe.`
3. `तो दुकानात आहे.` - `To dukānāt āhe.`
4. `दुकान गावात आहे.` - `Dukān gāvāt āhe.`

### Why these answers work

घरी is the home expression; शाळेत and दुकानात show two different noun shapes in location use. गावात places the shop within the village. The final आहे stays unchanged because the subjects remain singular. These answers report location rather than movement toward a destination.

### Retrieve and recombine

Move the book from the house to the shop in your imagined scene. Then ask where the person at school is. Retrieve the location form before building the rest of each sentence.


## Lesson 9: My, your and the thing possessed

**Outcome:** Describe whose house, notebook or friend you mean.

### Think before reading

A woman owns a house and a notebook. Does my change because the owner changes, or because the possessed noun changes?

### Build the pattern

माझा, माझी and माझे mean my with different singular noun genders. They agree with what is possessed or related, not with the owner’s gender. Thus any speaker can say माझे घर, my house, because घर is neuter. वही, notebook, is feminine and takes माझी. मित्र, a male friend, is masculine and takes माझा.

The same principle applies to तुझा, तुझी and तुझे for familiar your, and तुमचा, तुमची and तुमचे for respectful or plural your. Do not use the familiar possessive merely because the possessed object is small or familiar. Respect concerns the addressee, while agreement concerns the following noun. These are two separate choices made inside the same short phrase.

Marathi places the possessive before the noun: माझी वही. You can turn this into an identity statement with a demonstrative: ही माझी वही आहे, this is my notebook. Notice that both ही and माझी point to the notebook’s feminine gender. If you move to a neuter house, both become neuter. The speaker has not changed.

English also uses possession words for relationships, such as my friend, even though people are not owned. Marathi possessive grammar likewise expresses a broad relationship. Learn the grammatical term possession as a convenient label, not a claim about literal ownership. The next lesson will distinguish identifying something as mine from reporting that I have something available with me.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| हे माझे घर आहे. | He mājhe ghar āhe. | This is my house. |
| ही माझी वही आहे. | Hī mājhī vahī āhe. | This is my notebook. |
| हा माझा मित्र आहे. | Hā mājhā mitra āhe. | This is my male friend. |
| तुमचे घर मोठे आहे. | Tumche ghar moṭhe āhe. | Your house is big, respectfully. |
| तुझी वही इथे आहे. | Tujhī vahī ithe āhe. | Your notebook is here, familiarly. |
| माझी मैत्रीण घरी आहे. | Mājhī maitrīṇ gharī āhe. | My female friend is at home. |

### Common trap

A male speaker still says माझी वही. Matching my to the owner’s gender reverses the rule. Also, moving from तू to तुम्ही requires changing the possessive family from तुझ- to तुमच-, not only changing the sentence’s copula.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Identify this as your own house.
2. Identify this as your own notebook.
3. Identify this man as your male friend.
4. Tell a stranger their house is big.

***Answers:***

1. `हे माझे घर आहे.` - `He mājhe ghar āhe.`
2. `ही माझी वही आहे.` - `Hī mājhī vahī āhe.`
3. `हा माझा मित्र आहे.` - `Hā mājhā mitra āhe.`
4. `तुमचे घर मोठे आहे.` - `Tumche ghar moṭhe āhe.`

### Why these answers work

The first three answers pair demonstratives and possessives with the noun’s gender. In the fourth, तुमचे gives respectful your and मोठे describes the neuter house. The house is the sentence subject, so आहे remains singular even when your addresses several people.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine one notebook shared by two people. Say your notebook respectfully, then say my notebook. Explain to yourself why the feminine ending remains even though the relationship word changes.


## Lesson 10: Having something with you

**Outcome:** Report possession or availability using a person plus कडे.

### Think before reading

English says I have a book. Can Marathi instead locate the book with me and still express the useful idea?

### Build the pattern

माझ्याकडे पुस्तक आहे literally places a book with me and commonly means I have a book. The possessor is expressed through माझ्याकडे, while the available item and आहे complete the construction. This differs from माझे पुस्तक, which identifies a book as mine. You can have a borrowed book with you without owning it.

Useful person forms include माझ्याकडे, with me; तुझ्याकडे, with you familiarly; तुमच्याकडे, with you respectfully; त्याच्याकडे, with him; and तिच्याकडे, with her. These are not built by simply writing मी कडे. Pronouns take special related forms before this element. Learn the meaningful combinations as a small family.

A question follows the pattern already known: तुमच्याकडे पाणी आहे का, do you have water? This can ask about availability at a shop, in a bag or in a person’s supply. Context decides whether the intended relationship is literal possession, access or having an item present. A negative substitutes नाही: माझ्याकडे पैसे नाहीत is common with plural money, but the present exercises keep singular or mass-noun examples to avoid introducing plural negative agreement at the same time.

वेळ, time, can also occur in this construction. माझ्याकडे वेळ नाही means I do not have time. This is an extended meaning, not a physical location for time. It illustrates how a concrete with-me pattern can support practical abstract statements. Keep the object and its number in view, because the construction is centred on what exists or is available rather than on an English-style conjugation of have.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| माझ्याकडे पुस्तक आहे. | Mājhyākaḍe pustak āhe. | I have a book. |
| तुमच्याकडे पाणी आहे का? | Tumchyākaḍe pāṇī āhe kā? | Do you have water, respectfully? |
| तिच्याकडे वही आहे. | Tichyākaḍe vahī āhe. | She has a notebook. |
| माझ्याकडे वेळ नाही. | Mājhyākaḍe veḷ nāhī. | I do not have time. |
| त्याच्याकडे पुस्तक आहे. | Tyāchyākaḍe pustak āhe. | He has a book. |
| तुझ्याकडे दूध आहे का? | Tujhyākaḍe dūdh āhe kā? | Do you have milk, familiarly? |

### Common trap

Do not treat माझ्याकडे as a possessive adjective interchangeable with माझे. One locates availability with a person; the other modifies a noun. The difference matters when the item belongs to someone else.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you have a book.
2. Ask a stranger if they have water.
3. Say she has a notebook.
4. Say you do not have time.

***Answers:***

1. `माझ्याकडे पुस्तक आहे.` - `Mājhyākaḍe pustak āhe.`
2. `तुमच्याकडे पाणी आहे का?` - `Tumchyākaḍe pāṇī āhe kā?`
3. `तिच्याकडे वही आहे.` - `Tichyākaḍe vahī āhe.`
4. `माझ्याकडे वेळ नाही.` - `Mājhyākaḍe veḷ nāhī.`

### Why these answers work

The possessor appears in a कडे form in all four answers. The second adds sentence-final का, while the fourth uses नाही because the available thing is denied. The third uses तिच्याकडे to identify the female possessor; वही does not determine that pronoun’s gender.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine borrowing a friend’s book. State that it is the friend’s book, then state that you have a book. Keep ownership and present availability distinct while reusing the same object.


## Lesson 11: Wanting and needing with मला

**Outcome:** Express a simple want and decline an offered item.

### Think before reading

You know मी as I. Why might a sentence about something being wanted use मला instead?

### Build the pattern

Marathi often presents a want through the person experiencing it: मला चहा हवा आहे, I want tea. मला is a to-me form, not the ordinary subject pronoun मी. The desired item controls हवा, हवी or हवे according to its gender. चहा is masculine, मदत, help, is feminine, and पाणी is neuter.

This pattern gives you practical requests, but a statement of wanting is not automatically a polite command. In a shop or at a table, tone and the surrounding exchange matter. मला पाणी हवे आहे clearly expresses your need; a respectful request using give will be introduced separately. You can first state the need and then ask politely for the action.

To decline or say you do not want something, use नको: मला चहा नको. The short negative does not copy the positive हवा आहे. The table keeps a singular or mass item so that this refusal pattern remains manageable. Do not read नको as a universal replacement for नाही. नाही denies a fact or state in earlier lessons; नको expresses an unwanted item or, in other constructions, a prohibition.

Other experiencers include तुला, to you familiarly, तुम्हाला, to you respectfully, त्याला, to him, and तिला, to her. The same person forms will later help with liking and bodily experiences. The underlying connection is that something is relevant to or experienced by that person. English I is therefore not always translated by Marathi मी. Identify the relationship expressed by the whole predicate first.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मला चहा हवा आहे. | Malā chahā havā āhe. | I want tea. |
| मला पाणी हवे आहे. | Malā pāṇī have āhe. | I want water. |
| मला मदत हवी आहे. | Malā madat havī āhe. | I need help. |
| मला चहा नको. | Malā chahā nako. | I do not want tea. |
| तुम्हाला पाणी हवे आहे का? | Tumhālā pāṇī have āhe kā? | Do you want water, respectfully? |
| तिला मदत हवी आहे. | Tilā madat havī āhe. | She needs help. |

### Common trap

Using हवा for every desired thing loses agreement: help takes हवी and water takes हवे. Also, the experiencer मला is not chosen because the speaker is receiving a physical object in every case; help can be abstract.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you want tea.
2. Say you want water.
3. Say you need help.
4. Decline tea by saying you do not want it.

***Answers:***

1. `मला चहा हवा आहे.` - `Malā chahā havā āhe.`
2. `मला पाणी हवे आहे.` - `Malā pāṇī have āhe.`
3. `मला मदत हवी आहे.` - `Malā madat havī āhe.`
4. `मला चहा नको.` - `Malā chahā nako.`

### Why these answers work

The first three select हवा, हवे and हवी from the gender of the desired item. All use मला regardless of speaker gender. The fourth switches to the short unwanted-item construction with नको, so it does not retain the positive adjective or copula.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine needing help to find water. Build two statements with मला, then offer water to an adult stranger using तुम्हाला. Decide whether the situation requires a want statement or a direct request.


## Lesson 12: Polite requests that change the action

**Outcome:** Ask someone to sit, come, give or speak slowly.

### Think before reading

You address one adult respectfully. Does the command have to become familiar singular because there is only one listener?

### Build the pattern

Marathi commands distinguish familiar singular forms from respectful or plural ones. The present course favours the latter for public interactions: बसा, sit; या, come; जा, go; द्या, give; and बोला, speak. These are useful finite requests in their own right. Do not attach आहे to them.

The respectful forms of come and give show why a single mechanical suffix rule is insufficient. You may recognise the familiar ये and दे, but the respectful forms are या and द्या. Treat the table as a small productive set and add the relevant object or location before the command. इथे बसा asks someone to sit here, while मला पाणी द्या asks them to give water to me.

कृपया, please, can make a request more explicitly courteous, especially in writing or formal service encounters. Everyday politeness also comes from respectful verb choice, intonation and context. Repeating कृपया before every tiny utterance can sound more formal than necessary. Use it where the social situation calls for a clear polite request.

For a negative respectful command, place नका after the action form: जाऊ नका means do not go. That construction differs from the factual negative जात नाही, does not go. One tries to influence an action; the other reports a habit or occurrence. Slowly is हळू, and again is पुन्हा. These two adverbs let you manage a conversation even before you understand all its content. Asking for a slower repetition is a genuine language skill, not a failure to participate.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| कृपया इथे बसा. | Kṛpayā ithe basā. | Please sit here. |
| मला पाणी द्या. | Malā pāṇī dyā. | Give me water, respectfully. |
| कृपया हळू बोला. | Kṛpayā haḷū bolā. | Please speak slowly. |
| जाऊ नका. | Jāū nakā. | Do not go, respectfully. |
| पुन्हा बोला. | Punhā bolā. | Say it again, respectfully. |
| इथे या. | Ithe yā. | Come here, respectfully. |

### Common trap

नका is a negative-command component, not a substitute for नाही in an ordinary statement. Respectful singular listeners still take the respectful command; counting listeners is not enough to choose the social form.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Politely ask someone to sit here, with कृपया.
2. Ask an adult to give you water.
3. Ask someone to speak slowly, with कृपया.
4. Tell an adult not to go, respectfully.

***Answers:***

1. `कृपया इथे बसा.` - `Kṛpayā ithe basā.`
2. `मला पाणी द्या.` - `Malā pāṇī dyā.`
3. `कृपया हळू बोला.` - `Kṛpayā haḷū bolā.`
4. `जाऊ नका.` - `Jāū nakā.`

### Why these answers work

बसा, द्या and बोला are requests rather than descriptions. मला identifies the recipient of the water. जाऊ नका uses the separate negative-command construction. None needs an explicit तुम्ही because the addressed person is clear from the situation and the imperative form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine a noisy ticket counter. Ask the clerk to speak slowly and then repeat. Transfer the give request from water to a notebook while keeping the recipient and respectful verb unchanged.


## Lesson 13: Actions happening now

**Outcome:** Distinguish a current activity from a general habit.

### Think before reading

If मी पुस्तक वाचतो describes a male speaker’s habit, how could वाचत आहे shift attention to what is underway?

### Build the pattern

The combination of an action form in -त with the present copula presents an ongoing activity: मी पुस्तक वाचत आहे, I am reading a book. The same main action form can be used by a male or female speaker in this full construction. It is the copula that changes for person and number: आम्ही वाचत आहोत, we are reading.

Compare the habitual ती चहा पिते with ती चहा पीत आहे. The first can describe a regular behaviour; the second places the drinking in progress. Marathi tense and aspect do not align perfectly with every English label, and context can make some simple forms current. The explicit construction is still a clear beginner way to emphasise that an activity is underway.

आता means now and makes the time frame easy to establish. You can place it near the beginning: तो आता काम करत आहे. It does not itself conjugate the verb, and adding now to any random form will not repair the grammar. Learn the complete predicate करत आहे as the ongoing counterpart of करतो in this context.

In everyday speech you may hear contracted endings, including forms corresponding to करतोय. The written course uses the uncontracted construction so that its parts stay visible. Recognise contractions as normal speech rather than errors, but do not derive an unrestricted contraction rule from one word. A useful listening strategy is to identify the action stem first, then check whether the speaker reports a habit, a current event or another time frame through the rest of the phrase.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मी पुस्तक वाचत आहे. | Mī pustak vāchat āhe. | I am reading a book. |
| ती चहा पीत आहे. | Tī chahā pīt āhe. | She is drinking tea. |
| तो आता काम करत आहे. | To ātā kām karat āhe. | He is working now. |
| आम्ही पुस्तक वाचत आहोत. | Āmhī pustak vāchat āhot. | We are reading a book, excluding the listener. |
| तुम्ही काय करत आहात? | Tumhī kāy karat āhāt? | What are you doing, respectfully? |
| मी आता जात आहे. | Mī ātā jāt āhe. | I am going now. |

### Common trap

Do not combine a full habitual ending with the full ongoing pattern indiscriminately. The teaching form is वाचत आहे, not a word-for-word imitation of an English auxiliary plus an unchanged Marathi habitual verb.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you are reading a book.
2. Say she is drinking tea now, using the ongoing construction.
3. Say he is working now, including आता.
4. Say your group is reading a book, excluding the listener.

***Answers:***

1. `मी पुस्तक वाचत आहे.` - `Mī pustak vāchat āhe.`
2. `ती चहा पीत आहे.` - `Tī chahā pīt āhe.`
3. `तो आता काम करत आहे.` - `To ātā kām karat āhe.`
4. `आम्ही पुस्तक वाचत आहोत.` - `Āmhī pustak vāchat āhot.`

### Why these answers work

All answers use an action form followed by a present copula. The fourth changes आहे to आहोत with आम्ही. The third explicitly includes आता; the second already conveys an ongoing activity without requiring that adverb. The actor’s gender does not change वाचत in this construction.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe one daily habit and its current counterpart. Then imagine two people doing the current activity together and change the copula while keeping the action form. Explain the difference in time perspective.


## Lesson 14: Numbers, quantities and plural things

**Outcome:** Ask for small quantities and distinguish one item from several.

### Think before reading

If one book is पुस्तक, does two books require the same noun shape and the same copula?

### Build the pattern

Useful first numbers are एक, one; दोन, two; तीन, three; चार, four; पाच, five; and सहा, six. Put a cardinal number before the counted noun. Two books is दोन पुस्तके; two notebooks is दोन वह्या. Marathi plural formation depends on the noun class, so do not add one universal plural ending to every item.

The copula also signals plurality: दोन पुस्तके आहेत, there are two books. Compare one book with आहे. Neuter पुस्तक gives पुस्तके, feminine वही gives वह्या, and masculine मुलगा gives मुलगे. These are separate examples of real noun patterns, not a guarantee that every similarly spelled word behaves identically. Learn a noun’s ordinary singular and plural together when quantity becomes useful.

For a practical request, combine quantity, noun and द्या: दोन पुस्तके द्या, give two books. The respectful command is unchanged by the number of books because it addresses the listener. This contrasts with the existential statement, where the plural available items call for आहेत. The same plural noun therefore participates in two constructions with different agreement demands.

किती asks how much or how many. किती पुस्तके आहेत asks for the number of books, not a yes-or-no confirmation. In a price question, किती can instead ask an amount of money. English’s distinction between much and many does not require two separate Marathi question words here. Decide whether you are counting distinct objects or asking an amount, and let the noun and context make the question specific.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| दोन पुस्तके द्या. | Don pustake dyā. | Give two books, respectfully. |
| तीन वह्या द्या. | Tīn vahyā dyā. | Give three notebooks, respectfully. |
| दोन पुस्तके आहेत. | Don pustake āhet. | There are two books. |
| किती पुस्तके आहेत? | Kitī pustake āhet? | How many books are there? |
| चार मुलगे आहेत. | Chār mulge āhet. | There are four boys. |
| पाच रुपये. | Pāch rupaye. | Five rupees. |

### Common trap

द्या does not change to match two books or three notebooks. It is the listener-facing command. Conversely, a plural existence statement needs आहेत, so the request’s fixed verb cannot teach the copula agreement rule.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Request two books respectfully.
2. Request three notebooks respectfully.
3. Say there are two books.
4. Ask how many books there are.

***Answers:***

1. `दोन पुस्तके द्या.` - `Don pustake dyā.`
2. `तीन वह्या द्या.` - `Tīn vahyā dyā.`
3. `दोन पुस्तके आहेत.` - `Don pustake āhet.`
4. `किती पुस्तके आहेत?` - `Kitī pustake āhet?`

### Why these answers work

The first two use plural nouns after their quantities but retain द्या. The third uses आहेत with plural पुस्तके. The fourth replaces the known number with किती while preserving the plural noun and copula. Counting and asking for a count share the same noun phrase structure.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine preparing a small class. Ask for two books and three notebooks, then check how many books are present. Retrieve both plural nouns without assuming that their endings will match.


## Lesson 15: Time words and a workable appointment

**Outcome:** State when an activity happens and recognise the clock-time construction.

### Think before reading

Today and tomorrow are useful on their own. What extra shape might a number need when it tells the hour of an appointment?

### Build the pattern

आज means today, उद्या means tomorrow, and काल means yesterday in the ordinary uses here. Unlike Hindi कल, Marathi separates tomorrow and yesterday with these different words. That difference is especially helpful if you already know Hindi: do not let a familiar-looking time word overwrite Marathi’s distinction.

Clock time in an activity phrase uses a number with वाजता: तीन वाजता, at three o’clock. A statement of the current time instead uses वाजले आहेत: तीन वाजले आहेत, it is three o’clock. The two expressions perform different jobs. One places another event at an hour; the other tells the time itself. The exercises focus on the simpler appointment phrase.

सकाळी means in the morning and संध्याकाळी means in the evening. These forms can clarify whether a clock time is early or late in the day. They do not require a separate English-style in before them. रोज सकाळी makes a regular morning routine, while आज सकाळी names this morning.

For an immediately useful appointment, combine उद्या, an hour phrase and the respectful command या. उद्या तीन वाजता या asks someone to come tomorrow at three. It is a request about a future event, but you do not need to master a future indicative conjugation to make it. The imperative itself is already a valid action request. When interpreting a message, separate the action from its schedule: who is being asked, what they should do, and when they should do it. This makes even a short sentence carry reliable practical information.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| उद्या तीन वाजता या. | Udyā tīn vājatā yā. | Come tomorrow at three, respectfully. |
| आज इथे बसा. | Āj ithe basā. | Sit here today, respectfully. |
| मी रोज सकाळी वाचतो. | Mī roj sakāḷī vāchto. | I read every morning, male speaker. |
| ती संध्याकाळी काम करते. | Tī sandhyākāḷī kām karte. | She works in the evening. |
| काल. | Kāl. | Yesterday. |
| आता तीन वाजले आहेत. | Ātā tīn vājle āhet. | It is three o’clock now. |

### Common trap

Do not use काल for tomorrow because Hindi uses a similar form for both adjacent days. Also, वाजता attaches an hour to an activity; it is not the complete way to announce what time it is now.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask someone to come tomorrow at three.
2. Ask someone to sit here today.
3. Say you read every morning, male speaker.
4. Say she works in the evening.

***Answers:***

1. `उद्या तीन वाजता या.` - `Udyā tīn vājatā yā.`
2. `आज इथे बसा.` - `Āj ithe basā.`
3. `मी रोज सकाळी वाचतो.` - `Mī roj sakāḷī vāchto.`
4. `ती संध्याकाळी काम करते.` - `Tī sandhyākāḷī kām karte.`

### Why these answers work

उद्या and आज supply different days without changing the respectful command. रोज सकाळी creates a repeated time frame compatible with वाचतो. संध्याकाळी places her work in the evening. The answers distinguish an appointment request from a habitual description rather than treating all time words as tense endings.

### Retrieve and recombine

Move the appointment from three to four, then from tomorrow to today. Keep the action constant. Finally, retrieve the separate Marathi words for yesterday and tomorrow before checking the table.


## Lesson 16: Going, coming and a clear destination

**Outcome:** Describe everyday travel without confusing the speaker’s viewpoint.

### Think before reading

Someone moves toward the place where you are waiting. Would come or go express your viewpoint more naturally?

### Build the pattern

जाणे means to go and येणे means to come. Their contrast depends on a viewpoint: coming typically moves toward the speaker, listener or an established centre, while going moves away or toward another destination. English offers a useful initial comparison, but actual conversational viewpoints can shift, especially when arranging a visit.

Marathi destinations are not all expressed with one equivalent of English to. मी घरी जातो uses घरी for going home. ती शाळेत जाते uses the familiar school form, and तो पुण्याला जातो uses पुण्याला for going to Pune. Learn the place expression with a real travel sentence. पुणे changes shape before the ending in पुण्याला, so simply appending ला to the printed city name would miss the form taught here.

Transport can be expressed with an instrument-like ending: बसने, by bus. A sentence such as मी बसने जातो adds the means before the verb. Do not interpret the same ending as always identifying a past-tense agent; Marathi endings can participate in more than one construction, and the sentence’s predicate establishes their role.

For a respectful habitual question, तुम्ही कुठे जाता asks where you go. तुम्ही takes जाता here, not the first-person जातो. This lesson adds one highly useful respectful action form without forcing an entire table. The response can name a destination or give a fuller habitual statement. If you are asking about an activity happening now, the ongoing construction from Lesson 13 is available instead. Time perspective and destination are independent pieces of the message.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मी घरी जातो. | Mī gharī jāto. | I go home, male speaker. |
| ती शाळेत जाते. | Tī śāḷet jāte. | She goes to school. |
| तो पुण्याला जातो. | To puṇyālā jāto. | He goes to Pune. |
| तुम्ही कुठे जाता? | Tumhī kuṭhe jātā? | Where do you go, respectfully? |
| मी बसने जातो. | Mī basne jāto. | I go by bus, male speaker. |
| ती इथे येते. | Tī ithe yete. | She comes here. |

### Common trap

Do not replace every destination expression with a uniform ला suffix. घरी, शाळेत and पुण्याला show different established shapes. Also, come and go communicate a viewpoint, not simply the presence or absence of a named destination.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you go home, male speaker.
2. Say she goes to school.
3. Say he goes to Pune.
4. Ask an adult where they go habitually.

***Answers:***

1. `मी घरी जातो.` - `Mī gharī jāto.`
2. `ती शाळेत जाते.` - `Tī śāḷet jāte.`
3. `तो पुण्याला जातो.` - `To puṇyālā jāto.`
4. `तुम्ही कुठे जाता?` - `Tumhī kuṭhe jātā?`

### Why these answers work

The first three combine a destination with a subject-appropriate habitual verb. पुण्याला contains the city name in its destination form. The fourth uses respectful जाता and asks for the missing destination with कुठे. No extra English-style to is needed before the place expressions.

### Retrieve and recombine

Explain a daily route from home to school by bus. Then imagine waiting at school and describe a friend’s movement toward you. Decide whether the situation calls for येते or जाते.


## Lesson 17: Past states and arrivals

**Outcome:** Say where someone was and report a completed arrival or departure.

### Think before reading

A woman was at home and then went away. Should the words for was and went necessarily have the same stem?

### Build the pattern

Past location and completed motion are different constructions. होता, होती and होते are useful singular past forms of be for masculine, feminine and neuter subjects. तो घरी होता says he was at home; ती घरी होती says she was at home. The place expression stays the same while the past copula shows gender.

Completed motion uses a past form of the movement verb. जाणे has the irregular past stem गेल-: तो गेला, he went, and ती गेली, she went. येणे gives आला and आली for he came and she came. These are high-frequency forms worth learning directly. They cannot be obtained by attaching the same ending to the present word जातो or येते.

काल clearly places the event yesterday. It is useful but not compulsory whenever a past form appears: the predicate can already signal a completed event or past state. A narrative often establishes the day once and then continues without repeating it in every sentence. Start with a clear time phrase, then track the events through the verbs.

The practice deliberately uses third-person singular people. First-person past agreement has its own details, and transitive completed actions introduce another important agreement pattern in the next lesson. Do not extrapolate the entire Marathi past system from he went. Nevertheless, a small, accurate past contrast already lets you report whether someone was available, whether they arrived, and where they went. These are useful pieces of a real travel or meeting update.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| तो काल घरी होता. | To kāl gharī hotā. | He was at home yesterday. |
| ती काल घरी होती. | Tī kāl gharī hotī. | She was at home yesterday. |
| तो पुण्याला गेला. | To puṇyālā gelā. | He went to Pune. |
| ती शाळेत गेली. | Tī śāḷet gelī. | She went to school. |
| तो इथे आला. | To ithe ālā. | He came here. |
| ती इथे आली. | Tī ithe ālī. | She came here. |

### Common trap

Do not form went by adding a generic past ending to जातो. गेल- is an irregular stem. Nor should an English past auxiliary be inserted before every Marathi past verb; these completed forms can stand as full predicates.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say he was at home yesterday.
2. Say she was at home yesterday.
3. Say he went to Pune.
4. Say she went to school.

***Answers:***

1. `तो काल घरी होता.` - `To kāl gharī hotā.`
2. `ती काल घरी होती.` - `Tī kāl gharī hotī.`
3. `तो पुण्याला गेला.` - `To puṇyālā gelā.`
4. `ती शाळेत गेली.` - `Tī śāḷet gelī.`

### Why these answers work

The first pair uses past copulas because the predicate locates someone. The second pair uses गेल- because it reports motion as completed. Masculine and feminine forms differ in both pairs, but the location expressions remain unchanged. काल supplies yesterday only where requested.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create a two-event update about a woman: she was at home, then she came here. Before speaking, separate the past state from the completed movement and retrieve the appropriate stem for each.


## Lesson 18: Completed actions and object agreement

**Outcome:** Report reading or drinking as completed while tracking the object.

### Think before reading

He read a book and she read a book. If the book is neuter, could the completed verb stay the same even though the actors differ?

### Build the pattern

Many Marathi completed transitive constructions behave differently from the habitual sentences already learned. A third-person agent can take -ने, and the completed verb agrees with an unmarked object. Thus त्याने पुस्तक वाचले and तिने पुस्तक वाचले differ in the agent but share वाचले because पुस्तक is neuter singular.

With a masculine object such as चहा, the drinking form is प्याला; with neuter पाणी, प्याले. The relevant question is now what was consumed, not whether the drinker was a man or woman. This shift is one reason a course must not offer a single universal instruction to match every verb to the subject. Aspect and construction matter.

त्याने means he in this agent-marked construction, and तिने means she. They are not ordinary replacements for तो and ती in every sentence. Use the ordinary pronouns again for habitual drinking or intransitive coming. Likewise, the presence of -ने alone does not prove object agreement if the sentence belongs to another construction; earlier बसने meant by bus.

The pattern here is intentionally limited to third-person agents and unmarked singular objects. Object marking and first- or second-person agents introduce further details. Keeping the scope explicit helps you build reliable sentences now without mistaking a useful foundation for the entire system. Read the two book examples side by side: the event is complete, the reader changes, and the thing read does not. Then compare tea with water to see the object’s gender become visible in the completed verb.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| त्याने पुस्तक वाचले. | Tyāne pustak vāchle. | He read a book. |
| तिने पुस्तक वाचले. | Tine pustak vāchle. | She read a book. |
| त्याने चहा प्याला. | Tyāne chahā pyālā. | He drank tea. |
| तिने पाणी प्याले. | Tine pāṇī pyāle. | She drank water. |
| तिने चहा प्याला. | Tine chahā pyālā. | She drank tea. |
| त्याने पाणी प्याले. | Tyāne pāṇī pyāle. | He drank water. |

### Common trap

Choosing प्याली merely because the drinker is female misses the construction’s object agreement. The table’s tenses are completed transitive events; do not transfer their agent marking into a simple she goes sentence.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say he read a book.
2. Say she read a book.
3. Say he drank tea.
4. Say she drank water.

***Answers:***

1. `त्याने पुस्तक वाचले.` - `Tyāne pustak vāchle.`
2. `तिने पुस्तक वाचले.` - `Tine pustak vāchle.`
3. `त्याने चहा प्याला.` - `Tyāne chahā pyālā.`
4. `तिने पाणी प्याले.` - `Tine pāṇī pyāle.`

### Why these answers work

Both reading answers retain neuter वाचले with पुस्तक. The third uses masculine प्याला with चहा, and the fourth neuter प्याले with पाणी. त्याने and तिने identify different agents, but they do not determine the completed verb’s gender in these unmarked-object examples.

### Retrieve and recombine

Swap the agents in the tea and water examples without changing the objects. Predict which words remain fixed. Then retell one event as a regular habit and notice that its agreement pattern changes.


## Lesson 19: Future plans with जाणार

**Outcome:** State a planned action and change its person without changing the planning form.

### Think before reading

If जाणार expresses a prospective action, where might Marathi put the person information in we are going to go?

### Build the pattern

A useful way to discuss an intended or expected future action is the prospective form in -णार with a present copula. मी उद्या जाणार आहे means I am going to go tomorrow. The form जाणार is unchanged in ती जाणार आहे and आम्ही जाणार आहोत; the copula supplies the person and number contrast.

This construction is especially useful for plans, but do not equate it with every English future meaning. Marathi also has simple future forms, and context can express expectations in several ways. Learning one productive prospective pattern first gives you practical coverage without pretending that all future statements are interchangeable.

वाचणार is going to read, करणार is going to do, and येणार is going to come. With an object, keep it before the prospective form: ती पुस्तक वाचणार आहे. With a destination or time expression, place that material before the final predicate as well. The sentence can grow while its basic action-plus-copula organisation stays visible.

To deny a plan in the short pattern, use जाणार नाही. मी उद्या जाणार नाही states that I am not going to go tomorrow. It does not say I cannot go; inability requires a different construction. This distinction is useful when changing an appointment: a cancelled intention and an obstacle to ability are not necessarily the same explanation. In conversation you can ask a confirming question by adding का after the affirmative prospective sentence. Keep the actual plan clear before discussing its reason.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मी उद्या जाणार आहे. | Mī udyā jāṇār āhe. | I am going to go tomorrow. |
| ती पुस्तक वाचणार आहे. | Tī pustak vāchaṇār āhe. | She is going to read a book. |
| आम्ही काम करणार आहोत. | Āmhī kām karaṇār āhot. | We are going to work, excluding the listener. |
| मी उद्या जाणार नाही. | Mī udyā jāṇār nāhī. | I am not going to go tomorrow. |
| तुम्ही येणार आहात का? | Tumhī yeṇār āhāt kā? | Are you going to come, respectfully? |
| तो इथे बसणार आहे. | To ithe basaṇār āhe. | He is going to sit here. |

### Common trap

The prospective form does not become feminine merely because the subject is ती. Also, a negative plan is not automatically an inability. Avoid translating every English cannot with जाणार नाही.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you are going to go tomorrow.
2. Say she is going to read a book.
3. Say your group is going to work, excluding the listener.
4. Say you are not going to go tomorrow.

***Answers:***

1. `मी उद्या जाणार आहे.` - `Mī udyā jāṇār āhe.`
2. `ती पुस्तक वाचणार आहे.` - `Tī pustak vāchaṇār āhe.`
3. `आम्ही काम करणार आहोत.` - `Āmhī kām karaṇār āhot.`
4. `मी उद्या जाणार नाही.` - `Mī udyā jāṇār nāhī.`

### Why these answers work

जाणार, वाचणार and करणार name prospective actions. The third answer changes the copula to आहोत with आम्ही. The fourth uses the negative prospective pattern without a positive आहे. The plan form itself is stable across male and female singular subjects in these examples.

### Retrieve and recombine

Make a plan to come tomorrow, ask someone respectfully whether they will come, then cancel only your own plan. Keep the other person’s question distinct from your negative statement.


## Lesson 20: Ability and learning to do something

**Outcome:** Say that someone can perform an action and distinguish skill from intention.

### Think before reading

A plan to read tomorrow does not prove someone can read. What separate verb could carry the idea of ability?

### Build the pattern

शकणे supplies a useful ability or possibility construction. The main action appears in a form such as वाचू, speak as बोलू, or go as जाऊ, followed by an appropriate form of शकणे. मी वाचू शकतो means I can read for a male speaker; मी वाचू शकते gives the female-speaker form.

The agreement is visible on शकतो or शकते, not on the preceding main action. This is a useful contrast with the prospective -णार construction, whose form stayed fixed before a copula. Identify which component is finite before deciding where person or gender information belongs. Respectful तुम्ही takes शकता in the simple pattern.

An object or language name appears before the action: ती मराठी बोलू शकते, she can speak Marathi. This statement reports an ability. It does not state that she is speaking Marathi at the moment or that she intends to speak it tomorrow. The same action vocabulary can therefore participate in habitual, ongoing, planned and ability constructions, each with a different message.

The negative pattern uses शकत नाही: मी जाऊ शकत नाही, I cannot go. That can describe inability in the circumstances, not only a permanent lack of skill. When arranging a visit, it is often more precise than a negative plan if there is a real obstacle. For learned skills, Marathi also has constructions involving येणे, but this lesson does not collapse them into a single rule. Start with the productive शकणे pattern and keep ability separate from willingness.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मी वाचू शकतो. | Mī vāchū śakto. | I can read, male speaker. |
| मी वाचू शकते. | Mī vāchū śakte. | I can read, female speaker. |
| ती मराठी बोलू शकते. | Tī marāṭhī bolū śakte. | She can speak Marathi. |
| मी जाऊ शकत नाही. | Mī jāū śakat nāhī. | I cannot go. |
| तुम्ही येऊ शकता का? | Tumhī yeū śaktā kā? | Can you come, respectfully? |
| तो काम करू शकतो. | To kām karū śakto. | He can work. |

### Common trap

Do not put habitual वाचतो before शकतो. The first action takes वाचू in this construction. Also, can is not the same as will: a person may have an ability without making any plan to use it.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you can read, male speaker.
2. Say you can read, female speaker.
3. Say she can speak Marathi.
4. Say you cannot go.

***Answers:***

1. `मी वाचू शकतो.` - `Mī vāchū śakto.`
2. `मी वाचू शकते.` - `Mī vāchū śakte.`
3. `ती मराठी बोलू शकते.` - `Tī marāṭhī bolū śakte.`
4. `मी जाऊ शकत नाही.` - `Mī jāū śakat nāhī.`

### Why these answers work

The first two answers keep वाचू while changing the finite ability verb. The third adds मराठी as what can be spoken. The negative uses शकत नाही after जाऊ. The action remains identifiable even when the ability component changes its gender form or polarity.

### Retrieve and recombine

At a meeting, ask respectfully whether someone can come. Then describe a reading ability and a future reading plan separately. Explain which sentence answers a skill question and which answers a schedule question.


## Lesson 21: Liking and personal experience

**Outcome:** Express a preference with the person experiencing it in a to-form.

### Think before reading

You learned मला for wanting. Could liking tea also be organised around what is pleasing to me?

### Build the pattern

आवडणे expresses liking through an experiencer construction. मला चहा आवडतो means I like tea. The person is मला, while the liked thing determines the verb’s gender and number in these basic present examples. चहा is masculine and pairs with आवडतो; कॉफी is feminine and pairs with आवडते.

For neuter singular, standard written आवडते also appears, as in मला हे पुस्तक आवडते. The surface similarity to feminine आवडते does not mean the nouns share a gender. Marathi forms sometimes coincide across categories. Look at the noun’s identity rather than concluding that every repeated ending represents one grammatical category.

To identify another experiencer, use तिला or त्याला: तिला चहा आवडतो, she likes tea. The masculine आवडतो in that sentence belongs to tea, not to the woman. This recalls the want construction, in which हवा followed the wanted item. These two useful patterns make a broader lesson visible: the English subject I does not dictate Marathi मी.

The short negative is मला चहा आवडत नाही. It denies the preference without turning it into an active decision not to drink tea. Someone can like tea yet avoid it, or drink it without particularly liking it. Keeping liking, wanting and consuming separate makes your statements more accurate. For a natural exchange, ask what someone likes and answer with a specific item. You can offer an alternative without assuming that a negative preference is a criticism of the host.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मला चहा आवडतो. | Malā chahā āvaḍto. | I like tea. |
| मला कॉफी आवडते. | Malā kŏphī āvaḍte. | I like coffee. |
| तिला चहा आवडतो. | Tilā chahā āvaḍto. | She likes tea. |
| मला चहा आवडत नाही. | Malā chahā āvaḍat nāhī. | I do not like tea. |
| मला हे पुस्तक आवडते. | Malā he pustak āvaḍte. | I like this book. |
| तुम्हाला काय आवडते? | Tumhālā kāy āvaḍte? | What do you like, respectfully? |

### Common trap

Do not choose आवडते in she likes tea because the experiencer is female. Tea controls the basic verb agreement here. Also, I do not like is a preference statement, not the same statement as I do not consume.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you like tea.
2. Say you like coffee.
3. Say she likes tea.
4. Say you do not like tea.

***Answers:***

1. `मला चहा आवडतो.` - `Malā chahā āvaḍto.`
2. `मला कॉफी आवडते.` - `Malā kŏphī āvaḍte.`
3. `तिला चहा आवडतो.` - `Tilā chahā āvaḍto.`
4. `मला चहा आवडत नाही.` - `Malā chahā āvaḍat nāhī.`

### Why these answers work

The first and third keep आवडतो because चहा is unchanged, even though the experiencer changes. Coffee selects आवडते. The final answer uses the non-agreeing negative action form plus नाही. In every answer the person appears in a to-form rather than the ordinary मी or ती subject form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Choose a drink you like and one you do not want now. State both without making them contradict each other. Then ask an adult what they like using the respectful experiencer form.


## Lesson 22: Connecting ideas and giving a reason

**Outcome:** Join two statements while preserving each statement’s internal grammar.

### Think before reading

You want to say she reads but he works. Does adding but remove either clause’s need for its own appropriate verb?

### Build the pattern

आणि joins compatible pieces as and; पण introduces a contrast as but. In ती वाचते आणि तो काम करतो, each clause remains a complete grammatical statement. The conjunction links them without making their subjects or verbs agree with each other. Read each half separately first, then connect the ideas.

कारण introduces a reason. मी घरी आहे कारण मी काम करत आहे gives a reason for being at home: I am at home because I am working. The reason is another clause with its own predicate. There is no need to reverse the usual Marathi order inside that clause merely because English places because before it.

म्हणून commonly introduces a result, so or therefore. Compare a reason introduced after a claim with a result that follows from a stated situation. The direction of explanation matters even when the same two facts are involved. Begin with short clauses whose meaning is already secure.

किंवा means or and can offer alternatives. चहा किंवा कॉफी connects two noun phrases; चहा हवा आहे का asks whether tea is wanted. A choice question often uses की between alternatives, as चहा की कॉफी. This is why and, or and the question particle should not be treated as interchangeable connectors. The examples keep these relationships explicit. Once you can state each fact, choose the connector that tells the listener how those facts belong together.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ती वाचते आणि तो काम करतो. | Tī vāchte āṇi to kām karto. | She reads and he works. |
| मला चहा आवडतो पण कॉफी आवडत नाही. | Malā chahā āvaḍto paṇ kŏphī āvaḍat nāhī. | I like tea but do not like coffee. |
| मी घरी आहे कारण मी काम करत आहे. | Mī gharī āhe kāraṇ mī kām karat āhe. | I am at home because I am working. |
| चहा की कॉफी? | Chahā kī kŏphī? | Tea or coffee, as a choice? |
| तो तयार आहे म्हणून आपण जाऊ शकतो. | To tayār āhe mhaṇūn āpaṇ jāū śakto. | He is ready, so we can go, including the listener. |
| पुस्तक आणि वही. | Pustak āṇi vahī. | A book and a notebook. |

### Common trap

A conjunction does not erase agreement inside its clauses. Also, কারণ-like reasoning is directional: because marks a reason, while म्हणून presents a result. Merely listing two facts does not establish which explains the other.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say she reads and he works.
2. Say you like tea but do not like coffee.
3. Say you are at home because you are working.
4. Offer the spoken choice: tea or coffee?

***Answers:***

1. `ती वाचते आणि तो काम करतो.` - `Tī vāchte āṇi to kām karto.`
2. `मला चहा आवडतो पण कॉफी आवडत नाही.` - `Malā chahā āvaḍto paṇ kŏphī āvaḍat nāhī.`
3. `मी घरी आहे कारण मी काम करत आहे.` - `Mī gharī āhe kāraṇ mī kām karat āhe.`
4. `चहा की कॉफी?` - `Chahā kī kŏphī?`

### Why these answers work

The first answer preserves separate feminine and masculine habitual verbs. The second shares the experiencer मला across the contrasted preferences. The third provides a reason clause after कारण. The fourth is a short choice expression with की, not a complete yes-or-no question using final का.

### Retrieve and recombine

Connect a current activity to a location with कारण. Then offer two drinks as a choice. Explain why the connector changes even though both tasks involve combining two pieces of information.


## Lesson 23: Shopping as a sequence of decisions

**Outcome:** Ask a price, request a quantity and check availability.

### Think before reading

At a shop, what must the seller know before give it becomes a useful request: the item, its quantity, or both?

### Build the pattern

A shopping exchange works best as a sequence of small decisions. First identify the item, then ask its price or availability, and only then request the quantity you actually want. हे कितीला आहे asks how much this is. The form कितीला is useful for a price, whereas किती पुस्तके आहेत asks how many books exist.

मला दोन वह्या हव्या आहेत combines the wanting construction with a plural desired item: I want two notebooks. The feminine plural हव्या and plural आहेत reflect वह्या. This extends Lesson 11 beyond singular मदत हवी आहे. Quantity affects more than the numeral; it can change the noun and the predicate that describes the desired objects.

For a direct respectful request, दोन वह्या द्या remains straightforward. The command द्या still addresses the seller, so the number of notebooks does not change it. If you ask whether the seller has water, तुमच्याकडे पाणी आहे का uses availability with कडे. That is different from asking whether the seller personally wants water with तुम्हाला.

A short example exchange can therefore begin with a price question, continue with an availability question, and end with a quantity request. Do not memorise the entire exchange as one indivisible phrase. Track what each turn resolves. If the item changes from notebooks to books, rebuild the relevant plural noun and wanting agreement rather than swapping only the English meaning in your head. The practice answers are separate turns so that they can be reused at a stationery shop, a book stall or a small general store.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| हे कितीला आहे? | He kitīlā āhe? | How much is this? |
| मला दोन वह्या हव्या आहेत. | Malā don vahyā havyā āhet. | I want two notebooks. |
| दोन वह्या द्या. | Don vahyā dyā. | Give two notebooks, respectfully. |
| तुमच्याकडे पाणी आहे का? | Tumchyākaḍe pāṇī āhe kā? | Do you have water, respectfully? |
| मला दोन पुस्तके हवी आहेत. | Malā don pustake havī āhet. | I want two books. |
| धन्यवाद. | Dhanyavād. | Thank you. |

### Common trap

Do not confuse तुमच्याकडे with तुम्हाला: the former checks what the seller has, while the latter can ask what they want. Also, the plural desired noun changes हवी or हव्या and requires आहेत.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask the price of this item with कितीला.
2. Say you want two notebooks.
3. Request two notebooks respectfully.
4. Ask a seller whether they have water.

***Answers:***

1. `हे कितीला आहे?` - `He kitīlā āhe?`
2. `मला दोन वह्या हव्या आहेत.` - `Malā don vahyā havyā āhet.`
3. `दोन वह्या द्या.` - `Don vahyā dyā.`
4. `तुमच्याकडे पाणी आहे का?` - `Tumchyākaḍe pāṇī āhe kā?`

### Why these answers work

कितीला makes the first a price question. The second includes feminine plural हव्या आहेत with वह्या. The third expresses the purchase request through द्या instead of the wanting construction. The fourth checks availability through the seller’s कडे form, keeping water as a mass noun.

### Retrieve and recombine

Move this exchange from a stationery shop to a book stall. Replace the notebook request with two books and rebuild the want statement. Keep the price question and respectful social register available.


## Lesson 24: A visit, a delay and conversational repair

**Outcome:** Handle a short visit using plans, ability and a request for repetition.

### Think before reading

Someone planned to come but cannot come now. Which two constructions let you keep the original intention separate from the new obstacle?

### Build the pattern

A useful visit update separates intention, present circumstances and the next practical step. ती येणार आहे reports a plan or expectation that she will come. ती येऊ शकत नाही reports that she cannot come. These do not communicate the same information, even though both contain a form of come. A change in circumstances may make the second true after the first was announced.

आता and उद्या let you negotiate the schedule without learning a long new dialogue. मी आता येऊ शकत नाही says I cannot come now. उद्या तीन वाजता या offers a new appointment. The first is a statement about the speaker’s ability in the circumstances; the second is a respectful request directed to another person. Keep their person roles separate.

When you lose part of the conversation, पुन्हा सांगा asks the other person to say or tell it again. सांगा is a useful respectful form of सांगणे, to tell. कृपया हळू बोला asks for slower speech. The former requests repetition of content, while the latter requests a change in speed. Combining them as separate turns gives the listener a clear repair strategy.

You can also check one detail explicitly: उद्या का, tomorrow? As an elliptical confirmation this is understandable, but the practice uses full sentences where possible so that the referent remains clear outside a live conversation. Repair does not require pretending to understand. Identify whether you missed the action, the time or the person’s intention, then ask for that part again. This approach keeps a small vocabulary useful under real conversational pressure.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मी आता येऊ शकत नाही. | Mī ātā yeū śakat nāhī. | I cannot come now. |
| उद्या तीन वाजता या. | Udyā tīn vājatā yā. | Come tomorrow at three, respectfully. |
| कृपया पुन्हा सांगा. | Kṛpayā punhā sāṅgā. | Please say it again. |
| कृपया हळू बोला. | Kṛpayā haḷū bolā. | Please speak slowly. |
| ती येणार आहे. | Tī yeṇār āhe. | She is going to come. |
| ती येऊ शकत नाही. | Tī yeū śakat nāhī. | She cannot come. |

### Common trap

Do not report an inability by changing only the time adverb in a plan sentence. The predicate must express the obstacle. Also, asking for slower speech and asking for repetition are different requests, so choose the one you need.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you cannot come now.
2. Ask someone to come tomorrow at three.
3. Politely ask someone to say it again, using सांगा.
4. Politely ask someone to speak slowly.

***Answers:***

1. `मी आता येऊ शकत नाही.` - `Mī ātā yeū śakat nāhī.`
2. `उद्या तीन वाजता या.` - `Udyā tīn vājatā yā.`
3. `कृपया पुन्हा सांगा.` - `Kṛpayā punhā sāṅgā.`
4. `कृपया हळू बोला.` - `Kṛpayā haḷū bolā.`

### Why these answers work

The first uses the negative ability construction after येऊ. The second uses a respectful imperative with a precise appointment phrase. सांगा in the third asks for repeated content, and बोला in the fourth concerns speaking speed. कृपया supplies explicit politeness in the repair requests.

### Retrieve and recombine

Role-play a delayed visit in three turns: explain the obstacle, propose a new time, and request repetition of that time. Change the day once and check that the listener could still identify the appointment.


## Lesson 25: Cumulative review through a short day

**Outcome:** Recombine identity, location, completed events and plans into a coherent account.

### Think before reading

If a sentence contains yesterday, a woman and a book, is her gender alone enough to choose the completed reading form?

### Build the pattern

A coherent account depends on choosing the right construction for each fact. Begin with identity or location, describe what happened, and finish with a current need or future plan. Do not search for one tense ending that can carry the entire story. Marathi distributes information differently in habitual, ongoing, completed transitive and experiencer constructions.

Consider the four central practice situations as independent parts of a day. A person locates herself at home; a woman completed reading a book; the speaker needs water; and an inclusive group plans to come tomorrow. Each uses familiar vocabulary, but the grammatical controller changes. In the reading event, पुस्तक determines neuter वाचले after agent-marked तिने. In the water need, पाणी determines हवे after experiencer मला. The future plan keeps येणार stable and places group agreement on आहोत.

This is the course’s main transferable habit: identify the relationship before translating individual English words. Who acts? Who experiences a need? What is being described? Is the event a habit, a current activity or a completed action? These questions lead to different Marathi forms even when English places I or she first in every sentence.

Your next learning step should include listening to the chosen variety and asking a fluent speaker to review short messages you genuinely want to use. Written romanisation supports access but cannot fully teach vowel quality, breath release, the retroflex lateral or natural intonation. Keep the script alongside it. This foundation has introduced reusable patterns, not a promise of fluency. A useful review result is being able to explain a choice, rebuild a sentence in a new setting and recognise where additional vocabulary or feedback is needed.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| मी घरी आहे. | Mī gharī āhe. | I am at home. |
| तिने काल पुस्तक वाचले. | Tine kāl pustak vāchle. | She read a book yesterday. |
| मला पाणी हवे आहे. | Malā pāṇī have āhe. | I want water. |
| आपण उद्या येणार आहोत. | Āpaṇ udyā yeṇār āhot. | We are going to come tomorrow, including the listener. |
| तुमचे पुस्तक इथे आहे. | Tumche pustak ithe āhe. | Your book is here, respectfully. |
| कृपया पुन्हा सांगा. | Kṛpayā punhā sāṅgā. | Please say it again. |

### Common trap

A familiar English subject does not guarantee a familiar Marathi case form. Do not replace तिने with ती in the completed reading pattern or मला with मी in the water-need pattern merely to imitate English word order.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you are at home.
2. Say she read a book yesterday.
3. Say you want water.
4. Say we will come tomorrow as a plan, including your listener.

***Answers:***

1. `मी घरी आहे.` - `Mī gharī āhe.`
2. `तिने काल पुस्तक वाचले.` - `Tine kāl pustak vāchle.`
3. `मला पाणी हवे आहे.` - `Malā pāṇī have āhe.`
4. `आपण उद्या येणार आहोत.` - `Āpaṇ udyā yeṇār āhot.`

### Why these answers work

The location statement uses ordinary मी. The completed transitive event uses तिने and neuter वाचले. Wanting uses मला and neuter हवे. The inclusive plan uses आपण with आहोत. These four answers deliberately revisit different agreement systems rather than presenting one misleading universal rule.

### Retrieve and recombine

Build a six-sentence account of a visit using a location, a question, a completed event, a preference, a plan and a repair request. Change the visitor once and check only the forms that the change actually affects.
