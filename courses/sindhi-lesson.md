# Sindhi: A pattern-first foundation

This course uses standard Sindhi in Sindhi Perso-Arabic script, with a neutral everyday register broadly aligned with Sindhi Language Authority teaching materials. Sindhi also has an established Devanagari writing tradition, especially in India; that orthography is not represented as a mere font change here. Native text reads right to left. Romanisation is a separate access aid: ā ī ū and ee make useful vowel distinctions, tildes mark nasal vowels, ṭ ḍ ṛ ṇ mark retroflex sounds, ñ represents ڃ, and ɓ ɗ ɠ flag implosives. Short vowels, including some final vowels, are supplied for reading where ordinary script leaves them implicit. The romanisation is not a complete phonetic transcription.

### References and review notes

Consulted the Sindhi Language Authority’s original [present-tense lessons](https://learn.sindhila.edu.pk/grammar/tenses/347-present-tense), [ongoing constructions](https://learn.sindhila.edu.pk/grammar/tenses/348-present-continuous-tense), [habitual constructions](https://learn.sindhila.edu.pk/grammar/tenses/349-present-habitual-tense), [tense collection with past and future paradigms](https://learn.sindhila.edu.pk/grammar/tenses), [parts of speech](https://learn.sindhila.edu.pk/grammar/parts-of-speech) and [basic conversations](https://learn.sindhila.edu.pk/sentences/basic-sentences). These checks informed person, gender, perfective-agent and habitual-future distinctions; the situations and explanations are original. Some source English glosses and romanisations contain inconsistencies, so the course does not reproduce them mechanically. Short-vowel readings, regional negative forms and politeness remain fluent-speaker review priorities.

## Lesson 1: Identity in Sindhi script

**Outcome:** Introduce yourself and identify another person.

### Think before reading

If مان means I, would the final word for be necessarily be the same as after هو, he?

### Build the pattern

مان شاگرد آهيان means I am a student. The identity precedes the final copula آهيان. With a third-person singular subject, use آهي: هو شاگرد آهي, he is a student. English am and is therefore have a useful parallel in the person contrast, but the word order differs.

هو refers to he or a masculine distant referent; هوءَ refers to she. Keep the final hamza-bearing mark in هوءَ. It is meaningful writing, not decoration to remove when typing. The romanisation hūa makes the distinction visible for learners who cannot yet enter Sindhi script.

This course uses standard Sindhi in the Sindhi Perso-Arabic script, associated with the literary standard and Sindhi Language Authority teaching materials. The script is read from right to left. Letters may connect and change their visible shape according to position, while their identity remains stable. Romanisation appears separately from the native sentence so that left-to-right reading does not replace script recognition.

Short vowels are often not fully written in ordinary text. The reading aid supplies useful vowels, including some final short vowels, without claiming to be a phonetic transcript of every speaker. Sindhi also distinguishes sounds not found in English, including implosives. These require listening and guidance rather than an instruction to pronounce every familiar-looking letter as English. Start with the small identity pattern while keeping the native spelling in view.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| مان شاگرد آهيان. | Mā̃ shāgirdu āhiyā̃. | I am a student. |
| هو شاگرد آهي. | Hū shāgirdu āhe. | He is a student. |
| هوءَ استاد آهي. | Hūa ustādu āhe. | She is a teacher. |
| مان سنڌي آهيان. | Mā̃ sindhī āhiyā̃. | I am Sindhi. |
| سلام. | Salām. | Hello, a greeting. |
| هو تيار آهي. | Hū tayāru āhe. | He is ready. |

### Common trap

Do not replace Sindhi آهيان with Urdu ہوں because the scripts look related. Also, هو and هوءَ are different forms in these examples; omitting the final mark can erase the intended feminine reference.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you are a student.
2. Say he is a student.
3. Say she is a teacher.
4. Say you are Sindhi.

***Answers:***

1. `مان شاگرد آهيان.` - `Mā̃ shāgirdu āhiyā̃.`
2. `هو شاگرد آهي.` - `Hū shāgirdu āhe.`
3. `هوءَ استاد آهي.` - `Hūa ustādu āhe.`
4. `مان سنڌي آهيان.` - `Mā̃ sindhī āhiyā̃.`

### Why these answers work

The first and fourth answers use first-person آهيان. The middle answers use singular third-person آهي. The female teacher is identified by هوءَ; the copula itself does not change to a feminine form in this present identity construction.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce yourself, then identify two imaginary people. Retrieve مان, هو and هوءَ before looking back. Notice which part distinguishes person and which marks the feminine reference.


## Lesson 2: Respectful you and a shared group

**Outcome:** Choose familiar or respectful address and use the correct copula.

### Think before reading

Does addressing one adult respectfully require the familiar singular form just because only one person is present?

### Build the pattern

تون is familiar singular you. توهان is plural or respectful you and pairs with آهيو in the present copula pattern. تون takes آهين. The address relationship is therefore visible in both the pronoun and final verb. Use توهان with a new adult acquaintance unless the relationship supports familiarity.

اسان and اسين are common first-person plural forms. This course uses اسين in the early subject examples, with آهيون. Do not interpret them through Marathi or Gujarati inclusive-exclusive distinctions: Sindhi’s actual forms and uses need their own description. The present lesson simply gives a stable we pattern.

Third-person plural uses آهن, as in هو تيار آهن for a male group in the examples here. The written هو can be singular or plural according to the surrounding grammar and context. The copula helps distinguish he is from they are. A pronoun alone does not always reveal all the information a sentence supplies.

تيار, ready, is useful because it does not need a new gender ending in these examples. That leaves attention on person, number and respect. In a real interaction, respectful grammar is only part of courtesy: the content, tone and relationship matter as well. Keep the same address choice through a short exchange rather than switching to تون whenever a shorter form is easier to recall.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| توهان تيار آهيو. | Tavhā̃ tayāru āhiyo. | You are ready, respectfully or plurally. |
| تون تيار آهين. | Tū̃ tayāru āhī̃. | You are ready, familiarly. |
| اسين تيار آهيون. | Asī̃ tayāru āhiyū̃. | We are ready. |
| هو تيار آهن. | Hū tayāru āhin. | They are ready, male group. |
| مان تيار آهيان. | Mā̃ tayāru āhiyā̃. | I am ready. |
| هوءَ تيار آهي. | Hūa tayāru āhe. | She is ready. |

### Common trap

Do not treat هو as invariably singular regardless of its predicate. Also, respectful singular توهان retains آهيو; counting the people in front of you does not by itself determine the address form.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Tell an adult respectfully that they are ready.
2. Tell a close friend they are ready.
3. Say we are ready.
4. Say a male group is ready, using هو.

***Answers:***

1. `توهان تيار آهيو.` - `Tavhā̃ tayāru āhiyo.`
2. `تون تيار آهين.` - `Tū̃ tayāru āhī̃.`
3. `اسين تيار آهيون.` - `Asī̃ tayāru āhiyū̃.`
4. `هو تيار آهن.` - `Hū tayāru āhin.`

### Why these answers work

The first two contrast توهان آهيو with تون آهين. The we answer uses آهيون, while the male group uses آهن. تيار stays fixed, so the final copula carries the person and number distinctions.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine a host speaking to one visitor and then to several visitors. Use the respectful pattern in both cases, then state that your own group is ready with the we form.


## Lesson 3: Checking a fact and denying it

**Outcome:** Ask a yes-or-no question and negate a present state.

### Think before reading

Can a question particle check an unchanged statement without moving the verb before the subject?

### Build the pattern

ڇا can introduce a yes-or-no question: ڇا توهان تيار آهيو؟ asks whether the listener is ready. The subject and predicate remain in their ordinary order. There is no equivalent of English do-support and no need to invert the copula.

For a transparent negative state, place نه before the copula: هو تيار نه آهي, he is not ready. Everyday Sindhi also uses contracted negative copula forms such as ناهي. The course first keeps نه آهي visible so that the negative and the copula’s jobs are easy to distinguish.

ڇا also means what when it stands for missing information. Its function depends on the sentence, not just its dictionary entry. A complete proposition introduced by ڇا can ask for confirmation, while a name question containing ڇا asks for the name itself. The next lesson practises those information gaps.

ها means yes and نه means no. A full answer is often clearer than a bare particle, particularly when the original question contains a negative. The exercises therefore use affirmative questions and complete denials to avoid ambiguity about what is being accepted or rejected. Ask yourself which proposition the listener is checking, then answer that proposition rather than simply reacting to the presence of a question mark.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ڇا توهان تيار آهيو؟ | Chhā tavhā̃ tayāru āhiyo? | Are you ready, respectfully? |
| هو تيار نه آهي. | Hū tayāru na āhe. | He is not ready. |
| ڇا هو شاگرد آهي؟ | Chhā hū shāgirdu āhe? | Is he a student? |
| مان تيار نه آهيان. | Mā̃ tayāru na āhiyā̃. | I am not ready. |
| ها. | Hā. | Yes. |
| نه. | Na. | No. |

### Common trap

Do not insert Urdu نہیں or an English auxiliary into the Sindhi pattern. Also, the full نه آهي form and contracted ناهي should be recognised as related options rather than combined into a double negative.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask an adult whether they are ready, using ڇا.
2. Say he is not ready, using نه آهي.
3. Ask whether he is a student, using ڇا.
4. Say you are not ready, using the full negative copula.

***Answers:***

1. `ڇا توهان تيار آهيو؟` - `Chhā tavhā̃ tayāru āhiyo?`
2. `هو تيار نه آهي.` - `Hū tayāru na āhe.`
3. `ڇا هو شاگرد آهي؟` - `Chhā hū shāgirdu āhe?`
4. `مان تيار نه آهيان.` - `Mā̃ tayāru na āhiyā̃.`

### Why these answers work

The questions begin with ڇا and preserve the appropriate positive copula. The denials place نه before آهي or آهيان. The first-person negative still retains first-person copula agreement; negation does not erase who is being described.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask whether someone is a student, then ask whether they are ready. Give a negative answer to only one question and explain which fact it denies.


## Lesson 4: Who, what and where

**Outcome:** Ask for an identity, name or location.

### Think before reading

If هتي means here, which word should replace it when the location is not known?

### Build the pattern

ڪٿي means where and occupies the missing-location slot: هو ڪٿي آهي؟ asks where he is. ڪير means who and asks for a person’s identity. ڇا means what and can ask for the identity of an object or the content of a name.

هتي is here and اتي is there. A location reply can substitute one of these for ڪٿي while preserving the appropriate copula. مان هتي آهيان is a complete first-person location statement. The adverb does not require a separate at before it.

منهنجو نالو ڇا آهي؟ literally asks what my name is; a more useful introduction question to a familiar person is تنهنجو نالو ڇا آهي؟ Your name phrase will be analysed with possessives later. At this stage, recognise that ڇا represents the missing name rather than serving as a sentence-initial yes-or-no particle.

The native question mark is ؟. Its direction differs from the Latin question mark used in the romanisation, but both signal the same communicative function. Do not let punctuation distract from the question word itself. A who answer, a where answer and a what answer belong to different categories. Before replying, identify the missing category and choose vocabulary that can actually fill it. This makes a short exchange meaningful rather than a sequence of memorised questions.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| هو ڪٿي آهي؟ | Hū kithe āhe? | Where is he? |
| توهان ڪير آهيو؟ | Tavhā̃ kīr āhiyo? | Who are you, respectfully? |
| هي ڇا آهي؟ | Hī chhā āhe? | What is this? |
| مان هتي آهيان. | Mā̃ hite āhiyā̃. | I am here. |
| هوءَ اتي آهي. | Hūa ute āhe. | She is there. |
| تنهنجو نالو ڇا آهي؟ | Tuhinjo nālo chhā āhe? | What is your name, familiarly? |

### Common trap

Do not answer a where question with a person’s name merely because both questions end in a copula. Also, ڇا in a missing-identity slot has a different job from sentence-initial ڇا checking a complete proposition.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask where he is.
2. Ask an adult who they are.
3. Ask what this is.
4. Say you are here.

***Answers:***

1. `هو ڪٿي آهي؟` - `Hū kithe āhe?`
2. `توهان ڪير آهيو؟` - `Tavhā̃ kīr āhiyo?`
3. `هي ڇا آهي؟` - `Hī chhā āhe?`
4. `مان هتي آهيان.` - `Mā̃ hite āhiyā̃.`

### Why these answers work

ڪٿي requests a place, ڪير a person and ڇا an identity. The respectful question uses آهيو. The location reply replaces the unknown slot with هتي and uses first-person آهيان, so it supplies the information rather than asking for it.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine an unfamiliar object beside a classroom. Ask what it is, where a teacher is and who an adult visitor is. Predict the type of answer each question needs.


## Lesson 5: First actions with ٿو and ٿي

**Outcome:** Describe reading and writing with the correct subject gender.

### Think before reading

A male and female speaker both say I read. Which part can change while the first-person action form stays the same?

### Build the pattern

مان پڙهان ٿو means I read for a male speaker; مان پڙهان ٿي gives a female speaker’s sentence. The main verb پڙهان carries first-person information, while ٿو or ٿي contributes a gender distinction in this present construction. English I does not reveal that difference.

Third-person reading uses پڙهي: هو پڙهي ٿو and هوءَ پڙهي ٿي. Thus two choices are needed: the person-sensitive main verb form and the gender-sensitive auxiliary. Do not change only the pronoun while leaving the rest of the predicate untouched.

لکڻ is the citation form to write, and پڙهڻ to read. Their final ڻ is a retroflex nasal, different from ordinary ن. A dictionary form names an action but is not automatically the finite form used after مان. The readable romanisation ṇ helps preserve the contrast without pretending that English n is an exact substitute.

Sindhi present forms can be used across a range of current and ordinary-action contexts. Later lessons introduce a more explicitly habitual construction and an ongoing one. At this stage, the simple reading and writing sentences supply a useful foundation. Add an object before the predicate, as مان خط لکان ٿو, and the neutral subject-object-verb order becomes visible. The object’s gender does not control ٿو in this present construction.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| مان پڙهان ٿو. | Mā̃ paṛhā̃ tho. | I read, male speaker. |
| مان پڙهان ٿي. | Mā̃ paṛhā̃ thī. | I read, female speaker. |
| هو لکي ٿو. | Hū likhe tho. | He writes. |
| هوءَ لکي ٿي. | Hūa likhe thī. | She writes. |
| مان خط لکان ٿو. | Mā̃ khatu likhā̃ tho. | I write a letter, male speaker. |
| هوءَ ڪتاب پڙهي ٿي. | Hūa kitābu paṛhe thī. | She reads a book. |

### Common trap

Do not keep ٿو for a female subject in the pattern taught here. Also, the main verb changes between first-person پڙهان and third-person پڙهي; the auxiliary is not the only word carrying grammatical information.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you read, male speaker.
2. Say you read, female speaker.
3. Say he writes.
4. Say she writes.

***Answers:***

1. `مان پڙهان ٿو.` - `Mā̃ paṛhā̃ tho.`
2. `مان پڙهان ٿي.` - `Mā̃ paṛhā̃ thī.`
3. `هو لکي ٿو.` - `Hū likhe tho.`
4. `هوءَ لکي ٿي.` - `Hūa likhe thī.`

### Why these answers work

The first-person pair keeps پڙهان but changes ٿو to ٿي. The third-person pair keeps لکي while the pronoun and auxiliary identify male versus female. No separate آهي is required after these complete simple present predicates.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe reading as yourself, then as a woman being discussed. Identify both the main-verb person change and the auxiliary gender choice before adding a book object.


## Lesson 6: Objects for eating and drinking

**Outcome:** Build food sentences while keeping the actor as the present agreement controller.

### Think before reading

If a woman drinks masculine water, does the drink force a masculine present auxiliary?

### Build the pattern

The object normally comes before the action predicate. هو پاڻي پيئي ٿو means he drinks water, while هوءَ پاڻي پيئي ٿي means she drinks water. The drink stays the same, but the present auxiliary follows the subject’s gender in this construction.

پاڻي means water, کير milk, ماني bread or a meal according to context, and انب mango. Learn the food word through the actual activity. ماني کائڻ can refer to eating a meal, not only biting a piece of bread. An English one-word gloss cannot settle every practical interpretation.

The present eating form is کائي for third person. هو انب کائي ٿو and هوءَ ماني کائي ٿي therefore share the action family while identifying different actors and objects. The first-person drinking form پيئان will be used in a complete example rather than derived by guessing from the spelling of پيئي.

Sindhi spelling may leave short vowels implicit and can show variation in common vowel sequences. The course keeps the native forms alongside a readable pronunciation aid so that you can recognise the action despite those orthographic details. Do not turn romanisation into a substitute spelling system. For useful production, choose the person, then the food or drink, then the matching present predicate. Add quantity only when it is part of the message.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| هو پاڻي پيئي ٿو. | Hū pāṇī piye tho. | He drinks water. |
| هوءَ پاڻي پيئي ٿي. | Hūa pāṇī piye thī. | She drinks water. |
| هو انب کائي ٿو. | Hū ambu khāe tho. | He eats a mango. |
| هوءَ ماني کائي ٿي. | Hūa mānī khāe thī. | She eats bread or a meal. |
| مان کير پيئان ٿو. | Mā̃ khīru piyā̃ tho. | I drink milk, male speaker. |
| مان ماني کان ٿي. | Mā̃ mānī khā̃ thī. | I eat bread or a meal, female speaker. |

### Common trap

Do not make the present auxiliary agree with water or bread. It reflects the actor in these examples. Also, ماني can name a meal in context, so its practical meaning is broader than a rigid one-word gloss.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say he drinks water.
2. Say she drinks water.
3. Say he eats a mango.
4. Say she eats bread, using ماني.

***Answers:***

1. `هو پاڻي پيئي ٿو.` - `Hū pāṇī piye tho.`
2. `هوءَ پاڻي پيئي ٿي.` - `Hūa pāṇī piye thī.`
3. `هو انب کائي ٿو.` - `Hū ambu khāe tho.`
4. `هوءَ ماني کائي ٿي.` - `Hūa mānī khāe thī.`

### Why these answers work

The drink pair changes only the feminine reference and auxiliary. The eating pair uses کائي with the appropriate subject auxiliary. Objects remain before the predicate and do not replace the actor as the agreement controller in this present construction.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe two people sharing a meal but drinking different things. Change one drink without changing its actor, and explain why the auxiliary remains tied to the person.


## Lesson 7: Negative actions with نٿو and نٿي

**Outcome:** Deny a simple present activity.

### Think before reading

If نه combines with ٿو in a negative predicate, would the resulting form remain identical for a female subject?

### Build the pattern

A common simple present negative uses نٿو with a masculine singular subject and نٿي with a feminine singular subject. هو نٿو پڙهي means he does not read; هوءَ نٿي پڙهي means she does not read. The negative auxiliary precedes the main verb in these examples.

This differs from the positive order هو پڙهي ٿو taught first. Sindhi allows alternative placements in use, but the course gives a clear, consistent beginner pattern. Do not simply append نه after a complete positive predicate and assume every order has the same naturalness.

First-person main verbs remain person-sensitive: مان نٿو لکان for a male speaker, and مان نٿي لکان for a female speaker. The negative auxiliary identifies gender, while لکان keeps the first-person ending. A book or letter object can be inserted before the negative predicate.

A negative habit is not the same as a refusal at a single moment. Saying someone does not read can describe a general activity pattern or a contextual denial; it does not itself explain why. Similarly, not drinking tea may state a habit rather than rejecting one offered cup. Later want constructions let you express a more specific need or preference. Keep the intended message clear before selecting which kind of negative sentence to build.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| هو نٿو پڙهي. | Hū natho paṛhe. | He does not read. |
| هوءَ نٿي پڙهي. | Hūa nathī paṛhe. | She does not read. |
| مان نٿو لکان. | Mā̃ natho likhā̃. | I do not write, male speaker. |
| مان نٿي لکان. | Mā̃ nathī likhā̃. | I do not write, female speaker. |
| هو ڪتاب نٿو پڙهي. | Hū kitābu natho paṛhe. | He does not read a book. |
| هوءَ خط نٿي لکي. | Hūa khatu nathī likhe. | She does not write a letter. |

### Common trap

Do not use the same negative auxiliary for male and female subjects in this pattern. Also, the negative word does not remove person marking from the main verb: first-person لکان differs from third-person لکي.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say he does not read.
2. Say she does not read.
3. Say you do not write, male speaker.
4. Say you do not write, female speaker.

***Answers:***

1. `هو نٿو پڙهي.` - `Hū natho paṛhe.`
2. `هوءَ نٿي پڙهي.` - `Hūa nathī paṛhe.`
3. `مان نٿو لکان.` - `Mā̃ natho likhā̃.`
4. `مان نٿي لکان.` - `Mā̃ nathī likhā̃.`

### Why these answers work

نٿو and نٿي supply the gendered negative auxiliary before the action. پڙهي is third person in the first pair; لکان is first person in the second pair. The pronoun and predicate must be read together to identify the full message.

### Retrieve and recombine

Make a positive reading statement and its negative for a woman. Then switch to speaking as her and revise the main verb’s person form rather than changing only the pronoun.


## Lesson 8: Places with ۾ and تي

**Outcome:** Locate a person inside a building and an object on a surface.

### Think before reading

English puts in before a noun. Where does Sindhi place ۾ relative to the place?

### Build the pattern

Sindhi uses postpositions, which follow their noun phrase. گهر ۾ means in the house and ميز تي means on the table. The noun comes first, then the relationship. The ordinary final copula states the location: ڪتاب ميز تي آهي.

اسڪول ۾ means in the school and دڪان ۾ in the shop. These expressions can locate a person or an object. The postposition does not change because the subject is male or female, while the copula continues to reflect person and number where relevant.

مان گهر ۾ آهيان uses first-person آهيان; هو دڪان ۾ آهي uses third-person آهي. Do not let the location phrase distract you from the subject. A long phrase in the middle of a sentence still leaves the final predicate responsible for the relevant agreement.

ڪٿي can replace the whole location expression in a question. You do not normally need to attach ۾ to the ordinary where question merely because the answer might contain in. Ask for a place first, then supply its actual relationship when answering. A thing may be on a table, inside a shop or at a school, and those are different spatial scenes. Imagine the scene rather than translating an English preposition in isolation.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| مان گهر ۾ آهيان. | Mā̃ gharu mẽ āhiyā̃. | I am in the house. |
| هو دڪان ۾ آهي. | Hū dukānu mẽ āhe. | He is in the shop. |
| هوءَ اسڪول ۾ آهي. | Hūa iskūlu mẽ āhe. | She is in the school. |
| ڪتاب ميز تي آهي. | Kitābu mez te āhe. | The book is on the table. |
| پاڻي گهر ۾ آهي. | Pāṇī gharu mẽ āhe. | The water is in the house. |
| ڪتاب ڪٿي آهي؟ | Kitābu kithe āhe? | Where is the book? |

### Common trap

Do not put ۾ before its noun in English order. Also, on a table and in a table are different relationships; تي and ۾ are not interchangeable location decorations.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you are in the house.
2. Say he is in the shop.
3. Say she is in the school.
4. Say the book is on the table.

***Answers:***

1. `مان گهر ۾ آهيان.` - `Mā̃ gharu mẽ āhiyā̃.`
2. `هو دڪان ۾ آهي.` - `Hū dukānu mẽ āhe.`
3. `هوءَ اسڪول ۾ آهي.` - `Hūa iskūlu mẽ āhe.`
4. `ڪتاب ميز تي آهي.` - `Kitābu mez te āhe.`

### Why these answers work

The first three place ۾ after a building noun. The fourth uses تي after ميز for a surface location. First-person agreement appears only in the first answer’s آهيان; the other singular subjects take آهي.

### Retrieve and recombine

Move a book from a table to a shop in an imagined scene. Ask where it is each time, then choose the postposition that represents the actual location relationship.


## Lesson 9: Descriptions and noun gender

**Outcome:** Match a changing adjective to a masculine or feminine noun.

### Think before reading

If water and a car belong to the same woman, must both be described with feminine adjective forms?

### Build the pattern

Sindhi nouns are masculine or feminine. A changing adjective follows the noun’s gender, not the owner’s. گهر, house, is masculine, while گاڏي, vehicle or car, is feminine. وڏو گهر is a big house and وڏي گاڏي a big car.

سٺو and سٺي mean good with masculine and feminine singular nouns. ڪتاب is masculine, so سٺو ڪتاب. ماني is feminine, so سٺي ماني. The adjective can appear before the noun or as part of a predicate, as ڪتاب سٺو آهي. The noun remains the controller in either position.

Some adjectives remain unchanged across these contexts. گرم, hot, is useful with پاڻي or ماني without creating a new gender ending. Do not force every describing word into the -o and -ī class. Learn the adjective’s behaviour with a pair of examples.

The distinct consonants in وڏو and سٺو deserve attention. Sindhi has several contrasts among dental, retroflex, aspirated and implosive sounds; English spelling cannot fully represent them. The romanisation distinguishes implosive ɗ in وڏو from retroflex ṭh in سٺو, while the native script retains the actual letters. Begin by recognising that وڏو and وڏي belong to one adjective family, then connect their endings to the noun they describe.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| گهر وڏو آهي. | Gharu vaɗo āhe. | The house is big. |
| گاڏي وڏي آهي. | Gāḍī vaɗī āhe. | The car is big. |
| ڪتاب سٺو آهي. | Kitābu suṭho āhe. | The book is good. |
| ماني سٺي آهي. | Mānī suṭhī āhe. | The bread is good. |
| پاڻي گرم آهي. | Pāṇī garam āhe. | The water is hot. |
| ماني گرم آهي. | Mānī garam āhe. | The bread is hot. |

### Common trap

Do not choose the adjective ending from the owner’s gender. Also, گرم is not forced into a changing adjective pattern just because وڏو changes. The noun and adjective class both matter.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say the house is big.
2. Say the car is big.
3. Say the book is good.
4. Say the bread is good.

***Answers:***

1. `گهر وڏو آهي.` - `Gharu vaɗo āhe.`
2. `گاڏي وڏي آهي.` - `Gāḍī vaɗī āhe.`
3. `ڪتاب سٺو آهي.` - `Kitābu suṭho āhe.`
4. `ماني سٺي آهي.` - `Mānī suṭhī āhe.`

### Why these answers work

Masculine گهر and ڪتاب select وڏو and سٺو. Feminine گاڏي and ماني select وڏي and سٺي. The singular copula remains آهي because present be does not itself introduce this adjective’s gender contrast.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a good book and good bread, then describe both as hot where sensible. Identify which adjective family changes and which stays fixed across the two noun genders.


## Lesson 10: My, your and the related noun

**Outcome:** Identify whose book, car or name is being discussed.

### Think before reading

A male speaker says my car. Does my become masculine because the speaker is male?

### Build the pattern

منهنجو, my, is used with a masculine singular noun, while منهنجي is feminine in a direct noun phrase. منهنجو ڪتاب is my book; منهنجي گاڏي is my car. The ending follows the related noun, not the speaker’s gender.

تنهنجو and تنهنجي give familiar your. Respectful or plural your can use توهان جو and توهان جي. The two-word respectful phrase still follows the same agreement principle. Choose the address relationship first, then the noun’s gender. A respectful listener’s car takes توهان جي گاڏي.

منهنجو نالو سارا آهي introduces the name Sara. نالو is masculine, so منهنجو stays masculine even when Sara is a woman. This is a useful worked example because English-speaking learners often try to make every word associated with a woman feminine.

Possessive grammar also expresses relationships such as my friend and my teacher, not literal ownership. It links referents. If you change from identifying a book as mine to saying I have a book with me, you need the availability construction in the next lesson. The two statements can be true under different circumstances, especially with borrowed items. Keep the noun phrase’s agreement distinct from the broader sentence’s predicate.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| هي منهنجو ڪتاب آهي. | Hī muhinjo kitābu āhe. | This is my book. |
| هيءَ منهنجي گاڏي آهي. | Hīa muhinjī gāḍī āhe. | This is my car. |
| منهنجو نالو سارا آهي. | Muhinjo nālo Sārā āhe. | My name is Sara. |
| توهان جو ڪتاب هتي آهي. | Tavhā̃ jo kitābu hite āhe. | Your book is here, respectfully. |
| تنهنجي گاڏي اتي آهي. | Tuhinjī gāḍī ute āhe. | Your car is there, familiarly. |
| توهان جو نالو ڇا آهي؟ | Tavhā̃ jo nālo chhā āhe? | What is your name, respectfully? |

### Common trap

A woman still says منهنجو نالو because name is masculine. Do not make possession words agree with the owner’s gender. Also, respectful توهان جو is not replaced by familiar تنهنجو merely to shorten a sentence.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Identify this as your own book.
2. Identify this as your own car.
3. Say your name is Sara.
4. Tell an adult their book is here.

***Answers:***

1. `هي منهنجو ڪتاب آهي.` - `Hī muhinjo kitābu āhe.`
2. `هيءَ منهنجي گاڏي آهي.` - `Hīa muhinjī gāḍī āhe.`
3. `منهنجو نالو سارا آهي.` - `Muhinjo nālo Sārā āhe.`
4. `توهان جو ڪتاب هتي آهي.` - `Tavhā̃ jo kitābu hite āhe.`

### Why these answers work

Book and name select masculine منهنجو or جو. Car selects feminine منهنجي. The demonstrative هيءَ in the car example also reflects its feminine noun. The book remains the subject of the final location statement despite the respectful owner phrase.

### Retrieve and recombine

Keep Sara as the speaker and identify her name, book and car. Explain why the first two use a masculine possessive while the car uses a feminine one.


## Lesson 11: Having something with وٽ

**Outcome:** Report available objects and distinguish them from ownership.

### Think before reading

If a borrowed book is with you, can you say you have it without calling it your own book?

### Build the pattern

مون وٽ ڪتاب آهي means I have a book. وٽ follows the person form مون and locates the book with that person. The construction can express possession or availability without necessarily establishing ownership.

توهان وٽ means with you respectfully, and هن وٽ with this person or him or her in context. The pronoun form differs from ordinary مان because it stands in a relational phrase. Do not form the construction by writing the ordinary subject pronoun and treating وٽ as an English verb have.

A question such as ڇا توهان وٽ پاڻي آهي؟ checks whether water is available with the addressee. It might concern a shop’s stock, a host’s supply or a bottle someone is carrying. The intended kind of availability comes from context. The grammar does not specify all those practical details by itself.

For absence, use the familiar full negative pattern: مون وٽ ڪتاب نه آهي. This says I do not have a book, not that books never exist or that I dislike them. It denies the specific availability relation. Compare it with منهنجو ڪتاب هتي آهي, my book is here. One sentence centres on what is available with a person; the other identifies an owned or related object and gives its location. This distinction is especially useful when borrowing or returning things.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| مون وٽ ڪتاب آهي. | Mū̃ vaṭ kitābu āhe. | I have a book. |
| ڇا توهان وٽ پاڻي آهي؟ | Chhā tavhā̃ vaṭ pāṇī āhe? | Do you have water, respectfully? |
| هن وٽ گاڏي آهي. | Hin vaṭ gāḍī āhe. | This person has a car. |
| مون وٽ ڪتاب نه آهي. | Mū̃ vaṭ kitābu na āhe. | I do not have a book. |
| منهنجو ڪتاب هتي آهي. | Muhinjo kitābu hite āhe. | My book is here. |
| توهان وٽ ڪتاب آهي. | Tavhā̃ vaṭ kitābu āhe. | You have a book, respectfully. |

### Common trap

Do not confuse مون وٽ with منهنجو. One builds an availability statement and the other modifies a noun as my. A borrowed book can be with you while belonging to someone else.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you have a book.
2. Ask an adult whether they have water.
3. Say this person has a car.
4. Say you do not have a book, using the full negative copula.

***Answers:***

1. `مون وٽ ڪتاب آهي.` - `Mū̃ vaṭ kitābu āhe.`
2. `ڇا توهان وٽ پاڻي آهي؟` - `Chhā tavhā̃ vaṭ pāṇī āhe?`
3. `هن وٽ گاڏي آهي.` - `Hin vaṭ gāḍī āhe.`
4. `مون وٽ ڪتاب نه آهي.` - `Mū̃ vaṭ kitābu na āhe.`

### Why these answers work

وٽ follows the relevant person in every answer. The object then appears with آهي or نه آهي. The second answer adds initial ڇا to check the whole availability statement. Gender of the available object does not turn مون into a possessive adjective.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine borrowing a friend’s book. State that you have a book, then identify your own book as being at home. Explain why possession and present availability need not coincide.


## Lesson 12: A need with مون کي

**Outcome:** State a practical need and ask what someone needs.

### Think before reading

If water is needed by me, might Sindhi use a to-me form instead of ordinary مان?

### Build the pattern

مون کي پاڻي گهرجي means I need or want water. مون کي is a to-me phrase, and گهرجي supplies the need relation. The ordinary subject مان is not substituted mechanically. English I therefore corresponds to different Sindhi forms depending on the predicate.

توهان کي is the respectful or plural to-you counterpart. توهان کي ڇا گهرجي؟ asks what the addressee needs or wants. A shopkeeper or host can use this to discover a practical requirement, not necessarily an intense desire. Context determines the strength of need versus want in the English translation.

مدد means help and allows an abstract need: مون کي مدد گهرجي. The construction is not restricted to objects that can be handed over. A book can also be requested through مون کي ڪتاب گهرجي, but this states the requirement rather than directly commanding a specific listener to give it.

That difference is useful socially. First state what you need; then, if appropriate, ask someone to perform the action that would meet the need. The next lesson supplies a respectful give request. Do not confuse need, possession and liking: you can need a book you do not own and like a drink you do not currently want. Choosing the correct relation makes a small vocabulary much more precise.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| مون کي پاڻي گهرجي. | Mū̃ khe pāṇī ghurje. | I need water. |
| مون کي مدد گهرجي. | Mū̃ khe madad ghurje. | I need help. |
| توهان کي ڇا گهرجي؟ | Tavhā̃ khe chhā ghurje? | What do you need, respectfully? |
| مون کي ڪتاب گهرجي. | Mū̃ khe kitābu ghurje. | I need a book. |
| هن کي پاڻي گهرجي. | Hin khe pāṇī ghurje. | This person needs water. |
| مون وٽ پاڻي آهي. | Mū̃ vaṭ pāṇī āhe. | I have water. |

### Common trap

Do not replace مون کي with مون وٽ: needing water and having water are different statements. Also, a need does not by itself specify who must supply the item; a direct request needs its own action predicate.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you need water.
2. Say you need help.
3. Ask an adult what they need.
4. Say you need a book.

***Answers:***

1. `مون کي پاڻي گهرجي.` - `Mū̃ khe pāṇī ghurje.`
2. `مون کي مدد گهرجي.` - `Mū̃ khe madad ghurje.`
3. `توهان کي ڇا گهرجي؟` - `Tavhā̃ khe chhā ghurje?`
4. `مون کي ڪتاب گهرجي.` - `Mū̃ khe kitābu ghurje.`

### Why these answers work

The statements use مون کي as experiencer. The question uses respectful توهان کي and places ڇا in the needed-item slot. گهرجي remains the need predicate, while the object can be water, help or a book.

### Retrieve and recombine

State a need for a book and a separate fact that you already have water. Explain why the person phrase changes from کي to وٽ even though English begins both with I.


## Lesson 13: Polite action requests

**Outcome:** Ask someone to give, sit, come or speak slowly.

### Think before reading

A need statement says what would help. What verb makes the request to hand it over explicit?

### Build the pattern

ڏيو is a respectful or plural give command. مون کي پاڻي ڏيو asks someone to give water to me. The recipient مون کي precedes the object and final command. No positive copula is needed because the imperative is already a complete action request.

ويهـڻ, to sit, gives the useful respectful command ويهو. اچو means come. These forms should be learned as real words rather than produced through a universal suffix rule. Sindhi verbs can change stem shape, and some sounds have no close English counterpart.

مهرباني ڪري is an explicit please expression. It can introduce a careful request, while tone and respectful verb choice also contribute to politeness. آهستي ڳالهايو asks for slower speech. The initial ڳ in ڳالهايو represents a distinctive Sindhi consonant; the romanisation ɠ marks an implosive rather than ordinary English g.

A negative command can use نه before the imperative, as هتي نه ويهو, do not sit here. This aims to influence the listener’s next action, unlike a statement that a person does not sit somewhere habitually. Keep those meanings separate. With these short commands you can manage a classroom or service interaction and request slower speech when you need more time to understand.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| مون کي پاڻي ڏيو. | Mū̃ khe pāṇī ɗiyo. | Give me water, respectfully. |
| مهرباني ڪري هتي ويهو. | Miharbānī kare hite viho. | Please sit here. |
| هتي اچو. | Hite acho. | Come here, respectfully. |
| آهستي ڳالهايو. | Āhiste ɠālhāyo. | Speak slowly, respectfully. |
| هتي نه ويهو. | Hite na viho. | Do not sit here, respectfully. |
| مون کي ڪتاب ڏيو. | Mū̃ khe kitābu ɗiyo. | Give me a book, respectfully. |

### Common trap

Do not append آهي to an imperative. Also, ڏ and ڳ should not be collapsed into ordinary English d and g without recognising the sound distinction; the romanisation flags a listening priority.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask an adult to give you water.
2. Politely ask someone to sit here, with مهرباني ڪري.
3. Ask an adult to come here.
4. Ask an adult to speak slowly.

***Answers:***

1. `مون کي پاڻي ڏيو.` - `Mū̃ khe pāṇī ɗiyo.`
2. `مهرباني ڪري هتي ويهو.` - `Miharbānī kare hite viho.`
3. `هتي اچو.` - `Hite acho.`
4. `آهستي ڳالهايو.` - `Āhiste ɠālhāyo.`

### Why these answers work

ڏيو requests giving and مون کي identifies the recipient. ويهو, اچو and ڳالهايو are respectful action forms. The place and manner words specify how or where to act without replacing the command itself.

### Retrieve and recombine

At a desk, ask for a book and then ask the speaker to slow down. Transfer the give request from water to a book while keeping its recipient and respectful form intact.


## Lesson 14: What is happening now

**Outcome:** Use the explicit ongoing construction with رهيو and رهي.

### Think before reading

The main action is reading in both sentences. Which component should change between a male and female speaker who is reading now?

### Build the pattern

An explicit ongoing pattern combines a linking action form, رهيو or رهي, and a copula. مان پڙهي رهيو آهيان means I am reading for a male speaker. مان پڙهي رهي آهيان gives the female-speaker version. The action form پڙهي stays the same, while the ongoing component carries gender.

The final copula still marks person. Third-person هو ڪم ڪري رهيو آهي uses آهي, not first-person آهيان. This means a speaker change can affect more than one part of the predicate: a gender change affects رهيو or رهي, and a person change affects the copula.

Sindhi also has a very common ongoing construction using پيو and related forms. The Sindhi Language Authority presents both systems. This course teaches the expanded رهيو pattern first because its separate components are easy to inspect, and it identifies پيو as normal Sindhi rather than an error or a mandatory synonym in every context.

هاڻي means now and can make the current frame explicit. It does not itself create an ongoing verb. Compare a daily habit, a simple present activity and a current event in progress; the language offers more than one way to place attention on the action. Build the complete predicate appropriate to the intended meaning instead of treating every English -ing word as a fixed Sindhi suffix.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| مان پڙهي رهيو آهيان. | Mā̃ paṛhī rahiyo āhiyā̃. | I am reading, male speaker. |
| مان پڙهي رهي آهيان. | Mā̃ paṛhī rahī āhiyā̃. | I am reading, female speaker. |
| هو ڪم ڪري رهيو آهي. | Hū kamu kare rahiyo āhe. | He is working. |
| هوءَ هاڻي لکي رهي آهي. | Hūa hāṇe likhī rahī āhe. | She is writing now. |
| هو ڪتاب پڙهي رهيو آهي. | Hū kitābu paṛhī rahiyo āhe. | He is reading a book. |
| هوءَ ڪم ڪري رهي آهي. | Hūa kamu kare rahī āhe. | She is working. |

### Common trap

Do not leave first-person آهيان after changing the subject to هو. Also, the existence of the پيو pattern does not license mixing pieces from both constructions at random; learn each complete form in context.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you are reading, male speaker.
2. Say you are reading, female speaker.
3. Say he is working.
4. Say she is writing now, including هاڻي.

***Answers:***

1. `مان پڙهي رهيو آهيان.` - `Mā̃ paṛhī rahiyo āhiyā̃.`
2. `مان پڙهي رهي آهيان.` - `Mā̃ paṛhī rahī āhiyā̃.`
3. `هو ڪم ڪري رهيو آهي.` - `Hū kamu kare rahiyo āhe.`
4. `هوءَ هاڻي لکي رهي آهي.` - `Hūa hāṇe likhī rahī āhe.`

### Why these answers work

The first pair differs in رهيو versus رهي. The third shifts to third-person آهي and a work expression. The fourth uses feminine رهي with third-person آهي and an explicit now. The linking action forms do not themselves carry the subject’s gender.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a woman’s writing now, then speak as that woman. Keep the feminine ongoing component and change the copula for first person. Explain the two independent agreement choices.


## Lesson 15: Making a routine explicit

**Outcome:** Recognise the habitual participle plus copula.

### Think before reading

A form like ايندو can occur in a future statement. What changes when it is followed by a present copula and a daily time word?

### Build the pattern

Sindhi has a habitual construction with a participial form and a present copula. هو روز ايندو آهي means he comes every day. The participle ايندو is masculine singular; the copula آهي establishes the habitual present construction in this context.

The feminine version is هوءَ روز ايندي آهي. First-person male مان روز پڙهندو آهيان and female مان روز پڙهندي آهيان likewise differ in the participle while retaining the first-person copula. The pattern makes a repeated activity explicit rather than necessarily describing what is happening at this moment.

This is a point where isolated word lists can mislead. A future statement هو ايندو, he will come, and a habitual statement هو ايندو آهي share a visible form but differ in their full predicate. Do not assign an English tense from one word without reading the construction around it.

روز means every day, and صبح جو means in the morning. These expressions can make the regular schedule clear. A habit can be stated without repeating the frequency word every time once the context is established, but a beginner benefits from an explicit time frame. Compare the habitual construction with the ongoing pattern from the previous lesson. Both can describe the same person and action, yet one profiles repetition and the other an event in progress.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| هو روز ايندو آهي. | Hū roz eendo āhe. | He comes every day. |
| هوءَ روز ايندي آهي. | Hūa roz eendī āhe. | She comes every day. |
| مان روز پڙهندو آهيان. | Mā̃ roz paṛhando āhiyā̃. | I read every day, male speaker. |
| مان روز پڙهندي آهيان. | Mā̃ roz paṛhandī āhiyā̃. | I read every day, female speaker. |
| هو صبح جو ڪم ڪندو آهي. | Hū subhu jo kamu kando āhe. | He works in the morning habitually. |
| هو ايندو. | Hū eendo. | He will come. |

### Common trap

Do not identify ايندو as always future when it occurs inside ايندو آهي. The whole predicate matters. Also, habitual gender marking follows the actor in these examples, not the object that might later be added.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say he comes every day, using the habitual construction.
2. Say she comes every day, using the habitual construction.
3. Say you read every day, male speaker, with the habitual construction.
4. Say you read every day, female speaker, with the habitual construction.

***Answers:***

1. `هو روز ايندو آهي.` - `Hū roz eendo āhe.`
2. `هوءَ روز ايندي آهي.` - `Hūa roz eendī āhe.`
3. `مان روز پڙهندو آهيان.` - `Mā̃ roz paṛhando āhiyā̃.`
4. `مان روز پڙهندي آهيان.` - `Mā̃ roz paṛhandī āhiyā̃.`

### Why these answers work

ايندو and ايندي distinguish masculine and feminine habitual subjects. پڙهندو and پڙهندي do the same for reading. The copula remains آهيان in both first-person answers and آهي in both third-person answers.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a daily arrival and a current arrival as separate situations. Then remove the present copula from the third-person masculine habitual form and interpret the resulting future statement.


## Lesson 16: Numbers and plural objects

**Outcome:** Request a small quantity and recognise plural agreement.

### Think before reading

If two books are present, can the written noun look unchanged while the copula reveals plurality?

### Build the pattern

Useful numbers are هڪ, one; ٻه, two; ٽي, three; چار, four; پنج, five; and ڇهه, six. ٻه contains an implosive consonant, marked ɓ in the romanisation. Do not flatten this sound into ordinary b merely because English lacks it.

Sindhi plural formation depends on noun class. ڇوڪرو, boy, gives ڇوڪرا; ڇوڪري, girl, gives ڇوڪريون. Some consonant-final masculine nouns such as ڪتاب can look the same in ordinary unvocalised writing across singular and plural. The number phrase and copula help reveal the intended count.

ٻه ڪتاب آهن means there are two books. The plural copula آهن contrasts with singular آهي. A request ٻه ڪتاب ڏيو uses the same counted phrase but ends with a respectful give command. The number of books does not change the listener-facing imperative.

ڪيترا asks how many with masculine plural referents. ڪيترا ڪتاب آهن؟ therefore asks for the count of books. Feminine counted nouns have their own agreement forms, introduced only as needed. The goal is not to memorise an undifferentiated number list, but to combine a quantity, the correct noun form and a predicate suited to either requesting or reporting. A count becomes useful only when the listener knows what is being counted.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ٻه ڪتاب ڏيو. | Ɓa kitāba ɗiyo. | Give two books, respectfully. |
| ٽي ڇوڪرا آهن. | Ṭe chhokrā āhin. | There are three boys. |
| ٻه ڇوڪريون آهن. | Ɓa chhokriyū̃ āhin. | There are two girls. |
| ڪيترا ڪتاب آهن؟ | Ketrā kitāba āhin? | How many books are there? |
| ٻه ڪتاب آهن. | Ɓa kitāba āhin. | There are two books. |
| پنج انب. | Panja amba. | Five mangoes. |

### Common trap

Do not assume an unchanged unvocalised noun spelling proves singular number. Also, آهن belongs to plural existence statements, while ڏيو remains a command addressed to the listener.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Request two books respectfully.
2. Say there are three boys.
3. Say there are two girls.
4. Ask how many books there are.

***Answers:***

1. `ٻه ڪتاب ڏيو.` - `Ɓa kitāba ɗiyo.`
2. `ٽي ڇوڪرا آهن.` - `Ṭe chhokrā āhin.`
3. `ٻه ڇوڪريون آهن.` - `Ɓa chhokriyū̃ āhin.`
4. `ڪيترا ڪتاب آهن؟` - `Ketrā kitāba āhin?`

### Why these answers work

The request keeps ڏيو after the quantity phrase. The statements use explicit person-noun plurals and آهن. The count question uses masculine plural ڪيترا with books. The romanisation shows some final-vowel distinctions that ordinary unvocalised script leaves implicit.

### Retrieve and recombine

Prepare two books for three boys and two girls in an imagined class. State the quantities, then request the books. Explain why the noun spelling alone may not reveal every number distinction.


## Lesson 17: Today, yesterday and tomorrow

**Outcome:** Schedule a visit and ask when it happens.

### Think before reading

Can the where question ڪٿي discover the hour of an appointment, or do you need a different information word?

### Build the pattern

ڪڏهن asks when. It differs from ڪٿي, where. A person can know the destination but not the time, so the two question words solve different problems. Read the entire word rather than guessing from its first letter.

اڄ means today, ڪلهه yesterday, and سڀاڻي tomorrow. These distinct day words are useful for avoiding the adjacent-day ambiguity found in some neighbouring languages. هاڻي means now. A time expression gives context but does not replace the correct verb construction.

ٽن وڳي means at three o’clock in the appointment phrase used here. The number takes the form ٽن before وڳي. Do not assume the independent counting form ٽي remains unchanged in every grammatical environment. The hour expression is learned as a unit so that a useful appointment can be made immediately.

سڀاڻي ٽن وڳي اچو asks someone to come tomorrow at three. This is a respectful command scheduled in the future, not a future indicative conjugation. You can therefore arrange a simple visit using existing imperative knowledge. To report an expected arrival to another person, a future statement will be taught separately. Keep direct requests, clock-time expressions and reports distinct; each performs a different job in a meeting exchange.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| سڀاڻي ٽن وڳي اچو. | Subhāṇe ṭin vage acho. | Come tomorrow at three, respectfully. |
| اڄ هتي ويهو. | Aju hite viho. | Sit here today, respectfully. |
| هاڻي اچو. | Hāṇe acho. | Come now, respectfully. |
| توهان ڪڏهن ايندا؟ | Tavhā̃ kadahin eendā? | When will you come, respectfully, masculine reference? |
| ڪلهه. | Kalha. | Yesterday. |
| صبح جو. | Subhu jo. | In the morning. |

### Common trap

Do not replace ٽن with the independent counting form ٽي inside the taught hour phrase. Also, ڪڏهن asks for time and ڪٿي asks for place; a correct place answer can still fail to answer a time question.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask someone to come tomorrow at three.
2. Ask someone to sit here today.
3. Ask someone to come now.
4. Ask a man respectfully when he will come.

***Answers:***

1. `سڀاڻي ٽن وڳي اچو.` - `Subhāṇe ṭin vage acho.`
2. `اڄ هتي ويهو.` - `Aju hite viho.`
3. `هاڻي اچو.` - `Hāṇe acho.`
4. `توهان ڪڏهن ايندا؟` - `Tavhā̃ kadahin eendā?`

### Why these answers work

The first combines tomorrow, a clock-time phrase and a respectful imperative. The second and third change only the time frame of the requested action. The final answer is a future question with respectful masculine plural-form ايندا rather than an imperative.

### Retrieve and recombine

Move a visit from tomorrow to today while preserving its hour. Then ask when and where as separate questions. Explain why a complete appointment requires both kinds of information.


## Lesson 18: Travel and the distinction between location and direction

**Outcome:** Describe going home and ask where someone is heading.

### Think before reading

Does where are you going ask for exactly the same relationship as where are you now?

### Build the pattern

وڃڻ means go and اچڻ means come. In the simple present, هو وڃي ٿو and هو اچي ٿو distinguish the movement viewpoint. Coming normally approaches the speaker, listener or an established centre, while going identifies movement toward another destination.

گهر can appear directly as a home destination: هو گهر وڃي ٿو. A separate to is not required in this ordinary expression. اسڪول also appears as a destination with the movement verb. Do not insert a generic postposition before every place simply because English uses to.

ڪيڏانهن asks in which direction or to where. It differs from ڪٿي, the ordinary location question. تون ڪيڏانهن وڃين ٿو؟ asks a familiar male listener where he is going. The main verb وڃين and auxiliary ٿو preserve familiar singular and masculine information.

بس ۾ can describe travelling in or by bus. The same postposition previously located a person inside a building, but context now makes it part of a transport expression. This does not turn ۾ into a universal equivalent of English by. Learn the concrete travel phrase. Separating current location, destination and means of transport makes a small travel message more informative and prevents several different English where questions from collapsing into one memorised sentence.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| هو گهر وڃي ٿو. | Hū gharu vañe tho. | He goes home. |
| هوءَ اسڪول وڃي ٿي. | Hūa iskūlu vañe thī. | She goes to school. |
| هو هتي اچي ٿو. | Hū hite ache tho. | He comes here. |
| تون ڪيڏانهن وڃين ٿو؟ | Tū̃ keḍā̃h vañī̃ tho? | Where are you going, to a familiar man? |
| هو بس ۾ وڃي ٿو. | Hū bas mẽ vañe tho. | He goes by bus. |
| هو ڪٿي آهي؟ | Hū kithe āhe? | Where is he? |

### Common trap

Do not confuse a destination question with a location question. Also, وڃ in the go verb contains the distinct Sindhi nasal letter ڃ, represented here by ñ; preserve the actual script rather than substituting Urdu جاتا.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say he goes home.
2. Say she goes to school.
3. Say he comes here.
4. Ask a familiar man where he is going, using ڪيڏانهن.

***Answers:***

1. `هو گهر وڃي ٿو.` - `Hū gharu vañe tho.`
2. `هوءَ اسڪول وڃي ٿي.` - `Hūa iskūlu vañe thī.`
3. `هو هتي اچي ٿو.` - `Hū hite ache tho.`
4. `تون ڪيڏانهن وڃين ٿو؟` - `Tū̃ keḍā̃h vañī̃ tho?`

### Why these answers work

The first two give destinations before the motion predicate. The third changes the viewpoint to coming. The final answer uses the directional question word and familiar masculine predicate وڃين ٿو. Gender remains controlled by the actor in these present examples.

### Retrieve and recombine

Locate a friend in a shop, then describe the friend’s trip home by bus. Ask a separate destination question. Keep state, direction and transport as distinct parts of the scene.


## Lesson 19: Past states and completed movement

**Outcome:** Say where someone was and report a completed trip.

### Think before reading

If a person was at home and then went away, should both facts use the same past predicate?

### Build the pattern

Past location uses a past copula. هو گهر ۾ هو means he was in the house, while هوءَ گهر ۾ هئي means she was in the house. The first sentence contains هو twice with different jobs: the first is the pronoun, and the last is a past be form. Position and context distinguish them.

Completed going uses ويو for a masculine singular person and وئي for a feminine singular person. هو اسڪول ويو means he went to school. هوءَ اسڪول وئي gives the feminine counterpart. These are not formed by appending an English-like past suffix to the complete present وڃي ٿو.

Completed coming has آيو and آئي in third-person singular examples. The person can have arrived and later departed, so a past arrival does not itself guarantee present location. Tell the current location separately if it matters to the listener.

The lesson deliberately starts with third-person intransitive motion and simple past states. First-person past forms and completed transitive agreement have additional details. Avoid extending one he went form into a universal Sindhi past rule. You already have enough to give a useful update about a visitor’s availability and travel, provided you distinguish the past state from the completed event and preserve the masculine or feminine form required by the chosen subject.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| هو گهر ۾ هو. | Hū gharu mẽ ho. | He was in the house. |
| هوءَ گهر ۾ هئي. | Hūa gharu mẽ huī. | She was in the house. |
| هو اسڪول ويو. | Hū iskūlu viyo. | He went to school. |
| هوءَ اسڪول وئي. | Hūa iskūlu vaī. | She went to school. |
| هو هتي آيو. | Hū hite āyo. | He came here. |
| هوءَ هتي آئي. | Hūa hite āī. | She came here. |

### Common trap

The repeated هو in a past-location sentence is not an accidental duplicate. One is a pronoun and one a predicate. Also, a completed motion form is not interchangeable with a past copula merely because both translate an English past tense.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say he was in the house.
2. Say she was in the house.
3. Say he went to school.
4. Say she went to school.

***Answers:***

1. `هو گهر ۾ هو.` - `Hū gharu mẽ ho.`
2. `هوءَ گهر ۾ هئي.` - `Hūa gharu mẽ huī.`
3. `هو اسڪول ويو.` - `Hū iskūlu viyo.`
4. `هوءَ اسڪول وئي.` - `Hūa iskūlu vaī.`

### Why these answers work

The first pair uses past copulas to locate people. The second pair reports completed motion with ويو and وئي. The place expression stays the same within each pair, while the subject’s gender changes the predicate.

### Retrieve and recombine

Tell a two-event story about a woman who was in the house and came here. Then retell it about a man. Identify the two changed predicates rather than replacing every word.


## Lesson 20: Completed actions with an object

**Outcome:** Change the agent form and follow the completed object’s gender.

### Think before reading

If a woman drank water, would the completed verb necessarily be feminine because the drinker was female?

### Build the pattern

Completed transitive events differ from the intransitive movement pattern. مون ڪتاب پڙهيو means I read a book. The agent is مون rather than ordinary مان. The verb پڙهيو agrees with masculine ڪتاب in the unmarked-object construction taught here.

مون ماني کاڌي uses feminine کاڌي with ماني, while مون انب کاڌو uses masculine کاڌو with انب. The reader or eater’s gender is not the controller of these completed verb forms. The object determines the visible agreement in these simple examples.

پاڻي is masculine in Sindhi, despite being neuter in some other Indo-Aryan languages. مون پاڻي پيتو therefore uses masculine پيتو. This is an especially useful warning against transferring noun gender from a neighbouring language. Shared vocabulary does not guarantee shared grammatical class.

The course keeps first-person agents and unmarked singular objects so that the central contrast remains clear. Sindhi also has pronominal suffixes and more complex perfective patterns; this small lesson does not claim to exhaust them. Compare a present reading sentence with its completed counterpart. Both describe reading a book, but the agent form and predicate change because the event is presented differently. Learn that whole constructional shift rather than a single supposed past-tense ending.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| مون ڪتاب پڙهيو. | Mū̃ kitābu paṛhiyo. | I read a book as a completed event. |
| مون ماني کاڌي. | Mū̃ mānī khādhī. | I ate bread. |
| مون انب کاڌو. | Mū̃ ambu khādho. | I ate a mango. |
| مون پاڻي پيتو. | Mū̃ pāṇī pīto. | I drank water. |
| مون خط لکيو. | Mū̃ khatu likhiyo. | I wrote a letter. |
| مان ڪتاب پڙهان ٿو. | Mā̃ kitābu paṛhā̃ tho. | I read a book in the present, male speaker. |

### Common trap

Do not choose feminine پيتو-like alternatives from the drinker’s gender. Water is masculine in Sindhi and controls this completed verb. Also, ordinary مان is not retained as the agent in the completed transitive pattern shown here.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you read a book as a completed event.
2. Say you ate bread.
3. Say you ate a mango.
4. Say you drank water.

***Answers:***

1. `مون ڪتاب پڙهيو.` - `Mū̃ kitābu paṛhiyo.`
2. `مون ماني کاڌي.` - `Mū̃ mānī khādhī.`
3. `مون انب کاڌو.` - `Mū̃ ambu khādho.`
4. `مون پاڻي پيتو.` - `Mū̃ pāṇī pīto.`

### Why these answers work

All answers use agent مون. Book, mango and water are masculine here and take masculine completed forms; feminine bread takes کاڌي. These forms are the same for male and female first-person agents in the particular unmarked-object construction practised.

### Retrieve and recombine

Retell your present reading as a completed event, then compare eating bread and a mango. Explain why the object change matters while changing the speaker’s gender need not change these completed forms.


## Lesson 21: Future coming and the person’s ending

**Outcome:** Report a future visit and recognise masculine and feminine first-person forms.

### Think before reading

If I will come contains a person suffix, can you form it just by putting I before he will come?

### Build the pattern

A simple future can stand without a present copula. هو سڀاڻي ايندو means he will come tomorrow. هوءَ سڀاڻي ايندي is the feminine singular counterpart. The final future predicate already supplies the required finite structure.

First-person masculine is مان ايندس, represented as mā̃ eendus in the reading aid. First-person feminine can be written with vowel marks as مان ايندِيَسِ, making eendiyas visible. These short vowels are important because ordinary unvocalised writing can hide a distinction that pronunciation preserves. The practice retains helpful marks rather than stripping them.

Do not confuse the future هو ايندو with the habitual هو ايندو آهي from Lesson 15. The presence of the copula changes the construction and interpretation. Read the whole predicate before assigning an English tense.

A future statement may express a plan, expectation or promise according to context. Tomorrow makes the intended schedule explicit, but it does not determine the social strength of the commitment. If speaking as yourself, choose the appropriate first-person form; if reporting another person’s visit, choose the third-person form. The examples keep coming as the single action so that person, gender and the habitual-future contrast can be understood without adding several irregular stems at once.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| هو سڀاڻي ايندو. | Hū subhāṇe eendo. | He will come tomorrow. |
| هوءَ سڀاڻي ايندي. | Hūa subhāṇe eendī. | She will come tomorrow. |
| مان سڀاڻي ايندس. | Mā̃ subhāṇe eendus. | I will come tomorrow, male speaker. |
| مان سڀاڻي ايندِيَسِ. | Mā̃ subhāṇe eendiyas. | I will come tomorrow, female speaker. |
| هو روز ايندو آهي. | Hū roz eendo āhe. | He comes every day habitually. |
| توهان سڀاڻي ايندا. | Tavhā̃ subhāṇe eendā. | You will come tomorrow, respectful masculine reference. |

### Common trap

Do not append آهي to a future and assume the meaning is unchanged; ايندو آهي is a habitual construction. Also, preserve useful vowel marks in the feminine first-person future when they clarify a real pronunciation distinction.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say he will come tomorrow.
2. Say she will come tomorrow.
3. Say you will come tomorrow, male speaker.
4. Say you will come tomorrow, female speaker.

***Answers:***

1. `هو سڀاڻي ايندو.` - `Hū subhāṇe eendo.`
2. `هوءَ سڀاڻي ايندي.` - `Hūa subhāṇe eendī.`

4. `مان سڀاڻي ايندِيَسِ.` - `Mā̃ subhāṇe eendiyas.`

### Why these answers work

The first pair contrasts ايندو and ايندي. The first-person pair adds different person-and-gender endings, made explicit in the marked feminine form. No present copula follows any of the four future predicates.

### Retrieve and recombine

Compare he comes every day with he will come tomorrow. Then speak the future as a male and female first-person speaker, keeping the person-specific ending audible.


## Lesson 22: Ability with سگهي

**Outcome:** Say someone can read or come without making a future promise.

### Think before reading

Does a statement that someone will read tomorrow establish their ability to read Sindhi?

### Build the pattern

سگهڻ supplies a useful ability construction. مان پڙهي سگهان ٿو means I can read for a male speaker; مان پڙهي سگهان ٿي gives the female-speaker version. The main action appears in a linking form, and سگهان carries first-person information before the gendered auxiliary.

Third-person هو پڙهي سگهي ٿو uses سگهي with ٿو. A feminine subject uses ٿي. The construction therefore repeats a familiar principle: person information and gender information can be distributed across different words in the predicate.

سنڌي is the language name. مان سنڌي پڙهي سگهان ٿو states an ability to read Sindhi, not necessarily to speak it or understand rapid conversation. A precise skill statement should name the actual activity rather than overclaiming general proficiency.

Ability and intention are different. A person may be able to come but have no plan to attend, or intend to attend while facing an obstacle. The simple future from the previous lesson does not replace the can construction. Ask whether a person can perform the action when possibility is the relevant issue, and use a future statement when reporting the planned event. Keeping these meanings separate makes an appointment or learning conversation clearer.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| مان سنڌي پڙهي سگهان ٿو. | Mā̃ sindhī paṛhī saghā̃ tho. | I can read Sindhi, male speaker. |
| مان سنڌي پڙهي سگهان ٿي. | Mā̃ sindhī paṛhī saghā̃ thī. | I can read Sindhi, female speaker. |
| هو اچي سگهي ٿو. | Hū ache saghe tho. | He can come. |
| هوءَ اچي سگهي ٿي. | Hūa ache saghe thī. | She can come. |
| ڇا هو پڙهي سگهي ٿو؟ | Chhā hū paṛhī saghe tho? | Can he read? |
| ڇا هوءَ اچي سگهي ٿي؟ | Chhā hūa ache saghe thī? | Can she come? |

### Common trap

Do not replace can with a future ending merely because both concern a possible later action. Also, reading Sindhi is one specific skill; the sentence does not claim fluency across every speaking and listening situation.

### Why these answers work

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask whether a woman can come, then report separately that she will come tomorrow. Explain why the two statements are related but do not make identical claims.


## Lesson 23: Joining ideas and giving a reason

**Outcome:** Connect two statements with and, but or because.

### Think before reading

When two clauses have different subjects, does adding and remove their separate agreement requirements?

### Build the pattern

۽ means and, and پر means but. هو پڙهي ٿو ۽ هوءَ لکي ٿي joins two activities while preserving each subject’s gendered predicate. The conjunction links clauses but does not make them one new plural subject automatically.

پر marks a contrast. هو هتي آهي پر هوءَ اتي آهي contrasts two people’s locations. Both facts may be true, and but tells the listener what relationship to notice between them. The connector contributes meaning beyond simply separating two strings of words.

ڇاڪاڻ ته introduces a reason clause, because. مان گهر ۾ آهيان ڇاڪاڻ ته مان پڙهي رهيو آهيان explains why a male speaker is in the house: he is reading. The reason keeps its normal Sindhi order and first-person agreement. There is no English-style reversal inside the clause.

يا means or and can offer a choice between items such as پاڻي يا کير؟, water or milk? A choice question is not a yes-or-no question about one complete proposition. The listener must select an alternative, so a bare yes may not settle the offer. Build each fact or noun phrase accurately, then choose the connector that expresses addition, contrast, reason or choice. Longer language becomes manageable when its internal relationships remain visible.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| هو پڙهي ٿو ۽ هوءَ لکي ٿي. | Hū paṛhe tho ain hūa likhe thī. | He reads and she writes. |
| هو هتي آهي پر هوءَ اتي آهي. | Hū hite āhe par hūa ute āhe. | He is here but she is there. |
| مان گهر ۾ آهيان ڇاڪاڻ ته مان پڙهي رهيو آهيان. | Mā̃ gharu mẽ āhiyā̃ chhākāṇ ta mā̃ paṛhī rahiyo āhiyā̃. | I am in the house because I am reading, male speaker. |
| پاڻي يا کير؟ | Pāṇī yā khīru? | Water or milk? |
| ڪتاب ۽ خط. | Kitābu ain khatu. | A book and a letter. |
| اڄ يا سڀاڻي؟ | Aju yā subhāṇe? | Today or tomorrow? |

### Common trap

Do not let a conjunction erase gender agreement within its clauses. Also, a choice question and a yes-or-no question request different responses. A connector is part of the message, not an interchangeable decorative word.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say he reads and she writes.
2. Say he is here but she is there.
3. Say you are in the house because you are reading, male speaker.
4. Offer the choice water or milk.

***Answers:***

1. `هو پڙهي ٿو ۽ هوءَ لکي ٿي.` - `Hū paṛhe tho ain hūa likhe thī.`
2. `هو هتي آهي پر هوءَ اتي آهي.` - `Hū hite āhe par hūa ute āhe.`
3. `مان گهر ۾ آهيان ڇاڪاڻ ته مان پڙهي رهيو آهيان.` - `Mā̃ gharu mẽ āhiyā̃ chhākāṇ ta mā̃ paṛhī rahiyo āhiyā̃.`
4. `پاڻي يا کير؟` - `Pāṇī yā khīru?`

### Why these answers work

۽ adds activities, پر contrasts locations, and ڇاڪاڻ ته introduces the reason for being in the house. The final short question uses يا between two drink alternatives. Each full clause keeps its own predicate and agreement.

### Retrieve and recombine

Connect a location and an activity with a reason, then offer two drinks as a choice. Explain how the selected connector changes the listener’s task even when the vocabulary is familiar.


## Lesson 24: A purchase and a request for clarification

**Outcome:** Check stock, specify a quantity and ask for repetition.

### Think before reading

If a seller has books, does that alone specify how many you want or what action they should take?

### Build the pattern

A practical purchase can begin with availability: ڇا توهان وٽ ڪتاب آهن؟ asks whether the seller has books. The plural copula آهن matters even though ڪتاب can look unchanged in ordinary spelling. The question is about stock, not the seller’s personal desire to read.

A quantity request follows with مون کي ٻه ڪتاب ڏيو, give me two books. The recipient مون کي identifies the buyer, the number identifies the count, and ڏيو asks the seller to act. These components can be changed independently when the item or customer changes.

If you miss the reply, ٻيهر چئو asks the speaker to say it again. آهستي ڳالهايو requests slower speech. The first concerns repeated content; the second concerns pace. Both are useful even if you understand most of the exchange.

Do not treat a memorised question as proof that you can understand every possible answer. Price, stock and quantity are separate details, and unfamiliar number words may require clarification. The course’s small number set is a foundation for a clear limited exchange, not a substitute for all commercial vocabulary. Keep the transaction understandable by asking for the missing information and confirming the intended quantity. These patterns transfer to borrowing books in a classroom as well as buying them in a shop.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ڇا توهان وٽ ڪتاب آهن؟ | Chhā tavhā̃ vaṭ kitāba āhin? | Do you have books, respectfully? |
| مون کي ٻه ڪتاب ڏيو. | Mū̃ khe ɓa kitāba ɗiyo. | Give me two books, respectfully. |
| ٻيهر چئو. | Ɓīhar chao. | Say it again, respectfully. |
| آهستي ڳالهايو. | Āhiste ɠālhāyo. | Speak slowly, respectfully. |
| مون کي پاڻي گهرجي. | Mū̃ khe pāṇī ghurje. | I need water. |
| مهرباني. | Miharbānī. | Thank you, a brief courteous expression. |

### Common trap

Do not confuse availability with a purchase instruction. Also, the plural آهن is not optional merely because the noun’s unvocalised spelling resembles its singular. The rest of the sentence must preserve the intended count.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Ask a seller respectfully whether they have books.
2. Ask an adult to give you two books.
3. Ask someone to say it again.
4. Ask someone to speak slowly.

***Answers:***

1. `ڇا توهان وٽ ڪتاب آهن؟` - `Chhā tavhā̃ vaṭ kitāba āhin?`
2. `مون کي ٻه ڪتاب ڏيو.` - `Mū̃ khe ɓa kitāba ɗiyo.`
3. `ٻيهر چئو.` - `Ɓīhar chao.`
4. `آهستي ڳالهايو.` - `Āhiste ɠālhāyo.`

### Why these answers work

The first uses وٽ for stock and آهن for plural books. The second changes from checking availability to requesting a transfer with کي and ڏيو. The final two are separate repair requests for repetition and slower speech.

### Retrieve and recombine

Role-play a bookshop exchange with a stock question, a request for two books and one clarification. Then transfer the same language to borrowing books from a teacher.


## Lesson 25: Cumulative review through a visit

**Outcome:** Recombine identity, need, completed action and a future plan.

### Think before reading

Does English I always choose مان when the sentence reports reading a completed book or needing water?

### Build the pattern

A coherent account needs several Sindhi constructions rather than one universal sentence template. Ordinary مان introduces a present subject. مون appears as the agent in the completed transitive examples. مون کي introduces a need, and مون وٽ expresses availability. Their English translations may all begin with I, but their Sindhi relationships differ.

The central review moves from a current location to a completed book event, a need for water and a future visit. The book event uses masculine پڙهيو because ڪتاب is masculine in the unmarked-object pattern. The need uses گهرجي with مون کي. The future visitor uses ايندي for a woman and does not add the habitual copula.

Before producing a sentence, identify its task: locate someone, describe an activity, report completion, state a need, ask a question or request an action. Then select the corresponding person form and predicate. Shared Perso-Arabic letters do not make Sindhi grammar identical to Urdu, and shared Indo-Aryan vocabulary does not guarantee the same noun gender as Marathi or Gujarati.

Continue with audio and fluent-speaker review of the standard and register you intend to use. Sindhi’s implosive contrasts, short final vowels and connected speech need listening beyond a romanised aid. The Perso-Arabic course also does not replace the separate Devanagari tradition used by many Sindhi readers. A useful foundation lets you recombine known patterns, preserve meaningful script distinctions and ask for clarification. It does not certify fluency or remove the need for further feedback.

### Worked language

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| مان هتي آهيان. | Mā̃ hite āhiyā̃. | I am here. |
| مون ڪتاب پڙهيو. | Mū̃ kitābu paṛhiyo. | I read a book as a completed event. |
| مون کي پاڻي گهرجي. | Mū̃ khe pāṇī ghurje. | I need water. |
| هوءَ سڀاڻي ايندي. | Hūa subhāṇe eendī. | She will come tomorrow. |
| مون وٽ ٻه ڪتاب آهن. | Mū̃ vaṭ ɓa kitāba āhin. | I have two books. |
| مهرباني ڪري ٻيهر چئو. | Miharbānī kare ɓīhar chao. | Please say it again. |

### Common trap

Do not collapse مان, مون, مون کي and مون وٽ into one first-person expression. Also, the final future statement must not acquire a present copula accidentally, because that would change the construction you intended.

### Practice

Build one complete sentence for each situation.

1. Say you are here.
2. Say you read a book as a completed event.
3. Say you need water.
4. Say she will come tomorrow.

***Answers:***

1. `مان هتي آهيان.` - `Mā̃ hite āhiyā̃.`
2. `مون ڪتاب پڙهيو.` - `Mū̃ kitābu paṛhiyo.`
3. `مون کي پاڻي گهرجي.` - `Mū̃ khe pāṇī ghurje.`
4. `هوءَ سڀاڻي ايندي.` - `Hūa subhāṇe eendī.`

### Why these answers work

The first uses ordinary مان and a present copula. Completed reading uses مون with object-controlled پڙهيو. The need uses مون کي. The woman’s future visit uses ايندي as a complete future predicate with an explicit tomorrow expression.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create six short messages about a visit: identity, location, completed event, need, future plan and a repair request. Change one object and one participant, then inspect the forms affected by each change separately.
