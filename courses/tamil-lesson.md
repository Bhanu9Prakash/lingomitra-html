# Tamil: A pattern-first foundation

This foundation uses Tamil script and modern standard written Tamil, suitable for reading and careful formal speech. It does not present literary forms as the only natural everyday conversation. Spoken Tamil varies by region and community and often changes pronouns, case endings and verb shapes; selected contrasts are labelled explicitly. Romanisation is spelling-oriented: ā ī ū ē ō are long vowels; ṭ ṇ ḷ are retroflex, ṟ differs from r, and ḻ represents ழ. Double consonants are held longer. Written k, t and p can have softer pronunciations between vowels. Romanisation is a reading aid, not a phonetic transcription of one spoken dialect.

## Lesson 1: Names and objects without an obligatory is

### Build the pattern

You will identify yourself and a nearby object. Tamil can join a person or demonstrative directly to a name or noun in a present identity sentence. English am and is have no obligatory separate counterpart here. நான் nāṉ means I, இது itu means this, and அது atu means that. Tamil does not require an indefinite article corresponding to every English a. A noun such as புத்தகம் puttakam, book, can identify an object directly. Pronounce doubled tt as a longer closure, not as an optional spelling decoration. The final m belongs to the noun and should remain audible in careful reading.

### Think before reading on

If nāṉ is I and Ravi is a name, must a third word be inserted to express I am Ravi?

No. The name itself completes the identifying statement. This is a specific present identity pattern; later, locating someone requires a form of இரு iru, be or remain. Do not generalise the absence of a copula to every English sentence containing is.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| நான் ரவி. | Nāṉ Ravi. | I am Ravi. |
| நான் மீனா. | Nāṉ Mīṉā. | I am Meena. |
| இது புத்தகம். | Itu puttakam. | This is a book. |
| அது வீடு. | Atu vīṭu. | That is a house. |
| இது தண்ணீர். | Itu taṇṇīr. | This is water. |
| அது பள்ளி. | Atu paḷḷi. | That is a school. |

### Worked examples

`நான் ரவி.` - `Nāṉ Ravi.` means “I am Ravi.” The speaker identifies themselves directly with a name, without selecting a masculine or feminine first-person ending.

`நான் மீனா.` - `Nāṉ Mīṉā.` means “I am Meena.” A different name does not change the grammatical frame; first-person identification is not gendered in this construction.

`இது புத்தகம்.` - `Itu puttakam.` means “This is a book.” The nearby demonstrative introduces the object, and the category noun provides the identification without an article.

### Common trap

Do not insert irukkiṟēṉ after every personal name. Identity and location have different structures, even though English uses am in both kinds of sentence.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Tamil. Keep the stated person and register.

1. I am Ravi.
2. I am Meena.
3. This is a book.
4. That is a house.
5. This is water.
6. That is a school.

***Answers:***

1. `நான் ரவி.` - `Nāṉ Ravi.`
2. `நான் மீனா.` - `Nāṉ Mīṉā.`
3. `இது புத்தகம்.` - `Itu puttakam.`
4. `அது வீடு.` - `Atu vīṭu.`
5. `இது தண்ணீர்.` - `Itu taṇṇīr.`
6. `அது பள்ளி.` - `Atu paḷḷi.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The distant demonstrative changes the pointing relationship, while the long vowel in vīṭu distinguishes its pronunciation.

Answer 5: The doubled retroflex nasal and long vowel belong to the water word, which does not require an article.

Answer 6: The doubled retroflex lateral in paḷḷi should be heard as a sustained consonant, not an English l.

### Retrieve and recombine

Point to a book, then a house farther away. Introduce yourself between the two descriptions, noticing that the same simple frame works for names and object categories.


## Lesson 2: What, who and yes-or-no questions

### Build the pattern

You will ask for an identification or check a proposed answer. என்ன eṉṉa means what, and யார் yār means who. They normally occupy the position of the missing information. A yes-or-no question can attach -ஆ ā to the final word: புத்தகமா puttakamā asks whether the identification as a book is correct. The intervening m reflects the noun's shape. ஆம் ām is a standard yes; இல்லை illai can reject a statement, with more specific negative patterns introduced later. Tamil does not create questions by inserting English do or by reversing subject and verb.

### Think before reading on

How does asking இது என்ன itu eṉṉa differ from asking இது புத்தகமா itu puttakamā?

The first leaves the object's identity open. The second offers book as a candidate and asks for confirmation. The question word and the interrogative ending therefore ask different kinds of questions, even though both concern the same object.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| இது என்ன? | Itu eṉṉa? | What is this? |
| அது என்ன? | Atu eṉṉa? | What is that? |
| இது புத்தகமா? | Itu puttakamā? | Is this a book? |
| அது வீடா? | Atu vīṭā? | Is that a house? |
| அவர் யார்? | Avar yār? | Who is that person? (respectful) |
| ஆம். | Ām. | Yes. |

### Worked examples

`இது என்ன?` - `Itu eṉṉa?` means “What is this?” The question word replaces the unknown category without moving the demonstrative from the beginning of the sentence.

`அது என்ன?` - `Atu eṉṉa?` means “What is that?” Changing the pointing distance leaves the open identification question otherwise unchanged and introduces no additional verb.

`இது புத்தகமா?` - `Itu puttakamā?` means “Is this a book?” The final interrogative vowel makes a proposed identification into a question requiring confirmation or correction.

### Common trap

A question mark alone does not explain the grammatical form. Say the final long ā in a yes-or-no question; do not treat it as silent punctuation.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Tamil. Keep the stated person and register.

1. What is this?
2. What is that?
3. Is this a book?
4. Is that a house?
5. Who is that person? (respectful)
6. Yes.

***Answers:***

1. `இது என்ன?` - `Itu eṉṉa?`
2. `அது என்ன?` - `Atu eṉṉa?`
3. `இது புத்தகமா?` - `Itu puttakamā?`
4. `அது வீடா?` - `Atu vīṭā?`
5. `அவர் யார்?` - `Avar yār?`
6. `ஆம்.` - `Ām.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The final u of vīṭu gives way in this question form, so spelling changes accompany the suffix.

Answer 5: Avar refers respectfully to a person, while yār requests the identity rather than an object category.

Answer 6: This short standard response confirms a proposition, and a fuller conversation may repeat the confirmed information afterward.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine an unknown object and a visitor. Ask what the object is and who the visitor is, then check whether the object is a book.


## Lesson 3: Respectful address and personal names

### Build the pattern

You will ask a name respectfully and recognise familiar singular address. நீங்கள் nīṅkaḷ is plural you and respectful singular you. நீ nī is familiar singular. Their possessives are உங்கள் uṅkaḷ and உன் uṉ; என் eṉ means my. Tamil uses உங்கள் பெயர் என்ன uṅkaḷ peyar eṉṉa, your name what, as a straightforward name question. அவர் avar is respectful third-person singular reference, distinct from அவன் avaṉ, he, and அவள் avaḷ, she. Choosing an honorific pronoun is a social decision and later affects finite verb agreement. Spoken forms may shorten nīṅkaḷ to nīṅka, but practice keeps the written standard.

### Think before reading on

Should a question meaning your name use the subject pronoun nīṅkaḷ directly before peyar?

Use the possessive uṅkaḷ instead. Tamil distinguishes the person as subject from the person as owner, just as English distinguishes I and my. The respectful relationship survives the change in grammatical role.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| உங்கள் பெயர் என்ன? | Uṅkaḷ peyar eṉṉa? | What is your name? (respectful) |
| என் பெயர் ரவி. | Eṉ peyar Ravi. | My name is Ravi. |
| நீங்கள் மீனாவா? | Nīṅkaḷ Mīṉāvā? | Are you Meena? (respectful) |
| உன் பெயர் என்ன? | Uṉ peyar eṉṉa? | What is your name? (familiar) |
| இது என் புத்தகம். | Itu eṉ puttakam. | This is my book. |
| இது உங்கள் வீடா? | Itu uṅkaḷ vīṭā? | Is this your house? (respectful) |

### Worked examples

`உங்கள் பெயர் என்ன?` - `Uṅkaḷ peyar eṉṉa?` means “What is your name? (respectful)” The possessive modifies peyar, while the final question word asks for the missing name without a copula.

`என் பெயர் ரவி.` - `Eṉ peyar Ravi.` means “My name is Ravi.” The short possessive eṉ forms a name phrase that can be identified directly with the speaker's name.

`நீங்கள் மீனாவா?` - `Nīṅkaḷ Mīṉāvā?` means “Are you Meena? (respectful)” The question ending joins the vowel-final name through v, while the pronoun maintains respectful address.

### Common trap

Do not assume that singular English you requires familiar nī. A single stranger can be addressed with nīṅkaḷ, and the matching verb ending will remain respectful.

### Practice

Build these meanings in Tamil. Keep the stated person and register.

1. What is your name? (respectful)
2. My name is Ravi.
3. Are you Meena? (respectful)
4. What is your name? (familiar)
5. This is my book.
6. Is this your house? (respectful)

***Answers:***

1. `உங்கள் பெயர் என்ன?` - `Uṅkaḷ peyar eṉṉa?`
2. `என் பெயர் ரவி.` - `Eṉ peyar Ravi.`
3. `நீங்கள் மீனாவா?` - `Nīṅkaḷ Mīṉāvā?`
4. `உன் பெயர் என்ன?` - `Uṉ peyar eṉṉa?`
5. `இது என் புத்தகம்.` - `Itu eṉ puttakam.`
6. `இது உங்கள் வீடா?` - `Itu uṅkaḷ vīṭā?`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The familiar possessive changes the social relationship, while the position of the name and question word remains stable.

Answer 5: The owner appears before the noun, and the complete owned-object phrase identifies the nearby demonstrative.

Answer 6: The question ending belongs to the house noun, while uṅkaḷ can refer to one respected owner or several owners.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask a teacher's name, give your own, and identify your book. Repeat only the name question to a close friend using the explicitly familiar form.


## Lesson 4: Being here and there

### Build the pattern

You will locate people and objects with forms of இரு iru. நான் uses இருக்கிறேன் irukkiṟēṉ, அவன் uses இருக்கிறான் irukkiṟāṉ, அவள் uses இருக்கிறாள் irukkiṟāḷ, and respectful நீங்கள் uses இருக்கிறீர்கள் irukkiṟīrkaḷ. A nonhuman singular uses இருக்கிறது irukkiṟatu. The place words இங்கே iṅkē, here, அங்கே aṅkē, there, and எங்கே eṅkē, where, appear before the finite verb. These are standard written forms; everyday spoken equivalents are often shorter. The grammatical distinction between identity and location remains useful across varieties even where the surface verb sounds different.

### Think before reading on

Will replacing a man with a woman in a location statement leave irukkiṟāṉ unchanged?

No. Ordinary singular reference distinguishes masculine -āṉ and feminine -āḷ. Respectful avar uses an honorific form instead. Decide which person and level of reference the sentence uses before selecting the ending.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| நான் இங்கே இருக்கிறேன். | Nāṉ iṅkē irukkiṟēṉ. | I am here. |
| அவன் அங்கே இருக்கிறான். | Avaṉ aṅkē irukkiṟāṉ. | He is there. |
| அவள் இங்கே இருக்கிறாள். | Avaḷ iṅkē irukkiṟāḷ. | She is here. |
| புத்தகம் அங்கே இருக்கிறது. | Puttakam aṅkē irukkiṟatu. | The book is there. |
| நீங்கள் எங்கே இருக்கிறீர்கள்? | Nīṅkaḷ eṅkē irukkiṟīrkaḷ? | Where are you? (respectful) |
| அவர் இங்கே இருக்கிறார். | Avar iṅkē irukkiṟār. | That person is here. (respectful) |

### Worked examples

`நான் இங்கே இருக்கிறேன்.` - `Nāṉ iṅkē irukkiṟēṉ.` means “I am here.” The first-person ending identifies the speaker, while the preceding place word supplies the location being reported.

`அவன் அங்கே இருக்கிறான்.` - `Avaṉ aṅkē irukkiṟāṉ.` means “He is there.” The masculine singular ending belongs to the person located, not to the distant place expression.

`அவள் இங்கே இருக்கிறாள்.` - `Avaḷ iṅkē irukkiṟāḷ.` means “She is here.” The final retroflex lateral distinguishes ordinary feminine singular agreement from the masculine ending of the preceding example.

### Common trap

Do not use irukkiṟatu for all English is. It is the nonhuman singular form here, not an invariant copula that works equally with a person and a book.

***Answers:***

1. `நான் இங்கே இருக்கிறேன்.` - `Nāṉ iṅkē irukkiṟēṉ.`
2. `அவன் அங்கே இருக்கிறான்.` - `Avaṉ aṅkē irukkiṟāṉ.`
3. `அவள் இங்கே இருக்கிறாள்.` - `Avaḷ iṅkē irukkiṟāḷ.`

5. `நீங்கள் எங்கே இருக்கிறீர்கள்?` - `Nīṅkaḷ eṅkē irukkiṟīrkaḷ?`
6. `அவர் இங்கே இருக்கிறார்.` - `Avar iṅkē irukkiṟār.`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The nonhuman singular noun selects irukkiṟatu; English article choice is supplied by the surrounding context.

Answer 5: The question asks for a place while preserving respectful second-person agreement at the end of the sentence.

Answer 6: The honorific third-person ending differs from respectful second-person agreement even though both show respect toward a person.

## Lesson 5: Negating identity and existence

### Build the pattern

You will reject an identification and report absence. இல்லை illai is a common negative existential: இங்கே தண்ணீர் இல்லை says there is no water here. Written Tamil also distinguishes அல்ல alla for negative classification, especially in careful formal usage. Thus இது புத்தகம் அல்ல explicitly says this is not a book. Spoken Tamil often uses illai more broadly, and its pronunciation is frequently shortened; that variation should not be mistaken for a requirement to erase the written contrast. The negative existential in the simple examples does not vary across first-person, masculine and feminine subjects the way the positive present verb does.

### Think before reading on

Does this is not a book say that no object is present, or that the visible object's category is different?

It denies the category, so alla is a clear written-standard choice. There is no water denies presence and uses illai. Thinking about the positive claim being denied helps choose a negative construction rather than translating not alone.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| இது புத்தகம் அல்ல. | Itu puttakam alla. | This is not a book. |
| அது வீடு அல்ல. | Atu vīṭu alla. | That is not a house. |
| இங்கே தண்ணீர் இல்லை. | Iṅkē taṇṇīr illai. | There is no water here. |
| நான் அங்கே இல்லை. | Nāṉ aṅkē illai. | I am not there. |
| அவள் இங்கே இல்லை. | Avaḷ iṅkē illai. | She is not here. |
| அவர் அங்கே இல்லை. | Avar aṅkē illai. | That person is not there. (respectful) |

### Worked examples

`இது புத்தகம் அல்ல.` - `Itu puttakam alla.` means “This is not a book.” The formal classification negative rejects the noun category without claiming that the pointed-to object does not exist.

`அது வீடு அல்ல.` - `Atu vīṭu alla.` means “That is not a house.” The distant referent remains present in the conversation; only the proposed identification as a house is denied.

`இங்கே தண்ணீர் இல்லை.` - `Iṅkē taṇṇīr illai.` means “There is no water here.” Illai reports absence within the stated location and replaces a positive existential form rather than accompanying it.

### Common trap

Do not preserve a positive irukkiṟ- verb and simply append illai in this elementary absence pattern. The negative expression itself completes the clause.

***Answers:***

1. `இது புத்தகம் அல்ல.` - `Itu puttakam alla.`
2. `அது வீடு அல்ல.` - `Atu vīṭu alla.`
3. `இங்கே தண்ணீர் இல்லை.` - `Iṅkē taṇṇīr illai.`
4. `நான் அங்கே இல்லை.` - `Nāṉ aṅkē illai.`
5. `அவள் இங்கே இல்லை.` - `Avaḷ iṅkē illai.`

Answer 5: The feminine pronoun identifies the absent person, while the negative itself does not acquire the positive feminine ending.

Answer 6: Respect remains visible in the pronoun even though the invariant negative does not reproduce the honorific positive verb ending.

### Retrieve and recombine

## Lesson 6: Present actions and their endings

### Build the pattern

You will describe reading, drinking and speaking. Standard written Tamil present forms contain a marker such as -கிற் kiṟ or -க்கிற் kkiṟ plus person endings. படிக்கிறேன் paṭikkiṟēṉ means I read or am reading, while பேசுகிறேன் pēcukiṟēṉ means I speak or am speaking. Verb classes determine which stem and marker combination appears; do not insert kkiṟ everywhere. The ordinary neutral order places an object before the final verb. Present forms can describe an ongoing action or a regular activity, with context helping. இப்போது ippōtu, now, clarifies an activity taking place at the current time.

### Think before reading on

If a sentence changes from I drink water to I drink tea, must the ending -ēṉ change?

No. The speaker stays first-person, so the personal ending stays. Changing the drink is an object substitution. Changing the subject to she would require a different ending, showing why vocabulary and agreement should be considered separately.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| நான் புத்தகம் படிக்கிறேன். | Nāṉ puttakam paṭikkiṟēṉ. | I am reading a book. |
| நான் தண்ணீர் குடிக்கிறேன். | Nāṉ taṇṇīr kuṭikkiṟēṉ. | I am drinking water. |
| அவள் தேநீர் குடிக்கிறாள். | Avaḷ tēnīr kuṭikkiṟāḷ. | She is drinking tea. |
| அவன் புத்தகம் படிக்கிறான். | Avaṉ puttakam paṭikkiṟāṉ. | He is reading a book. |
| நான் தமிழ் பேசுகிறேன். | Nāṉ tamiḻ pēcukiṟēṉ. | I speak Tamil. |
| நீங்கள் என்ன செய்கிறீர்கள்? | Nīṅkaḷ eṉṉa ceykiṟīrkaḷ? | What are you doing? (respectful) |

### Worked examples

`நான் புத்தகம் படிக்கிறேன்.` - `Nāṉ puttakam paṭikkiṟēṉ.` means “I am reading a book.” The unmarked object names a reading activity, while the finite verb carries present meaning and first-person agreement.

`நான் தண்ணீர் குடிக்கிறேன்.` - `Nāṉ taṇṇīr kuṭikkiṟēṉ.` means “I am drinking water.” The substance object precedes the drinking verb, and the first-person ending is independent of the speaker's gender.

`அவள் தேநீர் குடிக்கிறாள்.` - `Avaḷ tēnīr kuṭikkiṟāḷ.` means “She is drinking tea.” The feminine singular ending follows the present stem, while tēnīr supplies the written-standard word for tea.

### Common trap

Do not call every written -kiṟ- form exclusively progressive. The simple present also expresses general or repeated activity, while context and additional constructions can specify aspect more narrowly.

***Answers:***

1. `நான் புத்தகம் படிக்கிறேன்.` - `Nāṉ puttakam paṭikkiṟēṉ.`
2. `நான் தண்ணீர் குடிக்கிறேன்.` - `Nāṉ taṇṇīr kuṭikkiṟēṉ.`
3. `அவள் தேநீர் குடிக்கிறாள்.` - `Avaḷ tēnīr kuṭikkiṟāḷ.`
4. `அவன் புத்தகம் படிக்கிறான்.` - `Avaṉ puttakam paṭikkiṟāṉ.`
5. `நான் தமிழ் பேசுகிறேன்.` - `Nāṉ tamiḻ pēcukiṟēṉ.`
6. `நீங்கள் என்ன செய்கிறீர்கள்?` - `Nīṅkaḷ eṉṉa ceykiṟīrkaḷ?`

### Why these answers work

Answer 4: The masculine ending distinguishes the reader from the woman in the preceding sentence without changing the object pattern.

Answer 5: The speaking verb uses pēcukiṟ- rather than the doubled k pattern of paṭikkiṟ-, illustrating verb-class differences.

Answer 6: The question word asks about the activity, and the final respectful second-person ending identifies the person addressed.

## Lesson 7: Specific objects and object pronouns

### Build the pattern

You will mark a specific object and distinguish I from me. Tamil accusative -ஐ ai appears in புத்தகத்தை puttakattai, the book as object, and வீட்டை vīṭṭai, the house as object. The noun stem can change before the ending. Pronouns likewise change: என்னை eṉṉai means me, உங்களை uṅkaḷai means you respectfully, and அவளை avaḷai means her. Specificity and animacy affect object marking; the earlier unmarked book in a general reading activity is not simply an error. This lesson uses explicitly identifiable objects so that the marked construction is easy to interpret.

### Think before reading on

Why is என்னை eṉṉai appropriate in he sees me, while நான் nāṉ is not the form taught for that role?

The speaker receives the seeing action rather than performing it. Tamil marks this role through an object form, comparable in function to English me. The subject's own ending still tells us who performs the action.

## Lesson 8: Requests that preserve respect

### Build the pattern

You will invite someone to come, sit and read. Standard polite imperatives often end in -ங்கள் ṅkaḷ, but the complete forms vary: வாருங்கள் vāruṅkaḷ, come; உட்காருங்கள் uṭkāruṅkaḷ, sit; படியுங்கள் paṭiyuṅkaḷ, read. Spoken Tamil commonly has forms such as வாங்க vāṅka for come politely. This course retains the full written forms in answers and labels the spoken comparison only for recognition. தயவுசெய்து tayavuceytu means please. It can soften a request, but it does not replace selecting an appropriate verb form. A familiar bare command has a different social effect from a polite invitation.

### Think before reading on

Should the polite command read be made by keeping the entire present form paṭikkiṟēṉ and adding a respectful suffix?

No. The command uses its own stem shape and ending: paṭiyuṅkaḷ. Present-tense first-person material has no place in a command addressed to another person. Learn the request as a usable contrasting form of the verb.

### Worked examples

`இங்கே வாருங்கள்.` - `Iṅkē vāruṅkaḷ.` means “Come here. (polite)” The location word names the place of arrival, and the full imperative belongs to the written-standard register of the course.

`உட்காருங்கள்.` - `Uṭkāruṅkaḷ.` means “Sit down. (polite)” The respectful imperative can stand alone when the addressee and available seat are clear from the situation.

`புத்தகம் படியுங்கள்.` - `Puttakam paṭiyuṅkaḷ.` means “Read a book. (polite)” The reading command uses paṭiyu- rather than the present-tense stem paṭikkiṟ-, illustrating a real morphological change.

### Common trap

Do not mix a full written ending with a shortened spoken stem at random. Both registers have patterns, and a hybrid is not automatically a natural middle register.

## Lesson 9: Location, destination and noun stems

### Build the pattern

You will distinguish being in a house from going to it. The locative suffix -இல் il gives வீட்டில் vīṭṭil, in the house or at home. The dative-destination form gives வீட்டுக்கு vīṭṭukku, to the house or home. கடை kaṭai, shop, gives கடையில் kaṭaiyil and கடைக்கு kaṭaikku. Tamil suffixes follow nouns, and stem changes or linking sounds can appear at the boundary. போகிறேன் pōkiṟēṉ means I go or am going. A location phrase combines naturally with irukkiṟēṉ, while a destination phrase tells where movement leads. These role distinctions are more dependable than English word order.

### Think before reading on

Would vīṭṭil and vīṭṭukku answer the same question about home?

No. One answers where someone is; the other answers where someone is going. The house noun is the same, but its relationship to the event differs. The ending makes that difference visible inside the noun phrase.

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |

| நான் வீட்டுக்குப் போகிறேன். | Nāṉ vīṭṭukkup pōkiṟēṉ. | I am going home. |
| அவள் கடையில் இருக்கிறாள். | Avaḷ kaṭaiyil irukkiṟāḷ. | She is in the shop. |
| அவன் கடைக்குப் போகிறான். | Avaṉ kaṭaikkup pōkiṟāṉ. | He is going to the shop. |
| நீங்கள் எங்கே போகிறீர்கள்? | Nīṅkaḷ eṅkē pōkiṟīrkaḷ? | Where are you going? (respectful) |
| புத்தகம் வீட்டில் இருக்கிறது. | Puttakam vīṭṭil irukkiṟatu. | The book is at home. |

### Worked examples

`நான் வீட்டில் இருக்கிறேன்.` - `Nāṉ vīṭṭil irukkiṟēṉ.` means “I am at home.” The doubled house stem appears before the locative ending, and the final existential reports a position rather than movement.

`நான் வீட்டுக்குப் போகிறேன்.` - `Nāṉ vīṭṭukkup pōkiṟēṉ.` means “I am going home.” The destination form replaces the locative, and a linking p appears before the following p in standard writing.

`அவள் கடையில் இருக்கிறாள்.` - `Avaḷ kaṭaiyil irukkiṟāḷ.` means “She is in the shop.” The vowel-final shop noun takes a linking y before the locative suffix, while the verb keeps feminine agreement.

### Common trap

Do not treat every suffix after a place name as equivalent to in. Location, destination and source will have distinct forms and cannot be swapped without changing the meaning.

## Lesson 10: Wanting, needing and the dative person

### Build the pattern

You will request water and ask what someone needs. எனக்கு eṉakku means to me, and உங்களுக்கு uṅkaḷukku means to you respectfully. With வேண்டும் vēṇṭum, these forms express wanting or needing: eṉakku taṇṇīr vēṇṭum. The object wanted appears before the final expression. வேண்டாம் vēṇṭām declines or says something is not wanted in this construction. Spoken Tamil frequently shortens vēṇṭum to vēṇum, but the practice uses written vēṇṭum. The person is an experiencer of need, not an ordinary agent performing a want action. That explains why nāṉ is not the model subject form here.

### Think before reading on

Why does the same speaker use nāṉ when reading but eṉakku when wanting water?

The constructions organise their participants differently. Reading has an agent; wanting here assigns a dative experiencer. Translating the English pronoun I without considering the predicate would hide this important Tamil pattern.

## Lesson 11: Ownership and temporary possession

### Build the pattern

You will distinguish whose book something is from having a book available. என் eṉ and உங்கள் uṅkaḷ modify nouns directly. என்னிடம் eṉṉiṭam means with me or in my possession; உங்களிடம் uṅkaḷiṭam is the respectful counterpart. A book being with someone can be stated with an existential verb, and its absence with illai. This construction does not necessarily assert legal ownership: a borrowed book can be eṉṉiṭam. Longer possessives such as என்னுடைய eṉṉuṭaiya make the possessive relation explicit, but the shorter forms remain useful. யாருடைய yāruṭaiya asks whose.

### Think before reading on

If a library book is temporarily in your bag, does saying it is with you necessarily mean that you own it?

No. The iṭam construction reports possession or availability in the relevant situation. A possessive phrase can identify ownership, while the context may also support looser associations. Keep availability and ownership separate when that distinction matters.

### Useful language in context

| Native script | Romanisation | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| இது என் புத்தகம். | Itu eṉ puttakam. | This is my book. |
| இது யாருடைய புத்தகம்? | Itu yāruṭaiya puttakam? | Whose book is this? |
| என்னிடம் புத்தகம் இருக்கிறது. | Eṉṉiṭam puttakam irukkiṟatu. | I have a book with me. |
| உங்களிடம் புத்தகம் இருக்கிறதா? | Uṅkaḷiṭam puttakam irukkiṟatā? | Do you have a book with you? (respectful) |
| என்னிடம் புத்தகம் இல்லை. | Eṉṉiṭam puttakam illai. | I do not have a book with me. |
| இது என்னுடைய வீடு. | Itu eṉṉuṭaiya vīṭu. | This is my house. |

### Worked examples

`இது என் புத்தகம்.` - `Itu eṉ puttakam.` means “This is my book.” The short possessive modifies the noun inside a direct identity statement and identifies the book's association with the speaker.

`இது யாருடைய புத்தகம்?` - `Itu yāruṭaiya puttakam?` means “Whose book is this?” The interrogative possessive asks for an owner rather than asking what category the object belongs to.

`என்னிடம் புத்தகம் இருக்கிறது.` - `Eṉṉiṭam puttakam irukkiṟatu.` means “I have a book with me.” The book is presented as available in the speaker's possession, and its nonhuman singular status selects the final verb.

### Common trap

Do not substitute eṉṉiṭam for eṉ directly before every noun. One forms a relational possession expression; the other is a noun modifier inside an owned-object phrase.

Answer 4: The person is expressed through the respectful possession form, while the question ending attaches to the existential predicate.

## Lesson 12: Counting objects and people

### Build the pattern

You will request one, two or three books and count people. ஒன்று oṉṟu is the independent number one; ஒரு oru is the common form before a noun. இரண்டு iraṇṭu and மூன்று mūṉṟu mean two and three. ## Lesson 13: Describing a noun and describing a state

### Build the pattern

Tamil adjectives in these positions do not copy the gender and person endings of finite verbs. நீளமான nīḷamāṉa means long as a modifier and demonstrates another productive descriptive shape.

Can every English adjective be moved unchanged from before the noun to after it in Tamil?

## Lesson 14: Past actions with a small set of stems

### Build the pattern

You will report coming, going, reading and eating in the past. Standard Tamil past forms vary by verb class: வந்தேன் vantēṉ, I came; போனேன் pōṉēṉ, I went; படித்தேன் paṭittēṉ, I read; சாப்பிட்டேன் cāppiṭṭēṉ, I ate. நேற்று nēṟṟu means yesterday. The past form is not created merely by adding yesterday to a present sentence. First-person -ēṉ remains recognisable, but the material before it marks the past in different ways. Begin with whole meaningful contrasts rather than trying to force every verb into the same stem rule. Objects and destinations keep their own grammatical roles when tense changes.

### Think before reading on

What must change when I am going home becomes I went home yesterday, besides adding the word yesterday?

The present going verb becomes the past form pōṉēṉ. The destination vīṭṭukku remains a destination, so it need not become a location expression. Tense changes the event's time, not automatically the role of every noun.

## Lesson 15: Past reports and respectful questions

### Build the pattern

You will report what another person did and ask a visitor when they arrived. Ordinary masculine past வந்தான் vantāṉ contrasts with feminine வந்தாள் vantāḷ and honorific வந்தார் vantār. Respectful second-person வந்தீர்கள் vantīrkaḷ belongs with நீங்கள் nīṅkaḷ. The stems remain past while the endings identify person, number and respect. எப்போது eppōtu means when. A yes-or-no question adds ā to the final verb, while a when-question uses eppōtu in the time position. Written distinctions may sound shorter in everyday speech, but they are not interchangeable in the practice standard.

### Think before reading on

Will a respectful question to the visitor use vantār simply because both the visitor and a third person deserve respect?

No. Direct respectful address uses second-person vantīrkaḷ. Vantār describes a respected third person. Respect does not erase the grammatical distinction between speaking to someone and speaking about someone.

## Lesson 16: Past negation with illai

### Build the pattern

You will say that an action did not happen. Tamil commonly combines an infinitive-like verb form with இல்லை illai, producing வரவில்லை varavillai, did not come, படிக்கவில்லை paṭikkavillai, did not read, and குடிக்கவில்லை kuṭikkavillai, did not drink. The resulting negative does not carry the positive personal endings in these examples. A past time expression such as nēṟṟu makes the interpretation explicit. இன்னும் iṉṉum means still or yet; இன்னும் வரவில்லை can describe an arrival that has not occurred yet. The same negative construction can have present-related readings in appropriate contexts, so the lesson does not label its form exclusively past in all uses.

### Think before reading on

If I did not come becomes she did not come, must varavillai gain a feminine -āḷ ending?

No. The explicit subject identifies the person, while varavillai remains unchanged in this construction. Positive and negative paradigms organise agreement differently; do not assume that every feature of a positive verb survives in the negative.

## Lesson 17: Future plans and habitual readings

### Build the pattern

You will say what you will do tomorrow and recognise a habitual use of future forms. வருவேன் varuvēṉ means I will come; படிப்பேன் paṭippēṉ means I will read; போவேன் pōvēṉ means I will go. The variation between v and pp reflects verb classes. நாளை nāḷai, tomorrow, clearly points to a future event. தினமும் tiṉamum, every day, can support a habitual reading of the same general future series. First-person -ēṉ does not distinguish gender. Ordinary third-person endings and respectful endings remain relevant when the subject changes, but they attach to the appropriate future stem.

### Think before reading on

Why does tomorrow I read need paṭippēṉ rather than a universal future ending attached to every dictionary verb?

Tamil future stems belong to different verb classes. Reading selects paṭipp-, while coming selects varuv-. The person ending is only one part of the form; the stem and tense material must also be appropriate to the verb.

## Lesson 18: Refusal, future negatives and prohibitions

### Build the pattern

You will refuse a future action and politely tell someone not to act. வரமாட்டேன் varamāṭṭēṉ means I will not come, and சாப்பிடமாட்டேன் cāppiṭamāṭṭēṉ means I will not eat. The negative auxiliary māṭṭ- takes person endings, unlike the invariant varavillai construction. A polite prohibition uses வராதீர்கள் varātīrkaḷ, do not come, or பேசாதீர்கள் pēcātīrkaḷ, do not speak. A refusal describes the speaker's intended nonaction; a prohibition addresses another person's behaviour. The distinction is practical as well as grammatical. இப்போது ippōtu limits a refusal or prohibition to now.

### Think before reading on

Is I will not come a polite way to tell someone else not to come?

No. The first-person ending makes it a statement about the speaker. A prohibition needs its own addressee-oriented form. Track who is meant to refrain from the action before choosing the negative construction.

## Lesson 19: Ability, permission and obligation

### Build the pattern

You will say that you can read and ask whether you may sit. முடியும் muṭiyum expresses ability or possibility with a preceding infinitive; முடியாது muṭiyātu is its negative. என்னால் eṉṉāl can identify the person able to perform the action. Permission can be asked with உட்காரலாமா uṭkāralāmā, may I sit, and வரலாமா varalāmā, may I come. Obligation or necessity uses an infinitive plus வேண்டும் vēṇṭum, as in போக வேண்டும் pōka vēṇṭum, must go. The same vēṇṭum that expressed wanting a noun now follows an action expression. These meanings should not be collapsed into one English can.

### Think before reading on

Does being able to sit prove that you have permission to sit in a particular seat?

No. Ability and permission answer different questions. Muṭiyum can describe capacity or practical possibility, while the -lāmā question requests permission in the situations used here. A learner should choose according to the intended interaction.

Do not interpret vēṇṭum as a fixed noun meaning want. Its construction changes when an action precedes it, allowing necessity or obligation rather than only desire for an object.

## Lesson 20: Liking, knowing and understanding

### Build the pattern

You will express preference, knowledge and a comprehension problem. எனக்கு eṉakku is the experiencer in எனக்குப் பிடிக்கும் eṉakkup piṭikkum, I like, and எனக்குத் தெரியும் eṉakkut teriyum, I know. தெரியாது teriyātu is the knowledge negative, while புரியவில்லை puriyavillai can report not understanding. The object of liking or knowing is placed before the final predicate. A language name with teriyum is a broad statement of knowledge, not a precise claim about reading or speaking level. கொஞ்சம் koñcam, a little, can narrow that claim. More specific ability statements remain available from the preceding lesson.

### Think before reading on

Why might I know Tamil and I speak Tamil use different forms of I in this course?

Knowing is presented through a dative experiencer, while speaking uses an agent subject. Tamil does not require the same grammatical frame for two English verbs merely because both take I as their English subject.

## Lesson 21: Companions and sources

### Build the pattern

You will say with whom someone came and where they came from. உடன் uṭaṉ expresses with; என்னுடன் eṉṉuṭaṉ means with me, and உங்களுடன் uṅkaḷuṭaṉ means with you respectfully. The source ending -இலிருந்து iliruntu expresses from a place, as in வீட்டிலிருந்து vīṭṭiliruntu, from home. Its shape includes material related to location, but the whole expression marks a starting point rather than a static position. Coming and going can both take source phrases. A language used as a medium takes -இல் il, giving தமிழில் tamiḻil, in Tamil, which is another use of an already familiar suffix.

### Think before reading on

Does with me in a journey have the same meaning as with me in the possession lesson?

Not exactly. Eṉṉuṭaṉ identifies a companion in an action, while eṉṉiṭam identifies possession or availability. English with covers both relationships, but Tamil offers distinct forms that make the intended relation clearer.

## Lesson 22: Joining actions without repeating the subject

### Build the pattern

You will connect successive actions with a nonfinal verb form. வந்து vantu, having come, and சாப்பிட்டு cāppiṭṭu, having eaten, can precede a final finite verb. The final verb supplies the sentence's tense, person or command force. வந்து உட்காருங்கள் therefore means come and sit politely, while வந்து உட்கார்ந்தாள் reports that she came and sat. The nonfinal form itself does not specify an English tense independently. Such combinations often describe a natural sequence involving the same subject. They are useful for following instructions and telling short stories without repeating the subject before every action.

### Think before reading on

Which word in vantu uṭkāruṅkaḷ tells you that the sequence is a polite request rather than a past report?

The final imperative uṭkāruṅkaḷ supplies the request force. Vantu introduces the arrival action without its own person ending. The same nonfinal form can therefore participate in both instructions and past narratives.

## Lesson 23: Conditions and contrasts

### Build the pattern

You will make an invitation conditional on time and contrast two facts. இருந்தால் iruntāl means if there is or if someone is; வந்தால் vantāl means if someone comes. The conditional clause supplies a circumstance, and the following clause supplies the consequence or request. ஆனால் āṉāl means but in the contrasting examples here. நேரம் nēram means time and can be described as available through the existential construction. Conditional forms do not themselves assert that the condition has happened. Tamil can use a past-looking stem in a conditional without making the condition a report of a completed past event.

### Think before reading on

Does vantāl mean that somebody definitely came earlier, simply because it resembles the past stem vant-?

No. The conditional ending changes the clause's function to if someone comes. The following clause states what would follow. Recognising the whole conditional form prevents a misleading word-by-word past translation.

| நேரம் இருந்தால் வாருங்கள். | Nēram iruntāl vāruṅkaḷ. | Come if there is time. (polite) |

### Worked examples

`நேரம் இருந்தால் வாருங்கள்.` - `Nēram iruntāl vāruṅkaḷ.` means “Come if there is time. (polite)” The condition concerns available time, while the final polite imperative supplies the invitation rather than a factual prediction.

### Common trap

Do not translate āṉāl as a universal and. In these sentences it introduces contrast, while the conditional -āl forms attach to verbs and establish a different relationship.

## Lesson 24: A purchase and a conversation repair

### Build the pattern

You will order a small quantity, ask its price and repair a misunderstood reply. எவ்வளவு evvaḷavu means how much, and விலை vilai means price. கொடுங்கள் koṭuṅkaḷ is the polite command give. மீண்டும் mīṇṭum means again, and சொல்லுங்கள் colluṅkaḷ means say or tell politely. A productive exchange combines already learned wanting, quantity and respectful requests. When the price is unclear, asking for repetition or slower speech is more useful than guessing. ரூபாய் rūpāy names the currency unit; ordinary expressions such as பத்து ரூபாய் pattu rūpāy mean ten rupees without forcing English plural morphology onto the noun.

### Think before reading on

If you understand the item but miss the price, should you restart the entire exchange or request the missing information?

Ask the price again or ask the speaker to repeat. A focused repair preserves what is already understood and narrows the next reply. Tamil's compact price question can identify the object with a demonstrative and ask the amount directly.

`எனக்கு இரண்டு புத்தகங்கள் கொடுங்கள்.` - `Eṉakku iraṇṭu puttakaṅkaḷ koṭuṅkaḷ.` means “Give me two books. (polite)” Eṉakku identifies the recipient here, while the quantity phrase names the items to be transferred by the polite command.

## Lesson 25: Cumulative review and the spoken bridge

### Build the pattern

You will tell a short account, ask for help and recognise why spoken Tamil may sound different. The practice remains modern standard written Tamil. Everyday speech can shorten nāṉ to nā, nīṅkaḷ to nīṅka, and vēṇṭum to vēṇum; verb forms also change systematically. These are register and variety differences, not permission to delete random syllables. A written foundation helps with reading and formal expression, but listening to a chosen spoken variety is a separate next step. In the review story, identity, present location, past action and future intention require different constructions rather than one universal translation of English be or do.

### Think before reading on

What changes when I am at home becomes I went to the shop yesterday: only the time expression, or the place relationship and verb as well?

The location becomes a destination, the house is replaced by the shop, and the present existential becomes a past movement verb. A meaningful transformation considers the whole event, not just an ending. The person can remain first-person throughout.
