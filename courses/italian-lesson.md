# Italian: A pattern-first foundation

This course teaches contemporary standard Italian in its Latin alphabet, with neutral everyday usage and a clear distinction between informal tu and courteous singular Lei. Regional pronunciations and Italy's other languages are not treated as errors, but the active examples use a consistent standard foundation. Italian is not Spanish with changed endings: articles, clitic pronouns, auxiliaries and idiomatic constructions need their own patterns.

Preserve written accents in è, sì, perché, caffè and other standard spellings. Ordinary Italian does not mark stress on every word. Pronunciation notes identify stress or sound contrasts when useful, while the answer keys retain standard orthography. Double consonants matter: pala and palla are different words. Italian h is silent, but it has important spelling functions in ho and in combinations such as che and chi.

### References and review notes

[Accademia della Crusca on courteous address](https://accademiadellacrusca.it/it/consulenza/gli-allocutivi-di-cortesia/142) was consulted for the distinction between tu, Lei and regional or historical voi. [Its guidance on indirect pronoun gli](https://accademiadellacrusca.it/it/consulenza/uso-di-gli-per-a-lui-a-loro-e-a-lei/102) was consulted to keep register-sensitive pronoun claims limited. The teaching prose and examples are original. Auxiliary choice, adjective placement and natural register remain priorities for fluent-speaker review.

## Lesson 1: Identity with essere

**Outcome:** introduce yourself and describe another person using common forms of essere.

### Think first

English changes am, are and is according to the subject. If Italian says sono for I am and è for he or she is, what information can the verb already provide?

### A small irregular verb with a large role

Essere means to be. Its present forms are io sono, tu sei, lui or lei è, noi siamo, voi siete and loro sono. The same sono serves first-person singular and third-person plural, so context matters. A pronoun can clarify who is meant, but Italian often omits it when the verb and conversation are sufficient.

Sono studente is a simple occupational identity for a male student; Sono studentessa is the corresponding female form. Italian often omits an indefinite article with an unmodified profession or role after essere. That does not mean articles are optional everywhere. Sono uno studente italiano includes an article with a modified noun phrase, a pattern to revisit after articles are introduced.

Tu addresses one person informally. Lei addresses one person courteously and takes third-person singular verb forms, even when speaking directly to a man. Lei è... is therefore courteous you are, while lowercase lei can also mean she. Capitalisation helps show the teaching distinction but is not an audible grammatical difference.

A person's nationality adjective normally begins with a lowercase letter: italiano or italiana. These forms agree with the person described. The verb sono does not change for the speaker's gender, but the adjective or role noun can. Keep those two agreement decisions separate.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| io sono | I am |
| tu sei | you are, informal singular |
| Lei è | you are, courteous singular |
| noi siamo | we are |
| voi siete | you are, plural |
| loro sono | they are |
| studente | male student |
| studentessa | female student |

### Worked examples

- `Sono studente.`: I am a male student; the subject pronoun can be omitted.
- `Sono studentessa.`: I am a female student; the noun changes, not sono.
- `Lei è italiana.`: She is Italian, or courteous you are Italian to a woman, according to context.

### Common trap

Do not use tu sei and Lei è interchangeably without considering the relationship. Also, è with an accent means is, while e without an accent means and. The accent is meaningful standard spelling.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say as a woman: I am a student.
2. Say as a man: I am Italian.
3. Tell a friend: You are a male student.
4. Say: We are here. Use qui.

***Answers:***

1. `Sono studentessa.`
2. `Sono italiano.`
3. `Tu sei studente.`
4. `Siamo qui.`

### Why these answers work

Sono works for either speaker gender, while studentessa and italiano encode the description. Tu sei selects informal address. Siamo identifies we, and qui supplies the location without requiring a repeated noi.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce yourself and one imaginary classmate, then address a stranger courteously. Explain which verb form changes with the relationship and which descriptive ending changes with the person described.


## Lesson 2: Reading c, g and double consonants

**Outcome:** read useful words with the main c/g spelling contrasts and ask what something is.

### Think first

C has a hard sound in casa but a ch-like sound in cena. What does the following vowel tell you, and why does che contain an h?

### Spelling gives sound clues

Before a, o and u, c normally has a k sound and g a hard g sound. Before e and i, c is like ch in chair and g like j in jam. An h preserves the hard sound before e or i: che, chi, ghe and ghi. The h is not separately pronounced. Thus chi means who with a k sound, while cinema begins with the ch-like sound.

Sc before e or i commonly represents a sh-like sound, as in scena. Gn represents the palatal sound heard in signora, somewhat like the middle of canyon. Gli in words such as famiglia has another palatal sound that needs listening rather than an English letter-by-letter reading. These are spelling patterns, not invitations to pronounce every letter independently.

Double consonants are held longer and can distinguish words. Pala means shovel, while palla means ball. A learner should not erase the difference merely because English spelling often doubles letters without a comparable sound contrast.

Che cos'è? asks “What is it?” Cos'è contracts cosa è with an apostrophe. È un libro means “it is a book.” This introduces a useful identification frame while you practise the sounds inside actual words. The article system will be explained next; today use the complete examples as anchors.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| casa | house; hard c |
| cena | dinner; soft c |
| chi | who; hard c maintained by h |
| cinema | cinema; soft c |
| signora | madam; woman, with gn |
| famiglia | family, with gli |
| palla | ball, double l |
| che cos'è | what is it |

### Worked examples

- `Che cos'è?`: What is it? The apostrophe marks contraction of cosa è.
- `È un libro.`: It is a book; è is a complete verb form.
- `È una palla.`: It is a ball; the double l belongs to the word's pronunciation.

### Common trap

Do not pronounce h as an English h in chi or ho. It is silent but meaningful in spelling. Also, a double consonant is not simply a decorative way to write a single consonant.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Ask: What is it?
2. Identify: It is a house.
3. Identify: It is a book.
4. Ask: Who is it?

***Answers:***

1. `Che cos'è?`
2. `È una casa.`
3. `È un libro.`
4. `Chi è?`

### Why these answers work

Cos'è uses an apostrophe to combine cosa and è. Chi retains a hard c because of h. The house and book identifications use the same essere frame, with article choice following the noun's gender.

### Retrieve and recombine

Read casa, cena and chi aloud, then identify a book and a ball. Explain the role of the following vowel or h in each c sound and preserve the long consonant in palla.


## Lesson 3: Articles, gender and plural nouns

**Outcome:** select common definite and indefinite articles and form regular noun plurals.

### Think first

Why does Italian say il libro but lo studente? Is the choice determined only by grammatical gender, or also by the sound beginning the noun?

### Gender plus sound shape

Many nouns ending in o are masculine and form plurals in i: libro, libri. Many ending in a are feminine and form plurals in e: casa, case. Nouns ending in e can be masculine or feminine and commonly form plurals in i: studente, studenti; notte, notti. Learn the article with an unfamiliar noun because the final letter alone does not always reveal gender.

The definite articles include il for many masculine singular nouns, lo before certain beginnings such as s plus consonant and z, and l' before a vowel. Their common plurals are i and gli. Thus il libro becomes i libri, while lo studente becomes gli studenti. Feminine la becomes le, and l' also appears before a feminine vowel-starting noun.

Indefinite articles include un, uno, una and un'. Un libro has no apostrophe. Un amico also has no apostrophe because masculine un is not treated as an elided una. Un'amica is feminine and does use an apostrophe. Uno studente matches the special masculine sound environment.

Accented final-vowel nouns such as caffè and many borrowed nouns have unchanged plurals. Article and context then help signal number. Start with the regular patterns but do not treat every final vowel as a universal plural machine.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| il libro | the book |
| i libri | the books |
| lo studente | the male student |
| gli studenti | the male or mixed students |
| la casa | the house |
| le case | the houses |
| un amico | a male friend |
| un'amica | a female friend |

### Worked examples

- `Il libro è qui.`: The book is here; the article marks a specific masculine noun.
- `Le case sono grandi.`: The houses are big; plural article and verb agree.
- `È uno studente.`: He is a student, presented as one member of that category; uno fits the initial st cluster.

### Common trap

Do not write un'amico for a masculine friend. The apostrophe belongs to feminine un'amica. Also, lo and gli are sound-conditioned article forms, not signs that the noun has a different kind of masculinity.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Produce the phrase: the books.
2. Produce the phrase: a female friend.
3. Produce the phrase: the male students.
4. Produce the phrase: the houses.

***Answers:***

1. `I libri.`
2. `Un'amica.`
3. `Gli studenti.`
4. `Le case.`

### Why these answers work

Libri takes masculine plural i, while studenti takes gli because of its initial s-plus-consonant cluster. Amica is feminine before a vowel and uses un'. Case is the regular feminine plural of casa and takes le.

### Retrieve and recombine

Choose one masculine and one feminine noun, then move each from indefinite singular to definite plural. State separately what changed in the noun and what changed in the article.


## Lesson 4: Adjectives that follow the noun

**Outcome:** describe familiar objects with matching gender and number endings.

### Think first

Un libro nuovo and una casa nuova use the same idea of new. Which noun feature determines the adjective's final vowel?

### Agreement links the description

A common adjective pattern has four forms: nuovo, nuova, nuovi and nuove. These match masculine singular, feminine singular, masculine plural and feminine plural nouns. Il libro è nuovo and la casa è nuova show agreement even when the adjective is the predicate after essere.

Many adjectives ending in e have only singular e and plural i, without a masculine/feminine difference: grande, grandi; interessante, interessanti. A large house is una casa grande, and large books are libri grandi. Do not force every adjective into the o/a pattern.

Adjectives frequently follow the noun, especially when classifying or distinguishing it: un libro italiano, a book in Italian or an Italian book. Some adjectives can occur before the noun, sometimes with a change in emphasis or meaning. This lesson uses the common after-noun position and does not claim that all other placements are incorrect.

Molto can intensify an adjective: molto grande, very big. In this adverbial use it stays unchanged, even with a feminine plural noun: case molto grandi. Later molto can also behave as a quantity word that agrees with a noun. The same spelling can therefore have different grammatical jobs.

The article, noun, adjective and verb each contribute agreement information, but you can build the sentence in stages. First identify the noun's gender and number, then choose its article and adjective, and finally match essere to singular or plural.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| nuovo | new, masculine singular |
| nuova | new, feminine singular |
| nuovi | new, masculine plural |
| nuove | new, feminine plural |
| grande | big, singular |
| grandi | big, plural |
| interessante | interesting, singular |
| molto | very, before an adjective |

### Worked examples

- `La casa è nuova.`: The house is new; nuova agrees with a feminine singular noun.
- `I libri sono interessanti.`: The books are interesting; the e adjective has plural i.
- `Le case sono molto grandi.`: The houses are very big; molto remains an adverb.

### Common trap

Do not change adverbial molto to molte before grandi. It means very and does not agree in that role. Also, grande has no separate feminine singular granda form in standard Italian.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: The book is new.
2. Say: The houses are new.
3. Say: The book is interesting.
4. Say: The house is very big.

***Answers:***

1. `Il libro è nuovo.`
2. `Le case sono nuove.`
3. `Il libro è interessante.`
4. `La casa è molto grande.`

### Why these answers work

Nuovo and nuove follow the noun's gender and number. Interessante has one singular form for both genders. Molto modifies grande as an adverb, while è or sono matches the singular or plural subject.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a book and a house with the same adjective, then pluralise both. Explain why nuovo changes differently from grande and why molto stays fixed when it means very.


## Lesson 5: Present actions with are verbs

**Outcome:** describe a routine or current activity with a regular first-conjugation verb.

### Think first

Parlare means to speak, while parlo means I speak. What remains when the infinitive ending are is removed, and what does the new ending tell you?

### Stem and personal ending

For a regular are verb, remove are and add the present endings o, i, a, iamo, ate and ano. Parlare gives parlo, parli, parla, parliamo, parlate and parlano. The ending identifies the subject, so parlo italiano can be a complete sentence without io.

Lavorare, to work, follows the same pattern: lavoro, lavori, lavora, lavoriamo, lavorate, lavorano. The present can describe a habit or an action occurring now. Oggi lavoro means “I work today” or “I am working today,” depending on context. Italian does not require a progressive construction every time English uses am working.

Courteous Lei takes the third-person singular form: Lei parla italiano? A question can retain the statement's order and use question intonation. Do not attach the informal tu ending merely because you are speaking directly to someone.

Some are verbs need spelling adjustments to preserve a sound, as with cercare becoming cerchi. Others have special contractions or stress patterns. The regular endings are productive, but the base spelling still matters. This lesson practises parlare and lavorare so you can focus on person agreement before meeting those adjustments.

Use an explicit pronoun when contrasting speakers: Io lavoro, lei studia. The pronoun is useful information in that setting, not an error just because Italian can often omit it.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| parlare | to speak |
| parlo | I speak |
| parli | you speak, informal singular |
| parla | he or she speaks; courteous you speak |
| parliamo | we speak |
| lavorare | to work |
| oggi | today |
| italiano | Italian language or adjective in context |

### Worked examples

- `Parlo italiano.`: I speak Italian; the verb ending identifies the speaker.
- `Oggi lavoriamo.`: We work today; iamo marks we.
- `Lei parla italiano?`: Do you speak Italian, courteously? The third-person form serves formal address.

### Common trap

Do not keep the infinitive after io as the ordinary present form. Io parlare is not I speak. Also, courteous Lei uses parla rather than the informal parli.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I work.
2. Say: We speak Italian.
3. Ask a friend: Do you work today?
4. Say: They speak Italian. Include loro.

***Answers:***

1. `Lavoro.`
2. `Parliamo italiano.`
3. `Lavori oggi?`
4. `Loro parlano italiano.`

### Why these answers work

Lavoro uses the first-person ending, parliamo the we ending and lavori the informal you ending. Parlano matches loro. Oggi supplies time without changing the verb into a separate English-style progressive form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Turn a statement about your work into a question for a friend and then for an unfamiliar adult. Explain why the relationship changes the personal form even though both English questions say you.


## Lesson 6: Ere and ire verbs, with useful irregular anchors

**Outcome:** read, open and understand using the common present patterns beyond are.

### Think first

Leggere gives leggo, while capire gives capisco. Does every ire verb insert isc, or is that a pattern to learn with particular verbs?

### Two more common families

Regular ere verbs use present endings o, i, e, iamo, ete and ono. Leggere gives leggo, leggi, legge, leggiamo, leggete and leggono. Aprire is an ire verb without isc: apro, apri, apre, apriamo, aprite, aprono. The endings overlap, but the second-person plural distinguishes leggete from aprite.

Some ire verbs insert isc in the singular forms and third-person plural. Capire gives capisco, capisci, capisce, capiamo, capite and capiscono. The we and plural-you forms do not contain isc. Learn capire as belonging to this pattern rather than adding isc to every ire verb.

Prendere means to take or have in contexts such as taking a coffee. Its present follows a familiar ere pattern: prendo, prendi, prende. Bere, to drink, is irregular in its stem: bevo, bevi, beve, beviamo, bevete, bevono. These frequent anchor forms make practical drink sentences possible without pretending all infinitives reveal a fully regular stem.

Non capisco, I do not understand, will become a key repair phrase in the next lesson. For now, notice the finite form capisco inside it. A useful sentence often contains one irregular but frequent word; learning that word accurately is better than avoiding all real conversation until every paradigm is complete.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| leggere | to read |
| leggo | I read |
| aprire | to open |
| apro | I open |
| capire | to understand |
| capisco | I understand |
| prendo | I take; I have in ordering contexts |
| bevo | I drink |

### Worked examples

- `Leggo un libro.`: I read a book; leggo is first-person present.
- `Apriamo la porta.`: We open the door; aprire does not insert isc.
- `Capisce l'italiano?`: Do you understand Italian, courteously? Capire inserts isc in this singular form.

### Common trap

Do not form aprisco by transferring the capire pattern to aprire. Also, capiamo and capite lack isc even though capisco has it. The verb's class and the particular person both matter.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I understand Italian.
2. Say: We read.
3. Say: I drink water. Use acqua without an article.
4. Ask a friend: Do you open the door?

***Answers:***

1. `Capisco l'italiano.`
2. `Leggiamo.`
3. `Bevo acqua.`
4. `Apri la porta?`

### Why these answers work

Capisco belongs to the isc pattern. Leggiamo uses the regular we ending. Bevo is a learned irregular stem form. Apri follows the non-isc ire pattern, and la porta is the ordinary definite object.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give one sentence with leggere, one with aprire and one with capire. Change each to we and notice that the isc insertion disappears from capiamo while the other stems stay recognisable.


## Lesson 7: Questions and negation without do

**Outcome:** ask simple questions and deny an action with non.

### Think first

English asks do you understand. If Italian already has capisci, does it need another verb corresponding to do just to form the question?

### Intonation and question words

A yes-or-no question can use the same word order as a statement: Capisci l'italiano? Question intonation and punctuation mark the function. Italian does not add an English auxiliary do or does to ordinary present questions. An explicit subject can appear when useful, but inversion is not a universal requirement.

Non normally precedes the finite verb: Non capisco, I do not understand. Non lavoro oggi, I am not working today. The negative word stays the same for all persons. It does not replace the verb's personal ending.

Che cosa, cosa and che can ask what in different contexts. We use Che cosa leggi? as a transparent full form: what are you reading? Dove asks where, quando when, perché why or because, and chi who. Quanto asks how much, with agreement forms in some noun phrases introduced later.

Sì with an accent means yes; si without an accent has other grammatical roles. No is the independent negative response, while non belongs inside the ordinary negative sentence. A correction can begin No, then state the actual fact.

A question's social form still matters. Capisci? addresses a friend; Capisce? can address someone courteously. Removing English do does not remove Italian's person and register distinctions.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| non | not, before a verb |
| sì | yes |
| no | no |
| che cosa | what |
| dove | where |
| quando | when |
| perché | why; because |
| chi | who |

### Worked examples

- `Non capisco.`: I do not understand; non precedes the finite verb.
- `Che cosa leggi?`: What are you reading, addressed informally? The question word supplies the missing object.
- `Dove lavora?`: Where do you work, courteously, or where does someone work in context?

### Common trap

Do not say faccio capire to mean do I understand by copying English question do. Fare has its own lexical meanings. Also, no and non are not interchangeable inside a normal negative statement.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I do not speak Italian.
2. Ask a friend: What are you reading?
3. Ask courteously: Do you understand?
4. Say: I am not working today.

***Answers:***

1. `Non parlo italiano.`
2. `Che cosa leggi?`
3. `Capisce?`
4. `Non lavoro oggi.`

### Why these answers work

Non preserves the underlying verb forms parlo and lavoro. Che cosa asks for the object before leggi. Capisce uses the courteous third-person singular form. No separate auxiliary is needed in either question.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask about an activity, answer negatively, then ask a more specific question with dove or quando. Keep the informal or courteous form consistent throughout the exchange.


## Lesson 8: Having, age and physical needs

**Outcome:** use avere for possession and several common states that English expresses with be.

### Think first

Italian says ho fame, literally I have hunger. Should you translate English I am hungry by selecting essere, or learn the Italian construction's own verb?

### Avere does more than possession

Avere means to have. Its present forms are ho, hai, ha, abbiamo, avete and hanno. The h is silent in ho, hai, ha and hanno, but it distinguishes these verb forms from other words in writing. Ho un libro means I have a book, while abbiamo una casa means we have a house.

Age uses avere: Ho vent'anni means I am twenty years old. The apostrophe in vent'anni reflects elision in this common expression. A learner should not substitute sono simply because English uses am for age. The same principle applies to ho fame, I am hungry; ho sete, I am thirsty; ho sonno, I am sleepy; and ho freddo, I feel cold.

These expressions describe the person's experience. È freddo can describe an object as cold, while ho freddo says that I feel cold. The difference is semantic, not just an arbitrary verb list. Context tells whether you are describing a thing's temperature or a person's sensation.

Questions use the same avere forms: Hai fame? to a friend, Ha sete? courteously. Negation goes before the verb: Non ho fame. Avere remains irregular, but its high frequency makes the six forms worth learning as stable anchors.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| ho | I have |
| hai | you have, informal singular |
| ha | he or she has; courteous you have |
| abbiamo | we have |
| avete | you have, plural |
| hanno | they have |
| fame | hunger |
| sete | thirst |
| sonno | sleepiness |
| freddo | cold |

### Worked examples

- `Ho un libro.`: I have a book; the silent h is part of the standard spelling.
- `Ho fame.`: I am hungry; Italian uses have with the noun hunger.
- `Ha sete?`: Are you thirsty, courteously? The question uses the third-person form.

### Common trap

Do not say sono fame or sono vent'anni for the standard constructions taught here. Also, ho and o are different words: ho means I have, while o means or.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I am thirsty.
2. Ask a friend: Are you hungry?
3. Say: We have a book.
4. Say: I am not sleepy.

***Answers:***

1. `Ho sete.`
2. `Hai fame?`
3. `Abbiamo un libro.`
4. `Non ho sonno.`

### Why these answers work

Sete, fame and sonno combine with avere to describe an experience. Abbiamo marks we in a possession statement. Non negates ho without changing the noun in the expression. The verb choice follows Italian usage, not English be.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe one object you possess and one physical need you have. Then contrast a cold drink with your own feeling of cold, choosing essere or avere according to the meaning.


## Lesson 9: Replacing a direct object with lo or la

**Outcome:** avoid repeating a known object by using a short direct-object pronoun.

### Think first

Leggo il libro names the book. If the listener already knows which book you mean, where would Italian place the equivalent of it?

### A small pronoun before the verb

Lo replaces a masculine singular direct object, and la a feminine singular direct object. Leggo il libro becomes Lo leggo, I read it. Apro la porta becomes La apro, I open it. The unstressed pronoun normally comes before a finite verb in these ordinary statements, unlike English it after the verb.

Plural objects use li for masculine or mixed nouns and le for feminine nouns: Li leggo, I read them, referring to books; Le apro, I open them, referring to doors. The pronoun's gender follows the replaced noun, not the speaker or owner.

Non precedes the pronoun: Non lo leggo, I do not read it. A question can keep the same structure: Lo leggi? The verb still changes for its subject, while the object pronoun independently identifies the object.

Before an infinitive, a direct-object pronoun can attach to the infinitive after dropping its final e: leggere becomes leggerlo, to read it. With a modal verb, both Lo voglio leggere and Voglio leggerlo are common patterns. We will develop modal verbs later; today's production stays with finite verbs to keep the pronoun position clear.

Do not confuse direct-object la with the feminine article la. The article accompanies a noun, while the pronoun replaces a noun phrase. Their sentence positions help reveal which job is being done.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| lo | it or him, masculine singular direct object |
| la | it or her, feminine singular direct object |
| li | them, masculine or mixed direct object |
| le | them, feminine direct object |
| lo leggo | I read it, masculine object |
| la apro | I open it, feminine object |
| non lo leggo | I do not read it |

### Worked examples

- `Il libro? Lo leggo.`: The book? I read it; lo replaces the masculine object.
- `La porta? La apro.`: The door? I open it; la replaces the feminine object.
- `I libri? Li leggi?`: The books? Are you reading them, informally? Li marks the plural object.

### Common trap

Do not put the ordinary unstressed pronoun after the finite verb by copying English word order. Also, choose lo or la from the noun being replaced, not from the gender of the person doing the action.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I read it, referring to il libro.
2. Say: I open it, referring to la porta.
3. Say: I do not read them, referring to i libri.
4. Ask a friend: Do you open them, referring to le porte?

***Answers:***

1. `Lo leggo.`
2. `La apro.`
3. `Non li leggo.`
4. `Le apri?`

### Why these answers work

Lo and la replace singular objects, while li and le replace plural ones. Non comes before the pronoun. Leggo and apri still identify the subject independently, so object agreement does not replace verb conjugation.

### Retrieve and recombine

Name an object once, then ask and answer a question about it using only the object pronoun. Repeat with a plural feminine noun and explain both the position and the form of the pronoun.


## Lesson 10: Location and existence

**Outcome:** say where something is and distinguish there is from there are.

### Think first

Il libro è qui locates a known book. What different construction would announce that a book is present at all?

### È versus c'è

È qui means it is here, with a known subject understood. C'è un libro means there is a book. The apostrophe in c'è represents ci plus è. For a plural item, use ci sono: Ci sono due libri, there are two books. Do not keep c'è before every plural noun in the standard pattern being taught.

Common location phrases include a casa, at home; a scuola, at school; in biblioteca, in the library; and in ufficio, at the office. Italian preposition choice is partly conventional. English at does not map to one Italian preposition in every place expression.

Many prepositions combine with definite articles: in plus il gives nel, a plus il gives al, and su plus il gives sul. Il libro è sul tavolo means the book is on the table. In the plural, su plus i gives sui. Learn these as grammatical combinations, not as separate new vocabulary for every object.

Dove asks where. Dov'è il libro? combines dove and è through elision. A complete answer can name a place, while a short answer sul tavolo is natural if the question supplies the subject. The course uses full forms in practice where they make agreement and structure easier to inspect.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| qui | here |
| lì | there |
| c'è | there is |
| ci sono | there are |
| a casa | at home |
| in biblioteca | in the library |
| sul tavolo | on the table |
| nel libro | in the book |

### Worked examples

- `Il libro è sul tavolo.`: The book is on the table; sul combines su and il.
- `C'è una biblioteca.`: There is a library; the construction introduces its existence.
- `Ci sono due libri.`: There are two books; plural existence uses ci sono.

### Common trap

Do not confuse locating a known thing with introducing its existence. Also, c'è has an apostrophe and an accented è. Leaving either out can obscure the grammatical form.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: The pen is here. Use la penna.
2. Say: There is a book.
3. Say: There are two houses.
4. Ask: Where is the book?

***Answers:***

1. `La penna è qui.`
2. `C'è un libro.`
3. `Ci sono due case.`
4. `Dov'è il libro?`

### Why these answers work

È locates the known pen. C'è introduces one book, while ci sono introduces a plural group. Dov'è contracts dove è. The article remains attached to the noun phrase, and the verb reflects singular or plural reference.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a room first by listing what exists there, then by locating one known object. Explain why the first task favours c'è or ci sono and the second uses an ordinary essere sentence.


## Lesson 11: Going, coming and time phrases

**Outcome:** describe a destination and place an activity in a simple time frame.

### Think first

If a casa means at home, can the same phrase also follow vado to mean homeward? What role does the verb play in interpreting the place phrase?

### Frequent movement verbs

Andare is irregular: vado, vai, va, andiamo, andate, vanno. Vado a casa means I go home. Venire gives vengo, vieni, viene, veniamo, venite, vengono. Vengo a scuola means I come to school. The verb distinguishes movement relative to a reference point; English go and come provide a useful initial comparison, but real usage depends on the speaker's perspective.

Cities commonly take a: vado a Roma. Countries commonly take in: vado in Italia. These are useful conventional patterns rather than a claim that every geographical name behaves identically. A casa remains a frequent fixed expression for both location and destination, with the predicate supplying the difference.

Oggi means today, domani tomorrow and ieri yesterday. These words normally appear without an English-style on. La mattina can mean in the morning as a habitual time frame, while alle otto means at eight. A plus le forms alle before the plural clock expression.

The present can describe a planned future when a time expression makes it clear: Domani vado a Roma. This is natural Italian, not a missing future suffix. We will later learn explicit future forms, but a clear present plan is immediately useful.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| vado | I go |
| vai | you go, informal singular |
| andiamo | we go |
| vengo | I come |
| vieni | you come, informal singular |
| domani | tomorrow |
| ieri | yesterday |
| alle otto | at eight |

### Worked examples

- `Vado a casa.`: I go home; the verb supplies movement to the familiar place phrase.
- `Domani andiamo a Roma.`: Tomorrow we go to Rome; present tense expresses a plan with a clear time frame.
- `Vengo alle otto.`: I come at eight; alle marks the clock time.

### Common trap

Do not make andare into a regular ando in the first-person present. Also, a city and a country often take different prepositions, so do not transfer a Roma mechanically to every geographical destination.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I go to Italy.
2. Ask a friend: Are you coming tomorrow?
3. Say: We go home at eight.
4. Say: I go to Rome tomorrow. Put tomorrow first.

***Answers:***

1. `Vado in Italia.`
2. `Vieni domani?`
3. `Andiamo a casa alle otto.`
4. `Domani vado a Roma.`

### Why these answers work

Vado and andiamo are learned forms of andare, while vieni belongs to venire. In Italia and a Roma follow the taught geographical patterns. Domani can make a present-tense plan future in reference without an explicit future form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe where you are now and where you go tomorrow using the same destination phrase once. Explain how the verb and time expression clarify whether the phrase names a location or a journey.


## Lesson 12: Can, must and want with an infinitive

**Outcome:** combine a modal verb with an action without conjugating both verbs for the same subject.

### Think first

In voglio leggere, I want to read, which word carries the speaker information? Why does leggere keep its infinitive ending?

### One finite verb leads the action

Volere means to want, potere can or be able to, and dovere must or have to. Common first-person forms are voglio, posso and devo. They take a following infinitive: voglio leggere, posso venire, devo lavorare. The second verb names the action rather than receiving another first-person ending.

Informal singular forms include vuoi, puoi and devi. Courteous Lei uses vuole, può and deve. Può aiutarmi? asks “Can you help me?” politely. Aiutare means to help, and mi attaches to the infinitive after the final e drops: aiutarmi. This introduces a useful request while reusing the pronoun-attachment pattern mentioned earlier.

A question with potere often functions as a request rather than a literal test of ability. Posso entrare? asks may I come in. The difference between permission and physical capacity is supplied by context. Non posso venire says I cannot come, while non voglio venire says I do not want to come; those are socially and semantically different messages.

Dovere can express practical need or obligation, depending on the source. Devo leggere questo libro might describe an assignment or a personal necessity. Avoid assuming that the same English must always has exactly the same force in every Italian context.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| voglio | I want |
| posso | I can; may I in a question |
| devo | I must; I have to |
| vuoi | you want, informal |
| può | he or she can; courteous you can |
| deve | he or she must; courteous you must |
| aiutare | to help |
| entrare | to enter |

### Worked examples

- `Voglio leggere questo libro.`: I want to read this book; leggere remains infinitive.
- `Non posso venire.`: I cannot come; non negates the modal.
- `Può aiutarmi?`: Can you help me, courteously? Mi attaches to the infinitive.

### Common trap

Do not say voglio leggo for I want to read. The second verb must remain infinitive in this construction. Also, può needs its accent, and inability should not be confused with an unwillingness expressed by volere.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I have to work.
2. Ask permission: May I enter?
3. Say: I want to go home.
4. Ask a friend: Can you come tomorrow?

***Answers:***

1. `Devo lavorare.`
2. `Posso entrare?`
3. `Voglio andare a casa.`
4. `Puoi venire domani?`

### Why these answers work

Devo, posso, voglio and puoi carry the person information. Lavorare, entrare, andare and venire remain infinitives. The destination and time phrases belong to the intended actions and do not change the modal pattern.

### Retrieve and recombine

State one action you want to do, one you can do and one you must do. Then turn the ability statement into a courteous request, explaining which form changes for the listener.


## Lesson 13: Possessives and the thing owned

**Outcome:** identify an owner with possessive adjectives and ask whose object something is.

### Think first

Why does the same speaker say il mio libro but la mia casa? Does the possessive ending describe the owner or the object owned?

### Agreement follows the possession

Mio, mia, miei and mie mean my and agree with the noun's gender and number. The owner's gender does not choose the form. A man says la mia casa, and a woman says il mio libro. Tuo follows the same pattern for informal your; suo for his, her or courteous singular your.

Italian commonly uses a definite article with a possessive: il mio libro, la tua casa, i suoi libri. This differs from English my book without the. Some close-family singular expressions, such as mia madre, normally omit the article, with important exceptions when the phrase is modified or otherwise structured. We keep ordinary objects in practice so the main article-plus-possessive pattern stays clear.

Di chi? asks whose? Di chi è questo libro? means whose book is this. A short answer can be È mio, it is mine, where the possessive stands as a predicate and agrees with libro. È mia would refer to a feminine object such as casa or penna.

Suo can be ambiguous between his, her and courteous your. Context or a phrase such as di Maria can clarify the owner. Do not infer a biological gender from the possessive's ending, since that ending belongs to the possessed noun. Ownership and object agreement are separate pieces of information.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| mio | my or mine, masculine singular |
| mia | my or mine, feminine singular |
| miei | my or mine, masculine plural |
| mie | my or mine, feminine plural |
| tuo | your, informal masculine singular |
| suo | his, her or courteous your, masculine singular |
| di chi | whose |
| la mia casa | my house |

### Worked examples

- `Il mio libro è qui.`: My book is here; article and possessive agree with libro.
- `La tua casa è grande.`: Your house is big, informally; tua follows feminine casa.
- `Di chi è questa penna?`: Whose pen is this? The question asks for the owner.

### Common trap

Do not choose mio or mia according to the owner's gender. Also, the article is normally present with ordinary possessed objects, even though English does not say the my book.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: My house is here.
2. Say: My books are new.
3. Ask: Whose book is this?
4. Say: It is mine, referring to la penna.

***Answers:***

1. `La mia casa è qui.`
2. `I miei libri sono nuovi.`
3. `Di chi è questo libro?`
4. `È mia.`

### Why these answers work

Mia agrees with casa or penna, while miei agrees with libri. The plural subject takes sono and the plural adjective nuovi. Di chi asks about ownership without deciding the owner's gender. Predicate È mia needs no noun repeated.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine an object owned by a person whose gender differs from the object's grammatical gender. Describe it in that person's voice, then answer a whose question with the correct predicate possessive.


## Lesson 14: A completed past event with avere

**Outcome:** report a past activity using the passato prossimo with avere.

### Think first

Ho means I have, but ho parlato means I spoke or have spoken. What job does avere perform when it is followed by a past participle rather than an owned object?

### Auxiliary plus participle

The passato prossimo combines a present auxiliary with a past participle. Many transitive verbs use avere: ho letto un libro, I read a book; ho aperto la porta, I opened the door. Avere here is an auxiliary helping form the tense, not a claim of possession.

Regular participles often end in ato for are verbs, uto for ere verbs and ito for ire verbs: parlato, creduto, dormito. Many common participles are irregular, including letto from leggere, scritto from scrivere and aperto from aprire. Learn these frequent forms as vocabulary rather than manufacturing leggiuto from an unreliable generalisation.

With avere and a following full noun object, the participle normally remains in its default masculine singular form: Maria ha letto il libro. It does not change to letta merely because the subject is a woman. Agreement with preceding direct-object clitics is a separate pattern, introduced in the review notes rather than mixed into the first examples.

Ieri supplies yesterday: Ieri ho lavorato. The passato prossimo can correspond to an English simple past or present perfect according to context and regional usage. This lesson teaches ordinary completed-event narration without asserting that one Italian tense always maps to one English tense.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| ho parlato | I spoke; I have spoken |
| hai lavorato | you worked, informal |
| ha letto | he or she read; courteous you read |
| abbiamo scritto | we wrote |
| parlato | spoken, regular participle |
| letto | read, irregular participle |
| scritto | written, irregular participle |
| aperto | opened, irregular participle |

### Worked examples

- `Ieri ho lavorato.`: I worked yesterday; ho acts as an auxiliary.
- `Maria ha letto il libro.`: Maria read the book; letto does not agree with the female subject here.
- `Abbiamo aperto la porta.`: We opened the door; abbiamo carries person and number.

### Common trap

Do not change letto to letta just because the subject is female in this avere construction. Also, a common irregular participle must be learned: leggere gives letto, not an invented regular-looking form.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I read the book yesterday. Put yesterday first.
2. Say: We spoke Italian.
3. Ask a friend: Did you open the door?
4. Say: I did not work yesterday.

***Answers:***

1. `Ieri ho letto il libro.`
2. `Abbiamo parlato italiano.`
3. `Hai aperto la porta?`
4. `Non ho lavorato ieri.`

### Why these answers work

Ho, abbiamo and hai carry the subject information while the participles identify the action. Non precedes the auxiliary. The full noun object remains after the participle, and the default participle form stays unchanged in these examples.

### Retrieve and recombine

Retell one present activity as yesterday's completed event, then change the subject to Maria. Explain why the auxiliary changes while the avere participle does not acquire feminine agreement here.


## Lesson 15: Past movement with essere

**Outcome:** report a past journey and make the participle agree with its subject.

### Think first

Why does a woman say sono andata rather than ho andato for I went? Which two choices differ from the avere pattern?

### Auxiliary choice and agreement

Andare uses essere in the passato prossimo: sono andato for a male speaker and sono andata for a female speaker. Venire similarly gives sono venuto or sono venuta. Arrivare, to arrive, gives sono arrivato or arrivata. With these essere constructions, the participle agrees in gender and number with the subject.

A group of men or a mixed group says siamo andati. An all-female group says siamo andate. The auxiliary siamo identifies we, while the participle ending identifies the subject's gender and number. The same past event can therefore have a different final vowel when a different group reports it.

Do not reduce auxiliary choice to all movement verbs take essere. Camminare, to walk, normally takes avere in its ordinary intransitive use: ho camminato. The lexical verb and construction determine the auxiliary. Learn andare, venire and arrivare with essere as reliable anchors rather than extending a vague motion rule to every action involving movement.

A courteous question to a man can be È arrivato? and to a woman È arrivata? The verb uses third-person singular for Lei, while the participle normally follows the addressee's natural gender in this usage. The course keeps explicit prompts so the answer's agreement is not guessed from an unspecified English you.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| sono andato | I went, male speaker |
| sono andata | I went, female speaker |
| siamo andati | we went, male or mixed group |
| siamo andate | we went, all-female group |
| è arrivato | he arrived; courteous you to a man |
| è arrivata | she arrived; courteous you to a woman |
| venuto | come, masculine participle |
| camminare | to walk, normally with avere here |

### Worked examples

- `Sono andata a Roma.`: I went to Rome, said by a woman; the participle agrees with the subject.
- `Siamo arrivati ieri.`: We arrived yesterday, male or mixed group.
- `Ho camminato.`: I walked; this movement-related verb normally uses avere.

### Common trap

Do not choose essere solely because an English verb involves motion. Also, with the essere verbs taught here, the participle must match the subject's gender and number rather than remain permanently andato.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say as a man: I went home.
2. Say as a woman: I arrived yesterday.
3. Say as an all-female group: We went to Rome.
4. Ask a woman courteously: Have you arrived?

***Answers:***

1. `Sono andato a casa.`
2. `Sono arrivata ieri.`
3. `Siamo andate a Roma.`
4. `È arrivata?`

### Why these answers work

Andato and arrivata match singular speaker gender. Siamo andate combines first-person plural auxiliary with feminine plural agreement. The courteous question uses third-person è while arrivata reflects the female addressee.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe the same trip first as one woman and then as a mixed group. Identify the auxiliary and participle changes separately, and contrast the result with a past sentence using avere.


## Lesson 16: Background and habits with the imperfetto

**Outcome:** distinguish a past background or routine from a bounded past event.

### Think first

“I read the book yesterday” reports an event. “When I was a child, I read every evening” presents a repeated background. Why might Italian choose a different past tense?

### A past seen from within

The imperfetto commonly describes past habits, ongoing situations and background states. Parlavo means I was speaking or used to speak, leggevo I was reading or used to read, and dormivo I was sleeping or used to sleep. The common endings include vo, vi, va, vamo, vate and vano after the relevant thematic vowel.

Essere has irregular forms ero, eri, era, eravamo, eravate and erano. Da bambino ero curioso means “as a boy I was curious.” A female speaker says da bambina and curiosa. The imperfetto verb ero itself does not change for gender, while the surrounding descriptions do.

Compare Ieri ho letto il libro with Da bambino leggevo ogni sera. The first presents a bounded reading event; the second presents a habit. The distinction is not simply recent past versus distant past. A habit yesterday can still use an imperfective view, and an event long ago can still be bounded.

A background can be interrupted by an event: Leggevo quando Maria è arrivata, “I was reading when Maria arrived.” The imperfetto supplies the ongoing scene, while the passato prossimo presents the arrival. This is a useful narrative relationship, not a rigid requirement that every sentence containing quando use these exact two tenses.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| parlavo | I was speaking; I used to speak |
| leggevo | I was reading; I used to read |
| dormivo | I was sleeping; I used to sleep |
| ero | I was |
| era | he or she was |
| ogni sera | every evening |
| da bambino | as a boy; when I was a child, male |
| da bambina | as a girl; when I was a child, female |

### Worked examples

- `Da bambina leggevo ogni sera.`: As a girl I read every evening; the tense presents a habit.
- `Ero a casa.`: I was at home; ero sets a background state.
- `Leggevo quando Maria è arrivata.`: I was reading when Maria arrived; background and event use different viewpoints.

### Common trap

Do not define imperfetto as the tense for long-ago events. The key distinction here is viewpoint, habit or background, not the number of years elapsed. Also, ero is irregular and should not be built as essevo.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I was reading.
2. Say: I was at home.
3. Say as a man: As a child I worked every day. Use ogni giorno.
4. Say: I was sleeping when Maria arrived.

***Answers:***

1. `Leggevo.`
2. `Ero a casa.`
3. `Da bambino lavoravo ogni giorno.`
4. `Dormivo quando Maria è arrivata.`

### Why these answers work

Leggevo and dormivo present ongoing background activities. Ero describes a state. Lavoravo with ogni giorno presents a repeated habit. Maria's arrival is a bounded event with essere and feminine participle agreement.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe an imagined past routine, then add one event that occurred during it. Explain your tense choice by the event's role in the story rather than by how long ago it happened.


## Lesson 17: Explicit future and a polite conditional

**Outcome:** state a future action and use vorrei for a considerate expression of desire.

### Think first

Domani vado already expresses a plan. What does andrò provide when you want an explicit future form, and how does vorrei differ from voglio in a request?

### Future forms carry their own endings

The future has endings ò, ai, à, emo, ete and anno attached to a future stem. Regular are verbs normally change the stem vowel to e: parlare gives parlerò, I will speak. Leggere gives leggerò, and dormire gives dormirò. The written accent on final ò or à is part of standard spelling.

Frequent verbs have irregular future stems: andare gives andrò, avere avrò, essere sarò and fare farò. Learn these common anchors directly. Sarò a casa domani means I will be at home tomorrow. A present form with an explicit future time can also be natural, so the future is an available choice rather than a correction of every present-tense plan.

Vorrei is the first-person conditional of volere, commonly used for “I would like.” Vorrei un caffè is a courteous service request. Vorrei leggere means I would like to read. It is not formed by attaching a polite word to voglio; the verb form itself changes the stance.

The conditional has many other uses, including hypothetical situations, but today's active scope is the conventional polite desire form. Knowing one useful conditional expression does not mean every if sentence can be constructed without additional grammar.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| parlerò | I will speak |
| leggerò | I will read |
| dormirò | I will sleep |
| andrò | I will go |
| sarò | I will be |
| avrò | I will have |
| vorrei | I would like |
| un caffè | a coffee |

### Worked examples

- `Domani leggerò il libro.`: Tomorrow I will read the book; the future ending is explicit.
- `Sarò a casa.`: I will be at home; essere has an irregular future stem.
- `Vorrei un caffè.`: I would like a coffee; the conditional softens the request.

### Common trap

Do not omit the accents in leggerò or sarà. Also, vorrei is not a future form meaning I will want; it is a conditional used here for a polite wish or request.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I will speak Italian.
2. Say: I will go home tomorrow.
3. Request politely: I would like water. Use acqua.
4. Say: I will be here.

***Answers:***

1. `Parlerò italiano.`
2. `Andrò a casa domani.`
3. `Vorrei acqua.`
4. `Sarò qui.`

### Why these answers work

Parlerò follows the regular are-to-ere future stem pattern. Andrò and sarò use learned irregular stems. Vorrei expresses a polite desire and is followed by a noun, showing that an infinitive is not required when the desired item is named directly.

### Retrieve and recombine

State one explicit future plan, then make a courteous request about something you would like now. Explain why the future and conditional forms express different relationships to the action or item.


## Lesson 18: Reflexive routines and getting ready

**Outcome:** describe a personal routine with the appropriate reflexive pronoun.

### Think first

Lavare means to wash something, while lavarsi can mean to wash oneself. What tells the listener that the action returns to the subject?

### Pronoun plus conjugated verb

Reflexive verbs use mi, ti, si, ci, vi and si with the corresponding subjects. Mi lavo means I wash myself; ti lavi, you wash yourself informally; si lava, he or she washes, or courteous you wash. The verb keeps its ordinary conjugation while the pronoun marks the reflexive relation.

Alzarsi means to get up. Mi alzo alle otto means I get up at eight. Chiamarsi is used to give a name: Mi chiamo Maria. Its literal structure differs from English my name is, but it is an ordinary Italian introduction. You do not need to force a possessive noun phrase into every naming exchange.

Reflexive verbs use essere in the passato prossimo: mi sono alzato for a man, mi sono alzata for a woman. The reflexive pronoun comes before the auxiliary, and the participle agrees with the subject. This combines two patterns already learned without creating a new subject-gender ending on the present verb.

Not every verb with si has a simple literal self meaning. Italian has pronominal verbs and other si constructions that require separate treatment. Today's examples are transparent routines and the conventional naming pattern, so do not infer that every occurrence of si can be translated with oneself.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| mi | myself, reflexive first person here |
| ti | yourself, informal singular here |
| si | himself, herself or courteous yourself here |
| ci | ourselves here |
| vi | yourselves here |
| mi alzo | I get up |
| mi lavo | I wash myself |
| mi chiamo | my name is; I am called |

### Worked examples

- `Mi alzo alle otto.`: I get up at eight; mi matches the speaker.
- `Come ti chiami?`: What is your name, informally? The reflexive naming pattern is conventional.
- `Mi sono alzata presto.`: I got up early, said by a woman; the past uses essere and feminine agreement.

### Common trap

Do not use the same reflexive pronoun for every subject. Also, si has several grammatical functions beyond literal reflexivity, so the patterns here should not be extended blindly to every sentence containing si.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: My name is Luca.
2. Say: We get up at eight.
3. Ask courteously: What is your name?
4. Say as a man: I got up early. Use presto.

***Answers:***

1. `Mi chiamo Luca.`
2. `Ci alziamo alle otto.`
3. `Come si chiama?`
4. `Mi sono alzato presto.`

### Why these answers work

Mi and ci match first-person singular and plural subjects. Courteous address uses si chiama, the third-person form. The past reflexive uses mi before sono and alzato to agree with the specified male speaker.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe your morning routine and ask another person about theirs. Change from informal to courteous address, checking both the reflexive pronoun and the verb ending.


## Lesson 19: Liking with piacere

**Outcome:** express a preference while making the verb agree with the thing liked.

### Think first

Mi piace il libro is roughly “the book is pleasing to me.” Which noun is the grammatical subject, and what should happen when books becomes plural?

### The liked thing controls agreement

Piacere does not follow the same structure as English I like. Mi piace il libro means I like the book, with il libro as the subject and mi identifying the person who experiences the liking. With plural books, use Mi piacciono i libri. The verb changes because the liked thing is plural, not because the person liking it changes.

An infinitive can be the liked activity: Mi piace leggere, I like reading. The activity is treated as a singular subject, so piace is used. Mi piace il caffè expresses a general liking for coffee with the definite article, a common Italian pattern where English often has no article.

Ti piace? asks a friend whether they like something. Le piace? is courteous singular you or to her in an appropriate context. Gli piace means he likes it in the standard singular distinction taught here. Spoken usage of gli has wider functions in some contexts, but this foundation keeps the clear standard contrast rather than presenting one form as universal.

Non comes before the clitic: Non mi piace il caffè. This placement resembles the direct-object pronoun pattern, but mi here is an indirect experiencer, not a direct object. Similar position does not guarantee the same grammatical role.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| mi piace | I like a singular thing or activity |
| ti piace | you like, informal singular |
| le piace | she likes; courteous you like |
| gli piace | he likes |
| mi piacciono | I like plural things |
| leggere | reading, as an infinitive subject |
| il caffè | coffee, general category in this context |

### Worked examples

- `Mi piace leggere.`: I like reading; the infinitive activity takes singular piace.
- `Mi piacciono i libri.`: I like books; the plural liked subject takes piacciono.
- `Non mi piace il caffè.`: I do not like coffee; non precedes the experiencer pronoun.

### Common trap

Do not conjugate piacere as piaccio merely because English begins I like. The liked thing is the subject in this construction. Also, plural books require piacciono even when only one person likes them.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I like tea. Use il tè.
2. Say: I like the houses.
3. Ask a friend: Do you like reading?
4. Say: I do not like this book.

***Answers:***

1. `Mi piace il tè.`
2. `Mi piacciono le case.`
3. `Ti piace leggere?`
4. `Non mi piace questo libro.`

### Why these answers work

Il tè, leggere and questo libro are singular subjects and take piace. Le case is plural and takes piacciono. Mi or ti identifies the experiencer independently of the liked subject's number. Non precedes the clitic.

### Retrieve and recombine

State one thing and one activity you like, then change the thing to a plural group. Explain why the verb changes for the plural object of English liking even though you remain the same experiencer.


## Lesson 20: Direct requests and courteous alternatives

**Outcome:** ask someone to speak slowly or repeat while maintaining a consistent form of address.

### Think first

Parla can be a command to a friend, while parli can be a courteous command. Why is a request's form not always identical to the present statement you learned earlier?

### Imperatives depend on address

For many are verbs, the affirmative tu imperative ends in a: parla, speak; ascolta, listen. For many ere and ire verbs it ends in i: leggi, read; apri, open. These forms can be followed by per favore, please. Parla lentamente, per favore asks a friend to speak slowly.

Courteous Lei commands use present-subjunctive forms: parli, legga, apra. A full subjunctive course is beyond this lesson, but these useful request forms can be learned directly. Parli lentamente, per favore is therefore addressed courteously, while Può parlare lentamente? is an indirect alternative using a modal question.

Ripetere means to repeat. Ripeta, per favore is a courteous request, and Ripeti, per favore addresses a friend. Scusi is courteous excuse me or sorry, while scusa is informal. Keep the register consistent rather than combining scusa with a command chosen for Lei by accident.

Negative tu commands commonly use non plus infinitive: Non parlare, do not speak. Courteous negative commands keep the relevant subjunctive form: Non parli. This is another reason not to reduce all imperatives to the verb stem. The practice emphasises positive repair requests, which are immediately useful in conversation.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| per favore | please |
| lentamente | slowly |
| parla | speak, informal command |
| parli | speak, courteous command |
| ripeti | repeat, informal command |
| ripeta | repeat, courteous command |
| scusa | excuse me, informal |
| scusi | excuse me, courteous |

### Worked examples

- `Parli lentamente, per favore.`: Please speak slowly, courteously; the command uses the Lei form.
- `Ripeti, per favore.`: Please repeat, addressed to a friend.
- `Può ripetere?`: Can you repeat, courteously? The modal question offers an indirect request.

### Common trap

Do not assume that a courteous imperative is always the same as the ordinary present Lei form. Parli is a courteous command, while Lei parla is a statement. Context and the grammatical construction determine the form.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Ask courteously: Please repeat.
2. Ask a friend: Please speak slowly.
3. Say courteously: Excuse me.
4. Ask courteously with a modal: Can you speak slowly?

***Answers:***

1. `Ripeta, per favore.`
2. `Parla lentamente, per favore.`
3. `Scusi.`
4. `Può parlare lentamente?`

### Why these answers work

Ripeta and scusi belong to courteous address. Parla addresses a friend. Può takes an infinitive in the indirect request, so parlare remains unchanged. The adverb lentamente describes manner without agreeing with the listener.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine misunderstanding a station announcement. Ask an unfamiliar adult for repetition and slower speech, then repeat the same requests to a friend using the appropriate informal forms.


## Lesson 21: Numbers, quantities and shopping phrases

**Outcome:** request a small quantity and distinguish very from many or much.

### Think first

Molto stayed unchanged in molto grande. If it directly describes a quantity of books, should it now agree with the noun?

### Quantity words have their own agreement

Basic numbers include uno, due, tre, quattro, cinque and sei. After two or more, a count noun normally takes its plural: due libri, tre case. This differs from Turkish and some other languages where a number can keep the noun singular. Italian's own number agreement should guide the phrase.

Molto as a quantity word agrees with the noun: molto pane, much bread; molta acqua, much water; molti libri, many books; molte case, many houses. Contrast molto grande, where molto means very and remains an adverb. The grammatical job explains the difference.

Un po' di means a little or some. The apostrophe in po' is standard; pò with a grave accent is not the form taught here. Un po' d'acqua uses elision before acqua. Vorrei un po' d'acqua is a useful polite request without requiring a precise measurement.

To ask a price, use Quanto costa? for a singular item and Quanto costano? for plural items. Quanto here asks the amount and does not agree with an implied price noun. By contrast, quanti libri asks how many books, with quanti matching the masculine plural noun. These similar-looking forms serve different sentence roles.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| uno | one |
| due | two |
| tre | three |
| quattro | four |
| molti libri | many books |
| molta acqua | much water |
| un po' d'acqua | a little water |
| quanto costa | how much does it cost |
| quanti libri | how many books |

### Worked examples

- `Vorrei due caffè.`: I would like two coffees; caffè has an unchanged plural form.
- `Ci sono molti libri.`: There are many books; molti agrees with libri.
- `Quanto costano questi libri?`: How much do these books cost? The verb agrees with the plural subject.

### Common trap

Do not write pò for the standard shortened po'. Also, molto changes for quantity but stays fixed when it means very before an adjective. The same word shape can belong to different grammatical jobs.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Request: I would like a little water.
2. Say: There are three books.
3. Ask: How much does this book cost?
4. Produce the phrase: many houses.

***Answers:***

1. `Vorrei un po' d'acqua.`
2. `Ci sono tre libri.`
3. `Quanto costa questo libro?`
4. `Molte case.`

### Why these answers work

Un po' d'acqua combines truncation and elision with their apostrophes. Tre requires plural libri. Costa agrees with singular libro. Molte is a quantity adjective agreeing with feminine plural case, unlike adverbial molto in very big.

### Retrieve and recombine

Order a small number of drinks, then ask the price of one book and several books. Explain which nouns remain unchanged in the plural and which verbs must change for number.


## Lesson 22: Connecting reasons, contrasts and sequences

**Outcome:** combine short statements with a clear logical relationship.

### Think first

E joins two ideas, but perché explains a cause. If you replace one connector with another, have you merely changed style or changed the meaning between the clauses?

### Connect the relationship you intend

E means and, ma means but, and perché means because or why according to its sentence role. Leggo e scrivo joins actions. Voglio venire, ma devo lavorare contrasts a wish with an obligation. Sono a casa perché lavoro explains the reason for being at home.

The accent in perché is an acute accent, while è uses a grave accent. Standard spelling preserves that distinction. In a question Perché studi? means why do you study. In an answer Studio perché mi piace, the same word introduces a reason. Word order and context reveal its job.

Poi means then or afterwards. Prima means before or first in appropriate constructions. Prima leggo, poi scrivo presents a sequence. A simple e does not always guarantee chronological order, so choose poi when the order is part of the message.

Quando means when and can connect a time clause: Quando arrivo, leggo. Italian often uses the present for a future routine or plan in a clear context; do not impose an English rule about will in every clause. This foundation keeps real or planned events in the indicative and leaves hypothetical constructions requiring other moods for later study.

Each clause keeps its subject and verb relationships. When the subject is the same, an explicit pronoun can often remain omitted. When it changes, a name or pronoun helps the listener follow the connection.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| e | and |
| ma | but |
| perché | because; why |
| poi | then; afterwards |
| prima | first; before |
| quando | when |
| scrivo | I write |
| arrivo | I arrive |

### Worked examples

- `Voglio leggere, ma devo lavorare.`: I want to read, but I have to work; the clauses contrast.
- `Studio perché mi piace.`: I study because I like it; the reason follows perché.
- `Prima leggo, poi scrivo.`: First I read, then I write; the sequence is explicit.

### Common trap

Do not omit the accent in perché or confuse e with è. Also, a connector does not replace the modal's infinitive requirement: devo lavorare remains intact inside a longer sentence.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I read and write.
2. Say: I cannot come because I work.
3. Say: First I study, then I read.
4. Ask a friend: Why are you studying?

***Answers:***

1. `Leggo e scrivo.`
2. `Non posso venire perché lavoro.`
3. `Prima studio, poi leggo.`
4. `Perché studi?`

### Why these answers work

E adds two actions. Perché introduces a reason after the negative ability statement, and asks for a reason in the final question. Prima and poi organise a sequence without adding new tense endings. Each finite verb keeps its ordinary subject form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Explain one plan with a reason and another with a contrast. Then arrange two activities in order, identifying the meaning that would be lost if every connector were replaced by e.


## Lesson 23: Relative che and choosing a specific object

**Outcome:** identify a person or object through a short relative clause.

### Think first

“The book is here” and “I read the book” repeat the same noun. Can che connect them into the book that I read without adding another it inside the clause?

### A noun followed by a specifying clause

Che can introduce a relative clause as subject or direct object. Il libro che leggo è interessante means “the book that I am reading is interesting.” The noun libro is the object understood inside che leggo, so Italian does not also insert lo there. The sentence already identifies the missing object through che.

La persona che parla italiano è qui means “the person who speaks Italian is here.” In this case che is the subject of parla. The same relative word can therefore have two different roles depending on the clause. It does not change for masculine, feminine, singular or plural by itself, but the verb still agrees with its subject.

Questi and queste mean these for masculine and feminine plural nouns. Questo libro che leggo is a more specific noun phrase, though an ordinary sentence often needs less repetition. Use a relative clause when it genuinely distinguishes the intended object, not simply to make every sentence longer.

After a preposition, standard relative constructions often use cui or a form of il quale rather than bare che: the person with whom I work requires a different pattern. That is outside today's active scope. We practise subject and direct-object relatives only, keeping the new structure precise and reviewable.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| che | who, which or that in a relative clause |
| la persona | the person |
| il libro che leggo | the book that I read |
| la persona che parla | the person who speaks |
| questo | this, masculine singular |
| questa | this, feminine singular |
| questi | these, masculine plural |
| queste | these, feminine plural |

### Worked examples

- `Il libro che leggo è nuovo.`: The book that I am reading is new; che supplies the relative object.
- `La persona che parla è qui.`: The person who is speaking is here; che is the relative subject.
- `I libri che leggo sono interessanti.`: The books I read are interesting; the main verb agrees with plural libri.

### Common trap

Do not add lo inside il libro che leggo as if the relative clause still lacks an object. Also, bare che is not the universal relative form after every preposition in standard Italian.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: The book that I read is here.
2. Say: The person who works is here.
3. Say: The books that I read are new.
4. Say: This is the book that I want to read.

***Answers:***

1. `Il libro che leggo è qui.`
2. `La persona che lavora è qui.`
3. `I libri che leggo sono nuovi.`
4. `Questo è il libro che voglio leggere.`

### Why these answers work

Che is the understood object of leggo or leggere in three answers and the subject of lavora in the second. The main verb è or sono agrees with the head noun. Voglio still requires an infinitive inside the relative clause.

### Retrieve and recombine

Identify one book by what you do with it and one person by what they do. Explain whether che is a subject or object in each relative clause, then check the main verb's agreement.


## Lesson 24: A café, a station and a repair

**Outcome:** combine a courteous request, a location question and a clarification in practical dialogue.

### Think first

At a café you ask for an item; at a station you ask where a place is. Which familiar structures handle these two goals without requiring a new tense?

### Use the smallest complete turn

Vorrei un caffè, per favore is a courteous order. Quanto costa? asks its price. Il conto, per favore requests the bill. Short noun phrases can be complete service turns because the situation supplies the desired action; you do not need to force every request into a full subject-verb-object sentence.

At a station, Scusi, dov'è la stazione? asks an unfamiliar person for the station's location. If you are already inside a large station, a more relevant noun might be il binario, platform or track, or l'uscita, exit. Dov'è l'uscita? reuses the same location question with a new object of inquiry.

A destra means to the right, a sinistra to the left and dritto straight ahead in a route context. These are short directional cues, not a complete navigation system. If a reply is unclear, Non capisco and Può ripetere, per favore? let you repair the interaction.

Grazie means thank you, and prego can respond as you're welcome or serve other invitation functions in context. Arrivederci is a broadly useful goodbye in courteous settings. Ciao is more informal. Choosing a suitable closing maintains the relationship already established by Lei forms rather than switching register accidentally at the final turn.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| il conto | the bill |
| la stazione | the station |
| il binario | the platform or track |
| l'uscita | the exit |
| a destra | to the right |
| a sinistra | to the left |
| dritto | straight ahead |
| arrivederci | goodbye, courteous or neutral |

### Worked examples

- `Vorrei un caffè, per favore.`: I would like a coffee, please; the conditional supports a courteous order.
- `Scusi, dov'è l'uscita?`: Excuse me, where is the exit? The question names the place sought.
- `Può ripetere, per favore?`: Can you repeat, please? A modal question repairs understanding.

### Common trap

Do not treat every directional reply as something you understood merely because it contains familiar words. Ask for repetition or slower speech. Also, keep courteous scusi with matching Lei forms rather than mixing address styles unintentionally.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Request: The bill, please.
2. Ask courteously: Excuse me, where is the station?
3. Ask courteously: Can you repeat, please?
4. Say: Goodbye, courteously.

***Answers:***

1. `Il conto, per favore.`
2. `Scusi, dov'è la stazione?`
3. `Può ripetere, per favore?`
4. `Arrivederci.`

### Why these answers work

The bill request is complete in context. Scusi and può maintain courteous singular address. Dov'è contracts dove è, and la stazione remains a singular subject. Arrivederci closes the exchange without an informal switch to ciao.

### Retrieve and recombine

Run a three-turn café exchange and a three-turn station exchange. Include one misunderstanding in each and recover with a courteous request rather than inventing an answer you did not hear.


## Lesson 25: Cumulative review: tell a useful short story

**Outcome:** combine identity, present needs, past events and a future plan in a new interaction.

### Think first

You want to say who you are, where you went yesterday, what you are reading now and what you would like tomorrow. Which choices concern person, which concern noun agreement and which concern tense or politeness?

### Build from the message's purpose

Start with a simple identity: Sono studente or Sono studentessa. Add a location using essere or a destination using andare. A known book can be named once and then replaced by lo, while a feminine object takes la. The pronoun goes before an ordinary finite verb, and non precedes it in a negative statement.

For a past event, choose the auxiliary with the verb. Ho letto uses avere and the learned participle letto. Sono andata uses essere and feminine subject agreement. Do not classify every movement-related verb by a single shortcut; ho camminato is a reminder that lexical constructions matter.

For background or a past habit, leggevo gives a different viewpoint from ho letto. For a future plan, leggerò is explicit, while a present with domani may also be natural. Vorrei expresses a courteous wish rather than the same future meaning. These distinctions let a short account say more than a list of memorised phrases.

The final practice combines patterns already taught. Where an English prompt specifies speaker gender or address, use that information. Where context leaves alternatives possible, the answer key gives one natural reference rather than claiming that all different wording is wrong. Continued listening and fluent-speaker feedback are the next steps, especially for clitic agreement, regional tense preferences and nuanced adjective placement.

### Working vocabulary

| Form | Meaning and use |
|---|---|
| sono studentessa | I am a female student |
| ho letto | I read; I have read |
| sono andata | I went, female speaker |
| leggevo | I was reading; I used to read |
| leggerò | I will read |
| vorrei | I would like |
| lo leggo | I read it, masculine object |
| mi piace leggere | I like reading |

### Worked examples

- `Ieri ho letto, ma oggi devo lavorare.`: Yesterday I read, but today I have to work; past event and present obligation contrast.
- `Sono andata a Roma e domani tornerò a casa.`: I went to Rome and tomorrow I will return home, female speaker; tornerò is the regular future of tornare.
- `Vorrei leggere il libro che hai comprato.`: I would like to read the book you bought; comprato is the regular participle of comprare.

### Common trap

Do not mistake a short foundation for complete control of every Italian mood or pronoun pattern. In particular, preceding direct-object clitics can trigger participle agreement with avere, as in l'ho letta for a feminine object, a pattern needing further practice beyond the first full-noun examples.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say as a woman: I went home yesterday.
2. Say: I have to read this book.
3. Ask courteously: Do you speak Italian?
4. Say: I would like a coffee because I am sleepy.

***Answers:***

1. `Sono andata a casa ieri.`
2. `Devo leggere questo libro.`
3. `Parla italiano?`
4. `Vorrei un caffè perché ho sonno.`

### Why these answers work

Andata agrees with the specified female subject after essere. Devo takes an infinitive. Parla is the courteous third-person singular form in the question. Vorrei softens the request, and ho sonno uses the Italian avere construction for sleepiness.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create a five-turn encounter in a new setting: introduce yourself, ask a location, recount one event, state a plan and make a courteous request. Explain one agreement choice, one auxiliary choice and one register choice from your own sentences.
