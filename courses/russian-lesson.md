# Russian: A pattern-first foundation

This course teaches contemporary standard Russian in Cyrillic. It starts with short, neutral everyday sentences and uses respectful вы with unfamiliar adults. Russian does not have articles. Case endings, verb aspect and stress carry information that English often distributes across word order and auxiliary verbs.

Readings use a consistent teaching transliteration: zh ж, kh х, ts ц, ch ч, sh ш, shch щ, y ы, yu ю, ya я, yo ё, and an apostrophe for ь. This is a spelling aid, not a phonetic transcription. Acute accents mark lexical stress in teaching examples and readings; ordinary Russian normally omits these accents. Ё is inherently stressed in the vocabulary here. Unstressed о often sounds closer to a reduced a; do not pronounce every written vowel with equal force. Final voiced consonants are often devoiced. Palatalisation, especially the contrast signalled by ь, needs listening practice alongside this text.

### References and review notes

[Cornell University Beginning Russian grammar](https://russian.cornell.edu/grammar/toc.htm) was consulted for the organisation of noun cases and the distinction between past and person-marked nonpast forms. The explanations and learning examples below are original. The course selects common singular patterns rather than pretending to cover every declension or aspectual pair. Stress shifts, motion verbs and natural register remain priorities for fluent-speaker review.

## Lesson 1: Identity without an audible am

**Outcome:** introduce yourself and identify another person without adding an English-style present-tense linking verb.

### Think first

If Russian places “I” next to “student,” what information is already clear? Would a separate word for “am” contribute anything necessary in a simple introduction?

### The pattern

For present identity, use a subject followed by a noun: я студе́нт means “I am a student.” The normal present form of “to be” is absent in this construction. This does not mean Russian has no verb meaning “be”; other tenses will need one. It also does not mean every English “is” can be deleted mechanically. We are learning one specific sentence type.

The noun may distinguish a man from a woman. Студе́нт is masculine; студе́нтка is feminine. A person's grammatical description does not make the pronoun я change. Russian я works for every speaker. In professional language, the choice of gendered occupational terms can vary, so begin with the transparent student pair and common врач, doctor.

Ты addresses one person informally. Вы addresses several people or one person respectfully. Start with вы when meeting an adult whose preferred relationship to you is unknown. The spelling Вы is sometimes used for respectful singular address in correspondence, but lowercase вы is normal in many other settings. Do not read the capital as a separate pronoun.

Это introduces an identification: э́то врач, “this is a doctor.” Here это stays the same before a masculine or feminine noun. Russian has no equivalent of the indefinite article “a.” In context the same bare noun can correspond to English “a doctor” or “the doctor.”

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| я | ya | I |
| ты | ty | you, informal singular |
| вы | vy | you, respectful or plural |
| он | on | he |
| она́ | oná | she |
| студе́нт | studént | male student |
| студе́нтка | studéntka | female student |
| врач | vrach | doctor |

### Worked examples

- `Я студе́нт.` - `Ya studént.`: I am a male student; there is no spoken am.
- `Она́ студе́нтка.` - `Oná studéntka.`: She is a student; the noun is feminine.
- `Э́то врач.` - `Éto vrach.`: This is a doctor; это identifies someone.

### Common trap

Do not add есть to every identity sentence. Я есть студент is not the neutral introduction being taught here. Also, a missing English article is normal, not an unfinished sentence.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I am a female student.
2. Say: He is a doctor.
3. Identify someone: This is a female student.
4. Tell an unfamiliar adult: You are a doctor.

***Answers:***

1. `Я студе́нтка.` - `Ya studéntka.`
2. `Он врач.` - `On vrach.`
3. `Э́то студе́нтка.` - `Éto studéntka.`
4. `Вы врач.` - `Vy vrach.`

### Why these answers work

The first answer keeps я and changes the occupational noun. The second has an ordinary subject and noun. Это does not agree with студентка in the third. The fourth chooses respectful вы without inserting an unnecessary linking verb.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce two imaginary classmates with different student nouns, then identify their doctor. Which word changes for the person described, and which identifying word stays fixed?


## Lesson 2: Reading Cyrillic through useful words

**Outcome:** recognise a small set of Cyrillic spellings and use them to identify objects.

### Think first

You see мама, метро and банк. Which letters look familiar? Which apparent English letters might mislead you when you encounter вода or ресторан?

### Sound and spelling

Some Cyrillic letters resemble Latin letters and have familiar values: а, м, к and т provide useful footholds. Others are visual traps. В represents v, н represents n, р represents r and с represents s. Read each whole Russian word with its Russian values. Switching between alphabets halfway through a word produces a pronunciation that belongs to neither language.

The word вода́ has stress on the final syllable. Its first written о is reduced in standard pronunciation, while the stressed а is clear. Our reading vodá preserves the spelling relationship; it is not an instruction to give the first vowel a full English “oh.” The word метро́ has final stress despite its familiar international shape. Learning the stress with the noun is part of learning the noun itself, not an optional decorative detail.

Э́то works with objects as well as people. Э́то дом can mean “this is a house.” To ask what something is, use Что э́то? The common standard pronunciation of что is approximately shto, although our consistent letter-based reading writes chto. This is an important high-frequency exception to hear and recognise.

In these short examples word order remains simple. The question asks for an identity, and the answer supplies a noun. You do not need grammatical gender yet to build an это sentence, so use this construction to start reading real signs without waiting for a complete alphabet course.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| дом | dom | house |
| банк | bank | bank |
| метро́ | metró | metro |
| вода́ | vodá | water |
| рестора́н | restorán | restaurant |
| что | chto | what; commonly pronounced shto |
| э́то | éto | this is; this |
| ма́ма | máma | mum |

### Worked examples

- `Что э́то?` - `Chto éto?`: What is this? The question requests an identity.
- `Э́то метро́.` - `Éto metró.`: This is the metro; the final syllable is stressed.
- `Э́то вода́.` - `Éto vodá.`: This is water; no article is necessary.

### Common trap

Do not infer stress from the word's final letter, and do not pronounce Cyrillic р as English p. The reading line helps locate letters, but listening is needed for reduction and soft consonants.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Identify a building: This is a bank.
2. Identify a building: This is a restaurant.
3. Ask: What is this?
4. Identify a building: This is a house.

***Answers:***

1. `Э́то банк.` - `Éto bank.`
2. `Э́то рестора́н.` - `Éto restorán.`
3. `Что э́то?` - `Chto éto?`
4. `Э́то дом.` - `Éto dom.`

### Why these answers work

All three identifications use the same это pattern regardless of the noun. The question begins with что. Банк and дом are single-syllable words, so there is no competing syllable for lexical stress; ресторан has a marked final stress.

### Retrieve and recombine

Read дом, банк and ресторан without the readings. Then imagine pointing at a bottle instead of a building and answer the same question with the appropriate noun.


## Lesson 3: Questions and simple corrections

**Outcome:** check an identity and correct it politely using a negative sentence.

### Think first

If Вы врач means “you are a doctor,” could a questioning voice turn that same sequence into a question? Where would “not” go when denying the occupation?

### Two small changes

A yes-or-no question can keep the statement's word order: Вы врач? Russian uses question intonation rather than English auxiliary inversion. In writing, the question mark makes the intended function clear. In speech, listen for the raised pitch on the information being checked. This is not permission to use exactly the same melody for every question; it is a starting contrast with English “are you.”

Не normally precedes the word or phrase being negated. Я не врач means “I am not a doctor.” There is still no present linking verb. Нет is the independent answer “no”; it is not a substitute for не inside this sentence. A short correction can therefore begin Нет, followed by the actual corrected statement.

Да means “yes.” A learner can answer Да, я студе́нт, but a natural short response can simply be Да. The longer form is useful here because it rehearses the structure. Russian does not require a repeated full clause every time someone asks a question.

А can introduce a contrast or a return question: А вы? means “And you?” When one person identifies themselves, this compact response invites the other to do the same. It is not a universal translation for every English “and.” Later we will distinguish it from и, which straightforwardly joins matching items or propositions. For now, connect introductions with the same small vocabulary rather than adding many new occupations.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| да | da | yes |
| нет | net | no |
| не | ne | not |
| а | a | and; but, with contrast |
| Вы врач? | Vy vrach? | Are you a doctor? |
| А вы? | A vy? | And you? |
| учи́тель | uchítel' | male teacher; teacher |
| учи́тельница | uchítel'nitsa | female teacher |

### Worked examples

- `Вы студе́нт?` - `Vy studént?`: Are you a male student? Word order is unchanged.
- `Я не учи́тель.` - `Ya ne uchítel'.`: I am not a male teacher; не negates the occupation.
- `Она́ врач?` - `Oná vrach?`: Is she a doctor? Intonation makes it a question.

### Common trap

Нет alone answers no, while не belongs inside the negative statement. Do not write я нет врач. Also avoid English-style word order such as placing the noun before вы merely to make a question.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Ask respectfully: Are you a female teacher?
2. Say: I am not a male student.
3. Ask after an introduction: And you?
4. Say: She is not a doctor.

***Answers:***

1. `Вы учи́тельница?` - `Vy uchítel'nitsa?`
2. `Я не студе́нт.` - `Ya ne studént.`
3. `А вы?` - `A vy?`
4. `Она́ не врач.` - `Oná ne vrach.`

### Why these answers work

The question preserves subject before occupation. Both negative sentences put не immediately before the denied noun. The return question uses а because the conversation moves from the first speaker to the other person, creating a contrast of participants.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create a mistaken identification of a classmate, then correct only the occupation. Keep the subject unchanged and listen for the difference between the question and the correction.


## Lesson 4: Gender and describing a thing

**Outcome:** describe one familiar object with a matching adjective.

### Think first

A new house is но́вый дом, but a new book is но́вая кни́га. What changed, and what feature of the noun might explain it?

### Agreement as a visible link

Most nouns ending in a hard consonant are masculine, many ending in а or я are feminine, and many ending in о or е are neuter. These are strong starting patterns, not exception-free definitions. Words ending in ь need their gender learned individually. Grammatical gender is a property of the noun, including words for inanimate things; a book is not being described as biologically female.

The adjective “new” has the forms но́вый, но́вая and но́вое for masculine, feminine and neuter singular. In a noun phrase the adjective usually comes before the noun: но́вый дом. In a description, the known noun can come first: Дом но́вый, “the house is new.” Both use agreement, and neither needs a present-tense word for “is.”

Э́то но́вая кни́га identifies something as a new book. The это remains fixed; the adjective agrees with книга. Notice how two earlier patterns can operate inside one sentence without competing. Это introduces the whole identification, while новая belongs inside the noun phrase.

Do not try to manufacture every adjective by attaching these exact endings to any English word. Learn a Russian dictionary form and its stem. Some adjectives have spelling changes or different stress. The small pair new/old is enough to practise a genuine agreement decision and to describe objects around a classroom.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| но́вый | nóvyy | new, masculine |
| но́вая | nóvaya | new, feminine |
| но́вое | nóvoye | new, neuter |
| ста́рый | stáryy | old, masculine |
| кни́га | kníga | book, feminine |
| окно́ | oknó | window, neuter |
| дом | dom | house, masculine |
| ста́рая | stáraya | old, feminine |

### Worked examples

- `Э́то но́вый дом.` - `Éto nóvyy dom.`: This is a new house; both noun and adjective are masculine.
- `Кни́га но́вая.` - `Kníga nóvaya.`: The book is new; feminine agreement remains.
- `Э́то но́вое окно́.` - `Éto nóvoye oknó.`: This is a new window; the adjective is neuter.

### Common trap

Это does not force a neuter adjective everywhere. In это новая книга, the adjective describes книга. Also, a final а does not always indicate a female person: папа, dad, is grammatically masculine.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: This is a new book.
2. Say: The house is old.
3. Say: The window is new.
4. Say: This is an old book.

***Answers:***

1. `Э́то но́вая кни́га.` - `Éto nóvaya kníga.`
2. `Дом ста́рый.` - `Dom stáryy.`
3. `Окно́ но́вое.` - `Oknó nóvoye.`
4. `Э́то ста́рая кни́га.` - `Éto stáraya kníga.`

### Why these answers work

Новая and старая match the feminine noun книга. Старый matches дом, and новое matches окно. The placement of the adjective changes between identification and description, but the gender relation remains the same in both constructions.

### Retrieve and recombine

Choose two real objects from the table. Identify one with это and describe the other with its noun first. Explain which noun, rather than which English translation, determines the adjective ending.


## Lesson 5: Present actions and the speaker

**Outcome:** say what you read or do, using a regular present-tense verb.

### Think first

Compare я чита́ю with вы чита́ете. Which part stays recognisable, and which part tells us who is reading?

### A verb carries person

The infinitive чита́ть means “to read.” For this regular pattern, the present stem is чита-, and personal endings produce я чита́ю, ты чита́ешь, он чита́ет, мы чита́ем, вы чита́ете, они́ чита́ют. The accent stays on the а in these forms. You are not adding a separate word equivalent to English “do” or “am.” A single present form can describe a habit or an action in progress, depending on context.

Я чита́ю means “I read” or “I am reading.” Сейча́с, now, favours the ongoing interpretation. Ка́ждый день, every day, makes a routine explicit. Russian does not require two separate conjugations merely because English chooses between a simple present and a progressive.

Use the pronoun while building the paradigm. Later, a clear context may allow a subject to be omitted, but endings do not make every pronoun redundant in real discourse. A stated я or вы can clarify a contrast. A question such as Вы чита́ете? still uses the ordinary word order.

Рабо́тать, to work, follows the same visible pattern: я рабо́таю, вы рабо́таете. The stress differs from that in the infinitive's written presentation only if the word was mislearned; it is рабо́тать. Learn the whole word with its correct stress, then preserve the familiar pattern across the specific forms taught here.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| чита́ть | chitát' | to read |
| чита́ю | chitáyu | I read |
| чита́ете | chitáyete | you read, respectful or plural |
| рабо́тать | rabótat' | to work |
| рабо́таю | rabótayu | I work |
| сейча́с | seychás | now |
| мы | my | we |
| они́ | oní | they |

### Worked examples

- `Я сейча́с чита́ю.` - `Ya seychás chitáyu.`: I am reading now; the adverb gives current context.
- `Мы рабо́таем.` - `My rabótayem.`: We work; the ending identifies first-person plural.
- `Вы чита́ете?` - `Vy chitáyete?`: Are you reading? No extra question verb is added.

### Common trap

Do not keep the infinitive after a pronoun: я читать is not the normal present sentence. Also, the endings shown apply to this pattern, not automatically to every Russian verb.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I work.
2. Say: She reads.
3. Ask respectfully: Are you working now?
4. Say: They read.

***Answers:***

1. `Я рабо́таю.` - `Ya rabótayu.`
2. `Она́ чита́ет.` - `Oná chitáyet.`
3. `Вы сейча́с рабо́таете?` - `Vy seychás rabótayete?`
4. `Они́ чита́ют.` - `Oní chitáyut.`

### Why these answers work

Работаю chooses the speaker ending. Читает pairs with она, while читают pairs with они. The question retains respectful вы and its corresponding ending. Сейчас supplies time without creating an English-style progressive auxiliary.

### Retrieve and recombine

Turn a statement about your present activity into a respectful question to someone else. Change the person ending, then decide whether сейчас is necessary for the meaning you intend.


## Lesson 6: Direct objects and the first case change

**Outcome:** say that you read a book or buy a newspaper, with the object form the verb requires.

### Think first

The dictionary has кни́га, but “I read a book” is Я чита́ю кни́гу. Why might the ending change even though the object is still the same book?

### Case marks a job

A noun's case signals its grammatical role. The dictionary form is nominative, often used for the subject. A direct object of читать or покупать normally uses accusative. For a regular feminine noun ending in а, accusative singular changes а to у: книга becomes книгу. A regular я ending commonly becomes ю, as with неде́ля to неде́лю, though we will keep our practice to а nouns here.

Many masculine inanimate nouns have an accusative form identical to the nominative: Я чита́ю журна́л. A visible change is therefore not necessary for a noun to be in accusative case. The grammatical role is still object. Animate masculine nouns behave differently and will come later; do not extend the unchanged pattern to all words for people.

The verb remains near the subject in our neutral examples: Я покупа́ю газе́ту. Russian word order is flexible for information structure, but flexibility is not randomness. A beginner can reliably use subject, verb, object while learning the endings that make alternative orders possible.

Negate the action with не before the verb: Я не чита́ю газе́ту. This course initially uses straightforward accusative objects under ordinary contrastive negation. Russian also uses genitive in some negative contexts; that wider choice is not needed to say that you are reading a book instead of a newspaper.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| кни́га | kníga | book, dictionary form |
| кни́гу | knígu | book, direct object |
| газе́та | gazéta | newspaper, dictionary form |
| газе́ту | gazétu | newspaper, direct object |
| журна́л | zhurnál | magazine |
| покупа́ть | pokupát' | to buy |
| покупа́ю | pokupáyu | I buy |
| чита́ет | chitáyet | he or she reads |

### Worked examples

- `Я чита́ю кни́гу.` - `Ya chitáyu knígu.`: I read a book; the feminine object ends in у.
- `Она́ покупа́ет газе́ту.` - `Oná pokupáyet gazétu.`: She buys a newspaper; the noun is an object.
- `Мы чита́ем журна́л.` - `My chitáyem zhurnál.`: We read a magazine; this inanimate form does not visibly change.

### Common trap

Do not decide that case is absent whenever a word looks unchanged. Журнал is accusative in the example. Conversely, do not change feminine objects to у when they are actually subjects.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I buy a book.
2. Say: He reads a newspaper.
3. Say: We buy a magazine.
4. Say: She does not read the book.

***Answers:***

1. `Я покупа́ю кни́гу.` - `Ya pokupáyu knígu.`
2. `Он чита́ет газе́ту.` - `On chitáyet gazétu.`
3. `Мы покупа́ем журна́л.` - `My pokupáyem zhurnál.`
4. `Она́ не чита́ет кни́гу.` - `Oná ne chitáyet knígu.`

### Why these answers work

The first two answers use the regular feminine accusative ending. Журнал stays unchanged because it is masculine and inanimate. In the fourth sentence не negates читает; книгу still identifies the particular object under discussion.

### Retrieve and recombine

Start with Это книга, then make the book an object of an action. Say both sentences and account for the different final vowels without treating them as two unrelated vocabulary items.


## Lesson 7: Another present pattern and knowing

**Outcome:** distinguish a second common conjugation pattern and say whether you understand Russian.

### Think first

You have seen чита́ете. Now compare говори́те, “you speak.” Does every present verb need the same vowel before its personal ending?

### Learn a pattern with anchor forms

Говори́ть belongs to a common second-conjugation pattern: я говорю́, ты говори́шь, он говори́т, мы говори́м, вы говори́те, они́ говоря́т. Several forms contain и where читать has е. The stress in говорю differs from the other singular forms, so the accent marks deserve attention. An infinitive ending can suggest a conjugation class, but exceptions mean you should learn a few anchor forms with a new verb.

Я говорю́ по-ру́сски means “I speak Russian.” The adverb по-ру́сски describes the language of speaking. It is written with a hyphen and does not behave like an ordinary direct-object noun. Не́много means “a little” and can qualify the statement without changing the verb.

Понима́ть follows the earlier first-conjugation pattern: я понима́ю, вы понима́ете. Я не понима́ю is a useful repair sentence, especially before you can name exactly what caused the difficulty. Хорошо́ and пло́хо can describe how well an action is performed. They are adverbs here and do not agree with the speaker's gender.

Knowing a fact or person is often expressed with знать: я зна́ю. Speaking and understanding are related abilities, but neither guarantees the other. Keeping these verbs distinct allows an honest account of your current language ability and gives a listener practical information about how to continue.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| говори́ть | govorít' | to speak |
| говорю́ | govoryú | I speak |
| говори́те | govoríte | you speak, respectful or plural |
| по-ру́сски | po-rússki | in Russian |
| понима́ю | ponimáyu | I understand |
| не́много | némnogo | a little |
| хорошо́ | khoroshó | well |
| зна́ю | znáyu | I know |

### Worked examples

- `Я не́много говорю́ по-ру́сски.` - `Ya némnogo govoryú po-rússki.`: I speak a little Russian.
- `Вы понима́ете?` - `Vy ponimáyete?`: Do you understand? This verb uses the familiar first pattern.
- `Она́ хорошо́ говори́т по-ру́сски.` - `Oná khoroshó govorít po-rússki.`: She speaks Russian well; the adverb stays fixed.

### Common trap

Do not attach the читать endings mechanically to говорить. Also, хорошо is an adverb in these examples, not an adjective agreeing with она. English “good” and “well” need the same distinction in the translation.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I do not understand.
2. Ask respectfully: Do you speak Russian?
3. Say: We speak Russian.
4. Say: I know.

***Answers:***

1. `Я не понима́ю.` - `Ya ne ponimáyu.`
2. `Вы говори́те по-ру́сски?` - `Vy govoríte po-rússki?`
3. `Мы говори́м по-ру́сски.` - `My govorím po-rússki.`
4. `Я зна́ю.` - `Ya znáyu.`

### Why these answers work

Понимаю and знаю use speaker forms from the first pattern. Говорите and говорим use the appropriate second-pattern endings. По-русски remains an adverb regardless of whether the speaker is one person or a group.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give a two-part account of your ability using speaking and understanding. Change the speaker to a classmate, and retrieve the correct говорить form without rebuilding it from English.


## Lesson 8: Being somewhere

**Outcome:** state a location and ask where a person or object is.

### Think first

Дом means “house,” but в до́ме means “in the house.” What does the added preposition tell us, and why does the noun also change?

### Preposition plus case

For a static location, в and на commonly take the prepositional case. Many singular nouns use е in this case: дом becomes доме, школа becomes школе, and банк becomes банке. The preposition and ending work together. В до́ме means “in the house”; в шко́ле means “at school” or “in the school.” English chooses “in” or “at” according to its own habits, so do not demand a single English equivalent for every Russian preposition.

На рабо́те means “at work.” Some places conventionally pair with на, others with в. Learn the whole location phrase as you learn the noun. Physical surface meanings can help with на, but they do not explain every institutional expression.

Где means “where” for location. Где банк? asks where the bank is. The answer Банк здесь uses здесь, here, and does not need a linking verb. Там means there. These adverbs provide a useful bridge while you are still learning case endings.

В and на also occur with a different case for direction. For now, keep the meaning static: someone is already at the place, or an action takes place there. Asking “where are you?” and “where are you going?” requires a distinction Russian marks explicitly. This lesson gives the first half of that distinction before we add movement.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| где | gde | where, location |
| здесь | zdes' | here |
| там | tam | there |
| в до́ме | v dóme | in the house |
| в шко́ле | v shkóle | at school |
| в ба́нке | v bánke | in the bank |
| на рабо́те | na rabóte | at work |
| шко́ла | shkóla | school |

### Worked examples

- `Я на рабо́те.` - `Ya na rabóte.`: I am at work; no present linking verb appears.
- `Кни́га в до́ме.` - `Kníga v dóme.`: The book is in the house; the location takes prepositional case.
- `Где шко́ла?` - `Gde shkóla?`: Where is the school? The school itself is not put in a location case.

### Common trap

Do not change every noun after где. In Где школа?, школа is the subject being located. The prepositional form appears in an answer such as в школе, after the relevant preposition.

2. Say: The bank is here.

4. `Мы рабо́таем в ба́нке.` - `My rabótayem v bánke.`

### Why these answers work

## Lesson 9: Going to a destination

**Outcome:** distinguish a destination from a place where someone already is.

### Think first

Compare Я в шко́ле with Я иду́ в шко́лу. What extra meaning appears in the second sentence, and which final vowel makes that difference visible?

### Direction is not location

With movement toward a destination, в and на commonly take accusative. Thus в школу means “to school,” while в школе means “at school.” The preposition is the same, but the case changes. For masculine inanimate банк, the directional phrase в банк has an unchanged-looking accusative form; location is в банке.

Идти́ describes going on foot in one direction in the situations taught here. Its present forms include я иду́, ты идёшь, он идёт, мы идём, вы идёте, они́ иду́т. Е́хать is the corresponding common verb for one-direction travel by transport, with я е́ду and вы е́дете. These are not interchangeable just because English uses “go” for both.

Куда́ asks “where to?” Use it to ask for a destination. Где asks for a location. Домо́й is the directional adverb “home,” whereas до́ма means “at home.” Neither needs в in this construction. The change in stress and ending is worth hearing because the two very common words look similar.

Russian has a wider motion system, including repeated or multidirectional movement. This lesson deliberately handles a concrete trip underway or intended now. A sentence about your daily commute needs a different motion choice, which we will introduce after this destination contrast is secure.

| е́ду | yédu | I am going by transport |

| домо́й | domóy | homeward |
| до́ма | dóma | at home |
| в шко́лу | v shkólu | to school |

### Worked examples

- `Я иду́ в шко́лу.` - `Ya idú v shkólu.`: I am walking to school; the destination is accusative.
- `Она́ идёт домо́й.` - `Oná idyót domóy.`: She is going home on foot; домой is directional.
- `Куда́ вы е́дете?` - `Kudá vy yédete?`: Where are you going by transport?

### Common trap

Do not use где for every English “where.” A destination question normally uses куда. Also avoid в домой: домой already expresses motion homeward without a preposition.

## Lesson 10: Having and not having

**Outcome:** describe possession and the absence of a needed object.

### Think first

Russian often says something like “at me there is a book.” How does that differ from simply placing an object after English “have”?

### The possessor is a phrase

У меня́ есть кни́га means “I have a book.” У introduces the possessor and takes genitive. The pronoun я therefore becomes меня in this phrase. У вас uses the respectful or plural form of “you.” The item possessed is the subject-like item whose existence is asserted, so книга is not the direct-object form книгу from “I read a book.”

Есть is useful when asserting that something exists or is available. It is often omitted in descriptions where another feature is the focus, but this lesson keeps it in affirmative possession statements so the pattern stays visible. Do not move it into every identity sentence from Lesson 1.

Absence uses нет: У меня́ нет кни́ги. The absent item is genitive. A regular feminine а noun commonly changes to ы, but spelling rules produce и after certain consonants, including г. That is why книга becomes книги. Машина becomes машины. Masculine телефон becomes телефона. Learn these examples as a connected pattern without assuming a single ending covers all nouns.

Есть and нет do not agree with the possessor. Whether one person or several people have something, the phrase identifying the possessor changes while these existential words stay the same. This provides a practical way to ask whether a colleague has a phone or whether the group has a car.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| у меня́ | u menyá | I have; at me |
| у вас | u vas | you have; at you |
| есть | yest' | there is; exists |
| нет | net | there is no; not available |
| телефо́н | telefón | phone |
| телефо́на | telefóna | phone, genitive |
| маши́на | mashína | car |
| кни́ги | knígi | book, genitive singular here |

### Worked examples

- `У меня́ есть телефо́н.` - `U menyá yest' telefón.`: I have a phone; телефон is the available item.
- `У вас есть маши́на?` - `U vas yest' mashína?`: Do you have a car? The possessor is respectful or plural.
- `У меня́ нет телефо́на.` - `U menyá net telefóna.`: I do not have a phone; absence requires genitive.

### Common trap

Не and нет are not interchangeable. Use нет in the absence construction, and change the absent noun to genitive. У меня нет книга leaves the required noun change unfinished.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I have a book.
2. Say: I do not have a car.
3. Ask respectfully: Do you have a phone?
4. Say: I do not have a book.

***Answers:***

1. `У меня́ есть кни́га.` - `U menyá yest' kníga.`
2. `У меня́ нет маши́ны.` - `U menyá net mashíny.`
3. `У вас есть телефо́н?` - `U vas yest' telefón?`
4. `У меня́ нет кни́ги.` - `U menyá net knígi.`

### Why these answers work

Affirmative possession keeps книга and телефон in their dictionary forms. Negative possession changes машина to машины and книга to книги. The latter uses и after г, showing why spelling rules matter when applying a case pattern.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine preparing for a journey. State one thing you have and one thing you lack, then ask another traveller about one item. Distinguish the possessor phrase from the possessed noun.


## Lesson 11: My, your and whose

**Outcome:** connect an owner to a noun with an agreeing possessive word.

### Think first

Why does Russian use мой телефо́н but моя́ кни́га when the same person owns both items? Is the ending describing the owner or the thing owned?

### Agreement follows the possession

Мой, моя́ and моё mean “my” with masculine, feminine and neuter singular nouns. The form follows the grammatical gender of the possessed noun, not the owner's gender. A woman says мой телефон, and a man says моя книга. For respectful or plural “your,” the corresponding forms are ваш, ва́ша and ва́ше.

Это мой телефон identifies an object and its owner. Мой телефон здесь locates that object. The possessive phrase can serve different jobs in a sentence, just like a noun by itself. Later, possessive words also change for case; this lesson stays with nominative phrases so that one new relation is clear at a time.

Чей? asks “whose?” It also agrees with the noun: чей телефон, чья книга, чьё окно. The soft-sign spellings are meaningful. In the readings, ch'ya and ch'yo signal the written separation and softness; do not turn the apostrophe into an extra spoken syllable.

Do not confuse the possessive question with кто, “who.” “Who is this?” identifies a person; “whose book is this?” identifies an owner. A natural exchange can therefore move from Что это? to Это книга and then to Чья это книга? Each question narrows a different missing piece of information. This is more flexible than memorising one long ownership phrase for every object.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| мой | moy | my, masculine |
| моя́ | moyá | my, feminine |
| моё | moyó | my, neuter |
| ваш | vash | your, masculine |
| ва́ша | vásha | your, feminine |
| ва́ше | váshe | your, neuter |
| чей | chey | whose, masculine |
| чья | ch'ya | whose, feminine |

### Worked examples

- `Э́то моя́ кни́га.` - `Éto moyá kníga.`: This is my book; моя agrees with книга.
- `Ваш телефо́н здесь.` - `Vash telefón zdes'.`: Your phone is here; the owner may be one respected person.
- `Чья э́то кни́га?` - `Ch'ya éto kníga?`: Whose book is this? The question word is feminine.

### Common trap

The sex or gender of the owner does not choose мой versus моя. The noun does. Also, это remains unchanged in the identifying question even though чья agrees with книга.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: This is my phone.
2. Ask: Whose phone is this?
3. Say respectfully: Your book is here.
4. Say: This is my window.

***Answers:***

1. `Э́то мой телефо́н.` - `Éto moy telefón.`
2. `Чей э́то телефо́н?` - `Chey éto telefón?`
3. `Ва́ша кни́га здесь.` - `Vásha kníga zdes'.`
4. `Э́то моё окно́.` - `Éto moyó oknó.`

### Why these answers work

Мой and чей match masculine телефон. Ваша matches feminine книга, and моё matches neuter окно. The locative adverb здесь does not affect possessive agreement. All noun phrases here remain in nominative form.

### Retrieve and recombine

Choose an object owned by someone whose gender differs from the noun's grammatical gender. Describe it with “my” in that person's voice and explain why the possessive ending still follows the object.


## Lesson 12: Time words and everyday routines

**Outcome:** place familiar actions in time and distinguish a current trip from a regular journey.

### Think first

Does “I go to school every day” describe one trip progressing in one direction, or a repeated pattern of trips? Why might Russian choose a different motion verb?

### Repeated movement

Ходи́ть is used for going on foot habitually or in multiple directions in the situations introduced here. Я хожу́ в шко́лу ка́ждый день means “I go to school every day.” Contrast я иду́ в шко́лу, which describes a particular one-direction walk. The destination still takes accusative; changing the motion verb does not turn a destination into a static location.

For repeated travel by transport, е́здить supplies the corresponding pattern: я е́зжу, вы е́здите. These forms need learning as whole forms because the consonant changes. This is a small introduction to the motion system, not a claim that English habitual meaning always mechanically requires one verb in every context.

У́тром, днём and ве́чером mean “in the morning,” “during the day” and “in the evening.” In these common adverbial expressions there is no added в. Сего́дня, today, has a conventional pronunciation closer to sevodnya than its letter-by-letter spelling suggests. Our reading segódnya stays orthographic; remember this familiar exception alongside что.

A time phrase can come near the beginning to establish the setting: Ве́чером я чита́ю. Moving it later can change emphasis without changing the basic event. Begin with the time-setting order so a listener knows whether you mean a present routine or the activity happening right now.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| ка́ждый день | kázhdyy den' | every day |
| у́тром | útrom | in the morning |
| днём | dnyóm | during the day |
| ве́чером | vécherom | in the evening |
| сего́дня | segódnya | today |
| хожу́ | khozhú | I go on foot habitually |
| е́зжу | yézzhu | I travel habitually |
| всегда́ | vsegdá | always |

### Worked examples

- `У́тром я рабо́таю.` - `Útrom ya rabótayu.`: I work in the morning; the time word needs no preposition.
- `Я хожу́ в шко́лу ка́ждый день.` - `Ya khozhú v shkólu kázhdyy den'.`: I go to school every day on foot.
- `Ве́чером мы чита́ем.` - `Vécherom my chitáyem.`: We read in the evening; the time frame comes first.

### Common trap

Do not use идти for every occurrence of English “go.” Check whether the meaning is a particular directed trip or a repeated pattern. Also avoid adding в to the taught adverb утром.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I read in the evening. Put the time first.
2. Say: I work today. Put the time first.
3. Say: I go to the bank on foot every day.
4. Say: We work in the morning. Put the time first.

***Answers:***

1. `Ве́чером я чита́ю.` - `Vécherom ya chitáyu.`
2. `Сего́дня я рабо́таю.` - `Segódnya ya rabótayu.`
3. `Я хожу́ в банк ка́ждый день.` - `Ya khozhú v bank kázhdyy den'.`
4. `У́тром мы рабо́таем.` - `Útrom my rabótayem.`

### Why these answers work

The time expressions are ready-made adverbs. Хожу marks a repeated walking routine, while в банк still marks a destination. Работаем retains its first-person plural ending; placing утром first does not alter the conjugation.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe your imagined morning routine and then one trip happening now. Keep the destination identical so that the contrast between habitual хожу and current иду is easy to hear.


## Lesson 13: Past actions and gender

**Outcome:** recount a simple completed time period using common past forms.

### Think first

Compare я чита́л and я чита́ла. Both begin with “I.” If person is unchanged, what information does the different past ending express?

### Past agreement is different

For many verbs, a basic past form is built from the infinitive stem plus л and an agreement ending. Читать gives читал for masculine singular, читала for feminine singular, читало for neuter singular and читали for plural. In the past, gender and number replace the present pattern of separate endings for I, you and he. A male speaker says я читал; a female speaker says я читала. The pronoun remains я.

Мы чита́ли and вы чита́ли both use the plural form. Respectful singular вы also takes the plural verb form, even when addressing one person. Do not switch a respectful question to a singular past ending merely because only one listener is present.

Вчера́ means yesterday. It locates the event in the past but does not itself conjugate the verb. Вчера я работала is a natural statement about a woman's work yesterday. The past form of an imperfective verb can describe activity without asserting that a particular task was completed. “I read yesterday” does not automatically mean “I finished the book.”

The verbs in this lesson are deliberately regular. Some very common verbs have irregular or shortened masculine past forms, so the л pattern is a useful construction rule rather than a universal promise. Keep the contrast between grammatical agreement and event completion separate; aspect will add the second distinction in a later lesson.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| вчера́ | vcherá | yesterday |
| чита́л | chitál | read, masculine past |
| чита́ла | chitála | read, feminine past |
| чита́ли | chitáli | read, plural past |
| рабо́тал | rabótal | worked, masculine past |
| рабо́тала | rabótala | worked, feminine past |
| рабо́тали | rabótali | worked, plural past |
| ве́чером | vécherom | in the evening |

### Worked examples

- `Вчера́ я чита́л.` - `Vcherá ya chitál.`: I read yesterday, said by a male speaker.
- `Она́ рабо́тала до́ма.` - `Oná rabótala dóma.`: She worked at home; the past form is feminine.
- `Вы вчера́ чита́ли?` - `Vy vcherá chitáli?`: Did you read yesterday? Respectful вы uses plural agreement.

### Common trap

Do not use a present personal ending after a past л form. Я читала already contains the necessary past agreement. Also, reading in the past does not necessarily imply finishing the material.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say as a woman: I worked yesterday. Put yesterday first.
2. Say: He read the newspaper.
3. Say: We worked at home.
4. Ask respectfully: Did you read the book?

***Answers:***

1. `Вчера́ я рабо́тала.` - `Vcherá ya rabótala.`
2. `Он чита́л газе́ту.` - `On chitál gazétu.`
3. `Мы рабо́тали до́ма.` - `My rabótali dóma.`
4. `Вы чита́ли кни́гу?` - `Vy chitáli knígu?`

### Why these answers work

Работала reflects a female singular speaker. Читал reflects он, while работали and читали agree with plural мы and respectful вы. The objects газету and книгу remain accusative; changing tense does not erase their grammatical role.

Retell the same yesterday sentence first as a man, then as a woman, then as a group. Identify exactly which ending changes and which time and object words remain stable.


## Lesson 14: Was, were and earlier locations

**Outcome:** describe where someone was and what something was like in the past.

### Think first

Я дома is present. If you add вчера, does Russian normally leave the linking verb absent, or does past time need an explicit form of “be”?

### The missing present verb returns

Быть means “to be.” Its common past forms are был, была́, бы́ло and бы́ли. Я был до́ма is “I was at home” for a male speaker; я была́ до́ма is the corresponding feminine form. Stress moves in this small paradigm, particularly in была, so do not copy the same accent position blindly.

The location phrases from earlier lessons keep their ordinary static form. Она была в школе means she was at school, not that she travelled there. В школе remains prepositional because the meaning is location. A past-tense verb does not automatically imply movement or change of state.

To describe weather or an impersonal situation, neuter было is useful: Бы́ло хо́лодно, “it was cold.” The English “it” does not require a separate Russian pronoun in this construction. Холодно is an impersonal predicative word here. We will also use тепло, warm, as a ready-made counterpart.

Occupational identity after быть can involve instrumental nouns, a pattern beyond today's location-focused practice. Rather than forcing nominative forms from Lesson 1 into every past sentence, build reliable past locations first. This is an example of limiting a construction by its actual grammar: the English word “was” occurs in many patterns, and Russian does not handle all of their complements identically.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| был | byl | was, masculine |
| была́ | bylá | was, feminine |
| бы́ло | býlo | was, neuter or impersonal |
| бы́ли | býli | were, plural |
| хо́лодно | khólodno | cold, impersonal |
| тепло́ | tepló | warm, impersonal |
| вчера́ | vcherá | yesterday |
| до́ма | dóma | at home |

### Worked examples

- `Он был в ба́нке.` - `On byl v bánke.`: He was in the bank; static location is prepositional.
- `Мы бы́ли до́ма.` - `My býli dóma.`: We were at home; the verb is plural.
- `Вчера́ бы́ло хо́лодно.` - `Vcherá býlo khólodno.`: It was cold yesterday; no separate subject pronoun is needed.

### Common trap

Do not confuse была with a present feminine adjective. It is a past form of быть. Also, в школу would mean a destination, so it does not replace в школе merely because the event was yesterday.

### Practice

1. Say: She was at school.
2. Say as a man: I was at home.
3. Say: It was warm yesterday. Put yesterday first.
4. Ask respectfully: Were you at work?

***Answers:***

1. `Она́ была́ в шко́ле.` - `Oná bylá v shkóle.`
2. `Я был до́ма.` - `Ya byl dóma.`
3. `Вчера́ бы́ло тепло́.` - `Vcherá býlo tepló.`
4. `Вы бы́ли на рабо́те?` - `Vy býli na rabóte?`

### Why these answers work

Была agrees with она, был with the specified male speaker, and были with respectful вы. Было is the neuter impersonal form used with тепло. Both school and work retain the location phrases learned earlier.

## Lesson 15: Future activity with буду

**Outcome:** say what you will be doing or will do as an activity tomorrow.

### Think first

If читать is an infinitive, how might a future helper carry the person information while читать itself remains unchanged?

### A compound future

The imperfective future combines a form of быть with an imperfective infinitive: я бу́ду чита́ть, “I will read” or “I will be reading.” The helper has person forms бу́ду, бу́дешь, бу́дет, бу́дем, бу́дете, бу́дут. The infinitive does not change to match the subject. This is different from the ordinary present, where читаю itself carries the speaker ending.

За́втра means tomorrow. Завтра я буду работать establishes a future work activity without necessarily presenting a bounded task as finished. English may translate it with either “will work” or “will be working,” according to context. Do not try to infer a one-to-one mapping from an English auxiliary to Russian aspect.

Negation normally precedes the future helper: Я не бу́ду чита́ть. A question can keep the same order: Вы бу́дете рабо́тать? The helper, not the infinitive, changes when you address someone respectfully.

Буду can also stand as a future form of “be” with a location: Я бу́ду до́ма, “I will be at home.” There is no second быть infinitive in that simple location sentence. Compare “I will read” and “I will be at home” by looking at what follows буду: one contains an action infinitive, the other a place expression. This prevents the tempting but unnecessary sequence буду быть in the basic construction.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| за́втра | závtra | tomorrow |
| бу́ду | búdu | I will; I will be |
| бу́дет | búdet | he or she will |
| бу́дем | búdem | we will |
| бу́дете | búdete | you will, respectful or plural |
| бу́дут | búdut | they will |
| чита́ть | chitát' | to read |
| рабо́тать | rabótat' | to work |

### Worked examples

- `За́втра я бу́ду чита́ть.` - `Závtra ya búdu chitát'.`: Tomorrow I will read; читать stays infinitive.
- `Мы бу́дем рабо́тать до́ма.` - `My búdem rabótat' dóma.`: We will work at home; будем carries the we ending.
- `Она́ бу́дет до́ма.` - `Oná búdet dóma.`: She will be at home; no extra infinitive is needed.

### Common trap

Do not combine буду with a present form such as читаю. The activity construction requires an infinitive. Also avoid adding быть after буду in a simple future location statement.

3. `Мы бу́дем до́ма.` - `My búdem dóma.`
4. `Я не бу́ду рабо́тать.` - `Ya ne búdu rabótat'.`

### Why these answers work

Буду, будете and будем carry person and number. Читать and работать remain unchanged infinitives. Дома is a place expression, so the third answer needs only the future form of быть. Не precedes буду in the negative statement.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give tomorrow's location and one activity you expect there. Then change the speaker from yourself to a group and retrieve the helper form while leaving the infinitive untouched.


## Lesson 16: Aspect and a finished result

**Outcome:** distinguish reading as an activity from finishing a bounded reading task.

### Think first

If someone says they read a book yesterday, do you know whether they finished it? What additional meaning would “read it through” contribute?

### Two views of an event

Читать is imperfective. Прочита́ть is a common perfective partner that can present reading as a completed whole. Я чита́л кни́гу tells us about reading activity; Я прочита́л кни́гу presents the book as read through. The distinction is not simply past versus present, since both sentences are past. Nor is imperfective a guarantee that the activity was unfinished. It simply does not assert the same bounded result.

Perfective nonpast forms normally refer to future events. Я прочита́ю кни́гу means “I will read the book through,” not “I am reading the book now.” This contrasts with я буду читать книгу, which frames a future activity. Do not add буду to the perfective form or treat it as an ordinary present progressive.

The prefix про- in this pair contributes a useful result interpretation, but prefixes are not a machine for producing the correct aspect partner of every verb. Learn aspect pairs as vocabulary with their meanings. Написать and писать, for example, have their own pair relationship; not every verb acquires the same prefix.

In conversation, the task matters. A question about how you spent an evening may invite the imperfective. A question about whether a report is ready may invite a completed result. This lesson practises one transparent pair so you can make that meaning decision before confronting a large list of irregular partners.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| чита́ть | chitát' | to read, imperfective |
| прочита́ть | prochitát' | to read through, perfective |
| прочита́л | prochitál | read through, masculine past |
| прочита́ла | prochitála | read through, feminine past |
| прочита́ю | prochitáyu | I will read through |
| прочита́ете | prochitáyete | you will read through |
| уже́ | uzhé | already |
| пото́м | potóm | afterwards; later |

### Worked examples

- `Я уже́ прочита́л кни́гу.` - `Ya uzhé prochitál knígu.`: I have already read the book through, male speaker.
- `Она́ чита́ла ве́чером.` - `Oná chitála vécherom.`: She read in the evening; activity is foregrounded.
- `Я прочита́ю газе́ту пото́м.` - `Ya prochitáyu gazétu potóm.`: I will read the newspaper through later.

### Common trap

Do not call прочитаю a present action just because its endings resemble читаю. Perfective nonpast forms have future reference in these ordinary statements. Also, imperfective past does not logically prove noncompletion.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say as a woman: I have already read the book through.
2. Say: I will read the book through.
3. Say as a man: I was reading yesterday. Put yesterday first.
4. Ask respectfully: Will you read the newspaper through?

***Answers:***

1. `Я уже́ прочита́ла кни́гу.` - `Ya uzhé prochitála knígu.`
2. `Я прочита́ю кни́гу.` - `Ya prochitáyu knígu.`
3. `Вчера́ я чита́л.` - `Vcherá ya chitál.`
4. `Вы прочита́ете газе́ту?` - `Vy prochitáyete gazétu?`

### Why these answers work

Прочитала combines a completed reading event with feminine past agreement. Прочитаю and прочитаете are perfective future forms. Читал presents past reading activity without adding a completion claim. The object endings remain accusative in either aspect.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine one book you are still reading and another you have finished. Describe yesterday's activity and the finished result separately, then make a bounded promise about tomorrow's reading.


## Lesson 17: Wanting and being able

**Outcome:** combine a finite verb of wanting or ability with an infinitive.

### Think first

In “I want to read,” which verb tells us who wants something? Should the second verb also acquire an I ending?

### One finite verb, one infinitive

Я хочу́ чита́ть means “I want to read.” Хочу is the speaker form of хоте́ть, to want. The infinitive читать names the desired activity and stays unchanged. Common forms include ты хо́чешь, он хо́чет, мы хоти́м, вы хоти́те and они́ хотя́т. This mixed-looking pattern is frequent enough to learn directly rather than trying to force it into one regular table.

Мочь means “can” or “be able to.” Я могу́ reads “I can,” while вы мо́жете is respectful or plural “you can.” Я могу прочитать книгу can mean that I am able to read the book through. The finite ability verb does not remove the aspect distinction from the infinitive: читать views an activity, прочитать a bounded result.

A question with можете can also function as a request, especially when followed by a helpful action: Вы можете помочь? means “Can you help?” Помо́чь is the infinitive “to help.” This sentence asks about practical willingness or possibility without giving a bare command.

In English, “want” sometimes sounds abrupt in a service interaction, and Russian тоже requires attention to context. Я хочу is a neutral statement of desire, but polite ordering often uses other constructions or пожалуйста. Grammar supplies the sentence; a considerate context and tone supply the social fit.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| хоте́ть | khotét' | to want |
| хочу́ | khochú | I want |
| хоти́те | khotíte | you want, respectful or plural |
| мочь | moch' | to be able |
| могу́ | mogú | I can |
| мо́жете | mózhete | you can, respectful or plural |
| помо́чь | pomóch' | to help |
| то́же | tózhe | also; too |

### Worked examples

- `Я хочу́ чита́ть.` - `Ya khochú chitát'.`: I want to read; only хочу carries person.
- `Мы хоти́м рабо́тать.` - `My khotím rabótat'.`: We want to work; the infinitive remains stable.
- `Вы мо́жете помо́чь?` - `Vy mózhete pomóch'?`: Can you help? This can serve as a polite request.

### Common trap

Do not say я хочу читаю. The second verb must be an infinitive. Also, могу and можете do not form a regular-looking pair by simply replacing the last vowel; learn the stem alternation.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I want to read the book through.
2. Say: I can help.
3. Ask respectfully: Do you want to work?
4. Say: I cannot read Russian. Use читать по-русски.

***Answers:***

1. `Я хочу́ прочита́ть кни́гу.` - `Ya khochú prochitát' knígu.`
2. `Я могу́ помо́чь.` - `Ya mogú pomóch'.`
3. `Вы хоти́те рабо́тать?` - `Vy khotíte rabótat'?`
4. `Я не могу́ чита́ть по-ру́сски.` - `Ya ne mogú chitát' po-rússki.`

### Why these answers work

Хочу and могу identify the speaker, while хотите matches вы. Прочитать selects a bounded reading task; читать in the last answer names an ability more generally. Не negates могу, and the following infinitive stays unchanged.

### Retrieve and recombine

State one thing you want to do and one thing you can do. Then ask another person for help using the respectful ability form, paying attention to which verb remains infinitive.


## Lesson 18: Need, permission and the dative experiencer

**Outcome:** say what you need to do and ask whether an action is allowed.

### Think first

Russian says мне ну́жно чита́ть, roughly “to me it is necessary to read.” How is that experiencer phrase different from the subject я in я читаю?

### Necessity without a personal subject

Мне is the dative form corresponding to я. Мне ну́жно рабо́тать means “I need to work.” The word нужно does not acquire an I ending. Вам is the corresponding dative for respectful or plural вы: Вам нужно читать. Dative marks the person to whom the need applies.

На́до is another common necessity word used with an infinitive. Мне надо идти means “I need to go.” We use нужно consistently in the answer keys, but knowing надо will help you recognise ordinary speech. This is not a contrast between correct and incorrect alternatives.

Мо́жно asks or states permission or possibility in an impersonal construction. Мо́жно войти́? means “May I come in?” The infinitive войти means “to enter.” The person is often understood from context. Нельзя́ expresses prohibition or impossibility: Здесь нельзя курить means “Smoking is not allowed here.” Курить is “to smoke.”

Compare я могу читать, “I am able to read,” with здесь можно читать, “reading is allowed or possible here.” English “can” can conceal this difference. Russian offers different patterns for personal ability and situational permission. Recognising who or what is responsible for the possibility helps you choose the construction rather than translating can word by word.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| мне | mne | to me, dative |
| вам | vam | to you, dative |
| ну́жно | núzhno | it is necessary |
| на́до | nádo | need to, common synonym |
| мо́жно | mózhno | it is allowed or possible |
| нельзя́ | nel'zyá | not allowed or impossible |
| войти́ | voytí | to enter |
| кури́ть | kurít' | to smoke |

### Worked examples

- `Мне ну́жно рабо́тать.` - `Mne núzhno rabótat'.`: I need to work; the person is dative.
- `Мо́жно войти́?` - `Mózhno voytí?`: May I come in? The requester is understood.
- `Здесь нельзя́ кури́ть.` - `Zdes' nel'zyá kurít'.`: Smoking is not allowed here; the rule is impersonal.

### Common trap

Do not combine я with нужно as if нужно were a personal verb. Use мне for the person experiencing the need. Also, нельзя is not formed by placing не before можно in this basic prohibition pattern.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I need to read.
2. Say respectfully: You need to work.
3. Ask: May I read here?
4. Say: Entry is not allowed. Use войти.

***Answers:***

1. `Мне ну́жно чита́ть.` - `Mne núzhno chitát'.`
2. `Вам ну́жно рабо́тать.` - `Vam núzhno rabótat'.`
3. `Здесь мо́жно чита́ть?` - `Zdes' mózhno chitát'?`
4. `Нельзя́ войти́.` - `Nel'zyá voytí.`

### Why these answers work

Мне and вам mark the affected person. Нужно stays fixed and takes an infinitive. Можно and нельзя describe the permission or possibility of the whole action rather than conjugating for a personal subject.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine a library rule and a personal task you need to finish there. Express the rule with можно or нельзя and your own need with мне нужно, keeping those two sources of obligation distinct.


## Lesson 19: Polite requests and instructions

**Outcome:** ask someone respectfully to repeat, speak slowly or provide an object.

### Think first

A direct command can be softened by a respectful form and пожалуйста. Which matters more: adding “please” mechanically or choosing a form appropriate to the listener?

### Respectful imperatives

Повтори́те means “repeat” when addressing someone respectfully or addressing several people. Говори́те is the corresponding imperative “speak,” and скажи́те means “say” or “tell.” The plural or respectful imperative often has те, but imperative formation is not identical for every verb. Learn the useful forms here as complete constructions.

Пожа́луйста means “please” in requests and can also answer thanks as “you are welcome.” Place it naturally before or after the main request, separated by commas in writing. Повторите, пожалуйста is a practical classroom repair. Ме́дленно means “slowly”; Говорите медленно, пожалуйста asks for an ongoing manner of speech rather than one repeated sentence.

Да́йте means “give” respectfully or to several people. Дайте, пожалуйста, книгу asks for the book. The object remains accusative, so книга changes to книгу. The polite request does not suspend the case rules introduced with reading and buying.

A bare imperative may sound abrupt in some situations even when it is grammatically respectful. A question with Вы можете can provide a more indirect alternative. We practise both styles across the course so you can choose based on context. Do not infer that one fixed phrase guarantees politeness regardless of tone, urgency or relationship.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| повтори́те | povtoríte | repeat, respectful imperative |
| говори́те | govoríte | speak, respectful imperative |
| скажи́те | skazhíte | say or tell, respectful imperative |
| да́йте | dáyte | give, respectful imperative |
| пожа́луйста | pozháluysta | please; you are welcome |
| ме́дленно | médlenno | slowly |
| спаси́бо | spasíbo | thank you |
| ещё раз | yeshchó raz | one more time |

### Worked examples

- `Повтори́те, пожа́луйста.` - `Povtoríte, pozháluysta.`: Please repeat; the request uses a respectful form.
- `Говори́те ме́дленно, пожа́луйста.` - `Govoríte médlenno, pozháluysta.`: Please speak slowly; the adverb describes manner.
- `Да́йте, пожа́луйста, кни́гу.` - `Dáyte, pozháluysta, knígu.`: Please give me the book; книгу is accusative.

### Common trap

Говорите can also be the present form “you speak”; context and intonation distinguish the request. Do not assume that identical spelling always means identical sentence function.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Ask respectfully: Please repeat one more time.
2. Ask respectfully: Please give me the newspaper.
3. Say: Thank you.
4. Ask respectfully: Please speak slowly.

***Answers:***

1. `Повтори́те ещё раз, пожа́луйста.` - `Povtoríte yeshchó raz, pozháluysta.`
2. `Да́йте, пожа́луйста, газе́ту.` - `Dáyte, pozháluysta, gazétu.`
3. `Спаси́бо.` - `Spasíbo.`
4. `Говори́те ме́дленно, пожа́луйста.` - `Govoríte médlenno, pozháluysta.`

### Why these answers work

Повторите and дайте are learned respectful imperative forms. Газету is the direct object of giving, so its ending changes. Медленно is an adverb and stays unchanged. Спасибо is a complete conventional response without an added verb.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine missing an announcement at a station. Ask for repetition, then ask for slower speech if needed. Replace the requested newspaper with a book and retain the same polite request structure.


## Lesson 20: With someone and the instrumental

**Outcome:** say whom you are with and identify simple means of writing.

### Think first

С means “with,” but “with a friend” is с дру́гом rather than с друг. What new job does the ending signal?

### Companionship and instrument

С takes instrumental case when it means “with” in a companionship phrase. A common masculine singular ending is ом: друг becomes другом. A common feminine ending is ой: подру́га becomes подру́гой. Я работаю с другом means “I work with a male friend.” The person is neither the subject nor a direct object; the case identifies a companion.

The pronoun pattern is irregular enough to learn directly: со мной means “with me,” and с вами means “with you.” The extra о in со makes the consonant sequence easier in this conventional form. Do not insert it before every noun following с.

Instrumental can also name an instrument without с: Я пишу́ ру́чкой, “I write with a pen.” Ру́чка becomes ручкой. English uses “with” for both a companion and a tool, but Russian distinguishes them by the presence of the preposition in these basic examples. Писа́ть has the present forms я пишу́ and вы пи́шете, which must be learned with their consonant and stress changes.

The instrumental has other roles, including some complements of быть, but this lesson keeps its practical scope to companions and tools. Recombining two previously learned actions with these phrases gives you a new case meaning without requiring a full six-case chart at once.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| друг | drug | male friend |
| с дру́гом | s drúgom | with a male friend |
| подру́га | podrúga | female friend |
| с подру́гой | s podrúgoy | with a female friend |
| со мной | so mnoy | with me |
| с ва́ми | s vámi | with you |
| ру́чкой | rúchkoy | with a pen, instrumental |
| пишу́ | pishú | I write |

### Worked examples

- `Я рабо́таю с дру́гом.` - `Ya rabótayu s drúgom.`: I work with a male friend; с marks companionship.
- `Она́ здесь со мной.` - `Oná zdes' so mnoy.`: She is here with me; the pronoun has a special form.
- `Я пишу́ ру́чкой.` - `Ya pishú rúchkoy.`: I write with a pen; the tool has instrumental without с.

### Common trap

Do not add с before ручкой when simply naming the writing instrument. Conversely, instrumental alone is not the normal way to say “together with a friend” in the companionship pattern taught here.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I work with a female friend.
2. Say: He is here with you. Use respectful you.
3. Say: I write with a pen.
4. Ask respectfully: Are you with me?

***Answers:***

1. `Я рабо́таю с подру́гой.` - `Ya rabótayu s podrúgoy.`
2. `Он здесь с ва́ми.` - `On zdes' s vámi.`
3. `Я пишу́ ру́чкой.` - `Ya pishú rúchkoy.`
4. `Вы со мной?` - `Vy so mnoy?`

### Why these answers work

Подругой and вами follow с for companionship. Ручкой names an instrument without a preposition. Со мной is a fixed pronoun combination. The final question still follows the no-present-copula pattern learned at the start.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a person you work with and a tool you write with. Explain why the English word “with” produces a preposition in one Russian sentence but only an instrumental ending in the other.


## Lesson 21: Small quantities and number patterns

**Outcome:** count a few familiar objects and recognise why the noun changes after numbers.

### Think first

Compare одна́ кни́га, две кни́ги and пять книг. Why would Russian use three different noun shapes within a single counting sequence?

### Count phrases have grammar

Один agrees with the noun: один телефон, одна книга, одно окно. For the number two, два is used with masculine and neuter nouns, while две is used with feminine nouns. In ordinary nominative counting phrases, two, three and four take a genitive singular noun: две книги, три телефона. Five and higher in this basic range take genitive plural: пять книг, пять телефонов.

These are counting constructions, not ordinary adjective agreement. Книги after две looks like a familiar plural spelling, but the traditional analysis here is genitive singular. The genitive plural книг has no added ending. Телефонов uses ов. There is no single genitive-plural ending that can be attached to every Russian noun.

Ско́лько? asks “how many” or “how much.” Сколько книг? uses genitive plural. A short answer can be a number phrase, such as три книги. We stay with numbers one through five so you can hear the noun-shape changes before adding larger numbers and their last-digit patterns.

Do not infer that one, two and five will always have exactly these forms in every case environment. A number phrase can itself be governed by a preposition or another construction. This lesson teaches simple counting and availability: here is one book, here are two books, I have five books. Other contexts need further case work.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| оди́н | odín | one, masculine |
| одна́ | odná | one, feminine |
| одно́ | odnó | one, neuter |
| два | dva | two, masculine or neuter |
| две | dve | two, feminine |
| три | tri | three |
| четы́ре | chetýre | four |
| пять | pyat' | five |
| ско́лько | skól'ko | how many; how much |

### Worked examples

- `У меня́ есть две кни́ги.` - `U menyá yest' dve knígi.`: I have two books; two takes the taught genitive singular pattern.
- `Э́то оди́н телефо́н.` - `Éto odín telefón.`: This is one phone; один is masculine.
- `Ско́лько книг?` - `Skól'ko knig?`: How many books? The noun is genitive plural.

### Common trap

Do not use the same noun form after every number. Also, feminine две belongs with книга, while masculine два belongs with телефон. English “two” hides a distinction that Russian makes visible.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: one book.
2. Say: three phones.
3. Say: five books.
4. Say: I have two phones.

***Answers:***

1. `Одна́ кни́га.` - `Odná kníga.`
2. `Три телефо́на.` - `Tri telefóna.`
3. `Пять книг.` - `Pyat' knig.`
4. `У меня́ есть два телефо́на.` - `U menyá yest' dva telefóna.`

### Why these answers work

Одна agrees with feminine книга. Три and два require телефона in these counting phrases. Пять requires the genitive plural книг. The possession construction leaves the number phrase intact as the quantity whose existence is asserted.

### Retrieve and recombine

Count one, two and five books without looking. Then switch to phones and identify both changes: the gender form of two and the noun's genitive plural ending.


## Lesson 22: Connecting reasons and contrasts

**Outcome:** connect two short statements without losing their individual grammar.

### Think first

If you already know “I read” and “she works,” what changes when you link them with “and” or “but”? Do the verbs suddenly need a shared ending?

### Small connectors, complete clauses

И joins parallel items or statements: Я читаю, и она работает. Each clause keeps its own subject and verb agreement. Но introduces a stronger contrast or obstacle: Я хочу читать, но мне нужно работать. The first clause describes desire and the second necessity, so the two different constructions remain intact.

А often contrasts topics or presents a counterpart: Я дома, а она на работе. English might translate this with “and” or “but.” The point is not that а has one secret English equivalent, but that it frequently sets one participant or situation against another. This develops the return question А вы? from the introduction lessons.

Потому́ что means “because” and introduces a reason. Я дома, потому что мне нужно читать means “I am at home because I need to read.” The reason is a full clause. In writing, a comma normally separates these clauses in the examples taught here.

Word order within the second clause does not need English auxiliary inversion or a special verb-final rule. You can connect reliable small units first, then learn more complex subordinate constructions later. The practical skill is to select the relationship you mean: addition, contrast, counterpart or cause. Longer sentences are useful only when that relationship is clear to the listener.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| и | i | and, addition |
| но | no | but, contrast |
| а | a | and or but, counterpart |
| потому́ что | potomú chto | because |
| поэ́тому | poétomu | therefore; so |
| то́же | tózhe | also |
| сего́дня | segódnya | today |
| за́втра | závtra | tomorrow |

### Worked examples

- `Я чита́ю, а она́ рабо́тает.` - `Ya chitáyu, a oná rabótayet.`: I am reading, while she is working; the subjects contrast.
- `Я хочу́ чита́ть, но мне ну́жно рабо́тать.` - `Ya khochú chitát', no mne núzhno rabótat'.`: I want to read, but I need to work.
- `Я до́ма, потому́ что мне ну́жно чита́ть.` - `Ya dóma, potomú chto mne núzhno chitát'.`: I am home because I need to read.

### Common trap

A connector does not make different subjects share one verb ending. Also, потому что introduces a cause, while поэтому introduces a consequence; reversing them reverses the logic.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I read and she works. Use и.
2. Say: I am at home, while he is at work. Use а.
3. Say: I want to work, but I cannot. Use могу.
4. Say: I read because I want to read.

***Answers:***

1. `Я чита́ю, и она́ рабо́тает.` - `Ya chitáyu, i oná rabótayet.`
2. `Я до́ма, а он на рабо́те.` - `Ya dóma, a on na rabóte.`
3. `Я хочу́ рабо́тать, но не могу́.` - `Ya khochú rabótat', no ne mogú.`
4. `Я чита́ю, потому́ что хочу́ чита́ть.` - `Ya chitáyu, potomú chto khochú chitát'.`

### Why these answers work

И adds two events; а contrasts two locations. The repeated subject я can be omitted in the third and fourth answers because it is clear. Хочу still takes an infinitive, while читаю remains a finite action verb.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give one reason for an imagined plan, then contrast your plan with another person's. Keep each clause short enough that you can identify its subject, finite verb and any required infinitive.


## Lesson 23: A café exchange

**Outcome:** request a drink, ask its price and respond to an offer in a short service exchange.

### Think first

You know дайте and accusative objects. If you ask for water rather than a book, what part of the request stays the same and what ending must change?

### Reuse the grammar in a transaction

Да́йте, пожа́луйста, во́ду is a straightforward request for water. Вода́ becomes во́ду in accusative singular, with a stress shift that must be learned. Чай is masculine and inanimate, so the direct-object form remains чай. Ко́фе is indeclinable in these ordinary uses; the noun does not acquire a new case ending just because it is an object.

Мне, пожа́луйста, чай is another natural ordering pattern, roughly “tea for me, please.” The dative мне identifies the recipient. It is a compact service expression rather than a complete model for every possible English sentence containing “I would like.”

Ско́лько сто́ит чай? asks “How much does the tea cost?” Сто́ит is a singular form of сто́ить, to cost. Do not confuse its stress with стои́т, “stands,” from a different verb. Цена́ means price, and счёт means bill or account. Счёт, пожалуйста is a common short request in a café context.

A server may ask Чай или кофе? Here и́ли means “or,” presenting alternatives. A reply can simply name the desired item. Short fragments are appropriate because the transaction supplies the missing context. The point of the dialogue is to recombine case, politeness and question structure, not to make every response longer than natural conversation requires.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| чай | chay | tea |
| ко́фе | kófe | coffee, indeclinable here |
| вода́ | vodá | water, nominative |
| во́ду | vódu | water, accusative |
| ско́лько сто́ит | skól'ko stóit | how much does it cost |
| цена́ | tsená | price |
| счёт | schyot | bill; account |
| и́ли | íli | or |

### Worked examples

- `Мне, пожа́луйста, чай.` - `Mne, pozháluysta, chay.`: Tea for me, please; мне marks the recipient.
- `Ско́лько сто́ит ко́фе?` - `Skól'ko stóit kófe?`: How much does the coffee cost? The noun remains indeclinable.
- `Чай и́ли ко́фе?` - `Chay íli kófe?`: Tea or coffee? The context supplies the omitted verb.

### Common trap

Water changes both ending and stress in воду. Do not preserve вода as the direct object of дайте. Also, сто́ит for price and стои́т for standing differ in stress, so the accent changes meaning.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Ask respectfully: Please give me water.
2. Ask: How much does the tea cost?
3. Request: The bill, please.
4. Order using мне: Coffee for me, please.

***Answers:***

1. `Да́йте, пожа́луйста, во́ду.` - `Dáyte, pozháluysta, vódu.`
2. `Ско́лько сто́ит чай?` - `Skól'ko stóit chay?`
3. `Счёт, пожа́луйста.` - `Schyot, pozháluysta.`
4. `Мне, пожа́луйста, ко́фе.` - `Mne, pozháluysta, kófe.`

### Why these answers work

Воду is the accusative object of дайте. Чай and кофе do not visibly change in these roles. Счёт is a context-supported request, and мне marks the person receiving the coffee rather than serving as a nominative subject.

### Retrieve and recombine

Run a three-turn café exchange: offer two drinks, choose one, then ask the price. Switch the order to water and retrieve its changed stress before saying the request aloud.


## Lesson 24: Finding a place and repairing a misunderstanding

**Outcome:** combine a polite opener, location question and repair request while navigating an unfamiliar area.

### Think first

When asking a stranger where the station is, do you need a movement verb? What changes if you ask where the stranger is going instead?

### Build the exchange in stages

Извини́те is a respectful “excuse me” or “sorry.” It is useful before a question, especially when interrupting someone. Извините, где метро? asks for the metro's location. The station is the thing being located, so the question uses где rather than куда.

Ря́дом means nearby or next to something in an expanded construction. Здесь and там can support a simple pointing answer. Нале́во and напра́во mean to the left and to the right, especially for direction. Пря́мо means straight ahead. We introduce them as short route cues rather than claiming they form a complete turn-by-turn navigation grammar.

If the answer is too fast, combine an honest comprehension statement with a request: Я не понимаю. Повторите, пожалуйста. These are two conversational turns or two sentences, each with a clear purpose. In the practice answer keys, each item remains one short sentence so the app can compare it reliably.

A listener may answer your location question with a route rather than a static description. That is pragmatically helpful, but it does not make где and куда interchangeable. Keep the question you intended distinct from the form of the answer you happen to receive. This awareness lets you recover from a misunderstanding without assuming every unfamiliar construction breaks the rules learned earlier.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| извини́те | izviníte | excuse me; sorry, respectful |
| ря́дом | ryádom | nearby |
| нале́во | nalévo | to the left |
| напра́во | naprávo | to the right |
| пря́мо | pryámo | straight ahead |
| вокза́л | vokzál | railway station building |
| ме́дленно | médlenno | slowly |
| метро́ | metró | metro |

### Worked examples

- `Извини́те, где вокза́л?` - `Izviníte, gde vokzál?`: Excuse me, where is the railway station?
- `Метро́ ря́дом.` - `Metró ryádom.`: The metro is nearby; the description is static.
- `Куда́ вы идёте?` - `Kudá vy idyóte?`: Where are you going on foot? This asks for a destination.

### Common trap

Do not substitute куда simply because the conversation concerns travel. A station has a location even when you intend to travel there. Choose the question word from the meaning of your actual question.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Ask respectfully: Excuse me, where is the bank?
2. Say: The station is nearby.
3. Ask respectfully: Please repeat.
4. Say: I am going to the metro on foot.

***Answers:***

1. `Извини́те, где банк?` - `Izviníte, gde bank?`
2. `Вокза́л ря́дом.` - `Vokzál ryádom.`
3. `Повтори́те, пожа́луйста.` - `Povtoríte, pozháluysta.`
4. `Я иду́ в метро́.` - `Ya idú v metró.`

### Why these answers work

Где requests the bank's location. Рядом supplies a static description without a linking verb. Повторите repairs comprehension. В метро expresses a destination after иду, but метро is indeclinable and therefore shows no visible accusative ending.

### Retrieve and recombine

Imagine receiving directions you did not understand. Ask the original location question, request repetition, then state your intended destination with a motion verb. Keep location and destination grammatically separate.


## Lesson 25: Cumulative review: a useful day in Russian

**Outcome:** connect identity, location, possession, plans and a practical request in a coherent short interaction.

### Think first

You meet someone at a library. You want to explain who you are, what book you have, what you did yesterday and what you need to do tomorrow. Which parts require a verb, and which can remain present identity or location statements?

### Reconstruct rather than recite

Begin with a present identity: Я студент or Я студентка. Locate yourself with я здесь or я в школе, choosing an actual setting. Describe possession with у меня есть книга, keeping книга nominative in the existence statement. If you then say you read it, the noun changes to книгу because its job is now direct object. The physical book did not change; the sentence's grammatical relation did.

For yesterday, choose an aspect before an ending. Читал or читала reports reading activity. Прочитал or прочитала presents the reading as a completed whole. For tomorrow, буду читать frames an activity, while прочитаю presents a bounded future reading. Gender matters in singular past forms, but present and future personal forms do not change for the speaker's gender.

A need is expressed with мне нужно and an infinitive. A request can use вы можете помочь or a learned respectful imperative with пожалуйста. These are different social and grammatical routes to obtaining help. Do not merge them into a sentence containing every useful word at once.

The review table groups six high-value patterns by their grammatical job. Use it to diagnose a hesitation: is the missing choice about person, case, aspect or the relationship to the listener? A precise question is more productive than deciding that a whole sentence is simply “difficult.”

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| я студе́нт | ya studént | present identity, male student |
| у меня́ есть | u menyá yest' | affirmative possession frame |
| мне ну́жно | mne núzhno | need plus infinitive |
| я чита́ла | ya chitála | past activity, female speaker |
| я прочита́ю | ya prochitáyu | future completed reading |
| вы мо́жете | vy mózhete | respectful ability or request frame |
| в шко́ле | v shkóle | static location |
| в шко́лу | v shkólu | destination |

### Worked examples

- `У меня́ есть кни́га, но мне ну́жно рабо́тать.` - `U menyá yest' kníga, no mne núzhno rabótat'.`: I have a book, but I need to work; two complete patterns are connected.
- `За́втра я прочита́ю кни́гу.` - `Závtra ya prochitáyu knígu.`: Tomorrow I will read the book through; the result is foregrounded.
- `Вы мо́жете помо́чь?` - `Vy mózhete pomóch'?`: Can you help? Respectful agreement supports the request.

### Common trap

Avoid treating the course as a universal ending generator. Animate accusatives, plural case patterns, prefixed motion verbs and broader aspect choices need further study. A reliable short sentence is a better foundation than an unexamined complicated one.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say as a woman: I read the book through yesterday. Put yesterday first.
2. Say: I need to work at home.
3. Ask respectfully: Do you have a book?
4. Say: Tomorrow we will read. Put tomorrow first.

***Answers:***

1. `Вчера́ я прочита́ла кни́гу.` - `Vcherá ya prochitála knígu.`
2. `Мне ну́жно рабо́тать до́ма.` - `Mne núzhno rabótat' dóma.`
3. `У вас есть кни́га?` - `U vas yest' kníga?`
4. `За́втра мы бу́дем чита́ть.` - `Závtra my búdem chitát'.`

### Why these answers work

The first answer combines feminine past agreement, perfective aspect and an accusative object. The second uses dative мне plus an infinitive. The third keeps the available book nominative. The fourth puts person on будем and leaves читать infinitive.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create a five-turn encounter in a new setting: introduce yourself, ask a location, mention possession, state a plan and request help. Then explain one case choice and one aspect choice without consulting a table.
