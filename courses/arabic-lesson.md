# Arabic: A pattern-first foundation

This course teaches Modern Standard Arabic (MSA), العربية الفصحى, in Arabic script. MSA is used in formal writing, education, news and cross-regional communication. It is not identical to the everyday spoken dialect of Cairo, Beirut, Rabat or another city. The course does not silently mix dialectal present prefixes, negation or pronouns into MSA. A later dialect course should name its regional variety explicitly.

Arabic is written from right to left. Examples use ordinary Arabic spelling with selective vowel marks where they clarify a distinction. Romanisation uses ā, ī and ū for long vowels; ʾ for hamza; ʿ for ع; ḥ, ṣ, ḍ, ṭ and ẓ for the distinct Arabic consonants; and th, dh, kh, gh and sh. These are learning aids, not an alternative Arabic orthography. Doubled consonants are written twice.

For readability, the main reading lines use a deliberately simplified MSA teaching convention: short final noun case vowels and short final indicative mood vowels are omitted, while meaningful person endings and long vowels are retained. This is not a complete transcription of fully inflected connected formal recitation. Tāʾ marbūṭa is usually read a in an isolated noun and at in a construct phrase. Lesson 10 explicitly introduces the case distinctions that the simplified readings leave unspoken. Fixed expressions such as shukran retain their familiar endings.

## Lesson 1: Identity without a present linking verb

**Outcome:** introduce yourself and identify another person in a simple MSA nominal sentence.

### Think first

If أنا means I and طالب means male student, what would أنا طالب communicate without a separate present word for am?

### A nominal sentence

أنا طالب means “I am a male student.” In an ordinary present identity sentence, MSA normally has no overt linking verb corresponding to English am, is or are. The sentence still has a complete subject and predicate. This does not mean Arabic lacks a verb meaning be; past and other constructions will use one later.

A female student is طالبة. The ending ة, called tāʾ marbūṭa, often marks a feminine noun or adjective. In our simplified isolated reading it is a, giving ṭāliba. The pronoun أنا stays the same for speakers of any gender, while the occupation noun can distinguish the person described.

هو means he, and هي means she. طبيب is a male doctor and طبيبة a female doctor. A sentence such as هي طبيبة therefore matches the pronoun and the occupational noun. Not every Arabic noun ending or gender relation is predictable from this pair, but it provides a transparent first pattern.

Arabic does not use an indefinite article corresponding to English a in these statements. A bare indefinite predicate noun is normal. The definite article ال will be introduced separately; adding it automatically to every occupation changes the structure or intended identification. Begin with a small set of reliable identity statements, then reuse them in questions.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| أنا | anā | I |
| هو | huwa | he |
| هي | hiya | she |
| طالب | ṭālib | male student |
| طالبة | ṭāliba | female student |
| طبيب | ṭabīb | male doctor |
| طبيبة | ṭabība | female doctor |

### Worked examples

- `أنا طالب.` - `Anā ṭālib.`: I am a male student; no present linking verb is needed.
- `أنا طالبة.` - `Anā ṭāliba.`: I am a female student; the noun changes, not أنا.
- `هي طبيبة.` - `Hiya ṭabība.`: She is a doctor; the occupational noun is feminine.

### Common trap

Do not insert a form of يكون into every present identity sentence. Also, the feminine noun does not require a different first-person pronoun: أنا works for every speaker.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say as a man: I am a doctor.
2. Say as a woman: I am a doctor.
3. Say: He is a male student.
4. Say: She is a female student.

***Answers:***

1. `أنا طبيب.` - `Anā ṭabīb.`
2. `أنا طبيبة.` - `Anā ṭabība.`
3. `هو طالب.` - `Huwa ṭālib.`
4. `هي طالبة.` - `Hiya ṭāliba.`

### Why these answers work

The first-person pronoun remains أنا in both doctor introductions. طبيب and طبيبة distinguish the person described. هو pairs with the masculine student noun and هي with the feminine one. None of the sentences requires an English-style article or present copula.

### Retrieve and recombine

Introduce two imaginary classmates and their doctor. Change one person's gender and identify precisely which pronoun or noun must change, rather than rebuilding the entire sentence from English.


## Lesson 2: Script, long vowels and this thing

**Outcome:** identify a nearby object and recognise several important spelling and sound distinctions.

### Think first

كتاب contains a long ā, while a short vowel may be absent from ordinary spelling. Does the absence of a written short-vowel mark mean the word has no vowel there?

### Read beyond English letter habits

Arabic normally writes consonants and long vowels, while short vowels are often omitted in everyday text. كتاب is read kitāb, book. The long ā is represented by ا, but the short i is supplied from knowledge of the word or from vowel marks in a fully vocalised text. The romanisation helps recover those sounds while you learn the script.

Letters change shape according to their position and joining behaviour. Dots distinguish otherwise related shapes, so they are meaningful parts of the letter. ب, ت and ث are different consonants. Hamza ء represents a glottal stop and can appear on different written seats. Do not discard it simply because English spelling handles that sound inconsistently.

هذا means this with a masculine singular referent, and هذه with a feminine singular referent. هذا كتاب means “this is a book.” هذه مدرسة means “this is a school.” The long ā in هذا is not written as an ordinary full alif in the familiar spelling; learn the whole high-frequency form.

ما هذا؟ asks “What is this?” for a masculine referent or unknown object treated with هذا. ما هذه؟ uses the feminine demonstrative. The question and answer give you a practical reason to read the script accurately instead of memorising isolated letter names without a sentence.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| هذا | hādhā | this, masculine |
| هذه | hādhihi | this, feminine |
| ما | mā | what, in this question |
| كتاب | kitāb | book, masculine |
| قلم | qalam | pen, masculine |
| مدرسة | madrasa | school, feminine |
| سيارة | sayyāra | car, feminine |
| باب | bāb | door, masculine |

### Worked examples

- `ما هذا؟` - `Mā hādhā?`: What is this? The demonstrative is masculine.
- `هذا كتاب.` - `Hādhā kitāb.`: This is a book; the long vowel is part of the noun.
- `هذه مدرسة.` - `Hādhihi madrasa.`: This is a school; the demonstrative matches the feminine noun.

### Common trap

Do not assume that unmarked short vowels are absent in speech. Also, هذا and هذه are not interchangeable merely because English uses one word this. The referent's grammatical gender matters.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Identify: This is a pen.
2. Identify: This is a car.
3. Ask using the feminine demonstrative: What is this?
4. Identify: This is a door.

***Answers:***

1. `هذا قلم.` - `Hādhā qalam.`
2. `هذه سيارة.` - `Hādhihi sayyāra.`
3. `ما هذه؟` - `Mā hādhihi?`
4. `هذا باب.` - `Hādhā bāb.`

### Why these answers work

قلم and باب use masculine هذا. سيارة uses feminine هذه. The feminine question preserves the same demonstrative choice. Long vowels in hādhā, sayyāra and bāb should not be shortened merely to resemble an English reading.

### Retrieve and recombine

Point to a book and an imagined car. Ask and answer the identification question for each, then explain why the demonstrative changes even though the English question looks identical.


## Lesson 3: You, yes-or-no questions and greetings

**Outcome:** address one person accurately and check an identity with هل.

### Think first

English you does not reveal the listener's gender. If MSA distinguishes أنتَ and أنتِ, what information must you decide before asking a direct question?

### Address and question structure

أنتَ, anta, addresses one male person; أنتِ, anti, addresses one female person. The short vowel distinguishes the forms even when ordinary unvocalised spelling shows the same letters. We preserve the vowel mark in teaching examples when it matters. These are gender forms, not a familiar-versus-respectful pair like French tu and vous.

هل introduces a yes-or-no question: هل أنتَ طالب؟ means “Are you a male student?” The identity sentence keeps its basic structure after the question word. You do not add an English auxiliary or reverse the subject and predicate. هل هي طبيبة؟ asks whether she is a doctor.

نعم means yes, and لا means no as an independent response. Later لا will also negate present action verbs, but an isolated no response should not be treated as a complete rule for every negative sentence type.

مرحبا is a common greeting, often pronounced marḥaban in its fixed form. صباح الخير is good morning, with a conventional reply صباح النور. Courtesy in MSA also uses titles, considerate wording and context; there is no need to invent a universal formal singular pronoun. The dialogue can stay simple: greet, ask an identity question, answer and return the question.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| أنتَ | anta | you, one male addressee |
| أنتِ | anti | you, one female addressee |
| هل | hal | yes-or-no question marker |
| نعم | naʿam | yes |
| لا | lā | no; not in later verb patterns |
| مرحبا | marḥaban | hello |
| صباح الخير | ṣabāḥ al-khayr | good morning |
| صباح النور | ṣabāḥ an-nūr | conventional good-morning reply |

### Worked examples

- `هل أنتَ طالب؟` - `Hal anta ṭālib?`: Are you a male student? هل introduces the question.
- `هل أنتِ طبيبة؟` - `Hal anti ṭabība?`: Are you a female doctor? The address and occupation are feminine.
- `هل هو طبيب؟` - `Hal huwa ṭabīb?`: Is he a doctor? The ordinary identity order remains.

### Common trap

Do not use أنتَ and أنتِ as a formality distinction. They distinguish singular addressee gender. Also, هل does not require an extra present form of be in an identity question.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Ask a woman: Are you a student?
2. Ask a man: Are you a doctor?
3. Answer simply: Yes.
4. Greet someone: Good morning.

***Answers:***

1. `هل أنتِ طالبة؟` - `Hal anti ṭāliba?`
2. `هل أنتَ طبيب؟` - `Hal anta ṭabīb?`
3. `نعم.` - `Naʿam.`
4. `صباح الخير.` - `Ṣabāḥ al-khayr.`

### Why these answers work

The first two questions align the addressee form and occupation noun. نعم is a complete short response. صباح الخير is a conventional greeting whose connected phrase should be learned as a unit rather than assembled from unrelated English words.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create a brief greeting and identity exchange with one male and one female addressee. Keep the social tone equally courteous while changing only the forms that encode gender.


## Lesson 4: The definite article and a useful contrast

**Outcome:** distinguish an indefinite noun, a definite noun and a complete description.

### Think first

كتاب means a book or book, while الكتاب means the book. If you say الكتاب جديد, where is the new information in the sentence?

### ال marks definiteness

The article ال attaches directly to a noun: كتاب becomes الكتاب, the book. There is no separate indefinite article. In the simplified readings we write al- before moon letters, such as al-kitāb. Before sun letters, the l assimilates to the following consonant in speech: الشمس is ash-shams, the sun, and الطالب is aṭ-ṭālib, the student. The written ل remains present despite the pronunciation change.

A definite subject plus an indefinite descriptive predicate forms a sentence: الكتاب جديد, “the book is new.” The adjective جديد is new. If both noun and adjective are definite, الكتاب الجديد is the noun phrase “the new book.” That phrase alone does not assert the same complete proposition unless a context supplies the rest.

Demonstratives combine with definite nouns in a specific noun phrase: هذا الكتاب, this book. Contrast هذا كتاب, “this is a book.” The presence of ال changes the structure. This small difference is powerful because it distinguishes identifying an object from referring to an already specified one.

The article does not change for masculine and feminine nouns. Gender agreement appears elsewhere, while definiteness is marked by the same written ال. Listen for sun-letter assimilation without spelling the article as if its ل had vanished.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| الكتاب | al-kitāb | the book |
| القلم | al-qalam | the pen |
| الطالب | aṭ-ṭālib | the male student |
| الشمس | ash-shams | the sun, feminine |
| جديد | jadīd | new, masculine |
| جديدة | jadīda | new, feminine |
| هذا الكتاب | hādhā al-kitāb | this book |
| الكتاب الجديد | al-kitāb al-jadīd | the new book |

### Worked examples

- `الكتاب جديد.` - `Al-kitāb jadīd.`: The book is new; the predicate is indefinite.
- `هذا الكتاب جديد.` - `Hādhā al-kitāb jadīd.`: This book is new; the demonstrative belongs to the definite subject phrase.
- `القلم الجديد هنا.` - `Al-qalam al-jadīd hunā.`: The new pen is here; the adjective is definite inside the noun phrase.

### Common trap

Do not confuse هذا كتاب with هذا الكتاب. The first identifies something as a book; the second means this book and may need a following predicate. Also, sun-letter assimilation changes pronunciation, not the article's spelling.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: The pen is new.
2. Say: This book is new.
3. Produce the phrase: the new book.
4. Identify: This is a book.

***Answers:***

1. `القلم جديد.` - `Al-qalam jadīd.`
2. `هذا الكتاب جديد.` - `Hādhā al-kitāb jadīd.`
3. `الكتاب الجديد.` - `Al-kitāb al-jadīd.`
4. `هذا كتاب.` - `Hādhā kitāb.`

### Why these answers work

The first two answers have definite subjects and indefinite predicates. The third has a definite adjective agreeing with a definite noun inside one phrase. The last omits the article because it identifies the object as an indefinite book.

### Retrieve and recombine

Compare a label meaning the new book with a sentence saying the book is new. Explain how definiteness on the adjective changes its relationship to the noun.


## Lesson 5: Gender agreement in descriptions

**Outcome:** describe familiar masculine and feminine nouns with matching adjectives.

### Think first

كتاب جديد is a new book, while سيارة جديدة is a new car. What changes in the adjective when the noun is feminine?

### Agreement follows the noun

Many adjectives form a feminine singular with ة: جديد becomes جديدة, كبير becomes كبيرة, and صغير becomes صغيرة. The adjective normally follows the noun it modifies. سيارة كبيرة means a big car; the car is feminine grammatically, so the adjective uses its feminine form.

A predicate description also agrees: السيارة كبيرة, “the car is big.” The adjective is indefinite because it supplies the description rather than forming the definite phrase السيارة الكبيرة, the big car. Gender and definiteness are separate agreement choices that can operate together.

Not every feminine noun ends in ة. الشمس, sun, is feminine, as are other nouns whose gender must be learned. Conversely, noun morphology includes exceptions and specialised patterns beyond this first regular adjective set. Learn the gender with each useful noun rather than treating the final letter as an infallible test.

The demonstrative also follows gender: هذه السيارة كبيرة, “this car is big.” Here هذه agrees with السيارة, and كبيرة describes the same noun. The sentence contains several visible links, but no present linking verb. A careful learner can build it one relation at a time: choose the noun, mark it definite, choose the demonstrative, then select the predicate's gender form.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| كبير | kabīr | big, masculine |
| كبيرة | kabīra | big, feminine |
| صغير | ṣaghīr | small, masculine |
| صغيرة | ṣaghīra | small, feminine |
| جميل | jamīl | beautiful, masculine |
| جميلة | jamīla | beautiful, feminine |
| المدرسة | al-madrasa | the school, feminine |

### Worked examples

- `السيارة كبيرة.` - `As-sayyāra kabīra.`: The car is big; the adjective is feminine and indefinite.
- `هذا الكتاب صغير.` - `Hādhā al-kitāb ṣaghīr.`: This book is small; the adjective is masculine.
- `هذه المدرسة جميلة.` - `Hādhihi al-madrasa jamīla.`: This school is beautiful; demonstrative and adjective follow the feminine noun.

### Common trap

Do not make the predicate adjective definite merely because the subject is definite. السيارة الكبيرة is a noun phrase, while السيارة كبيرة is the complete description being taught here.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: The school is small.
2. Say: This pen is big.
3. Say: This car is new.
4. Produce the phrase: the beautiful school.

***Answers:***

1. `المدرسة صغيرة.` - `Al-madrasa ṣaghīra.`
2. `هذا القلم كبير.` - `Hādhā al-qalam kabīr.`
3. `هذه السيارة جديدة.` - `Hādhihi as-sayyāra jadīda.`
4. `المدرسة الجميلة.` - `Al-madrasa al-jamīla.`

### Why these answers work

صغيرة and جديدة match feminine nouns. كبير matches masculine قلم. The final phrase gives both noun and adjective the definite article because they form one definite noun phrase rather than a subject-predicate statement.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a book and a car with the same size contrast. Then turn one full description into a definite noun phrase, tracking gender and definiteness as two separate decisions.


## Lesson 6: Present actions and personal prefixes

**Outcome:** say who writes or studies in the present using a common sound-verb pattern.

### Think first

أكتب means I write, while يكتب means he writes. Which part of the word identifies the person, and how does that differ from the nominal sentences already learned?

### Prefixes and endings carry agreement

The verb كتب has the basic past dictionary form kataba, he wrote. Its present stem gives أكتب, aktub, I write; يكتب, yaktub, he writes; تكتب, taktub, she writes or you masculine singular write; and نكتب, naktub, we write. Our simplified readings omit the final indicative mood vowel, but the person prefixes remain essential.

For a female singular addressee, تكتبين, taktubīn, includes an ending as well as the prefix. أنتِ تكتبين means “you write” to a woman. The long īn ending is not an optional case vowel and is retained in the readings. You cannot produce all persons merely by changing the first letter.

يدرس, yadrus, means he studies; أدرس, adrus, means I study. Both verbs have the u vowel in this present pattern, but other basic verbs use different internal vowels. A root does not let you predict every vowel without learning the verb's pattern.

The present form can describe a current action or a habit. الآن, now, highlights the current action. A subject pronoun may be included for clarity or contrast, but the verb already carries substantial person information. We keep explicit pronouns where they make gender and person choices easy to inspect.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| أكتب | aktub | I write |
| يكتب | yaktub | he writes |
| تكتب | taktub | she writes; you male singular write |
| تكتبين | taktubīn | you female singular write |
| نكتب | naktub | we write |
| أدرس | adrus | I study |
| الآن | al-ān | now |
| نحن | naḥnu | we |

### Worked examples

- `أنا أكتب.` - `Anā aktub.`: I write; the initial أ marks the speaker.
- `هي تدرس.` - `Hiya tadrus.`: She studies; ت marks this third-person feminine form.
- `أنتِ تكتبين.` - `Anti taktubīn.`: You write, addressed to a woman; the ending is part of agreement.

### Common trap

Do not assume تكتب always means she writes. With أنتَ it means you masculine singular write. Also, a female addressee needs تكتبين in the ordinary indicative pattern, not just the same form as a male addressee.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: He writes.
2. Say: We study.
3. Say to a woman: You study.
4. Say: I am writing now.

***Answers:***

1. `هو يكتب.` - `Huwa yaktub.`
2. `نحن ندرس.` - `Naḥnu nadrus.`
3. `أنتِ تدرسين.` - `Anti tadrusīn.`
4. `أنا أكتب الآن.` - `Anā aktub al-ān.`

### Why these answers work

يكتب identifies third-person masculine, and ندرس identifies we. تدرسين includes the female-addressee ending. الآن makes the current time explicit without adding an English-style am auxiliary to the Arabic action verb.

### Retrieve and recombine

Change one writing sentence from I to he, she and a female addressee. Identify which forms differ by prefix alone and which require an ending as well.


## Lesson 7: Direct objects and neutral sentence order

**Outcome:** add a familiar object to a present action and recognise two ordinary MSA clause orders.

### Think first

If أقرأ means I read, where can الكتاب fit to tell us what is read? Does Arabic need an English-style word for the action's object relation?

### A verb can take an object

أنا أقرأ الكتاب means “I read the book.” أقرأ, aqraʾ, is the first-person present of قرأ, to read. The hamza is a real consonant and remains in the spelling and reading. أشرب, ashrab, means I drink, with a different internal present vowel from أكتب. Learn these as complete useful forms.

A neutral learner pattern is subject, verb, object: هو يقرأ الكتاب. MSA also commonly uses verb, subject, object: يقرأ الطالب الكتاب, “the student reads the book.” Both are legitimate; do not claim Arabic has only one fixed order. Agreement details differ with some plural subjects in verb-first clauses, so this course initially uses singular subjects or explicit pronoun-first clauses.

The object has accusative case in fully inflected MSA. Ordinary unvocalised writing often does not show the short final case vowel, and our simplified readings omit it. The grammar is still present even when the visible noun looks unchanged. Lesson 10 will make those endings explicit.

Using a definite object avoids introducing the extra spelling conventions of indefinite accusative nouns before the case lesson. الماء, water, and الشاي, tea, provide immediately useful definite drink objects. Their article can be natural in general statements without requiring English to say the every time.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| أقرأ | aqraʾ | I read |
| يقرأ | yaqraʾ | he reads |
| أشرب | ashrab | I drink |
| يشرب | yashrab | he drinks |
| الماء | al-māʾ | water; the water |
| الشاي | ash-shāy | tea; the tea |
| الكتاب | al-kitāb | the book |
| الرسالة | ar-risāla | the message or letter |

### Worked examples

- `أنا أقرأ الكتاب.` - `Anā aqraʾ al-kitāb.`: I read the book; the object follows the verb.
- `هي تشرب الماء.` - `Hiya tashrab al-māʾ.`: She drinks water; ت marks the feminine subject form.
- `يقرأ الطالب الرسالة.` - `Yaqraʾ aṭ-ṭālib ar-risāla.`: The male student reads the message; the verb comes first.

### Common trap

Do not infer that case is absent just because ordinary spelling leaves out its short vowel. Also, أقرأ ends with hamza; it is not the same spelling or sound as an imagined word without that consonant.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I drink tea.
2. Say: He reads the message.
3. Say: She reads the book.
4. Say: We drink water.

***Answers:***

1. `أنا أشرب الشاي.` - `Anā ashrab ash-shāy.`
2. `هو يقرأ الرسالة.` - `Huwa yaqraʾ ar-risāla.`
3. `هي تقرأ الكتاب.` - `Hiya taqraʾ al-kitāb.`
4. `نحن نشرب الماء.` - `Naḥnu nashrab al-māʾ.`

### Why these answers work

The verbs agree with the explicit subjects through أ, ي, ت and ن prefixes. The following nouns are objects even though their final case vowels are omitted in the teaching readings. Sun-letter assimilation appears in ash-shāy and ar-risāla.

### Retrieve and recombine

Replace the book with a message in a reading sentence, then change the subject from he to we. Keep the object relation stable while changing only the verb's person information.


## Lesson 8: Present negation and negative identity

**Outcome:** distinguish لا with an action from ليس with a nominal predicate.

### Think first

“I do not write” denies an action, while “I am not at home” denies a state or location. Why might MSA use different negative constructions?

### لا and ليس are not interchangeable

لا precedes an ordinary present indicative verb to negate it: لا أكتب, “I do not write”; هو لا يقرأ الكتاب, “he does not read the book.” The verb retains its person agreement. لا is also the independent no response, but the sentence construction gives it a specific grammatical role here.

ليس is an inflected negative verb used for many present nominal sentences. لستُ في البيت means “I am not at home.” The first-person form lastu works for any speaker gender. He is not is ليس, laysa, and she is not is ليست, laysat. To a male addressee, لستَ is lasta; to a female addressee, لستِ is lasti.

For negative identity or adjective predicates, ليس affects case: an indefinite predicate commonly becomes accusative in fully inflected MSA. We avoid concealing that rule behind an unmarked identity example by practising location complements first. في البيت is a prepositional phrase and keeps its own genitive relation.

The spoken dialects use several different negative systems. Egyptian مش or Levantine مو may be heard in regional speech, but they are not substituted into the MSA answer keys here. Learning the distinction now keeps the named standard consistent while allowing you to recognise that real-world Arabic has multiple varieties.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| لا أكتب | lā aktub | I do not write |
| ليس | laysa | he is not |
| ليست | laysat | she is not |
| لستُ | lastu | I am not |
| لستَ | lasta | you male singular are not |
| لستِ | lasti | you female singular are not |
| في البيت | fī al-bayt | at home; in the house |
| البيت | al-bayt | the house |

### Worked examples

- `أنا لا أشرب الشاي.` - `Anā lā ashrab ash-shāy.`: I do not drink tea; لا negates an action.
- `هو ليس في البيت.` - `Huwa laysa fī al-bayt.`: He is not at home; ليس negates a nominal location statement.
- `هي ليست في المدرسة.` - `Hiya laysat fī al-madrasa.`: She is not at school; the negative verb matches her.

### Common trap

Do not use لا as the complete replacement for ليس in a negative nominal sentence. Also, lastu is first person regardless of the speaker's gender; the final vowel in lasta or lasti instead identifies the addressee.

### Why these answers work

لا precedes finite action verbs without removing their person prefixes. لستُ and ليست are inflected negative nominal predicates. The location phrase retains في and its noun rather than taking an invented action-verb negation pattern.

## Lesson 9: Location, source and destination

**Outcome:** state a location, name a source and indicate a destination.

### Think first

في البيت means at home. If someone is going to school rather than already there, which relationship should the preposition express?

### Prepositions carry the relationship

في means in or at in many location phrases: في المدرسة, at school. إلى means to or toward: إلى المدرسة, to school. من means from: من البيت, from home. All three govern genitive nouns in fully inflected MSA, although our simplified readings omit the short final case vowels.

أين asks where. أين الكتاب؟ means “Where is the book?” The answer الكتاب هنا says the book is here. هنا means here and هناك means there. A present location sentence needs no overt be verb, just as an identity sentence does not.

أذهب means I go, from ذهب. أذهب إلى المدرسة means “I go to school.” English sometimes distinguishes walking, driving and other travel modes with additional vocabulary. The verb ذهب itself does not encode the Russian-style walking-versus-transport contrast; do not import that grammar from another language.

على means on or upon in a basic spatial relation: على الطاولة, on the table. الطاولة is table. The article assimilates before ط, giving aṭ-ṭāwila in the reading. A single sentence can locate an object on a surface without requiring a new tense or a gendered adjective: الكتاب على الطاولة. The relationship is supplied by the prepositional phrase.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| في | fī | in; at |
| إلى | ilā | to; toward |
| من | min | from |
| على | ʿalā | on; upon |
| أين | ayna | where |
| هنا | hunā | here |
| هناك | hunāka | there |
| أذهب | adhhab | I go |
| الطاولة | aṭ-ṭāwila | the table |

### Worked examples

- `الكتاب على الطاولة.` - `Al-kitāb ʿalā aṭ-ṭāwila.`: The book is on the table; a prepositional phrase supplies the predicate.
- `أنا أذهب إلى المدرسة.` - `Anā adhhab ilā al-madrasa.`: I go to school; إلى marks the destination.
- `أين القلم؟` - `Ayna al-qalam?`: Where is the pen? No present linking verb is inserted.

### Common trap

Do not use في for a destination simply because English says at in a related sentence. Also, the same case after several prepositions does not make their meanings interchangeable: source, location and destination remain distinct.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: The pen is here.
2. Say: I am at school.
3. Say: I go home. Use إلى البيت.
4. Say: The book is on the table.

***Answers:***

1. `القلم هنا.` - `Al-qalam hunā.`
2. `أنا في المدرسة.` - `Anā fī al-madrasa.`
3. `أنا أذهب إلى البيت.` - `Anā adhhab ilā al-bayt.`
4. `الكتاب على الطاولة.` - `Al-kitāb ʿalā aṭ-ṭāwila.`

### Why these answers work

هنا is a location adverb. في introduces a static place, while إلى gives a destination after أذهب. على expresses a surface relation. Each sentence can be built from a subject plus the relevant verb or prepositional predicate.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe a book on a table and yourself going to school. Then ask where the book is. Explain why the object's static location and your destination require different prepositions.


## Lesson 10: Seeing the case endings behind the simplified readings

**Outcome:** recognise nominative, accusative and genitive in a small fully vocalised contrast.

### Think first

The same written noun الكتاب can be a subject, object or noun after a preposition. What information can a final short vowel reveal in fully inflected MSA?

### Three common case roles

For a regular definite singular noun such as الكتاب, nominative commonly ends in u, accusative in a and genitive in i. Thus الْكِتَابُ can be a subject, الْكِتَابَ a direct object, and الْكِتَابِ the noun after a preposition. These are case roles, not three different lexical words for book.

Our usual reading lines omit these short endings to keep early MSA production manageable. In this lesson's explicit case table and selected answers, the endings are shown and pronounced because they are the point of the exercise. This is a local teaching exception to the simplified reading convention, not a silent shift into a dialect.

Indefinite nouns often have nunation, written with doubled vowel marks: كِتَابٌ, kitābun; كِتَابًا, kitāban; كِتَابٍ, kitābin. The accusative spelling often adds an alif, as in كتابًا, but there are spelling exceptions. The definite article and nunation do not normally occur together on the same noun.

A preposition such as في governs genitive, while a transitive verb can assign accusative to its object. A nominal subject is nominative in the basic construction. Other noun classes and grammatical environments introduce additional patterns, so these regular singular examples are a foundation rather than a complete case system.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| الْكِتَابُ | al-kitābu | the book, nominative |
| الْكِتَابَ | al-kitāba | the book, accusative |
| الْكِتَابِ | al-kitābi | the book, genitive |
| كِتَابٌ | kitābun | a book, nominative indefinite |
| كِتَابًا | kitāban | a book, accusative indefinite |
| كِتَابٍ | kitābin | a book, genitive indefinite |
| فِي | fī | in, governing genitive |

### Worked examples

- `الْكِتَابُ جَدِيدٌ.` - `Al-kitābu jadīdun.`: The book is new, with explicit nominative endings on subject and predicate.
- `أَقْرَأُ الْكِتَابَ.` - `Aqraʾu al-kitāba.`: I read the book, with an explicit accusative object.
- `فِي الْكِتَابِ.` - `Fī al-kitābi.`: In the book, with genitive after the preposition.

### Common trap

Do not combine ال with ordinary nunation, such as an invented al-kitābun. Also, an omitted ending in simplified reading is not evidence that formal MSA has no case grammar.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Give the definite nominative form: the book, with final u.
2. Give the definite accusative form: the book, with final a.
3. Say fully inflected: in the book.
4. Give the indefinite accusative form: a book.

***Answers:***

1. `الْكِتَابُ.` - `Al-kitābu.`
2. `الْكِتَابَ.` - `Al-kitāba.`
3. `فِي الْكِتَابِ.` - `Fī al-kitābi.`
4. `كِتَابًا.` - `Kitāban.`

### Why these answers work

The first answer marks a nominative role, the second accusative. في governs genitive in the third. The fourth omits the definite article and includes accusative nunation with its standard supporting alif for this noun.

### Retrieve and recombine

Take one book through three roles: it is new, you read it, and a word is in it. Identify the expected case before looking at any final vowel mark.


## Lesson 11: Possession through an iḍāfa phrase

**Outcome:** identify a book's owner without inserting an Arabic word for of.

### Think first

If كتاب is book and الطالب is the student, what relation might كتاب الطالب express when the two nouns stand together?

### A linked noun construction

كتاب الطالب means “the student's book.” This is an iḍāfa, a construct phrase linking a possessed item to an owner or another specifying noun. The first noun does not take the definite article or ordinary nunation in the construct state. The second noun is genitive in fully inflected MSA. If the second noun is definite, the whole phrase is definite in this simple construction.

Compare كتاب طالب, “a student's book,” with كتاب الطالب, “the student's book.” The definiteness of the second noun changes the phrase's reference. There is no inserted word corresponding to English of. A personal name can also occupy the second position: كتاب مريم, Maryam's book.

A noun ending in ة is pronounced with t when it is the first member of the construct: سيارة مريم, sayyārat Maryam, Maryam's car. This explains why the same written tāʾ marbūṭa is not always read as isolated a. The connection makes its t audible in the teaching reading.

An adjective modifying the possessed noun normally follows the completed construct phrase and agrees with the relevant noun. That creates ambiguity in some longer phrases, so today's active examples keep possession chains short. Learn the two-noun relation clearly before adding multiple adjectives or owners.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| كتاب الطالب | kitāb aṭ-ṭālib | the male student's book |
| كتاب الطالبة | kitāb aṭ-ṭāliba | the female student's book |
| كتاب مريم | kitāb Maryam | Maryam's book |
| سيارة مريم | sayyārat Maryam | Maryam's car |
| باب المدرسة | bāb al-madrasa | the school door |
| مريم | Maryam | Maryam, personal name |
| أحمد | Aḥmad | Ahmad, personal name |

### Worked examples

- `كتاب الطالب هنا.` - `Kitāb aṭ-ṭālib hunā.`: The student's book is here; the first noun has no article.
- `سيارة مريم جديدة.` - `Sayyārat Maryam jadīda.`: Maryam's car is new; tāʾ marbūṭa is read t in the construct.
- `باب المدرسة كبير.` - `Bāb al-madrasa kabīr.`: The school door is big; the phrase is a definite subject.

### Common trap

Do not write الكتاب الطالب to mean the student's book. In the ordinary iḍāfa, the first noun lacks ال. Also, the second noun is not an adjective agreeing with the first; it supplies an ownership or specification relation.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: Ahmad's book is here.
2. Say: Maryam's car is small.
3. Produce the phrase: the female student's pen.
4. Say: The school door is new.

***Answers:***

1. `كتاب أحمد هنا.` - `Kitāb Aḥmad hunā.`
2. `سيارة مريم صغيرة.` - `Sayyārat Maryam ṣaghīra.`
3. `قلم الطالبة.` - `Qalam aṭ-ṭāliba.`
4. `باب المدرسة جديد.` - `Bāb al-madrasa jadīd.`

### Why these answers work

The owner follows the possessed noun without an of word. سيارة is read sayyārat before Maryam. قلم and باب remain without the article because they begin an iḍāfa. The adjectives describe the gender of the possessed item.

### Retrieve and recombine

Build an ownership phrase for a book and a car belonging to the same person. Explain why only the car makes the tāʾ marbūṭa connection audible.


## Lesson 12: My book, your name and having

**Outcome:** use attached possessive pronouns and express simple possession with عند.

### Think first

If كتابي means my book, where is the owner encoded? Could the same kind of suffix attach to a word meaning at to create an everyday possession statement?

### Pronoun suffixes

The suffix ي marks my: كتابي, kitābī, my book; اسمي, ismī, my name. Masculine singular your is كَ, ka, and feminine singular your is كِ, ki: كتابكَ, kitābuka, and كتابكِ, kitābuki in the fully connected possessive citation forms used here. These explicit possessive citation forms show nominative u before the pronoun suffix. Other case environments change that vowel; the forms are not presented as case-free replacements for every occurrence of your book.

His is ه, read hu after suitable vowels, and her is ها, hā. كتابه can be read kitābuhu in a nominative fully inflected phrase, with vowel changes in other case environments. To avoid implying one case-free full form applies everywhere, the table focuses on first-person and the stable location-possession expressions.

عندي means “I have,” literally at me. عندك means you have, pronounced ʿindaka to a man and ʿindaki to a woman. عندي كتاب means “I have a book.” The book is the item whose existence is asserted, not an English-style object of have.

ما اسمك؟ asks your name, with ismuka to a man and ismuki to a woman in the explicit MSA readings. The short written vowel marks clarify the addressee distinction. These are not formality levels; a courteous question can add a greeting or suitable title.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| كتابي | kitābī | my book |
| اسمي | ismī | my name |
| عندي | ʿindī | I have |
| عندكَ | ʿindaka | you have, male singular |
| عندكِ | ʿindaki | you have, female singular |
| ما اسمكَ؟ | mā ismuka? | what is your name, to a man |
| ما اسمكِ؟ | mā ismuki? | what is your name, to a woman |

### Worked examples

- `اسمي مريم.` - `Ismī Maryam.`: My name is Maryam; the suffix marks the speaker's possession.
- `عندي كتاب.` - `ʿIndī kitāb.`: I have a book; عندي introduces the possessor.
- `هل عندكِ قلم؟` - `Hal ʿindaki qalam?`: Do you have a pen, addressed to a woman?

### Common trap

Do not replace MSA suffix vowels with familiar dialectal ones without labelling a variety change. Also, the suffix in عندكَ differs from عندكِ according to the addressee, while عندي does not change for the speaker's gender.

***Answers:***

### Why these answers work

اسمي and عندي identify the speaker through ي. عندكَ selects a male addressee, and اسمكِ selects a female addressee. هل asks availability, while ما asks for the unknown name. Neither question requires a present be verb.

## Lesson 13: Time words and daily activity

**Outcome:** place a familiar action today, tomorrow or at a time of day.

### Think first

If أدرس can mean I study or I am studying, what does الآن add? Would every English time phrase need the same Arabic preposition?

### Let time expressions guide interpretation

اليوم means today, غدًا means tomorrow and أمس means yesterday. الآن means now. These common adverbial expressions can locate a clause without a separate preposition. أنا أدرس اليوم means “I study today” or “I am studying today,” depending on context.

في الصباح means in the morning, and في المساء means in the evening. The preposition belongs to those expressions. كل يوم means every day, with kull yawmin in fully inflected connected speech; our simplified reading kull yawm omits the final case ending. Do not place في before every time expression merely because English sometimes uses in or on.

MSA present forms can describe repeated activity as well as an action underway. أنا أقرأ كل يوم gives a habit. أنا أقرأ الآن gives a current event. There is no mandatory dialectal b- prefix in these MSA examples, and adding one would shift the variety rather than create a more correct MSA present.

A time expression can come at the beginning or later in a sentence according to emphasis. We use subject, verb, object, time in several examples so the new phrase simply extends a familiar structure. Beginning with اليوم can instead make today the explicit frame for what follows.

### Working vocabulary

|---|---|---|
| اليوم | al-yawm | today |
| غدًا | ghadan | tomorrow |
| أمس | ams | yesterday |
| الآن | al-ān | now |
| في الصباح | fī aṣ-ṣabāḥ | in the morning |
| في المساء | fī al-masāʾ | in the evening |
| كل يوم | kull yawm | every day |
| يوم | yawm | day |

### Worked examples

- `أنا أقرأ كل يوم.` - `Anā aqraʾ kull yawm.`: I read every day; the time phrase indicates a habit.
- `هي تدرس الآن.` - `Hiya tadrus al-ān.`: She is studying now; the current frame is explicit.
- `أنا أشرب الشاي في الصباح.` - `Anā ashrab ash-shāy fī aṣ-ṣabāḥ.`: I drink tea in the morning; the phrase uses في.

### Common trap

Do not add في before الآن or كل يوم in the basic expressions taught here. Also, a regional present prefix heard in a dialect is not silently inserted into a standard MSA sentence.

## Lesson 14: Past actions and suffix agreement

**Outcome:** report a simple past event with common sound verbs.

### Think first

Present أكتب identifies I with a prefix. In كتبتُ, katabtu, where does the first-person information appear instead?

### Past agreement follows the stem

The basic past of كتب gives كتبَ, kataba, he wrote; كتبتْ, katabat, she wrote; كتبتُ, katabtu, I wrote; and كتبنا, katabnā, we wrote. The short marks in the teaching forms help distinguish words that share the same ordinary consonant spelling. The first-person form does not change with the speaker's gender.

A male addressee wrote is كتبتَ, katabta, while a female addressee wrote is كتبتِ, katabti. The final vowel is part of the personal suffix, not a noun case ending, so it must be retained in the reading. Compare these with present أنتِ تكتبين, where both prefix and suffix identify the person.

درس gives درستُ, darastu, I studied. ذهب gives ذهبتُ, dhahabtu, I went. The internal vowels belong to the verb's learned past stem; do not assume every root can be supplied with the same vowels by guesswork.

أمس sets a clear past frame: درستُ أمس, “I studied yesterday.” The past form can be used without an explicit أنا because its suffix identifies the speaker. Objects and prepositional phrases retain their grammatical roles. ذهبتُ إلى المدرسة describes a past journey, with إلى still marking its destination.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| كتبَ | kataba | he wrote |
| كتبتْ | katabat | she wrote |
| كتبتُ | katabtu | I wrote |
| كتبتَ | katabta | you male singular wrote |
| كتبتِ | katabti | you female singular wrote |
| كتبنا | katabnā | we wrote |
| درستُ | darastu | I studied |
| ذهبتُ | dhahabtu | I went |

### Worked examples

- `كتبتُ الرسالة.` - `Katabtu ar-risāla.`: I wrote the message; the suffix identifies the speaker.
- `هي درستْ أمس.` - `Hiya darasat ams.`: She studied yesterday; the feminine past has at.
- `ذهبنا إلى المدرسة.` - `Dhahabnā ilā al-madrasa.`: We went to school; nā marks we.

### Common trap

Do not drop the final vowel distinguishing katabtu, katabta and katabti. These are personal endings, unlike the short case vowels omitted in the simplified noun readings. Ordinary unvocalised spelling relies on context to distinguish them.

### Practice

2. Say to a woman: You wrote the message.
3. Say: We wrote.
4. Say: I went home. Use إلى البيت.

***Answers:***

1. `درستُ أمس.` - `Darastu ams.`
2. `أنتِ كتبتِ الرسالة.` - `Anti katabti ar-risāla.`
3. `كتبنا.` - `Katabnā.`
4. `ذهبتُ إلى البيت.` - `Dhahabtu ilā al-bayt.`

### Why these answers work

درستُ and ذهبتُ use the first-person tu suffix. كتبتِ matches the female addressee, while كتبنا marks a group including the speaker. Destination إلى and the definite object الرسالة retain their roles in the past sentences.

### Retrieve and recombine

Retell a present writing sentence as yesterday's event, then address the same event to a man and a woman. Explain how person information moves from the present prefix pattern to past suffixes.


## Lesson 15: Was and earlier locations

**Outcome:** describe where someone was and recognise how كان changes a nominal sentence.

### Think first

أنا في البيت means I am at home. What explicit verb becomes necessary when the location belongs to yesterday rather than the present?

### كان supplies past time

كان, kāna, means was for a masculine singular subject in the basic pattern. كانت, kānat, is feminine singular. كنتُ, kuntu, means I was, and كنا, kunnā, means we were. Thus كنتُ في البيت أمس means “I was at home yesterday.” The prepositional location remains unchanged.

The first-person form كنتُ is the same for a male or female speaker. For a male addressee, كنتَ is kunta; for a female addressee, كنتِ is kunti. These suffix distinctions resemble the past-action pattern, but the stem changes from kān- to kun- before certain suffixes. Learn the common forms directly.

كان also affects the case of a nominal predicate. In fully inflected MSA, a noun or adjective predicate is commonly accusative after كان, while the subject remains nominative. Location phrases are a useful first practice because their prepositions continue to govern genitive without requiring a new adjective ending in every answer.

The present sentence omits an overt be verb, but the past sentence normally includes it. This is a tense-sensitive construction, not a universal Arabic rule that be is always absent. Comparing present and past locations makes the difference visible while keeping the same place vocabulary.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| كان | kāna | he was |
| كانت | kānat | she was |
| كنتُ | kuntu | I was |
| كنتَ | kunta | you male singular were |
| كنتِ | kunti | you female singular were |
| كنا | kunnā | we were |
| أمس | ams | yesterday |
| في المدرسة | fī al-madrasa | at school |

### Worked examples

- `كنتُ في البيت أمس.` - `Kuntu fī al-bayt ams.`: I was at home yesterday; كنتُ supplies past time.
- `هي كانت في المدرسة.` - `Hiya kānat fī al-madrasa.`: She was at school; كانت is feminine.
- `كنا هنا.` - `Kunnā hunā.`: We were here; the past form identifies the group.

### Common trap

Do not use كان for every subject without changing it. Also, the first-person stem is kun- in كنتُ, not a mechanically copied kān- plus tu. Past predication has its own learned forms.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I was at school.
2. Say: He was at home.
3. Ask a woman: Were you here?
4. Say: We were at home yesterday.

***Answers:***

1. `كنتُ في المدرسة.` - `Kuntu fī al-madrasa.`
2. `هو كان في البيت.` - `Huwa kāna fī al-bayt.`
3. `هل كنتِ هنا؟` - `Hal kunti hunā?`
4. `كنا في البيت أمس.` - `Kunnā fī al-bayt ams.`

### Why these answers work

كنتُ marks the speaker, كان agrees with هو, and كنتِ identifies a female addressee in the question. كنا expresses we. The locations remain prepositional phrases or the adverb هنا, so their basic structure does not change.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe your location now and yesterday, using the same place once. Explain why the present sentence can omit be while the past version needs the appropriate كان form.


## Lesson 16: Future reference with سـ and سوف

**Outcome:** state a future action with a common MSA future marker.

### Think first

أكتب is a present form. If سـ attaches before it, does the person prefix disappear or remain part of the verb?

### A future marker before the present form

سـ attaches directly to an indicative present form: سأكتب, sa-aktub in a segmented explanation and saʾaktub in the reading, “I will write.” سيكتب means he will write, and سنكتب means we will write. The present person marking remains inside the future expression.

سوف is a separate word that can also introduce future reference: سوف أدرس, “I will study.” Both forms are MSA. They can differ in discourse and stylistic preference, but a beginner should not impose a rigid rule that سـ always means soon and سوف always means a distant future. Context supplies the time scale.

غدًا, tomorrow, makes the future frame explicit: سأذهب إلى المدرسة غدًا, “I will go to school tomorrow.” The destination and object patterns remain unchanged. A future reading sentence still has a reading object, and a future journey still has a destination.

To ask about a female addressee's future action, the ordinary indicative ending remains: هل ستكتبين؟, “Will you write?” This provides a useful check that the future prefix does not flatten all personal forms. Negating a future action uses another construction, introduced in the next lesson, rather than simply assuming every positive marker survives unchanged after not.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| سأكتب | saʾaktub | I will write |
| سيكتب | sayaktub | he will write |
| ستكتبين | sataktubīn | you female singular will write |
| سنكتب | sanaktub | we will write |
| سوف | sawfa | separate future marker |
| سأذهب | saʾadhhab | I will go |
| سأدرس | saʾadrus | I will study |
| غدًا | ghadan | tomorrow |

### Worked examples

- `سأكتب الرسالة غدًا.` - `Saʾaktub ar-risāla ghadan.`: I will write the message tomorrow; the speaker prefix remains.
- `سوف أدرس في البيت.` - `Sawfa adrus fī al-bayt.`: I will study at home; سوف is separate.
- `هل ستكتبين؟` - `Hal sataktubīn?`: Will you write, addressed to a woman? The īn ending remains.

### Common trap

Do not delete the personal prefix after سـ. Also, سـ is attached in Arabic spelling, while سوف is a separate word. Neither form alone guarantees a precise near-versus-distant future distinction.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I will go home tomorrow.
2. Say: We will study.
3. Ask a woman: Will you read the book?
4. Say using سوف: I will write.

***Answers:***

1. `سأذهب إلى البيت غدًا.` - `Saʾadhhab ilā al-bayt ghadan.`
2. `سندرس.` - `Sanadrus.`
3. `هل ستقرئين الكتاب؟` - `Hal sataqraʾīn al-kitāb?`
4. `سوف أكتب.` - `Sawfa aktub.`

### Why these answers work

سأذهب preserves the first-person form after the attached future marker. سندرس keeps the we prefix. ستقرئين retains the female-addressee ending and hamza spelling. سوف أكتب illustrates the separate future word with the same person-marked verb.

### Retrieve and recombine

Make tomorrow's plan with سـ, then restate one action with سوف. Change the addressee to a woman and check that the future marker has not removed the required personal ending.


## Lesson 17: Negative past and future: لم and لن

**Outcome:** deny a completed past event and a future event using the appropriate MSA particles.

### Think first

If لم precedes a present-shaped verb but refers to the past, can the visible verb shape alone determine the sentence's time meaning?

### The particle changes the construction

لم plus a jussive verb commonly negates a past event: لم أكتب, “I did not write.” The verb has present-style person marking but the whole construction is past negative. For a regular sound verb, the final indicative u is absent in the jussive. Our usual simplified reading already omits that short vowel, so the distinction may not be audible in the basic first-person example; the grammar is still different.

لن plus a subjunctive verb negates a future event: لن أكتب, “I will not write.” A fully vocalised sound-verb form has final a after لن, as لَنْ أَكْتُبَ. The simplified main reading omits that short mood vowel, but this lesson names it explicitly rather than pretending mood does not exist.

In the female-addressee forms, the difference is more visible. تكتبين becomes تكتبي after لم or لن, losing the indicative n. لم تكتبي means you, a woman, did not write; لن تكتبي means you will not write. The long ī remains part of the personal form.

These are MSA patterns. Dialectal negative systems may use other particles, prefixes or suffixes. Do not mix them into the standard sentences without deliberately changing the variety being learned.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| لم | lam | past-negating particle with jussive |
| لن | lan | future-negating particle with subjunctive |
| لم أكتب | lam aktub | I did not write |
| لن أكتب | lan aktub | I will not write |
| لم تكتبي | lam taktubī | you female singular did not write |
| لن تكتبي | lan taktubī | you female singular will not write |
| لَنْ أَكْتُبَ | lan aktuba | explicitly vocalised future negative |

### Worked examples

- `لم أدرس أمس.` - `Lam adrus ams.`: I did not study yesterday; لم gives past negative meaning.
- `لن أذهب غدًا.` - `Lan adhhab ghadan.`: I will not go tomorrow; لن marks future negation.
- `أنتِ لم تكتبي الرسالة.` - `Anti lam taktubī ar-risāla.`: You did not write the message, addressed to a woman; n is dropped.

### Common trap

Do not keep تكتبين unchanged after لم or لن in the standard forms taught here. Also, a present-shaped verb after لم does not make the sentence present in meaning.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I did not read the book.
2. Say: I will not study tomorrow.
3. Say to a woman: You will not write.
4. Say: We did not go.

***Answers:***

1. `لم أقرأ الكتاب.` - `Lam aqraʾ al-kitāb.`
2. `لن أدرس غدًا.` - `Lan adrus ghadan.`
3. `أنتِ لن تكتبي.` - `Anti lan taktubī.`
4. `لم نذهب.` - `Lam nadhhab.`

### Why these answers work

لم gives past negative meaning to أقرأ and نذهب in jussive constructions. لن gives future negative meaning. تكتبي drops the indicative n while retaining the long ī identifying the female addressee. The objects and time expressions stay independent.

### Retrieve and recombine

Deny the same writing event once as yesterday's event and once as tomorrow's plan. Then address both statements to a woman and identify the visible ending change.


## Lesson 18: Wanting and an action clause

**Outcome:** express a desire using أريد أن followed by the appropriate verb form.

### Think first

English want to read uses an infinitive. If Arabic instead says “I want that I read,” what information must the second verb still carry?

### أن introduces the desired action

أريد means I want. أريد أن أقرأ الكتاب means “I want to read the book.” أن introduces a subjunctive verb, and the second verb still marks its subject. The first-person prefix in أقرأ is therefore not redundant grammar to remove. MSA does not have an infinitive identical to the English to-plus-verb construction.

In a fully vocalised sound-verb example, أريد أن أكتب has أكتبَ after أن, with final subjunctive a. Our simplified reading omits the short final mood vowel, but the rule remains active. With a female addressee, هل تريدين أن تكتبي؟ keeps the first verb's indicative n but drops it from the second verb after أن. This is a useful visible contrast inside one sentence.

أحب means I like or love. أحب القراءة means “I like reading,” using the verbal noun القراءة. That is another way to refer to an action as a concept. Verbal nouns have their own vocabulary patterns, so this lesson does not pretend every English ing form can be generated mechanically from a root.

A want statement is not automatically the most courteous service request. You can add considerate wording or use a request pattern learned later. First make the grammar clear: identify the desire-holder, then the subject and form of the desired action.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| أريد | urīd | I want |
| يريد | yurīd | he wants |
| تريدين | turīdīn | you female singular want |
| أن | an | introduces a subjunctive action clause |
| أحب | uḥibb | I like; I love |
| القراءة | al-qirāʾa | reading, verbal noun |
| الكتابة | al-kitāba | writing, verbal noun |

### Worked examples

- `أريد أن أقرأ الكتاب.` - `Urīd an aqraʾ al-kitāb.`: I want to read the book; the second verb still marks I.
- `أحب القراءة.` - `Uḥibb al-qirāʾa.`: I like reading; القراءة is a verbal noun.
- `هل تريدين أن تكتبي؟` - `Hal turīdīn an taktubī?`: Do you want to write, addressed to a woman? Only the second verb follows أن.

### Common trap

Do not treat أن as a label that allows a bare root afterward. The following verb remains person-marked and takes subjunctive form. Also, the first verb in تريدين أن تكتبي does not lose its n merely because the second one does.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I want to go home.
2. Say: I like writing.
3. Ask a woman: Do you want to read?
4. Say: He wants to study.

***Answers:***

1. `أريد أن أذهب إلى البيت.` - `Urīd an adhhab ilā al-bayt.`
2. `أحب الكتابة.` - `Uḥibb al-kitāba.`
3. `هل تريدين أن تقرئي؟` - `Hal turīdīn an taqraʾī?`
4. `هو يريد أن يدرس.` - `Huwa yurīd an yadrus.`

### Why these answers work

The desired action agrees with its subject after أن. تقرئي drops the indicative n after أن, while تريدين remains indicative. الكتابة is a verbal noun serving as the object of أحب. The destination remains attached to the going action.

### Retrieve and recombine

State an action you like in general and a different action you want to do today. Explain the distinction between a verbal noun and a person-marked clause after أن.


## Lesson 19: Ability, need and practical help

**Outcome:** say you can act, state a need and ask whether someone can help.

### Think first

Does wanting to read prove that you are able to read? Which verb should carry the difference between desire and ability while keeping the following action clause familiar?

### Ability with يستطيع

أستطيع means I can or I am able. أستطيع أن أقرأ means “I can read.” The following أن clause works as in the previous lesson: its verb retains person information and takes the appropriate subjunctive form. هو يستطيع أن يكتب means he can write.

أحتاج إلى means I need, followed by the needed thing in this construction. أحتاج إلى الماء means “I need water.” The preposition إلى governs the noun rather than creating a direct object pattern identical to English need. أحتاج إلى المساعدة means “I need help,” with المساعدة as a noun.

هل تستطيع أن تساعدني؟ asks a male addressee “Can you help me?” تساعدني contains the object suffix ني, me. A female addressee requires هل تستطيعين أن تساعديني؟, with the second verb losing its indicative n after أن before the object suffix. This longer form is introduced as a complete contrast, while the exercises keep most active production shorter.

A can question can function as a request in context. It does not merely test physical capability. Adding please-like wording can soften it further, but clarity and an appropriate tone matter too. MSA polite interaction uses grammatical gender, suitable address and phrasing without a universal informal-versus-formal singular pronoun pair.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| أستطيع | astaṭīʿ | I can |
| يستطيع | yastaṭīʿ | he can |
| أحتاج إلى | aḥtāj ilā | I need, with a preposition |
| المساعدة | al-musāʿada | help; assistance |
| تساعدني | tusāʿidanī | you help me, male addressee after أن |
| من فضلكَ | min faḍlika | please, to a man |
| من فضلكِ | min faḍliki | please, to a woman |

### Worked examples

- `أستطيع أن أقرأ.` - `Astaṭīʿ an aqraʾ.`: I can read; the ability verb introduces an action clause.
- `أحتاج إلى المساعدة.` - `Aḥtāj ilā al-musāʿada.`: I need help; the noun follows إلى.
- `هل تستطيع أن تساعدني؟` - `Hal tastaṭīʿ an tusāʿidanī?`: Can you help me, addressed to a man? The object suffix means me.

### Common trap

Do not omit إلى from the taught need construction. Also, helping me is not expressed by a separate nominative أنا after the verb; the object suffix ني marks the person receiving help.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I can write.
2. Say: I need water.
3. Ask a man: Can you help me?
4. Say: I cannot read.

***Answers:***

1. `أستطيع أن أكتب.` - `Astaṭīʿ an aktub.`
2. `أحتاج إلى الماء.` - `Aḥtāj ilā al-māʾ.`
3. `هل تستطيع أن تساعدني؟` - `Hal tastaṭīʿ an tusāʿidanī?`
4. `لا أستطيع أن أقرأ.` - `Lā astaṭīʿ an aqraʾ.`

### Why these answers work

أستطيع and لا أستطيع distinguish ability and inability while retaining the action clause. إلى introduces the needed noun. تساعدني includes the me object suffix, and its preceding أن explains the subjunctive form in the explicit reading.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe one ability and one practical need in an imagined study situation. Then ask another person for help, identifying the verb that expresses ability and the suffix that identifies the recipient.


## Lesson 20: Respectful requests and imperatives

**Outcome:** ask someone to write, open a door or repeat, while matching the addressee.

### Think first

If a male listener is told اكتب and a female listener اكتبي, what part of the request changes even when the desired action is identical?

### Imperatives have person and gender

اكتب, uktub, is the masculine singular command write. اكتبي, uktubī, addresses one woman. The initial vowel helps pronounce the consonant cluster at the start of this imperative. It belongs to the learned verb pattern; not every Arabic imperative begins with the same vowel.

افتح, iftaḥ, means open to a man, and افتحي, iftaḥī, to a woman. The different initial vowel illustrates why an imperative should be learned with its verb rather than guessed from one example. افتح الباب requests opening the door, with الباب as the direct object.

من فضلكَ or من فضلكِ means please according to the addressee. It can accompany a command, but a considerate tone and context remain important. A question with تستطيع can be a more indirect alternative for a substantial favour.

كرر, karrir, means repeat to a man, and كرري, karrirī, to a woman. This is a derived verb pattern with doubled consonants; do not remove the doubling because the unvocalised spelling looks short. مرة أخرى means one more time. The conventional thank-you شكرا is read shukran and retains its familiar accusative ending as a fixed expression.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| اكتب | uktub | write, to one man |
| اكتبي | uktubī | write, to one woman |
| افتح | iftaḥ | open, to one man |
| افتحي | iftaḥī | open, to one woman |
| كرر | karrir | repeat, to one man |
| كرري | karrirī | repeat, to one woman |
| مرة أخرى | marra ukhrā | one more time |
| شكرا | shukran | thank you |

### Worked examples

- `اكتب الرسالة من فضلكَ.` - `Uktub ar-risāla min faḍlika.`: Please write the message, addressed to a man.
- `افتحي الباب من فضلكِ.` - `Iftaḥī al-bāb min faḍliki.`: Please open the door, addressed to a woman.
- `كرر مرة أخرى من فضلكَ.` - `Karrir marra ukhrā min faḍlika.`: Please repeat one more time, addressed to a man.

### Common trap

Do not use a masculine imperative with a female singular addressee in the standard pattern taught here. Also, the initial vowel of اكتب differs from افتح, so one memorised imperative vowel cannot be applied to every verb.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Ask a woman: Please write.
2. Ask a man: Please open the door.
3. Ask a woman: Please repeat.
4. Say: Thank you.

***Answers:***

1. `اكتبي من فضلكِ.` - `Uktubī min faḍliki.`
2. `افتح الباب من فضلكَ.` - `Iftaḥ al-bāb min faḍlika.`
3. `كرري من فضلكِ.` - `Karrirī min faḍliki.`
4. `شكرا.` - `Shukran.`

### Why these answers work

The feminine imperatives retain long ī, and their please phrase uses ki. The masculine open request uses iftaḥ and ka. شكرا is a fixed courtesy expression, not a personal verb form that changes for the listener.

### Retrieve and recombine

Ask two people of different genders to perform the same small action, then thank them. Explain the difference between grammatical addressee agreement and the social directness of the request.


## Lesson 21: Plurals and nonhuman agreement

**Outcome:** recognise common broken plurals and describe a group of nonhuman objects.

### Think first

كتاب becomes كتب in the plural. If the inside of the word changes, can an English-style plural suffix rule explain the whole pattern?

### Several plural systems

Arabic has sound plurals and broken plurals. Broken plurals change the internal word pattern: كتاب, kitāb, becomes كتب, kutub, books; قلم, qalam, becomes أقلام, aqlām, pens; بيت, bayt, becomes بيوت, buyūt, houses. Learn a useful plural with its singular rather than assuming every noun takes one universal ending.

Many feminine nouns form a sound feminine plural with ات, as in طالبة to طالبات, female students. Human masculine sound plurals and dual forms have their own case-sensitive endings, introduced selectively elsewhere. A complete Arabic plural system is larger than any single table.

A central MSA agreement rule is that nonhuman plurals commonly take feminine singular agreement. الكتب جديدة means “the books are new,” using feminine singular جديدة. هذه كتب جديدة means “these are new books,” with feminine singular هذه. This is not a claim that the books are literally one female object; it is the standard grammatical agreement pattern.

Human plurals behave differently. Do not apply feminine singular agreement mechanically to a group of male students merely because it is plural. Today's active descriptions focus on books, pens and houses so the nonhuman pattern stays clear. The distinction depends on the noun's referent category, not just the visual shape of its plural.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| كتاب | kitāb | book |
| كتب | kutub | books, broken plural |
| أقلام | aqlām | pens, broken plural |
| بيوت | buyūt | houses, broken plural |
| طالبات | ṭālibāt | female students, sound plural |
| جديدة | jadīda | new, feminine singular |
| كبيرة | kabīra | big, feminine singular |
| هذه | hādhihi | this; these with nonhuman plurals |

### Worked examples

- `الكتب جديدة.` - `Al-kutub jadīda.`: The books are new; nonhuman plural takes feminine singular agreement.
- `هذه أقلام.` - `Hādhihi aqlām.`: These are pens; the demonstrative follows the nonhuman agreement pattern.
- `البيوت كبيرة.` - `Al-buyūt kabīra.`: The houses are big; the adjective remains feminine singular.

### Common trap

Do not use a masculine plural adjective merely because the English subject is plural. Nonhuman plurals commonly take feminine singular agreement in MSA. Also, broken plural shapes must be learned, not generated from one suffix.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: These are books.
2. Say: The pens are new.
3. Say: The houses are small.
4. Say: These books are new.

***Answers:***

1. `هذه كتب.` - `Hādhihi kutub.`
2. `الأقلام جديدة.` - `Al-aqlām jadīda.`
3. `البيوت صغيرة.` - `Al-buyūt ṣaghīra.`
4. `هذه الكتب جديدة.` - `Hādhihi al-kutub jadīda.`

### Why these answers work

هذه and the feminine singular adjectives agree with nonhuman plurals. The final answer uses a definite noun after the demonstrative to mean these books, while هذه كتب identifies the objects as books. Plurality and definiteness are separate choices.

### Retrieve and recombine

Describe two sets of objects using their learned broken plurals. Then explain why the adjective looks singular and why that rule should not be transferred automatically to human groups.


## Lesson 22: One, two and small counted groups

**Outcome:** recognise the dual and form a few carefully limited number phrases.

### Think first

If Arabic has a special form for two books, does it need the ordinary plural كتب after the number two?

### The dual and selected counts

The nominative dual commonly ends in ان, pronounced ān in simplified citation: كتابان, kitābān, two books. In accusative and genitive environments it ends in ين, pronounced ayn: كتابين, kitābayn. These endings include case information, unlike the simplified short vowels omitted earlier. We retain them because they are visible and grammatically meaningful.

One normally follows the noun and agrees with gender: كتاب واحد, one book; سيارة واحدة, one car. For numbers three through ten, a traditional gender-polarity pattern applies, and the counted noun is genitive plural. ثلاثة كتب means three books, while ثلاث سيارات means three cars. The number form differs according to the singular noun's gender.

This lesson teaches those specific phrases rather than a full counting system. Higher numbers, compound numbers and counted measures add further rules. Avoid guessing a restaurant quantity with an unlearned numeral pattern merely because you know the individual number words.

كم asks how many or how much. In a basic direct quantity question, it is followed by a singular accusative counted noun: كم كتابًا؟, how many books? The written accusative alif is visible. This contrast shows why Arabic counting cannot be reduced to number plus an unchanged English-style plural.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| كتاب واحد | kitāb wāḥid | one book |
| سيارة واحدة | sayyāra wāḥida | one car |
| كتابان | kitābān | two books, nominative dual |
| كتابين | kitābayn | two books, accusative or genitive dual |
| ثلاثة كتب | thalāthat kutub | three books |
| ثلاث سيارات | thalāth sayyārāt | three cars |
| كم كتابًا؟ | kam kitāban? | how many books? |

### Worked examples

- `عندي كتابان.` - `ʿIndī kitābān.`: I have two books; the available item is nominative dual.
- `هذا كتاب واحد.` - `Hādhā kitāb wāḥid.`: This is one book; one follows and agrees with the noun.
- `كم كتابًا عندكَ؟` - `Kam kitāban ʿindaka?`: How many books do you have, addressed to a man?

### Common trap

Do not use the nominative dual ending after every preposition or as every object. Also, the three-to-ten pattern does not match English number plus plural mechanically; the number's gender form and the counted noun's case both matter.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Produce the phrase: one car.
2. Say: I have two books.
3. Produce the phrase: three books.
4. Ask: How many books?

***Answers:***

1. `سيارة واحدة.` - `Sayyāra wāḥida.`
2. `عندي كتابان.` - `ʿIndī kitābān.`
3. `ثلاثة كتب.` - `Thalāthat kutub.`
4. `كم كتابًا؟` - `Kam kitāban?`

### Why these answers work

واحدة agrees with feminine سيارة. كتابان is nominative dual in the possession construction. ثلاثة كتب uses the learned three-to-ten pattern with a masculine singular source noun. كم takes singular accusative كتابًا in this direct count question.

### Retrieve and recombine

Compare one book, two books and three books as three different grammatical constructions. Identify which changes are number agreement, which are dual case endings and which involve a plural noun.


## Lesson 23: Connecting statements and giving a reason

**Outcome:** join familiar clauses with addition, contrast and a simple reason.

### Think first

If you already know I study and she writes, does adding and require both verbs to share one person prefix?

### Each clause keeps its grammar

و means and and is attached to the following word in Arabic spelling. أنا أدرس وهي تكتب means “I study and she writes.” The reading can show wa- as a prefix to make the connection visible. Each verb keeps its own subject agreement; the connector does not turn two different subjects into one shared person.

لكن means but. أريد أن أذهب، لكنني لا أستطيع means “I want to go, but I cannot.” لكنني includes a first-person suffix in this common construction. The difference between لكنْ as a lighter conjunction and لكنّ as a particle with case effects belongs to fuller grammar study; the lesson uses complete conventional forms rather than leaving you to guess their endings.

لأن means because and can take an attached pronoun: لأنني, because I; لأنه, because he; لأنها, because she. أنا في البيت لأنني أدرس means “I am at home because I am studying.” The reason follows the main statement in this example.

ثم means then and indicates a sequence more explicitly than a simple و. A speaker can say أقرأ ثم أكتب, “I read, then write.” Do not assume every and sentence proves chronological order. Choose the connector according to the relationship you actually want to communicate, and keep each clause short enough to check its person marking.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| و | wa | and, attached to the next word |
| لكن | lākin | but |
| لكنني | lākinnanī | but I |
| لأنني | liʾannanī | because I |
| لأنه | liʾannahu | because he |
| لأنها | liʾannahā | because she |
| ثم | thumma | then |

### Worked examples

- `أنا أدرس وهي تكتب.` - `Anā adrus wa-hiya taktub.`: I study and she writes; each clause retains its agreement.
- `أنا في البيت لأنني أدرس.` - `Anā fī al-bayt liʾannanī adrus.`: I am at home because I am studying.
- `أقرأ ثم أكتب.` - `Aqraʾ thumma aktub.`: I read, then write; ثم states the sequence.

### Common trap

Do not drop the second clause's person marking after و. Also, و is written attached to the following word, while ثم is separate. A simple and connection does not necessarily specify which event happened first.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I read and he writes.
2. Say: I study, then write.
3. Say: I am at home because I read.
4. Say: I want to go, but I cannot.

***Answers:***

1. `أنا أقرأ وهو يكتب.` - `Anā aqraʾ wa-huwa yaktub.`
2. `أدرس ثم أكتب.` - `Adrus thumma aktub.`
3. `أنا في البيت لأنني أقرأ.` - `Anā fī al-bayt liʾannanī aqraʾ.`
4. `أريد أن أذهب، لكنني لا أستطيع.` - `Urīd an adhhab, lākinnanī lā astaṭīʿ.`

### Why these answers work

و joins two independently agreeing clauses. ثم adds sequence. لأنني introduces a reason whose subject is the speaker. لكنني contrasts the earlier desire clause with an inability statement, preserving both constructions intact.

### Retrieve and recombine

Give a reason for an imagined study plan, then list two activities in a definite order. Explain why لأنني, و and ثم would not all express the same relationship.


## Lesson 24: A café request and a repair

**Outcome:** request a drink, ask its price and recover when the reply is unclear.

### Think first

You know أريد for wanting and من فضلك for please. Can these make a clear MSA service request while remaining distinct from a local café dialect?

### A formal cross-regional exchange

أريد الشاي من فضلكَ means “I would like tea, please,” addressed to a man. The same request to a woman ends من فضلكِ. In a particular café, local dialect may be more usual, but the MSA sentence is deliberately kept standard. The course does not claim this is the only natural phrase used in every Arabic-speaking city.

بكم هذا؟ asks “How much is this?” literally with how much. كم السعر؟ asks what the price is. السعر is pronounced as-siʿr because the article assimilates before س. A price question does not assert any current price, and the lesson supplies no real-world cost claim.

الحساب من فضلك means “the bill, please,” with the addressee ending chosen as before. شكرا thanks the server. These short service turns can be complete in context without an explicit verb in every sentence.

If you do not understand, لا أفهم means “I do not understand.” أعد من فضلكَ asks a man to repeat, from أعاد, with the imperative aʿid. To a woman, أعيدي is aʿīdī. This is a different verb from كرر, already learned; both have uses, but the practice retains familiar كرر forms for a predictable repair request. A clear repair is more useful than pretending that a partially understood price was certain.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| السعر | as-siʿr | the price |
| بكم هذا؟ | bikam hādhā? | how much is this |
| الحساب | al-ḥisāb | the bill; account |
| لا أفهم | lā afham | I do not understand |
| أعد | aʿid | repeat, to a man |
| أعيدي | aʿīdī | repeat, to a woman |
| الشاي | ash-shāy | tea |
| القهوة | al-qahwa | coffee |

### Worked examples

- `أريد القهوة من فضلكَ.` - `Urīd al-qahwa min faḍlika.`: I would like coffee, please, addressed to a man.
- `بكم هذا الكتاب؟` - `Bikam hādhā al-kitāb?`: How much is this book? The question names a specific item.
- `لا أفهم.` - `Lā afham.`: I do not understand; the statement opens a repair.

### Common trap

Do not silently replace MSA grammar with a regional dialect while still labelling the course standard Arabic. Also, the please ending follows the addressee, not the gender of coffee or tea.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Ask a woman: I would like tea, please.
2. Ask: How much is this?
3. Request the bill from a man.
4. Say: I do not understand.

***Answers:***

1. `أريد الشاي من فضلكِ.` - `Urīd ash-shāy min faḍliki.`
2. `بكم هذا؟` - `Bikam hādhā?`
3. `الحساب من فضلكَ.` - `Al-ḥisāb min faḍlika.`
4. `لا أفهم.` - `Lā afham.`

### Why these answers work

أريد expresses the desired item, and من فضلكِ addresses a woman regardless of the drink's grammatical gender. بكم asks price. The bill request is a context-supported noun phrase, while لا أفهم is an ordinary present negative verb sentence.

### Retrieve and recombine

Run a short MSA café exchange with an order, a price question, a repetition request and thanks. Then adapt the same exchange to a bookshop without inventing new grammar.


## Lesson 25: Cumulative review and a clear next step

**Outcome:** combine identity, possession, location, past and future in a short standard Arabic interaction.

### Think first

You have a book, you read it yesterday, and you want to write a message tomorrow. Which nouns are possessed items, which are direct objects, and which verbs need past or future marking?

### Reconstruct the relationships

Begin with a present identity such as أنا طالب or أنا طالبة. Locate yourself with أنا في البيت. The absence of a present linking verb is normal in both constructions. If the location was yesterday, use كنتُ في البيت أمس. The same place phrase survives while the predicate gains explicit past time.

Possession can use عندي كتاب. If you then read the book, it becomes a direct object in أقرأ الكتاب. In fully inflected MSA, those roles affect case even when ordinary spelling or the simplified teaching reading does not show a final vowel. Keep the case analysis available rather than treating the simplified reading convention as a new grammar.

For an action yesterday, use a learned past form such as كتبتُ. For tomorrow, سأكتب adds future marking to the person-marked present form. A negative past uses لم, while a negative future uses لن. The female-addressee ending changes after mood-governing particles, so a familiar-looking verb still needs attention to its grammatical environment.

Finally choose the social action: a statement of desire, a question about ability or a direct request with suitable courtesy. MSA and local dialects offer different resources, and a future dialect course should identify its variety explicitly. This foundation gives reliable building blocks and honest limits, not a claim of fluency or native review.

### Working vocabulary

| Native form | Reading | Meaning and use |
|---|---|---|
| أنا طالبة | anā ṭāliba | present identity, female student |
| عندي كتاب | ʿindī kitāb | I have a book |
| كنتُ في البيت | kuntu fī al-bayt | I was at home |
| كتبتُ | katabtu | I wrote |
| سأكتب | saʾaktub | I will write |
| لم أكتب | lam aktub | I did not write |
| لن أكتب | lan aktub | I will not write |
| من فضلكِ | min faḍliki | please, to a woman |

### Worked examples

- `عندي كتاب، وأريد أن أقرأه.` - `ʿIndī kitāb, wa-urīd an aqraʾahu.`: I have a book, and I want to read it; ه is the masculine object suffix in this explicit connected form.
- `كنتُ في المدرسة أمس.` - `Kuntu fī al-madrasa ams.`: I was at school yesterday; the past copula supplies the time relation.
- `سأكتب الرسالة غدًا.` - `Saʾaktub ar-risāla ghadan.`: I will write the message tomorrow; future marking preserves person agreement.

### Common trap

Do not treat the omitted short case and mood vowels as proof that MSA lacks those categories. Also, a familiar sentence from a regional dialect should be labelled before it is mixed into standard practice.

### Practice

Build the requested sentence before opening the answer key.

1. Say: I wrote the message yesterday.
2. Say: I want to study at home.
3. Ask a woman: Do you have a book?
4. Say: We will go to school tomorrow.

***Answers:***

1. `كتبتُ الرسالة أمس.` - `Katabtu ar-risāla ams.`
2. `أريد أن أدرس في البيت.` - `Urīd an adrus fī al-bayt.`
3. `هل عندكِ كتاب؟` - `Hal ʿindaki kitāb?`
4. `سنذهب إلى المدرسة غدًا.` - `Sanadhhab ilā al-madrasa ghadan.`

### Why these answers work

كتبتُ uses the first-person past suffix. أن introduces the desired action clause with its own first-person prefix. عندكِ marks a female addressee in a possession question. سنذهب combines future سـ with the we prefix, while إلى marks the destination.

### Retrieve and recombine

Create a five-turn encounter in a new study setting: introduce yourself, ask a location, mention possession, recount one event and request help. Explain one agreement choice, one case role and one reason the exchange remains MSA.
